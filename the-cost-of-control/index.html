The Cost of Control

As AI turns capital-intensive, value shifts from those who use intelligence to those who own its critical infrastructure and still earn ROIC above WACC.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 24, 2026 5 min read Capital

“The first race was about models, the second about capacity, the third is about returns.”

U.S. five-year Treasury yields are above 5%. That is the key benchmark: roughly 5% is what investors can earn on medium-term U.S. government debt before taking corporate, execution or equity risk. SoftBank is paying up to 9.75% to finance OpenAI. Microsoft is committing more than $10 billion to Gulf AI infrastructure. Meta is building proprietary hardware to reduce platform dependence. Washington and Beijing are negotiating over rare earths, technology access and AI governance.

The central thesis is simple: as AI becomes more capital-intensive, value shifts from those that merely use intelligence to those that can own its critical infrastructure and still generate returns above the cost of capital.

AI is becoming easier to access. The infrastructure required to monetize it is becoming harder—and more expensive—to own. That moves the competitive test from AI capability to economic capture: who owns the critical layers, who keeps the margin, and who earns ROIC above WACC?

The significance of a 5% five-year Treasury yield is not that every project must earn 5%. It means investors already have a roughly 5% sovereign alternative before taking additional risk.

01

Five Signals

From capability to economic capture

  1. SoftBank is issuing $11.1 billion of bonds to finance OpenAI, with dollar coupons reaching 9.75%. AI capital is no longer cheap. The U.S. five-year Treasury yield above 5% is the risk-free reference rate for medium-term dollar capital; a corporate borrower or AI infrastructure project must then pay an additional premium for credit risk, execution risk, illiquidity and equity risk. So 5% Treasury ≠ 5% WACC — and ROIC must exceed WACC. signal: Return on Invested Capital must exceed the Weighted Average Cost of Capital.

  2. Meta is moving into proprietary AI hardware to reduce dependence on third-party platforms. Value is migrating from model capability toward the customer interface. Agents that execute transactions shift economics toward whoever owns identity, distribution and checkout. The chain runs: agent → transaction → distribution → margin. signal: Favor platforms positioned closest to monetization.

  3. Microsoft is investing more than $10 billion across Gulf AI and cloud infrastructure. AI infrastructure is becoming critical infrastructure. Secure power, redundancy and connectivity determine uptime; uptime determines utilisation and revenue. The chain runs: capacity → resilience → uptime → cash flow. signal: Resilient infrastructure should command a premium over nominal capacity alone.

  4. Rare earths, AI governance, tariffs and market access are converging into one negotiation. Strategic dependencies are becoming bargaining instruments. Control of constrained inputs and market access increases negotiating power without requiring full separation. The chain runs: dependency → leverage → pricing power. signal: Assets located at geopolitical bottlenecks acquire strategic as well as financial value.

  5. Europe's proposed AI gigafactories require materially more recurring demand than current public procurement can support. Europe's constraint is not simply access to compute — it is the ability to aggregate demand, power and capital at continental scale. Subsidized capacity without sufficient utilisation creates weak economics; infrastructure serving existing industrial demand can monetize earlier and with greater visibility. Europe: infrastructure before frontier. Do not buy "European AI" indiscriminately — favor the layers where Europe already possesses industrial depth and monetizable demand. signal: Europe's investment edge is industrial conversion, not model imitation.

The Great Split

Control Owners vs. Control Renters

01

Control Owners

Own critical inputs. Secure energy. Own distribution. Finance at scale. Retain pricing power.

02

Control Renters

Buy critical inputs. Depend on external infrastructure. Rent distribution. Borrow at higher cost. Absorb higher input costs.

Key takeaways

The third race is about returns

  • The first AI race was about models, the second about capacity, the third is about returns.
  • 5% Treasury ≠ 5% WACC — projects must clear a real, risk-adjusted hurdle.
  • Value accrues to Control Owners of inputs, energy, distribution and financing.
  • Europe's strongest opportunity is industrial conversion, not frontier imitation.

Where Markets, Capital and Geopolitics Collide

The AI correction is no longer about valuations — it is becoming a test of financial endurance, as the selloff spreads into leverage, credit spreads and capital flows.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann November 6, 2026 4 min read Capital

“Technology determines the frontier of opportunity; capital productivity determines value; financial endurance determines who survives long enough to capture it.”

The signal1 min
  1. The AI correction is becoming a financing shock, spreading from equity valuations into leverage, credit spreads and cross-border capital flows.
  2. Wall Street erased ~$3 trillion on November 5; the Nasdaq fell 5.52% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped more than 12%.
  3. The shock reached Asia, with Korea's KOSPI off more than 8% and triggering a circuit breaker, Japan's Nikkei sharply lower.
  4. SoftBank's 5Y CDS hit 462 basis points, its highest since October 2022, reflecting a reassessment of AI financiers' creditworthiness.
  5. India drew ~$1.6 billion of net foreign equity buying, its largest single day in nearly six years, as capital rotates from crowded AI trades.
  6. Remain constructive on AI's productivity, but financial resilience and control of scarce capabilities now decide where capital accumulates.
Read the full note ↓

The most consequential development in global markets this morning is the transmission of the AI selloff from equity valuations into leveraged positions, credit-risk pricing and international capital flows. This is a potentially decisive inflection point.

Wall Street's November 5 collapse erased roughly $3 trillion from S&P 500 market capitalization. The market is no longer questioning only the valuation of AI-related earnings; it is beginning to reassess the creditworthiness of the companies financing the investment cycle. An equity correction redistributes wealth — a funding shock can redistribute future productive capacity.

01

Five Signals Shaping The Next Economy

from valuation to financing

  1. Thursday's US collapse triggered forced selling across Asian tech; Korea temporarily eased margin rules and the KOSPI recovered part of its losses. Hong Kong's Aspex ($14bn) lost ~16% in October. SoftBank's 5Y CDS rose 17.4bp to 462 — the chain runs equity losses → collateral pressure → forced selling → wider spreads → costlier financing → delayed investment. signal: The danger is markets constraining AI investment before technological demand deteriorates.

  2. The selloff obscures a structural shift: Yole expects advanced-packaging revenue to exceed traditional packaging for the first time in 2026, with capex near $28bn. China's YMTC targets up to 70% domestic equipment for a planned NAND plant — a target not yet achieved. signal: The next winners may be set by cost per unit of intelligence, not chip volume.

  3. Brent rebounded toward $100 (~$99.58) but remained on course for a ~12% weekly decline amid Strait of Hormuz disruptions. The Bank of England raised rates to 4.00% (8–1), seeing UK inflation near 5% by mid-2027; eurozone inflation hit 4.0%, energy inflation 20.8%. signal: A lower spot oil price does not immediately deliver easier monetary conditions.

  4. The ECB's October survey showed a net 9% tightening of corporate credit standards. ACER reported 15 of 18 countries expect higher 2030 grid investment — the Netherlands nearly five times 2025 levels — colliding with restrictive financing. signal: Europe's edge depends on integrating capital markets, energy infrastructure and industrial execution.

  5. Indian equities advanced as the Nifty 50 gained ~0.5% and foreign investors bought ~$1.6bn — the largest single day in almost six years — helped by limited AI exposure and lower oil. But one day is not yet a durable trend. signal: The rotation may broaden from financing intelligence to applying it across the real economy.

02

Three Scenarios For The Next Phase

illustrative three-month judgments

45%

The Selective Reset

AI valuations stabilize while financing remains available to strong borrowers; rotation toward cash flow and productivity.

35%

The Financing Feedback Loop

Wider credit spreads and weaker equities begin delaying investment; further pressure on leveraged infrastructure and suppliers.

20%

The Productivity Reacceleration

Strong demand and improving monetization restore confidence in returns; broader recovery across AI and industrial technology.

Key Takeaways

for investors, boards and policymakers

  • Today's US October payrolls (~+59,000 expected, unemployment near 4.2%) at 14:30 CET is the critical macro test.
  • Equity investors: the next AI opportunity will be determined by financing quality, not demand alone.
  • Credit investors: watch the transmission from equity valuations into funding conditions.
  • Corporate boards: financial resilience is becoming a strategic asset — preserve capacity to invest through volatility.
  • The Great Split is now about access to capital under stress.

The AI Supercycle is entering its most consequential test: whether the capital markets that accelerated the revolution can keep financing it as the price of risk rises — and the next great market rotation will follow that distinction.

The Next Economy Research Centre

The technology to produce intelligence is getting faster and cheaper, while the capital and power to deploy it grow more demanding.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 22, 2026 5 min read Capital

“The speed of intelligence creates opportunity; the cost of capital imposes discipline.”

The AI Supercycle has reached a consequential inflection: the technology required to produce intelligence is becoming faster and more efficient, while the financial and physical conditions required to deploy it are becoming more demanding.

Three developments define today's assessment. Nvidia has introduced an industrial architecture aimed at compressing AI infrastructure deployment from six months to as little as 14 days. Semiconductor-equipment firms continue reporting exceptional results. And Europe's financing environment has deteriorated materially, with France's 10-year yield reaching 5%.

The central question: can technological efficiency and industrial productivity improve fast enough to offset the higher cost of capital and the physical constraints limiting AI deployment? The next Great Split will fall between economies that merely finance computing and those that produce intelligence efficiently enough to earn returns above the rising cost of capital.

01

The Industrialization of the AI Factory

Accelerated computing

At GTC Berlin on October 21, Jensen Huang unveiled DSX POD, a factory-integrated system combining Nvidia's Vera Rubin architecture with preassembled networking, power distribution and liquid cooling. Nvidia claims deployment at a prepared site can fall from roughly six months to as little as two weeks, with commercial availability expected in early 2027. The claim applies only to infrastructure-ready sites — it does not eliminate grid, planning or permitting delays.

The relevant metric is becoming time to productive intelligence: the interval between committing capital and generating commercially valuable output.

Upstream earnings confirm the cycle's strength. Lam Research reported record revenue of $8.22 billion (up from $6.72B), a 40% GAAP operating margin, and December-quarter guidance of $8.6 billion ±$400M. Besi reported Q3 orders of €342.6 million, +96% year over year and above the €298M consensus. The asymmetry matters: suppliers monetize equipment demand now; operators must still monetize the capacity that equipment creates.

02

Toward Autonomous Industrial Operations

Productivity

Nvidia also introduced OpenShell, a reference architecture for governed enterprise AI agents, naming Siemens, SAP, Dassault Systèmes and major financial institutions among early developers. A related announcement with Nokia extends accelerated computing into telecom infrastructure, with Deutsche Telekom, BT and Elisa among operators running AI-RAN trials — broader commercial deployment is planned for 2027.

These are architectural advances, not yet evidence of widespread autonomous operations. Siemens' reported gains — up to 85% less engineering effort and 40% shorter production-introduction times — remain application-specific. There is no new economy-wide productivity acceleration today.

03

The Electricity Constraint

Energy & grids

Nvidia says its MaxLPS software can deliver equal output on up to 30% less power, and that a 2 GW deployment could save roughly 54 million liters of water daily versus conventional cooling. An Nscale evaluation in Iceland achieved 49% higher inference throughput within the same 264-kilowatt power budget — an important but limited test. These developments raise output within existing electricity allocations; they do not resolve grid access, as Australia's NextDC showed with M3 Melbourne delays pushed to FY2028. Lower power per token does not automatically mean lower aggregate demand.

04

The Bond Market Becomes a Strategic Constraint

Capital & the Great Split

France's benchmark 10-year OAT reached approximately 5% on October 21 — its highest since 2002 — up around eight basis points on the session and more than 20 over the week. The spread over German bunds traded near 150 basis points, approaching a 15-year high near 160.

The transmission is not automatic, but sovereign yields anchor the pricing of long-duration industrial, energy and technology investment. Strong balance sheets retain flexibility; leveraged developers face more demanding conditions.

Tesla illustrates the counterpoint: it cut 2026 capex to roughly $20 billion from a prior outlook above $25B, posted Q3 free cash flow of $1.53 billion — but net income fell 43% year over year. Positive cash flow partly reflects spending below plan, not improving profitability. The optimum is neither maximum capex nor maximum short-term cash, but investment earning sustainable returns above the cost of capital.

The technological clock is accelerating. The financial clock is becoming less forgiving.

05

Europe's Four Problems

Policy watch

Lam Research's revenue geography underscores Asian dominance: China 35%, South Korea 20%, Taiwan 18% — 73% combined — against Europe's 4%. Europe remains indispensable in lithography, industrial software, power systems and physical AI; Germany's Agile Robots plans 1,000–2,000 humanoid robots next year using Nvidia technology.

The EU's AI Gigafactories initiative targets up to seven facilities and over €30 billion (up to €10B public, at least €20B private) — a program target, not committed capital. Europe must address four interlocking problems simultaneously: computing access, energy readiness, capital compatible with sovereign stability, and productivity — converting computing into industrial returns. Public support should follow a measurable sequence: financing readiness → electricity readiness → commissioning → utilization → industrial value.

Key takeaways

Where advantage accrues

  • The AI infrastructure model is industrializing; judge DSX POD by real deployment schedules and cash flow, not claims.
  • Semiconductor suppliers keep capturing exceptional returns — distinct from leveraged infrastructure developers.
  • France's 5% yield makes sovereign financing a strategic constraint on long-duration investment.
  • Energy efficiency and grid readiness now determine capital productivity; technology cannot substitute for unavailable power.
  • The next rotation favors firms combining technological advantage with financial conversion.

The speed of intelligence creates opportunity. The cost of capital imposes discipline. The conversion of intelligence into productive cash flow determines who wins.

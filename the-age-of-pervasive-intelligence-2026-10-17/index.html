The Age of Pervasive Intelligence

Pervasive Agentic AI is reversing the sixty-year logic of computing — and the next Great Split will separate companies that build intelligent products from those that control the intelligence connecting them.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 17, 2026 9 min read Technology
60 yrsof application-centric computing now beginning to reverse
1985IBM 3090 — the mature expression of the mainframe era
50Toyota vehicle models to run the Arene software platform
~10Mnew Toyota vehicles annually on Arene by 2030

“The application becomes a tool. The device becomes a node. The agent becomes the interface.”

For sixty years, computing has evolved around one enduring principle: humans operate machines through applications. The human initiated, the application mediated, the machine executed. Pervasive Agentic AI is beginning to reverse that relationship.

The emerging paradigm is organized not around applications that users open, but around intelligent agents capable of interpreting intentions, understanding context, coordinating devices and executing tasks across digital and physical environments. This is not another generation of personal computing. It is the emergence of a new computational architecture — and potentially a new economic order.

The Apple–Meta collaboration reported this week is an early signal of that transition. Meta's Muse Spark Open model has been demonstrated on Apple's Mac Studio computing architecture using the MLX framework, while Meta extends multimodal intelligence into the physical world through AI-native smart glasses and Apple expands its integrated personal computing ecosystem. Their technologies are converging; their competitive ambitions are colliding.

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

What happens to economic power when intelligence is no longer confined to individual machines, applications or cloud platforms — but becomes pervasive across the physical world?

01

The End Of Computing's Organizing Principle

From instruction to objective

The mainframe centralized computing. The personal computer decentralized access. The internet connected information. The smartphone made applications mobile. The cloud recentralized computational resources. Generative AI introduced capable conversational interfaces. But the next transition is more fundamental.

Agentic AI changes the unit of computing from the instruction to the objective. Instead of opening several applications to organize a journey, the user delegates the desired outcome. The applications remain necessary — but they become resources invoked by agents rather than destinations selected by humans.

This introduces a new competitive hierarchy. The value of owning an application may decline relative to the value of controlling the agent that decides when and how it is used. The next great platform may not be the operating system that launches applications. It may be the intelligence system that orchestrates them.

02

Why This Time Is Different

Three structural shifts

Intelligence becomes goal-directed. Agentic computing can interpret an objective, break it into tasks, select tools, monitor results and adjust. The human role moves from direct operation toward delegation and supervision. This does not imply unrestricted autonomy — high-value deployment requires permissions, verification, accountability and reliable human intervention. But the relevant measure becomes the cost, reliability and value of a completed task, not application sessions or tokens consumed.

Intelligence becomes contextual. Smart glasses, vehicles, connected homes and industrial systems introduce access to physical context — visual, locational, environmental and operational. With appropriate permissions, computing moves from episodic interaction toward continuity of context.

Intelligence becomes distributed. No single device must perform every task: glasses provide perception, the smartphone manages identity, the PC supplies local inference, the cloud executes demanding reasoning, the vehicle provides physical execution. The strategic advantage increasingly lies in coordinating these capabilities. The next platform may be the system connecting devices — not any individual device.

03

Apple–Meta: Convergence Below, Collision Above

Selective interoperability, intense rivalry

The immediate achievement is technical: sophisticated models can be deployed on specialized local computing systems rather than exclusively through remote cloud. It does not establish that the same model runs economically on ordinary smartphones or glasses, nor does it constitute an announced consumer integration.

Still, the direction matters. Apple brings control of silicon, operating systems, devices, identity and software integration. Meta brings advanced AI research, open-weight distribution, social platforms and an expanding smart-glasses ecosystem. At the model and infrastructure layers their capabilities are complementary; at the interface layer their ambitions compete.

The strategic collision will emerge around personal context, agent permissions, identity, payments and the authority to execute actions. The most plausible architecture is therefore neither exclusive partnership nor complete separation — it is selective interoperability combined with intense platform rivalry.

04

The Automobile Becomes An Agentic Platform

The next battle is software architecture

The transformation is already visible in automotive investment. Toyota plans to deploy its Arene software platform across 50 vehicle models and roughly 10 million new vehicles annually by 2030. Volkswagen and Rivian are developing a shared zonal electronics and software architecture. Mercedes-Benz and Nvidia have extended their automated-driving collaboration. Rivian is building a proprietary vehicle-agent framework capable of supporting selected third-party AI agents. Apple's CarPlay Ultra shows how an external ecosystem can extend across vehicle displays and functions.

These initiatives differ in scope and maturity, but reveal a common direction: manufacturers increasingly compete over who controls the vehicle's software architecture — the foundation for future intelligent services. The traditional industry emphasized manufacturing scale, engineering, brand and physical performance. The emerging industry adds software control, computing architecture, customer identity, agentic orchestration, recurring services and fleet intelligence. The next frontier is not simply the software-defined vehicle; it is the agentically coordinated mobility ecosystem.

05

The Automotive Value Migration

Four economic layers

01

Layer I — Physical mobility

Vehicle engineering, batteries, powertrains, manufacturing, safety systems and distribution.

02

Layer II — Embedded intelligence

Operating systems, sensors, diagnostics, over-the-air updates, driver assistance and automated driving.

03

Layer III — Agentic orchestration

Personal mobility agents coordinating destinations, preferences, navigation, energy and authorized external interactions.

04

Layer IV — Distribution & monetization

Charging, parking, insurance, subscriptions, fleet management, payments and multimodal journey services. The migration of value occurs primarily between layers three and four. A platform controlling mobility intent can influence which vehicle is selected, which service is purchased and how the journey is organized — a new source of bargaining power. But the outcome is not predetermined: manufacturers retain real advantages in engineering, safety certification, embedded systems and customer relationships. The critical question is whether they retain the agentic layer or become dependent on external platforms.

06

Rivian: Testing An Open Agentic Architecture

A defining compromise

Rivian has described an in-house orchestration system routing requests between local vehicle models and cloud AI by task complexity, and plans to support selected external agents — while drawing an essential boundary: external agents will not receive authority over safety-critical steering or braking.

That distinction separates consumer agentic computing from autonomous vehicle control. A sophisticated conversational interface does not establish autonomous driving, nor does autonomous technology automatically create a profitable mobility-services platform. Rivian's approach — open the vehicle to external intelligence without surrendering control of its operating architecture — may become a defining competitive model.

07

The Great Split Enters The Physical Economy

Five second-order effects

First, the economic unit shifts from the device to the completed outcome. Second, software distribution becomes more powerful — the platform coordinating agents influences which services and providers are selected. Third, physical assets become programmable: vehicles, buildings, robots and infrastructure participate in coordinated workflows. Fourth, computing demand becomes more pervasive, raising demand for efficient processors, memory, networking, secure identity and edge infrastructure. Fifth, capital concentration may accelerate toward companies controlling orchestration, trusted interfaces and large ecosystems.

This creates a reinforcing loop: more devices → more authorized context → better agent execution → greater user reliance → stronger platform position. The loop is not inevitable — trust, interoperability, regulation and competition can constrain it. Three scenarios bracket the outcome: hybrid convergence (selective interoperability expands the market while preserving competition), platform concentration (a few companies control dominant interfaces and value migrates to identity, orchestration and recurring services), and fragmented ecosystems (privacy, cybersecurity and regulation slow seamless coordination). The decisive variables: interoperability, cost per completed task, user trust and the reliability of autonomous execution.

08

The Investor Hierarchy

Where value accumulates

The new paradigm implies a different hierarchy of value: Tier I — agentic operating environments controlling identity, permissions, orchestration and cross-device distribution; Tier II — AI-native interfaces (glasses, wearables, voice, contextual computing); Tier III — distributed computing infrastructure (efficient silicon, memory, networking, local inference, secure edge); Tier IV — intelligent physical systems (automotive, robotics, industrial automation, connected infrastructure); Tier V — productivity capture, companies applying agentic intelligence to measurable operating gains.

Key metrics will increasingly include cost per successfully completed agentic task, reliability, human-intervention requirements, interoperability, user retention, trusted access to personal context, recurring revenues and free-cash-flow conversion. The most vulnerable models rely on application-level distribution without differentiated data, workflow ownership or integration into agentic environments.

Five Key Takeaways For Investors

Positioning for the agent-centric economy

  • The agentic platform becomes the new control point. Prioritize companies that can orchestrate intelligence across devices and services while demonstrating retention and recurring returns.
  • The application economy faces a structural repricing. Differentiate software that owns critical business processes from software dependent mainly on user-interface engagement.
  • Automotive value will follow control of intelligence. Revalue manufacturers by software architecture, customer ownership, recurring-service economics and capital productivity — not vehicle deliveries alone.
  • Distributed AI creates a new infrastructure supercycle. The opportunity extends beyond hyperscale data centres to personal, enterprise, automotive and industrial computing; follow those reducing the cost of reliable inference.
  • The next Great Split is between ecosystem controllers and ecosystem dependents. The decisive test is converting ecosystem influence into sustainable free cash flow and returns on invested capital.
09

Final Take: The End Of The Application Empire

From the machines we operate to the intelligence that operates them

For sixty years the computer was the center of the system. Pervasive Agentic AI challenges that entire hierarchy. Intelligence will move across devices; agents will coordinate workflows; vehicles, homes, wearables and industrial machines will become participants in a distributed computational ecosystem. The manufacturer may continue to build the machine — but the agentic platform may increasingly control the relationship with the customer.

That migration of power extends far beyond automotive, reshaping banking, healthcare, retail, logistics, robotics and industrial infrastructure. The first computing age centralized processing; the second democratized access; the third connected people, applications and devices; the fourth will distribute intelligence throughout the physical world. The next battle will not be decided by who sells the most devices, builds the largest model or controls the most applications — but by who can coordinate intelligence across environments, earn the trust required to act, and convert autonomous execution into measurable economic value.

The future belongs not simply to those who build intelligent machines, but to those who control the trusted intelligence connecting machines, people and economic activity.

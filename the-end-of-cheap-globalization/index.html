The End of Cheap Globalization.

A new capital cycle is emerging around AI, defense, energy and industrial sovereignty.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann July 13, 2026 8 min read Geopolitics · Capital · Macro
36%TSMC sales growth — the AI cycle accelerating
$4.4Tcombined value of the leading AI giants
$650B+global AI infrastructure investment underway

“Every great capital cycle begins with a geopolitical shift that markets initially dismiss.”

What if the end of cheap globalization is not a geopolitical event — but the beginning of the largest reallocation of capital since the end of the Cold War? Markets are still debating inflation. The world has already moved on.

For more than three decades, global capital rewarded efficiency: optimized supply chains, outsourced production, minimized inventories, globalization at almost any cost. That world no longer exists. Today, resilience commands a premium. Strategic control is replacing operational efficiency. Governments are becoming strategic investors. Institutional capital is migrating toward assets that cannot be outsourced, duplicated or disrupted. Today’s headlines — renewed strikes involving Iran, volatile oil, TSMC’s surging revenues, Europe’s weakening outlook, China’s grip on critical minerals, accelerating defense spending and the race for sovereign space — are not separate stories. They are evidence of one structural transformation.

Not yet priced

Markets are still pricing volatility. They have not yet begun pricing the new economic order.

01

Five structural shifts

Tap each shift to expand the read.

  1. The latest escalation between the United States and Iran is about far more than oil — it marks the normalization of geopolitical uncertainty as a permanent feature of markets. The Strait of Hormuz still carries around 20% of global oil and LNG flows; every escalation raises insurance, shipping, inventories and long-term investment in energy security.

    Signal Oil is no longer the story — strategic uncertainty is, and the risk premium is becoming structural.

  2. TSMC’s 36% surge in sales sends one unmistakable message: the AI Supercycle is accelerating. Despite endless valuation debates, hyperscalers keep investing hundreds of billions into AI infrastructure. Just as electricity transformed the industrial economy, AI will transform the intelligent economy — and the companies building accelerated computing, AI factories, inference infrastructure and advanced memory are becoming the utilities of the Next Economy.

    Signal AI is no longer a technology cycle — it is becoming foundational infrastructure for every industry.

  3. Europe’s outlook is weakening while its strategic importance is rising. Its shortage of sovereign launch capabilities reveals a larger vulnerability: without sovereign access to space there is no sovereign AI, and without sovereign AI there is no technological sovereignty. Europe has world-class engineering, manufacturing, mobility, aerospace and energy — its challenge is no longer ambition, it is execution.

    Signal The next decade decides whether Europe becomes a strategic power or a strategic dependency.

  4. China keeps expanding its influence across critical minerals, mining assets and commodity pricing. The objective is no longer simply manufacturing — it is controlling the inputs on which every advanced economy depends. Critical minerals are becoming the oil of the AI age, and pricing power is moving upstream.

    Signal The next industrial revolution is increasingly determined before a factory even begins production.

  5. One of 2026’s most important rotations is happening almost unnoticed: capital is moving toward defense, energy infrastructure, industrial automation, healthcare, advanced manufacturing, drones and autonomous mobility. AI is leaving the cloud and entering the physical economy.

    Signal The next trillion-dollar opportunities come from building the infrastructure that lets intelligence operate in the real world.

02

What the market still doesn’t see

01

AI has become industrial infrastructure

No longer a technology sector — it is becoming the operating system of the physical economy.

02

Sovereignty is an investment factor

Governments are allocating capital, reshaping supply chains and redefining industrial policy. National resilience is becoming an investable asset.

03

Europe is more valuable than markets assume

Home to global champions in mobility, automation, aerospace, energy and engineering — combined with AI and defense, potentially a decade of upside surprise, with Spain strengthening fast.

04

Control is the new scarcity

The last thirty years rewarded efficiency; the next thirty reward control — of energy, semiconductors, AI infrastructure, critical minerals and space.

History shows every major geopolitical transition triggers a profound reallocation of capital. After World War II, capital financed reconstruction. After the fall of the Berlin Wall, it financed globalization. Today a third chapter is beginning — capital flowing toward energy security, AI infrastructure, semiconductors, critical minerals, defense, sovereign space and industrial resilience. This is not another investment cycle. It is a new economic architecture.

Key numbers to watch

The indicators of the reallocation

  • 36% — TSMC sales growth, confirming the strength of the AI infrastructure cycle.
  • ~20% — share of global oil and LNG flows transiting the Strait of Hormuz.
  • $4.4T — combined market value of the leading AI companies, an unprecedented concentration.
  • $650B+ — estimated global AI infrastructure investment now underway.
  • €200B — European AI and strategic industrial investment initiatives.
  • China’s strategic mineral investments and NATO defense spending — structural indicators of the shift to resilience.
Key takeaways

What to carry into the next session

  • The era of cheap globalization is over.
  • The world has entered the largest reallocation of capital since the end of the Cold War.
  • Strategic sovereignty is replacing efficiency as the dominant driver of long-term investment.
  • AI is becoming the new industrial infrastructure of the global economy.
  • Energy, semiconductors, critical minerals, defense and space are converging into one structural theme.
  • Europe’s future will be determined by execution — not ambition.
  • The greatest opportunities belong to those who control strategic infrastructure, not those who merely build on top of it.

The largest reallocation of capital since the end of the Cold War is no longer ahead of us. It has already begun.

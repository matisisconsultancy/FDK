China Breaks the Cuda Wall

DeepSeek and Huawei are turning technological constraint into a sovereign AI stack, moving the contest from chip vs. chip to stack vs. stack.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 30, 2026 6 min read Capital
90+major open-source projects supported on Ascend
40+models natively pretrained on Ascend/CANN
5,200+monthly active CANN developers
128chips in the optimized Ascend 950 supernode

“The old objective was to replace Nvidia. The new strategy is to make Nvidia less necessary.”

China's pivotal AI advance is no longer a better model or a better chip. It is the beginning of an alternative intelligence-production system.

DeepSeek has released programming infrastructure optimized for Huawei's Ascend processors, including an Ascend-compatible version of TileLang and compute and communications libraries. DeepSeek and Huawei have also optimized a 128-chip Ascend 950 supernode.

The significance goes far beyond DeepSeek. Nvidia's moat has never been the GPU alone — it is GPU × CUDA × LIBRARIES × DEVELOPERS × INSTALLED BASE. China has spent years trying to reduce dependence on the first variable. It is now attacking the other four.

STACK VS. STACK

The race moves from chip vs. chip to stack vs. stack — and that is a far larger battlefield.

02

The Second-Order Effects

five recursive consequences

  1. Huawei does not need Ascend to outperform Nvidia's best accelerator on every benchmark. CANN does not need to replace CUDA globally. China needs its combined system to become sufficiently capable that dependence on the American stack stops being economically prohibitive.

    The relevant metric changes from GPU performance to useful intelligence / total system cost. A weaker accelerator combined with better interconnect, more efficient software and a DeepSeek-style sparse model can still deliver competitive economics across large categories of inference. signal: The distinction between parity and sufficiency lowers the technological hurdle dramatically.

  2. U.S. restrictions still impose real costs. China remains behind in leading-edge fabrication, frontier accelerators and software maturity. But constraints change incentives.

    The recursive chain is increasingly: export controls → scarcity → higher returns to substitution → domestic software investment → lower switching costs → greater domestic adoption → larger ecosystem → lower dependency. signal: Restricting access can preserve the frontier gap while accelerating the alternative system beneath it — both effects can coexist.

  3. DeepSeek began as the symbol of Chinese model efficiency. Its strategic function is becoming broader. By helping adapt programming tools and workloads to Huawei hardware, DeepSeek can become the software bridge between Chinese intelligence and Chinese compute.

    DeepSeek improves Ascend economics. Ascend reduces DeepSeek's dependence on U.S. infrastructure. More deployment creates more optimization. More optimization makes the Chinese stack more competitive. signal: DeepSeek matters increasingly not as a model company, but as part of China's technology architecture.

  4. The U.S. ecosystem compounds through its installed base: more Nvidia → more CUDA developers → better software → more applications → greater Nvidia demand. China is attempting to create the same mechanism: more Ascend → more CANN developers → better optimization → lower migration friction → more workloads → greater Ascend demand.

    Once the second loop reaches sufficient scale, technological decoupling stops being driven primarily by policy. signal: Decoupling becomes economically endogenous — and that is much harder to reverse.

  5. Efficient models reduce the amount of frontier hardware required to produce useful intelligence: model efficiency ↑ → compute required per task ↓ → domestic hardware viability ↑ → inference cost ↓ → deployment ↑.

    China is attacking the U.S. compute advantage from both sides. Huawei increases the effective supply of domestic compute. DeepSeek reduces the amount of compute required. signal: China is not simply trying to produce more chips — it is reducing the strategic value of each missing American chip.

03

The Global Implication

two increasingly distinct offers

01

U.S. Stack

Maximum capability. Nvidia/CUDA, hyperscale cloud, frontier agents, premium economics.

02

Chinese Stack

Sufficient capability. Open models, domestic compute, lower system cost, sovereign deployment, industrial integration.

03

Emerging Markets

Many countries and enterprises do not require the absolute frontier — they require intelligence that is capable, inexpensive, deployable, customizable and sovereign. The Chinese proposition could become particularly attractive across markets seeking technological optionality.

04

Why This Matters for the AI Supercycle

duplication, not substitution

Key takeaways

seven observations

  • China's pivotal advance is system-level — the connection of model + compute + interconnect + software + developers.
  • America still owns the stronger frontier; advanced fabrication and frontier compute remain substantial U.S.-aligned advantages.
  • But China no longer needs parity everywhere — it needs a sufficiently capable stack at a competitive total system cost.
  • DeepSeek is becoming strategic infrastructure, extending from model efficiency to making Chinese compute economically usable.
  • Export controls constrain Chinese capability today while increasing the return on substitution tomorrow.
  • Watch adoption, not announcements: CANN developers, native Ascend workloads, utilization, migration rates and cost per completed task.
  • The AI Supercycle gets larger — two independent intelligence systems require two enormous physical infrastructure stacks.

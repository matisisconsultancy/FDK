The Great Collision

The AI Supercycle is becoming large enough to reshape the financial conditions required to sustain its own expansion.

Read the full note ↓

The most consequential development this morning is the growing collision between the financing requirements of the AI Supercycle and the financial-stability objectives of central banks.

The Bank of England has warned that increasingly interconnected AI financing arrangements could amplify financial risks. Hyperscaler bond issuance is projected to exceed $305 billion in 2026, accounting for approximately 8% of global investment-grade issuance, while borrowing across the broader AI infrastructure ecosystem is nearly twice its 2025 level.

Meanwhile, Tokyo inflation is accelerating, oil prices are approaching $97, and Europe faces a consequential monetary leadership transition. The fundamental question: can the private sector finance the Next Economy at unprecedented scale without undermining the financial stability of the economies it is transforming?

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

Can the private sector finance the Next Economy at unprecedented scale without undermining the financial stability of the economies it is transforming?

01

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

capital implications

  1. The Bank of England highlights growing interdependencies among hyperscalers, banks, bond markets and private credit. signal: Financially autonomous technology leaders and transparent financing structures may command higher premiums.

  2. New American restrictions on advanced computing exports and remote access are intensifying technological fragmentation. signal: Semiconductor efficiency, diversified suppliers and sovereign computing infrastructure gain strategic importance.

  3. Europe's evolving ECB leadership and unresolved budget negotiations expose the difficulty of financing industrial transformation amid sovereign differentiation. signal: Competitive energy, credible financing and productive industrial investment will become more decisive.

  4. Brent's recovery reflects renewed Middle Eastern supply concerns. Persistent energy pressure could reinforce restrictive monetary policy while increasing infrastructure costs. signal: Electricity networks, power management, storage and industrial efficiency remain attractive strategic areas.

  5. VINCI's investment in LISEA and new European rail modernization contracts highlight demand for established infrastructure and long-duration operating assets. signal: Intelligent railways, asset optimization, predictive maintenance and reliable transport infrastructure may benefit from more selective investment.

02

The Hidden Pattern — The Financial Contagion Loop

a self-reinforcing mechanism

The emerging risk is a self-reinforcing financial mechanism: AI Investment → Corporate Borrowing → Credit Concentration → Financial-Stability Concerns → Monetary and Lending Constraints → Higher Capital Hurdles.

The Great Split is becoming a divide between financial autonomy and financial dependency.

03

Three Second-Order Effects

what deserves attention

01

Capital Crowding

AI-related borrowing may compete with sovereigns and industrial companies for financing.

02

Competitive Consolidation

Financially resilient platforms may strengthen their advantages as weaker operators face refinancing constraints.

03

Productivity Rotation

Capital may move from speculative infrastructure toward industrial AI applications delivering measurable cash flows.

04

Morning Velocity Reading

FDK qualitative assessment

Market regime: liquidity rebound, structural risk repricing.

05

What to Watch Before the Close

thin volumes, early session

US equity markets close early today at 19:00 CET for the post-Thanksgiving session. Monitor AI-linked credit spreads, semiconductor performance, Treasury yields, European sovereign bonds and energy prices.

Thin trading volumes could amplify market moves. The decisive signal is whether credit markets confirm the recovery in equity valuations.

06

The Final Take — The Next Capital Rotation

from acceleration to returns

The AI Supercycle remains technologically powerful. But its financial architecture is becoming more interconnected, capital-intensive and vulnerable to changes in financing conditions.

For investors, financial autonomy and returns on invested capital are becoming critical. For corporate boards, the priority is accelerating productivity without compromising balance-sheet resilience. For policymakers, the challenge is preserving financial stability while financing industrial modernization.

The next market leaders will increasingly be those capable of converting technological acceleration into sustainable economic returns without depending on uninterrupted access to external capital.

The Velocity Insight

technology, capital, productivity, independence

  • Technology creates possibility.
  • Capital finances expansion.
  • Productivity creates value.
  • Financial independence sustains leadership.

The next hierarchy of global economic power will be determined by who can accelerate without becoming financially dependent on the acceleration itself.

As AI leadership confronts its first institutional crisis, bond yields rise and capital returns to the broader economy, a new hierarchy of financial power is emerging.

Read the full note ↓

The most consequential signal from global markets this weekend is a reversal in equity leadership: the companies that dominated the AI-driven expansion are losing their relative advantage while the broader economy begins to outperform.

Since October 6, the cap-weighted S&P 500 has fallen about 2.9% while its equal-weighted counterpart has gained 3.9% — a 6.8-point divergence within the same universe. This is not evidence that the AI Supercycle is ending, but that investors are reassessing the value of earnings predictability and cash-flow durability.

The central thesis is changing: the market is beginning to reward the capacity to generate economic value under uncertainty, rather than simply the capacity to promise extraordinary future growth.

01

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

what the rotation reveals

  1. The FT's November 15 analysis identifies a rotation from technology leaders toward healthcare, staples and utilities; Walmart has risen ~20%, Coca-Cola 13% and J&J 12% since October 6. LSEG Lipper data show $50.99bn of weekly global equity-fund inflows, $35.13bn to Europe. But defensives are no longer cheap — technology trades near 19x forward earnings versus more than 20x for healthcare and staples. signal: Favour durable cash generation and reasonable valuations; do not treat defensiveness itself as an advantage.

  2. Nvidia's November 12 leadership transition has focused attention on its supplier-financing and strategic equity relationships. Anthropic's proposed IPO could raise up to $64.4bn alongside a $26bn private placement, with Nvidia's planned ~$10bn participation drawing scrutiny. The decisive question is whether companies can sustain growth without continuous capital support from their own ecosystem. signal: Monitor credit guarantees, customer concentration and free-cash-flow conversion ahead of November 17 results and the November 19 debut.

  3. The US 10-year yield has climbed above 5%; BoE's Huw Pill signalled further rate rises may be needed in 2027. S&P affirmed Italy at BBB+ with a positive outlook on November 13, while the Bank of Italy warned on November 12 that frequent financial-sector tax changes distort investment — the government proposes €5.9bn of additional bank-sector revenue. signal: The next sovereign premium will reward countries that stabilize public finances without undermining the institutions financing growth.

  4. Brent ended Friday at $95.84, down ~2.5% on the week, with US crude inventories up 4.7m barrels. Iranian authorities then reported three tanker mine incidents on Saturday; Reuters could not independently verify them. The risk is a renewed inflation shock while rates and sovereign financing costs are already elevated. signal: Watch energy-sensitive industrials, shipping insurance, refining margins and European gas as the transmission from shipping to margins stays live.

  5. The FAA's November 13 ground stop disrupted more than 20,000 flights, with a router upgrade affecting 17 of 22 en-route centres; Congress has provided $12.5bn for modernization against ~$50bn sought. China exported ~927,000 passenger cars in October, up 62.3% YoY, as domestic sales fell 27.4%. signal: The most valuable industrial AI may improve existing physical systems rather than demand new capital-intensive infrastructure.

The Great Split is no longer between technological leaders and laggards — it is between those who convert acceleration into sustainable value and those financing it without retaining a sufficient share of the rewards.

02

Three Scenarios for the Next Phase

illustrative three-month probabilities

45%

The Great Earnings Broadening

AI demand remains strong while leadership broadens toward businesses showing earnings resilience, capital efficiency and industrial productivity.

35%

The Sovereign–Energy Squeeze

Persistent inflation, renewed shipping disruptions and rising sovereign yields constrain capital-intensive investment and weaken broader earnings.

20%

The AI Productivity Reacceleration

AI monetization, semiconductor competition and industrial deployment generate stronger cash returns, supporting a renewed but broader technology rally.

Five Takeaways

for investors, boards and policymakers

  • Equity investors: the next opportunity is broader, but not indiscriminately cheaper — seek durable earnings at reasonable valuations.
  • Credit investors: distinguish contractual revenue from cash received, and strategic guarantees from completed investment.
  • Corporate boards: operational resilience — reliability, supplier diversification, capital efficiency — is becoming a financial asset.
  • European policymakers: turn capital inflows into productive capital formation for enterprise, energy and mobility.
  • Global investors: the divide is between financial returns and financial dependence.

The next great capital rotation will follow the economic profits of technological acceleration — not merely its technological pioneers. Who creates the value, who finances it, and who retains it.

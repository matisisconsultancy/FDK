GVI Countries Rankings: In Focus

The world is splitting into Velocity Leaders, Challengers and the At Risk — measured by who converts compute, energy, capital and AI into productivity fastest.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 19, 2026 4 min read Macro

“Velocity is no longer primarily digital.”

The world is separating into three groups. Velocity Leaders are creating self-reinforcing ecosystems in which capital, compute, energy and adoption compound together. Velocity Challengers possess significant industrial or technological strengths but face one or two major bottlenecks—usually capital formation, energy or diffusion. Velocity At Risk economies risk paying the higher global cost of AI capital without capturing enough of its productivity dividend.

Velocity is no longer primarily digital. It increasingly depends upon the ability to combine Accelerated Computing × Energy × Capital Formation × Industrial AI × Institutional Execution.

THE VELOCITY EQUATION

Compute × Energy × Capital × Industrial AI × Execution = Velocity.

The New GVI Equation

Five dimensions of national Velocity

  1. Compute capacity, semiconductor access, AI infrastructure and model ecosystem. signal: Frontier capability begins with compute access.

  2. Electricity availability, grid expansion, storage, price and resilience. signal: Compute without energy cannot scale.

  3. AI CapEx, venture capital, infrastructure finance and depth of domestic capital markets. signal: Energy without capital cannot be converted into infrastructure.

  4. How quickly AI migrates from models into factories, mobility, logistics, healthcare, energy and services. signal: Infrastructure without adoption cannot generate productivity.

  5. Permitting, regulation, skills, infrastructure execution and coordination of public and private investment. signal: Execution decides who closes the loop.

THE SELF-REINFORCING CYCLE

Capital → Energy + Compute → AI Capability → Industrial Diffusion → Productivity → Cash Flow → More Capital ↺. Countries that close the circle accelerate; countries that break it at any point begin losing relative Velocity.

Key takeaways

The Velocity Insight

  • The first race asked who has the best models; the second, who has the most accelerated computing.
  • The third race is now beginning: who can turn intelligence into productivity at national scale?
  • Compute needs energy; energy needs capital; infrastructure needs adoption; productivity needs cash flow to finance the next cycle.
  • The question is no longer simply which country is ahead — but which is accelerating faster than everyone else.

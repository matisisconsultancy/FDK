The Capital Reckoning

The AI supercycle is not over — but the era of unconditional capital is, as credit markets begin to decide who can finance the future.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 31, 2026 4 min read Capital

“Follow credit before equity. Follow free cash flow before capital expenditure.”

The signal1 min
  1. Capital allocation is changing: future promises are now tested against present financing capacity, not technological ambition alone.
  2. Credit scrutiny reached AI: S&P revised Meta's outlook to negative, projecting free-cash-flow deficits near $39bn in 2026 and $71bn in 2027.
  3. France entered a fiscal-growth trap: Fitch revised its outlook to negative, projecting debt rising to ~124% of GDP by 2028.
  4. Yields and spreads diverged: Treasuries rallied to 4.93% even as Fed officials pushed for further tightening and corporate financing risk rose.
  5. Physical and digital costs climbed: Brent gained ~13.6% in October and Binance reported a ~$6.3bn cyberattack.
  6. Follow credit before equity: free cash flow, not capital expenditure, now selects the next winners.
Read the full note ↓

Friday, October 30 delivered a consequential shift. The Nasdaq fell 2.86%, the Philadelphia semiconductor index lost 7.2%, and Intel declined 28.6%, while investors moved into Treasuries, pushing the ten-year yield down to roughly 4.93%.

The decisive developments extended beyond equities. S&P Global revised Meta's credit outlook to negative; Fitch revised France's sovereign outlook to negative; the Financial Times reported OpenAI cut its latest fundraising target by $10 billion. Within hours, the financial sustainability of a major AI platform and one of Europe's largest sovereign borrowers came under renewed scrutiny.

This is not a generalized earnings recession — of the 299 S&P 500 companies reporting, 82% beat estimates, with aggregate Q3 growth projected at 31.6%. It is a repricing of capital intensity. The AI supercycle is not over; the era of unconditional capital is.

01

The Five Signals That Change the Outlook

Credit, sovereigns, energy and trust

  1. AWS revenue rose 39% to $46bn and Amazon's quarterly operating income hit $28.1bn, yet trailing-twelve-month free cash flow fell to -$42.4bn on infrastructure spend. S&P revised Meta's outlook to negative — not a downgrade — projecting cash-flow deficits of ~$39bn (2026) and $71bn (2027). Equity investors reward future earnings; credit investors weigh repayment, cash flow and refinancing risk. signal: The next AI winners will be selected by both technological performance and creditworthiness.

  2. France's economy unexpectedly contracted 0.1% in Q3 as harmonized inflation accelerated to 3.8%. Fitch kept the A+ rating but revised the outlook to negative, projecting debt rising from 115.7% of GDP in 2025 to ~124% by 2028, as roughly 70,000 police deployed amid renewed protests. The danger is not default but a self-reinforcing fiscal-growth trap. signal: France's greatest risk is not the debt stock but that servicing it erodes future growth.

  3. The ten-year fell to ~4.93% even as Fed officials — Logan, Musalem, Barkin — argued for more tightening, and the Bank of Japan held at 1.25%. A fall in government yields does not automatically cut borrowing costs for riskier firms: corporate cost = risk-free yield + credit spread. signal: The market can price lower government yields and higher corporate financing risk simultaneously.

  4. Brent gained ~13.6% over October; the active January contract closed at $113.43 after a tanker attack in the Strait of Hormuz. Energy is a core input into accelerated computing, raising AI operating costs and reinforcing inflation together — a particular constraint for Europe. signal: The AI supercycle is a digital revolution with an increasingly physical cost structure.

  5. Binance reported a ~$6.3bn cyberattack, reserving over $6.34bn; TRM Labs attributed it, with high confidence, to a North Korean group. The episode does not prove a systemic crisis, but it shows how operational failures become market events. signal: In the Next Economy, trust is infrastructure, and its failure has a cost of capital.

02

Europe's Great Split

One currency, different conditions

Euro-area GDP rose 0.4% in Q3, but the national figures diverge sharply: Spain +0.6%, Germany +0.2%, Italy 0.0%, France -0.1%. The ECB's October 30 survey revised expected euro-area inflation up to 3.0% for 2026 and 2.6% for 2027, though long-term expectations remain anchored at 2.0%. Member states enter the next investment cycle from very different starting positions — the challenge is to turn divergence into integration rather than fragmentation.

03

The Next Rotation

Within the supercycle, not away from it

Capital is becoming more selective, not more scarce. FDK is overweight cash-generating AI businesses, grid modernization and Industrial AI; selective on high-quality corporate credit and energy infrastructure; underweight refinancing-dependent computing capacity and vulnerable sovereigns. A business can grow revenue fast while becoming financially less attractive — the next cycle may reward capital productivity more consistently than capital-expenditure growth.

The AI supercycle is not being replaced by a financial crisis — it is being subjected to a more demanding financial discipline. The future will belong not to those who command the most capital, but to those who turn capital into the greatest productive advantage.

The European AI race is shifting from who builds the largest data center to who owns the system that converts electricity into intelligence—and intelligence into industrial cash flow.

“The geography of AI follows the geography of convertible electricity.”

Today brings a meaningful shift in the European race. There is no new EuroHPC winner or site award, but Spain and Germany are moving the architecture underneath the competition: Spain is materially expanding the grid and introducing a capacity market; Germany is integrating private capital, sovereign cloud, models and large-scale accelerated computing.

The Commission itself now explicitly describes Gigafactories as catalysts for a European semiconductor ecosystem, not merely data centers.

BANKABLE INTELLIGENCE ECOSYSTEMS

The relevant unit is no longer announced or even bankable megawatts—it is the full architecture of capital, power, grid, computing, cloud, models, distribution and industrial demand.

01

The Moving Architecture

five signals reshaping the race

  1. Spain has raised planned electricity-transmission investment through 2030 to more than €17 billion—30% above the earlier proposal and 144% above the current planning cycle. The revised plan includes 193 substations, 6,706 km of planned lines, and 300 transmission-grid positions serving new distribution demand. Demand accounted for 41% of submissions during consultation, with data centers among the large new loads driving expansion.

    Simultaneously, Spain is establishing a capacity market open to generation, storage and demand, with stronger consumer charges during the most stressed hours. The strategic question changes from "Can Spain supply enough electricity to AI?" to "Can AI become an active participant in Spain's electricity system?" Yet the contradiction remains unresolved: the CNMC has entered the dispute over the proposed data-center decree, while industry challenges the 80% renewable-matching and hourly-certification provisions. signal: Excellent physical infrastructure policy and unresolved regulatory bankability, at the same time.

  2. Cohere and Aleph Alpha have signed their definitive combination agreement. The combined company will operate as Cohere with headquarters in Berlin and Toronto, while Heidelberg remains a research center. Schwarz Group is supporting the transaction with €500 million and will provide computing capacity through STACKIT.

    Behind this sits the larger asset: Schwarz is investing roughly €11–13 billion in a German data center capable of hosting approximately 100,000 AI chips. The pieces—Schwarz capital, a large AI data center, STACKIT sovereign cloud, Cohere/Aleph Alpha, enterprise AI, and Lidl/Kaufland plus external industrial customers—assemble into a vertically integrated AI industrial stack. Captive demand delivers utilization visibility, revenue visibility, lower offtake risk and greater bankability. signal: Vertical integration changes infrastructure economics because affiliated customers reduce offtake risk.

  3. The Rome cybersecurity company Exein raised approximately €234 million, reaching a roughly €1.45 billion valuation. The EIB Group participated through the European Tech Champions Initiative's first direct company co-investment. Exein focuses on security for connected devices and Physical AI environments.

    This matters because Gigafactories require downstream demand, and Italy possesses an unusually large potential market: manufacturing, robotics, mobility, machinery, aerospace, energy and infrastructure. The opportunity is not simply to host accelerated computing—it is to connect that capacity to Italy's industrial system. signal: The strategic chain runs from Gigafactory capacity through Physical AI to industrial cash flow.

  4. Fresh EMEA market data reinforce the thesis. Operational data-center capacity has surpassed 12.1 GW, up more than 36% in two years, while the committed pipeline has reached 18.3 GW. Helsinki has now joined London, Frankfurt, Dublin, Paris, Amsterdam and Milan as a major EMEA powerhouse. Future capacity is increasingly migrating toward Southern Europe and Scandinavia, where power and land can be delivered faster. signal: The geography of AI follows the geography of convertible electricity.

  5. Christine Lagarde's September 14 intervention adds the financial dimension: Europe's Gigafactories are only a beginning, and Europe must build computing infrastructure at a far greater pace while developing European models and access to frontier capabilities. The Commission now explicitly describes Gigafactories as a mechanism for creating predictable demand for European-designed AI processors and eventually indigenous manufacturing. signal: Gigafactories can become a capital-formation mechanism for the Next Economy.

Key takeaways

what today's signals mean

  • Spain's €17 billion grid expansion and new capacity market could turn AI load into an active participant in the electricity system.
  • Germany's Schwarz–STACKIT–Cohere stack shows how vertical integration lowers offtake risk and raises bankability.
  • Italy's Exein round signals downstream Physical AI demand that can connect Gigafactory capacity to industry.
  • EMEA operational capacity has passed 12.1 GW with an 18.3 GW pipeline, migrating toward the South and Scandinavia.
  • The decisive question is value capture, not raw megawatts.

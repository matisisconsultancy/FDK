The Contracted Supercycle

AI scarcity is no longer enough — the next winners are locking in power, silicon, permits and cash flow before everyone else prices them out.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 6, 2026 10 min read Capital

~$100Brent crude, tactical relief

“Contract the bottleneck before the bottleneck prices you out.”

Tuesday looked like a classic relief rally. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq reached fresh records, the U.S. 10-year Treasury eased to about 5.26%, the 30-year to 5.66%, and Brent moved back toward $100 as Middle East export flows improved. The STOXX 600 closed 0.5% higher and French bonds recovered part of their recent losses.

But underneath the rally, something more important happened. Google contracted 3.59 GW of power from Constellation. Marvell lifted its fiscal-2028 revenue target to $20 billion and projected as much as $90 billion by 2031. AMD said it must substantially expand advanced-chip supply in 2027 and is already planning wafer and memory capacity three to five years ahead. Spain's Aragon revealed a pipeline exceeding €60 billion of digital-infrastructure investment, with permitting compressed from years to less than twelve months.

These are not four separate stories. They are the new operating model of the AI Supercycle: contract the bottleneck before the bottleneck prices you out. Data cut: approximately 19:30 CEST. European markets are closed; U.S. equity readings are the latest verified intraday levels.

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

Is the next AI winner the company with the best technology — or the company that secures scarce capacity first? Today increasingly points toward the second answer.

01

Part I — The Five Signals

What moved beneath the surface

  1. At the latest verified U.S. reading, the Dow was up roughly 0.7%, the S&P 500 0.86% at 7,840.66, and the Nasdaq 0.83% at 27,705.89. All eleven S&P sectors were higher. The 10-year Treasury eased about 4 basis points to 5.26%, while the 30-year fell to approximately 5.66%.

    The equity rally did not require falling earnings expectations. It required a temporary easing of the hurdle rate. The Productivity Spread widened. signal: The rally was a denominator story, not a numerator one.

  2. Nvidia gained again, pushing its market capitalization toward $6 trillion. Yet the more consequential number is broader: consensus now expects roughly 27%–30% year-on-year S&P 500 earnings growth in Q3, with more than half of that increase linked to companies benefiting from AI infrastructure spending.

    The AI trade can survive expensive capital only if earnings velocity remains exceptional. The valuation story is gradually becoming an earnings story. That is healthy — and much harder. signal: The defense against 5%-plus yields is earnings velocity, not multiples.

  3. Brent traded around $100.03 late in the European session as roughly 12 million barrels per day of crude and 2 million barrels per day of refined products moved out of the Middle East during the previous week, and G7 emergency-stock releases reduced immediate scarcity fears.

    But the EIA simultaneously raised its fourth-quarter Brent forecast to approximately $105, citing depleted global inventories and an exceptionally tight diesel market; U.S. retail diesel is expected to remain above $6 per gallon in October. signal: The shock has eased. The scarcity premium has not disappeared.

  4. France's 10-year yield fell roughly 10 basis points to 4.75% and the euro recovered to around $1.126. But the France–Bund spread had reached 158.7 basis points last week, its widest since the sovereign-debt crisis, and markets have sharply scaled back the number of ECB increases they expect as high borrowing costs begin acting as monetary tightening themselves.

    ECB policymakers now confront a paradox: inflation remains well above target, yet long-term yields are already suppressing demand and limiting energy-price pass-through. Europe has moved from a simple inflation problem to inflation + sovereign risk + investment scarcity. signal: Europe's problem is no longer one-dimensional.

  5. San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said today that AI-related demand for chips could reinforce inflationary pressure if it persists alongside tariffs and energy shocks. Meanwhile, the New York Fed's Global Supply Chain Pressure Index rose to 1.28 in September from 1.20 in August.

    That is the two-stage AI regime increasingly visible across markets: capital-formation inflation first, productivity disinflation later. Central banks are beginning to price the first stage before the second has fully arrived. signal: Policymakers are pricing AI's inflationary stage before its disinflationary one.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN

The previous phase rewarded ownership of scarce assets — GPUs, HBM, data centers, power. The next phase rewards companies that can contract scarcity before they need it. The competitive advantage is becoming capacity control.

02

Part II — Capital Is Moving Toward Control

Where the money went today

01

Google × Constellation: 3.59 GW becomes the new AI contract

Google signed agreements for 3,590 MW of electricity from Constellation Energy, including a 20-year agreement for 890 MW of additional nuclear generation and a 15-year agreement for another 2,700 MW in the PJM grid. Constellation will invest more than $4.3 billion to expand its fleet; its shares surged more than 13%. PJM capacity prices have risen more than elevenfold since 2024. The template: compute + power + long-term contract = strategic capacity.

02

Marvell: custom silicon moves to industrial scale

Marvell raised its fiscal-2028 revenue forecast to ~$20 billion, above the $18.2 billion consensus, and projected fiscal-2031 revenue of $70–90 billion. The $80 billion midpoint compares with analyst expectations of only $46.85 billion. Its August agreement with Google could generate as much as $120 billion through fiscal 2033. Marvell rose ~7%, Broadcom ~4%. Contracts — not benchmarks — are becoming the key economic asset.

03

AMD: the next scarcity is supply-chain reservation

AMD, now valued at more than $1 trillion, said it will substantially increase chip supply in 2027. Lisa Su is meeting TSMC, Foxconn, Samsung and SK Hynix as the company seeks more advanced wafer and memory capacity, planning requirements three to five years ahead. The question is shifting from who designs the best accelerator to who has guaranteed access to wafers, packaging and memory.

04

Software: the productivity layer reclaims leadership

The S&P 500 software and services index rose 1.3% today to its highest level since November 2025. Expected 2026 sector earnings growth has risen from 13.8% in March to 20.6% today, with CrowdStrike, Fortinet and Palo Alto Networks posting triple-digit gains this year. SAP's acquisition of Belgian workforce-data firm TechWolf points the same way: AI is becoming a margin and productivity layer inside software.

05

Europe: Aragon shows that permitting is capital

Aragon expects more than €60 billion of digital-infrastructure investment over the next decade — AWS alone plans €33.7 billion, its largest cloud and AI investment outside the U.S. The fast-track PIGA mechanism can cut permitting to under twelve months. Yet Spain's grid is the constraint: €17 billion authorized, but roughly 70% of transmission nodes already have no spare connection capacity. Capacity could quadruple to 3.9 GW by 2030.

The Second-Order Effects

Beyond the trade

04

Part III — Wednesday's Five Tests

The next 24 hours

  1. India's central bank announces Wednesday. Roughly 60% of economists in a Reuters poll expect a 25-basis-point increase from 5.25%, while swap markets fully price that move and imply some probability of a 50-basis-point hike. The rupee closed Tuesday at 96.42 per dollar, its weakest in more than two months. signal: A 50bp surprise confirms the tightening cycle is broader than the Fed.

  2. The Fed releases minutes of its September 15–16 meeting at 2:00 p.m. ET — the meeting that produced its first rate increase in three years. Markets price only ~22% probability of another hike in October, but ~84% by December. The crucial question is how much of the concern was cyclical and how much structural. signal: Any explicit discussion of AI capex, energy, tariffs or supply constraints would matter enormously for the long end.

  3. The EIA publishes its weekly petroleum report Wednesday at 10:30 a.m. ET. Analysts expect U.S. crude inventories to have risen roughly 1.8 million barrels last week. But the real inflation test is refined products: the EIA now expects Brent to average $105 in Q4 while diesel remains unusually scarce. signal: If crude stocks rise but diesel stays tight, the inflation problem is not solved.

  4. EU ambassadors are expected Wednesday to approve roughly 1,650 new Russia-related sanctions designations — the largest single package since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine — most aimed at Russia's military-industrial complex. Sanctions reshape energy routes, industrial inputs, shipping, critical materials and supply-chain redundancy. signal: Security policy increasingly changes corporate WACC.

  5. German factory orders fell 10.6% in August, largely as large transport-equipment orders collapsed 61.5% from July's exceptional level; excluding large orders, bookings were almost flat. Roughly 3,000 startups were created in the first half of 2026, up 52%, about one-third AI-focused — yet German AI funding this year is estimated at only €5.7 billion versus €308 billion in the United States. signal: Wednesday tests whether Europe's AI acceleration is reaching its industrial base.

NEXT 90 DAYS

The AI Supercycle is entering a fourth phase. Model capability, then compute scarcity, then capital formation — and now capacity control. Competitive advantage evolves: ownership → access → contract → control.

Key takeaways

The Law of Contracted Scarcity

  • When a strategic input becomes scarce, advantage migrates from whoever can buy it today to whoever has already secured it for tomorrow.
  • A company with demand but no power cannot monetize; GPUs without memory cannot deploy; permits without a grid connection are not an asset.
  • The constructive configuration: 10Y below 5.25%, Brent below $100, RBI +25 not +50, and FOMC minutes showing patience — broadening the rally into software, utilities, grid, automation and mobility.
  • The adverse configuration: 10Y above 5.35%, diesel re-tightening, RBI +50, and minutes revealing structural inflation concern — then the denominator takes control again.
  • At 2% money, scarcity could be purchased later. At 5% money, later is expensive.

Google locked down electricity. AMD locked down semiconductor capacity. Marvell locked down custom-silicon demand. The next Great Split is no longer between companies that have AI and those that do not — it is between those that control the bottleneck and those that must buy it at market price. Secure the power. Secure the silicon. Secure the grid. Secure the customer. Then let Velocity compound the return.

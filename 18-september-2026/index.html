Intelligence is getting cheaper; the infrastructure beneath it is getting more expensive — and the real pricing power now sits at the bottlenecks that abundance creates.

“Abundance creates velocity. Bottlenecks create value.”

Compute prices are rising, memory remains scarce, optical capacity is being expanded, sovereign capital is pouring into AI factories, and central banks are tightening into the boom. The Next Economy is discovering where the real pricing power sits: not in abundance, but at the bottlenecks that abundance creates.

Markets enter Friday with an intriguing split. Asian equities are stronger, oil is easing and the U.S. 10-year Treasury has retreated below 5%. But the apparent relief hides a more important structural move: the price of intelligence may be falling, but the price of producing intelligence is not.

THE GREAT SPLIT

The Intelligence Cost Curve is falling. The Infrastructure Scarcity Curve is rising. The divide is now between those exposed to falling intelligence prices and those owning the bottlenecks required to deliver intelligence.

02

The Five Most Important Overnight Signals

What moved and why it matters

  1. The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate from 1% to 1.25%, the highest in 31 years, by a 7–2 vote. The two dissents were more cautious, and the yen weakened toward 156.9 per dollar as investors judged the decision less hawkish than it might have been.

    Japan has spent decades supplying cheap capital to the world. Now: Japan rates up → domestic yields up → carry-trade economics weaken → Japanese capital reprices → global funding cost up. The Fed has restarted hiking, the ECB tightened last week, the BOE is warning of the same, and Australia says inflation risks are becoming real. The world is entering an unusual regime: an AI capex boom alongside global monetary tightening, with capital priced at 4%–5% sovereign rates. That makes capital productivity a strategic advantage. signal: This infrastructure boom is happening with expensive capital — capital productivity becomes a moat.

  2. Nebius will raise pay-as-you-go prices for selected Nvidia GPUs from October 1 by 17%–21%, some CPU-only instances by 25%, and selected memory offerings by roughly 41%. It is the company's second price increase in three months; recent AI-cloud contracts average more than $1 billion each.

    When demand grows faster than installed capacity, technological improvement can coexist with higher clearing prices: cost per unit of intelligence falls while the price of guaranteed compute rises. This is one of the purest examples yet of the Access Premium becoming a Scarcity Rent. Value may accrue not to the cheapest models but to those holding the GPUs, power, memory and network capacity everyone else needs. signal: Cheaper intelligence and dearer compute are not a contradiction — they are the mechanism.

  3. Crusoe raised $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion valuation, co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners, with QIA and Nvidia participating. It now reports more than $140 billion in contracted value, over 6 GW of contracted capacity, and around 1 GW operational.

    This is more than venture financing. Gulf capital is moving from portfolio diversification toward ownership of the physical infrastructure of intelligence — gaining exposure to compute rents, energy demand, data-center real estate, infrastructure financing, long-duration contracts and strategic technology relationships. The convergence of sovereign capital, AI infrastructure, energy and long-term contracts will become a defining capital-formation architecture of the Next Economy. signal: Sovereign wealth is buying the utility layer of intelligence, not just equity in it.

  4. China's CXMT is preparing to expand from DRAM into NAND flash amid a global memory shortage executives expect to persist through at least 2027 — capex redirected toward DRAM and HBM has limited NAND additions. Separately, GlobalFoundries and Marvell expanded a multi-year agreement to increase silicon-germanium capacity for high-speed optical connections, citing surging demand for optical transceivers, near-packaged and co-packaged optics.

    Scarcity does not disappear — it migrates. Solve the GPU bottleneck and memory tightens; solve memory and networking tightens; solve networking and electricity tightens; solve electricity and grid connections tighten. The highest-return opportunities may repeatedly shift one layer upstream from the constraint investors currently focus on. signal: Follow the constraint — it moves accelerated computing → memory → optics → power → grid → duration.

  5. Anthropic says Claude now "leads" 26% of the R&D work used to build its next-generation systems, up from roughly 1% in March. More than 90% of its research now involves human-AI collaboration, and around 30,000 AI agents were operating on its main internal platform at any one time in August, under human oversight. Roughly 6% of AI research compute in a sampled July week went to safety work — rising to about 12% for research led by AI.

    This creates reflexive demand: AI capability up → research productivity up → AI builds better AI → more agents → more tokens → more inference → more compute demand. AI infrastructure is no longer merely serving end-users; AI is itself becoming one of the largest consumers of AI infrastructure. Compute demand may compound faster than conventional application forecasts imply. signal: The customer of accelerated computing is increasingly the intelligence-production process itself.

03

Five Hidden Patterns Beyond the News

The deeper structure

1

Falling Unit Costs ≠ Falling Revenues

The Jevons Paradox applied to intelligence: if the price of intelligence falls 50% but consumption rises tenfold, total infrastructure demand rises dramatically. The AI deflation thesis and the AI infrastructure supercycle can both be correct simultaneously.

2

AI Assets Are Becoming Utilities

Crusoe's 6 GW of contracted capacity is industrial infrastructure, not software economics. The emerging model resembles telecom towers, electric grids, LNG terminals, pipelines and rail — where duration, utilization and contracted cash flow may matter as much as technological leadership.

3

Safety Itself Expands the Market

More autonomous AI requires monitoring, testing, red-teaming, governance, cybersecurity, evaluation and containment — all of which consume compute. Governance capex becomes compute demand; the safety response creates another demand category rather than shrinking it.

4

China Is Attacking Scarcity Through Industrial Policy

CXMT's NAND push is more than diversification. It raised roughly $8.6 billion in July in Asia's largest IPO of the year and, like YMTC, benefits from state support. The U.S.–China contest is becoming a race to eliminate bottlenecks from the domestic stack.

5

Europe's Opportunity Is Physical

The EU has proposed seven AI computing hubs and pledged ~€3 billion in future compute purchases, while six mostly U.S. hyperscalers are expected to spend over $1.3 trillion by 2027. But Lucid and Bolt plan 25,000 autonomous vehicles across European cities, targeting 100,000 by 2035 using Nvidia Hyperion. Europe's best AI strategy may be industrialization rather than imitation.

  • 26% — Claude now leads more than a quarter of Anthropic's R&D: the beginning of recursive capital demand.
  • 2027 — how long industry expects memory shortages to persist; the AI race is not waiting for supply to catch up.

The United States is beginning to contest China on the diffusion layer itself. The strategic unit of competition is shifting from the model to the entire frontier-to-inference system.

The consequential development on September 16, 2026 is not a new DeepSeek release. It is the combination of two American moves occurring at opposite ends of the AI infrastructure chain: Washington is trying to tighten the physical supply-chain perimeter around AI hardware, while Anthropic is simultaneously building one of the world's largest dedicated inference footprints outside the United States.

Together, they materially refine our Velocity Edge thesis. Until now, the clean formulation has been that America dominates frontier creation while China increasingly dominates the economics and diffusion of intelligence. Today suggests that the United States may no longer be willing to concede that second battlefield.

# The U.S. Is Trying To Turn North America Into An AI-Hardware Bloc
The Wall Street Journal reports today that Washington is pressing Mexico to accept tougher rules of origin for AI chips, servers and related hardware as part of the USMCA negotiations, specifically to prevent Chinese or other non-North-American components from entering the U.S. market through Mexican assembly operations. The proposal is still under negotiation and has not been adopted, but its scope is strategically important.

Mexico exported approximately $82.9 billion of computer servers during the first half of 2026, up 172.1% year over year, according to S&P Global Market Intelligence. Roughly 93.9% went to the United States. AI servers have become important enough to challenge automobiles as Mexico's defining export category. Only a decade ago, China dominated much of this equipment supply chain; by H1 2026, China's share of U.S. computer-equipment imports had fallen dramatically while Taiwan and Mexico captured most of the market.

Washington is moving beyond GPU export controls. The perimeter now runs: semiconductors → HBM → servers → system assembly → rules of origin → regional AI infrastructure. America appears to be trying to create something analogous to the automotive regional-value architecture — but for the AI production system.
signal: If implemented, third-country assembly ceases to be a back door into the North American AI market.

# Anthropic Builds 2.16 GW Dedicated Largely To Inference
Reuters reports today that Anthropic has signed its first Australian data-centre agreement, securing capacity at a planned 2.16-gigawatt campus roughly 250 km from Brisbane. The facility is expected to begin coming online in 2027 and remains subject to Australian regulatory approval.

The striking point is not the size but the workload: the facility is intended primarily for inference rather than frontier-model training. Training creates intelligence once; inference monetizes it billions or trillions of times. Frontier-model companies are beginning to build physical infrastructure around the expectation of enormous recurring consumption of intelligence.

Australia offers abundant renewable-energy potential, available land and proximity to Asian markets at a moment when U.S. and Asian power grids are increasingly constrained. Reuters notes Australian AI infrastructure investment could reach around A$150 billion — roughly US$107 billion — by 2030. OpenAI already secured Australian capacity last year; Anthropic is now following.
signal: The American ecosystem is moving from global model distribution toward global physical distribution of inference capacity.

::section The Thesis Changes Subtly But Importantly | Two architectures of diffusion
The previous formulation was **UNITED STATES — Frontier Capability** versus **CHINA — Economics + Diffusion**. It should now read **UNITED STATES — Frontier + Infrastructure Distribution** versus **CHINA — Efficiency + Mass Diffusion**.

The American chain: frontier models → accelerated computing → AI factories → regional inference campuses → lower latency and greater sovereignty → enterprise deployment → revenue → more infrastructure. The Chinese chain: efficient models → lower hardware intensity → cheaper inference → open ecosystems → mass deployment → industrial AI → more data → still greater efficiency.

This does not invalidate the thesis that China is the primary challenger on AI economics. Chinese labs continue narrowing the capability gap while operating with substantially greater hardware constraints; AP's assessment today describes DeepSeek, Moonshot, Z.ai and Alibaba as increasingly competitive with leading U.S. models even while the U.S. retains leadership across key frontier capabilities. But America is beginning to build a distribution architecture of its own — not primarily through cheaper open models, but through infrastructure.

::patterns The Emerging Great Split | Different mechanisms, same goal
# China Optimizes Intelligence / Hardware
Make each unit of accelerated computing produce more intelligence: efficient models, lower hardware requirement, lower cost, mass deployment.

# America Optimizes Hardware × Energy × Capital × Reach
Make accelerated computing available everywhere economically valuable intelligence will be consumed. These are different mechanisms for reducing the effective cost of AI deployment — and they can collide directly.

::section Why This Matters For The AI Supercycle | Training to inference
The Anthropic announcement strengthens one of our core propositions: the AI Supercycle is migrating from training to inference. The first capital wave built GPUs → training clusters → frontier models. The next wave increasingly requires regional AI factories → sovereign inference → enterprise agents → local data residency → low latency → Physical AI → continuous token production.

Training workloads are episodic; inference workloads scale with economic activity. Every additional AI employee, coding agent, autonomous vehicle, robot, financial agent, logistics system or scientific model generates persistent inference demand. Better models → more economically useful tasks → more inference → larger regional AI factories → lower latency and unit cost → more applications → still more inference. That is the Supercycle — and it points to a much larger infrastructure market than training alone.

The new Velocity equation: **AI Power = Frontier Capability × Inference Economics × Infrastructure Scale × Supply-Chain Control × Diffusion Velocity**. The U.S. remains strongest in Frontier Capability, Infrastructure Scale and Supply-Chain Control; China in Inference Economics and Diffusion Velocity. The overlap is growing — and that is exactly what makes the competition more consequential.

::takeaways Key takeaways | The September 16 inflection
- America is not suddenly defeating China on low-cost inference — it has not.
- The U.S. is attacking the structural foundations of diffusion: secure the hardware supply chain at home, then place enormous inference capacity closer to users abroad.
- The American causality chain: supply-chain control → AI factories → regional inference → lower deployment friction → greater diffusion.
- China's response: model efficiency → lower hardware requirement → lower cost → mass deployment → greater diffusion.
- America is trying to industrialize the global production and distribution of inference; China, the efficiency with which inference is produced and consumed.

::close The U.S. is no longer fighting only to own the frontier — it is beginning to fight to own the ==infrastructure through which the frontier reaches the world==, making the Supercycle broader, more physical and considerably more capital intensive.

Intelligence Ahead of Change.
THE VELOCITY OF INTELLIGENCE
From Accelerated Computing to Compounding Learning: The New Geography of Economic Power
THE ESPRESSO INDEX — WHY IT MATTERS
In 1986, The Economist introduced the Big Mac Index to make purchasing power parity understandable through the price of a familiar product across countries.
The Velocity Edge’s Espresso Index applies a similarly intuitive idea to a radically different economic question.
The Big Mac Index compares the purchasing power of currencies.
The Espresso Index seeks to compare the productive power of intelligence across economies.
Its starting point is the emergence of the token economy: a world in which accelerated computing, GPU architectures, optimized acceleration libraries and increasingly capable AI models are reducing the cost of producing intelligence while expanding the speed at which machines and organizations can learn.
What previously required months of computation, experimentation and human coordination can increasingly be explored in days, hours or minutes.
But the true revolution is not simply faster computation.
It is the compounding of learning.
Accelerated computing reduces the cost of experimentation. More experimentation generates data. Data improves models and operational decisions. Better models enable more autonomous actions. Those actions generate additional feedback, potentially accelerating the next learning cycle.
COMPUTE → TOKENS → LEARNING → ACTION → DATA → BETTER INTELLIGENCE
The process is circular rather than linear.
And its economic consequences may be unprecedented.
The central question of the Espresso Index is therefore:
How fast can an economy convert accelerated intelligence into cumulative learning, productive action and financial returns?
That question matters more than simply counting GPUs, data centres, AI companies or announced investment.
A country can possess enormous computing capacity yet fail to translate it into industrial productivity.
Another can possess less frontier compute but deploy AI more effectively across manufacturing, logistics, energy, finance and public services.
The Espresso Index is designed to identify that difference.
It is not a measure of national intelligence or an official economic statistic. It is a proposed proprietary indicator of the speed at which the token economy translates technological capability into economic progress.
IN FOCUS | THE BIG TAKE
THE NEXT AI DIVIDE IS THE SPEED OF LEARNING
The AI Supercycle has already demonstrated that it can generate extraordinary infrastructure profits.
Third-quarter S&P 500 earnings are expected to rise approximately 31%, with roughly two-thirds of the increase concentrated in technology and major AI-exposed companies. Semiconductor earnings are forecast to grow approximately 136%.
These figures describe a powerful but concentrated earnings cycle.
The next stage is more consequential.
Can the learning generated by AI spread across the economy fast enough to produce a broad productivity dividend?
That is where the Espresso Index differs from conventional AI rankings.
It is not designed simply to reward technological scale.
It aims to capture the velocity of economic conversion.
The underlying causal chain is:
ACCELERATED COMPUTING → LOWER INTELLIGENCE COST → FASTER LEARNING → INDUSTRIAL DEPLOYMENT → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW
The speed of that chain—and its ability to reinforce itself—will increasingly determine national competitive advantage.

To turn this proposition into a measurable research product, NERC should track five complementary dimensions.

The resulting score would range from 0 to 100, with each driver normalized across countries using comparable data. The weights are proposed NERC methodology, not yet a statistically validated index.
The distinguishing feature is the interaction between the dimensions.
A country with excellent computing infrastructure but weak industrial deployment should not automatically outrank an economy that converts a smaller compute base into faster productivity gains.
The Espresso Index must therefore measure both level and acceleration: where an economy stands, and how quickly its learning-to-productivity cycle is improving.

THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECT
Why the rankings may change faster than conventional economic rankings
The most consequential feature of the token economy is that learning can become self-reinforcing.
Countries that combine compute, research, industrial applications and capital can generate a recursive advantage:
More deployment → more feedback → better models → cheaper execution → more deployment.
This could widen economic differences even when access to foundational AI models becomes more widespread.
The United States retains the strongest combined position in frontier development and private capital.
China possesses exceptional potential to convert intelligence into physical output through manufacturing and robotics.
South Korea and Taiwan occupy indispensable semiconductor layers.
Europe’s opportunity lies in connecting its engineering and industrial base to increasingly accessible intelligence.
The financial consequence is a possible rotation from countries producing intelligence toward countries deploying intelligence most productively.
That rotation is not yet established. It is precisely what future Espresso Index releases should test.
FIVE KEY TAKEAWAYS FOR INVESTORS
1.The next AI premium is learning velocity. Look beyond installed compute toward the speed at which organizations turn inference into better decisions, productive actions and recurring revenue.
2. Distinguish AI producers from AI converters. The United States dominates capital formation; China is particularly strong in industrial deployment. These represent different sources of financial return.
3. Watch the second tier of the ranking. South Korea, Japan, Taiwan and leading European industrial economies can capture substantial value through semiconductors, automation and specialized industrial systems.
4. Europe needs an industrial conversion strategy. Germany, France, the Netherlands, Spain and Italy possess complementary capabilities. Their potential increases if energy, capital, data and procurement operate as a more integrated market.
5. Earnings diffusion is the decisive market catalyst. With roughly two-thirds of forecast U.S. third-quarter earnings growth concentrated in technology and AI leaders, investors should watch for verified margin and ROIC improvements in industries adopting AI.
THE ESPRESSO VERDICT
THE WORLD IS ENTERING THE ECONOMICS OF COMPOUNDING INTELLIGENCE
The Big Mac Index made the purchasing power of currencies intuitive.
The Espresso Index aims to make the productive velocity of intelligence measurable.
The distinction matters.
In the industrial economy, competitive advantage was built through capital accumulation, manufacturing scale and organizational efficiency.
In the AI-native economy, those advantages increasingly interact with accelerated computing and machine learning systems capable of improving through repeated feedback.
The decisive economic unit may therefore shift from the quantity of intelligence an economy can access toward the speed at which that intelligence compounds into productive capability.
That changes the question for investors, boards and policymakers.
Not simply:
Who has the most GPUs?
Not merely:
Who produces the best models?
But:
WHICH ECONOMY LEARNS FASTEST—AND TURNS THAT LEARNING INTO THE HIGHEST SUSTAINABLE RETURN ON CAPITAL?
Because in the next economy, intelligence is increasingly available to everyone.
The capacity to compound it into productive economic power will not be.
COMPUTE CREATES INTELLIGENCE.
LEARNING CREATES VELOCITY.
CONVERSION CREATES WEALTH.
THE FUTURE WILL NOT BELONG SIMPLY TO THE ECONOMIES THAT KNOW THE MOST. IT WILL BELONG TO THOSE THAT LEARN, ADAPT AND COMPOUND THE FASTEST.

America is building increasingly powerful intelligence. China is building increasingly abundant intelligence.

1 October 2026 | FDK

The Big Take

The U.S.–China AI race is being misread.

It is no longer primarily a contest over who has the best model.

It is becoming a contest between two fundamentally different economic systems for producing and deploying intelligence.

AMERICA IS MAXIMIZING THE VALUE OF INTELLIGENCE.

CHINA IS MAXIMIZING THE VOLUME OF INTELLIGENCE.

That distinction changes the investment thesis.

America pushes the frontier higher.

China pushes the deployment cost lower.

One creates scarcity value.

The other creates abundance.

And together they are making the AI Supercycle larger.

⸻

1. AMERICA: INTELLIGENCE AS A HIGH-VALUE CAPITAL ASSET

The American system starts with one assumption:

Superior intelligence creates disproportionate economic value.

Its loop is:

CAPITAL → COMPUTE → CAPABILITY → HIGH-VALUE TASKS → CASH FLOW → MORE CAPITAL

The U.S. combines the deepest capital markets with frontier laboratories, advanced semiconductors, hyperscale cloud and enormous AI factories.

Its objective is not cheap intelligence first.

It is better intelligence first.

Better reasoning.

Better coding.

Better science.

Better agents.

Better autonomous research.

Better Physical AI.

The economic bet is enormous:

IF INTELLIGENCE BECOMES MORE CAPABLE, ITS ECONOMIC VALUE RISES FASTER THAN ITS COST.

That justifies the extraordinary CAPEX.

America is effectively financializing the frontier.

⸻

2. CHINA: INTELLIGENCE AS AN INDUSTRIAL INPUT

China starts from almost the opposite assumption.

Intelligence becomes most powerful when it becomes cheap enough to put everywhere.

Its loop is:

EFFICIENCY → LOWER COST → DEPLOYMENT → SCALE → DATA → OPTIMIZATION → STILL LOWER COST

DeepSeek attacks model economics.

Huawei attacks compute constraints.

CANN attacks CUDA dependence.

Chinese memory and networking attack infrastructure bottlenecks.

Robotics and manufacturing turn cheaper intelligence into physical output.

And offshore compute access can potentially bridge whatever frontier gap remains.

The Chinese objective is therefore not necessarily:

build the world’s single best model.

It is:

MAKE POWERFUL INTELLIGENCE CHEAP ENOUGH TO BECOME AN INDUSTRIAL COMMODITY.

That is a very different strategy.

⸻

3. THE DEEPER DIVIDE: SCARCITY vs. ABUNDANCE

This may be the defining distinction.

🇺🇸 AMERICA MONETIZES SCARCITY

Create intelligence few others can produce.

Deploy it into high-value tasks.

Capture premium economics.

Reinvest the rents into the next frontier.

🇨🇳 CHINA INDUSTRIALIZES ABUNDANCE

Make intelligence increasingly sufficient.

Compress its cost.

Deploy it across enormous volumes.

Turn scale into data, learning and lower unit economics.

The American system asks:

HOW VALUABLE CAN THE NEXT UNIT OF INTELLIGENCE BECOME?

China increasingly asks:

HOW MANY UNITS OF INTELLIGENCE CAN THE ECONOMY ABSORB?

That is not a semantic difference.

It is a different production function.

⸻

4. CHINA’S PIVOTAL ADVANCE: IT NO LONGER NEEDS PARITY

This is where the competitive analysis changes.

China does not necessarily need:

Ascend = Nvidia

or

CANN = CUDA

or

DeepSeek = the best U.S. frontier model.

It needs:

CHINESE STACK ECONOMICS ≤ ECONOMIC VALUE OF CHINESE AI

That is a much lower hurdle.

A weaker accelerator can be compensated by:

better interconnect,

more efficient models,

cheaper energy,

lower-cost inference,

greater manufacturing scale,

and faster deployment.

The strategic objective therefore shifts from technological parity to economic sufficiency.

That is far more achievable.

⸻

5. THE AMERICAN RISK IS NOT LOSING THE FRONTIER

This is the most consequential second-order implication.

The biggest American risk may not be China building a better frontier model.

It may be China making frontier superiority less economically decisive.If an American system produces intelligence at 100 capability for $100, while a Chinese system delivers 90 capability for $10, enormous categories of economic activity may choose the second.

Not frontier science.

Not necessarily the hardest reasoning.

But:

manufacturing,

customer service,

logistics,

coding,

mobility,

robotics,

industrial automation,

edge AI,

and emerging-market deployment.

China does not need to eliminate the capability gap.

IT NEEDS TO MAKE THE GAP WORTH LESS.

That is the strategic challenge.

⸻

6. THE CHINESE RISK IS THE OPPOSITE

China’s model has its own vulnerability.

If frontier intelligence begins producing non-linear economic returns, then being slightly behind may become extremely expensive.

Suppose better AI accelerates:

AI research,

drug discovery,

chip design,

software engineering,

robotics,

scientific discovery.

Then:

better AI → faster innovation → still better AI.

The American capability advantage becomes recursive.

That is the U.S. counter-loop:

FRONTIER AI → AI BUILDS BETTER AI → FRONTIER MOVES FASTER

If that mechanism dominates, abundance below the frontier may not compensate for falling behind at the top.

This is why the race remains genuinely unresolved.

⸻

7. THE REAL BATTLE: COMPOUNDING

The decisive question is therefore no longer:

Who has the best model?

It is:

WHICH SYSTEM COMPOUNDS FASTER?

THE AMERICAN COMPOUNDING LOOP

Better intelligence
→ higher-value applications
→ higher margins
→ more capital
→ more compute
→ better intelligence.

THE CHINESE COMPOUNDING LOOP

Cheaper intelligence
→ more deployment
→ more users and machines
→ more data and optimization
→ lower costs
→ cheaper intelligence.

America compounds through:

CAPABILITY × CAPITAL

China compounds through:

EFFICIENCY × SCALE

That is the Great Split.

⸻

8. DIGITAL AMERICA vs. PHYSICAL CHINA

The distinction becomes even more consequential as AI leaves the cloud.

America is strongest where intelligence creates high-value digital outputs:

science
→ coding
→ finance
→ agents
→ knowledge work.

China possesses an extraordinary transmission mechanism into:

factories
→ robots
→ vehicles
→ logistics
→ energy systems
→ infrastructure.

That gives China a potentially powerful advantage in the next phase:

PHYSICAL AI.

The strategic asymmetry becomes:

America may invent intelligence faster.

China may embody intelligence faster.

And economic power ultimately depends on both.

⸻

9. BUILD vs. RENT ADDS ANOTHER CHINESE OPTION

The reported Tencent–Oracle arrangement makes the Chinese model even more interesting.

China can potentially pursue three strategies simultaneously:

BUILD

Huawei + CANN + domestic memory + sovereign infrastructure.

RENT

Access frontier American compute offshore where possible.

DIFFUSE

Deploy intelligence through China’s enormous industrial ecosystem.

The strategy becomes:

RENT WHAT YOU CANNOT YET BUILD.

BUILD WHAT YOU CANNOT SAFELY RENT.

DEPLOY EVERYTHING.

That is not autarky.

It is strategic optionality.

⸻

10. THE TWO ECONOMIC EQUATIONS

The divergence can now be expressed simply.

🇺🇸 AMERICAN AI POWER

CAPABILITY × COMPUTE × CAPITAL × VALUE PER TASK

Optimize:

THE VALUE OF INTELLIGENCE

🇨🇳 CHINESE AI POWER

EFFICIENCY × SYSTEM COST × INDUSTRIAL SCALE × DEPLOYMENT VELOCITY

Optimize:

THE ABUNDANCE OF INTELLIGENCE

Neither equation is inherently superior across every economic activity.

That is precisely why both can scale simultaneously.

⸻

THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECT

And here lies the most important investment conclusion.

The two systems do not cancel one another.

THEY ACCELERATE ONE ANOTHER.

America raises capability.

China lowers cost.

Higher capability creates more valuable use cases.

Lower cost makes more use cases economical.

The addressable market expands from both directions.

The loop becomes:

**Capability ↑

• Cost ↓
    → Applications ↑↑
    → Inference ↑↑
    → Infrastructure ↑
    → Capital Formation ↑
    → Capability ↑ + Cost ↓**

That is the AI Supercycle.

⸻

KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. Stop treating U.S.–China AI as one race.The systems increasingly optimize different economic variables.

2. America owns the capability frontier.
Its advantage is strongest where marginal intelligence creates disproportionate economic value.

3. China is building the economic frontier.
Its challenge is to make sufficiently powerful intelligence cheap, deployable and ubiquitous.

4. China’s objective is not necessarily parity.
It is to make American superiority less economically decisive.

5. America’s counterweapon is recursive intelligence.
If frontier AI accelerates AI research itself, the capability gap can compound faster than China can commoditize it.

6. Physical AI may become China’s strongest battlefield.
Its manufacturing installed base can convert cheap intelligence into industrial output at extraordinary scale.

7. The infrastructure thesis strengthens under either outcome.
More capability and lower costs both drive more compute, memory, networking and power.

⸻

THE VELOCITY INSIGHT

The U.S.–China AI race is becoming a confrontation between two theories of economic power.

America asks:

HOW POWERFUL CAN INTELLIGENCE BECOME?

China asks:

HOW ABUNDANT CAN INTELLIGENCE BECOME?

America raises the ceiling.

China lowers the floor.

And the distance between them becomes the economic territory AI can conquer.

That leads to the deeper conclusion:

AMERICA IS BUILDING THE FRONTIER.

CHINA IS BUILDING THE MARKET.

If America wins, intelligence becomes extraordinarily valuable.

If China succeeds, intelligence becomes extraordinarily abundant.

If both succeed—as increasingly appears possible—

INTELLIGENCE BECOMES BOTH MORE POWERFUL AND CHEAPER AT THE SAME TIME.

And that is the most expansionary outcome of all.

The frontier creates value. Abundance creates volume. Together, they create the AI Supercycle.

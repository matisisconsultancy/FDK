Implications for Investors
The investment strategy should move one level deeper into the physical system.
The most attractive assets increasingly possess some combination of:
scarcity, pricing power, capacity visibility, contractual demand and short capital payback.
Soitec’s multi-year commitments are a good example of the kind of evidence investors should look for.
Not simply:
AI exposure.
But:
AI scarcity + contractual economics.
At the same time, the denominator must remain central.
Warsh has shifted rate expectations.
Oil is above $90 again.
Japanese yields are rising.
The long-duration hurdle rate remains high.
That means the most vulnerable assets are still those whose valuation depends on:
cash flows arriving far in the future, high leverage, continuous refinancing and aggressive utilization assumptions.
The best AI exposure increasingly combines:
scarcity rents with proximity of cash flow.
⸻
Implications for Executives
Enterprise AI architecture should now be designed around bottleneck independence.
It is no longer sufficient to secure:
a model
or
accelerated computing.
Executives increasingly need to think across:
models, accelerators, memory, optical networking, cloud capacity, energy and financing.
But that does not mean owning everything.
It means designing substitution.
The key board-level question becomes:
If one scarce layer doubles in price or becomes geopolitically unavailable, can the architecture still operate?
That is resilience.
And resilience is increasingly part of productivity.
⸻
Implications for Policymakers
Europe should draw three conclusions from this morning.
First, strategic autonomy should begin with actual bottlenecks, not slogans.
Soitec’s photonics position is strategically more meaningful than announcing another generic AI fund.
Second, Europe must avoid answering China solely through tariffs.
Germany’s improving industrial outlook is encouraging: its export-expectations index reached a four-year high in August, helped in part by AI, digitalization and government-backed investment. But its trade deficit with China and competitive pressure remain severe.
Third, energy and capital-market integration are increasingly inseparable from technology policy.
French harmonized inflation has already accelerated to 2.7%, German inflation data arrive today, and euro-area inflation this week will influence expectations for an ECB move on September 10.
Europe’s strategic objective should therefore be:
Productive Intelligence per Euro of Scarce Capital
—not maximum subsidy expenditure.

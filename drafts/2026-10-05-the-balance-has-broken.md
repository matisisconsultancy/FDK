Delfin has moved. The shareholder arithmetic has tightened. October 29 is now a test of whether MPS can still assemble the coalition required to keep the Lovaglio architecture alive.

5 October 2026

⸻

THE BIG TAKE

The Italian banking Risiko has entered its decisive phase.

Delfin — MPS’s largest shareholder with roughly 17.6% — has committed its stake to Intesa Sanpaolo’s revised offer and aligned itself against the defensive resolutions.

That matters more than Intesa’s additional €800 million.

The contest has moved from industrial logic to shareholder arithmetic.

**INTESA HAS THE LEAD.

MPS STILL HAS A PATH — BUT IT HAS NARROWED.**

October 29 is now a three-trigger test:

Banco BPM.
Banca Generali.
The capital restructuring supporting the defence.

Approval of any one can jeopardise Intesa’s offer.

Failure of all three leaves the Intesa path open.

⸻

1 | WHAT CHANGED

DELFIN CREATED THE FIRST HARD BLOCK

Intesa raised the cash component of its bid to €1.25 per MPS share, alongside 1.6 Intesa shares.

But price did not fundamentally change the contest.

Delfin did.

Its commitment reduces the pro-MPS voting pool and makes Caltagirone and the institutional investors the next decisive actors.

THE FIRST ORGANISED SHAREHOLDER BLOCK NOW BELONGS TO INTESA.

⸻

2 | THE DECISIVE ISSUE

THE VOTING ARITHMETIC

The hurdle is a two-thirds majority of votes cast on each defensive resolution, not two-thirds of total MPS capital.

That produces three useful thresholds:

Delfin alone — 17.6% NO: MPS needs turnout above roughly 52.8%.

Delfin + disclosed Caltagirone — about 27.9% NO: MPS needs turnout above roughly 83.7%.

Treasury-adjusted breakpoint — about 31.7% NO: with the Treasury’s roughly 4.9% absent, that level of organised opposition would effectively exhaust MPS’s mathematical room.

This is the arithmetic that now governs the contest.

⸻

3 | THE THREE-TRIGGER MECHANICS

0 = INTESA. 1–2 = DISLOCATION. 3 = FULL MPS DEFENCE.

TRIGGER 1 | BANCO BPM

Approval preserves the banking-scale leg of the Lovaglio strategy.

TRIGGER 2 | BANCA GENERALI

Approval preserves the wealth and distribution leg.

TRIGGER 3 | CAPITAL REDUCTION

Approval preserves the capital mechanics supporting the defensive structure.

The outcomes are asymmetric:

None passes → Intesa remains actionable.

One or two pass → Intesa can fall away, but MPS is left with an incomplete architecture.

All three pass → the full Lovaglio defence survives.

THE MOST DISRUPTIVE OUTCOME IS A PARTIAL MPS VICTORY.

8 | POLITICS AND REGULATION

PRIVATE CAPITAL CHOOSES. REGULATORS CLEAR. GOVERNMENTS CONDITION.

The Treasury will not vote its residual MPS stake.

The remaining gates are:

Consob,
Italian Antitrust,
Golden Power.

Across Europe the same model is emerging around UniCredit–Commerzbank:

shareholders choose the transaction; regulators determine executability; governments negotiate strategic safeguards.

That is Banking Union advancing transaction by transaction.

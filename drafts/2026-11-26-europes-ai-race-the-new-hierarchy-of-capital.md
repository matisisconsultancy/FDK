Spain consolidates its Gigafactory candidature, regulatory uncertainty intensifies, and Portugal emerges as an alternative destination for AI infrastructure investment.
THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
Will Europe's AI investment geography be determined more by access to electricity, regulatory certainty and capital formation than by technological capabilities alone?
THE BIG TAKE
Three consequential developments reveal an emerging reallocation of European AI infrastructure capital. Spain is strengthening its Gigafactory proposal while regulatory uncertainty threatens investment economics. Portugal is positioning itself as an alternative destination for large-scale computing infrastructure.

1 | SPAIN — MERLIN STRENGTHENS THE GIGAFACTORY BID
Merlin Properties has reportedly reserved 20 MW at its Tres Cantos campus near Madrid to support Spain's European AI Gigafactory candidature.
The proposed architecture combines a principal computing facility in Móra la Nova, Tarragona, with complementary capacity in Madrid.
Financial implication: Electricity-connected real estate is becoming an increasingly valuable component of AI infrastructure investment. Final site selection, financing and construction authorizations remain outstanding.
2 | SPAIN — REGULATORY UNCERTAINTY ENTERS THE COURTS
SpainDC filed an appeal before Spain's Supreme Court on November 24, requesting the suspension of the recently adopted data-center decree.
The regulation provides differentiated treatment for qualifying European sovereign AI initiatives.
No judicial suspension has been confirmed.
Second-order effect: A two-tier investment market could emerge, with regulatory eligibility influencing financing costs, expected returns and the allocation of infrastructure capital.
3 | PORTUGAL — A NEW COMPETITOR FOR AI CAPITAL
Fortitude Capital's proposed 202 MW Hermes AI campus in Sines reinforces Portugal's ambitions to attract large-scale computing investment.
Meanwhile, Iberdrola has withdrawn its previously announced €426 million hydrogen and ammonia project in the same industrial zone.
These are separate developments; they do not establish that capital or electricity has been transferred from one project to the other.
The hidden pattern: Energy-intensive industries are increasingly competing for the same scarce resources—electricity connections, suitable land, infrastructure investment and institutional capital.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
Investors: Prioritize secured electricity, credible financing and regulatory stability over announced megawatts.
Banks: Incorporate permitting, grid availability and political uncertainty into infrastructure risk premiums.
CEOs and boards: Integrate power procurement, computing infrastructure and Industrial AI demand into long-term investment strategies.
Policymakers: Recognize that regulatory uncertainty can influence cross-border capital allocation.
THE CLOSING VERDICT
Europe's AI infrastructure race is becoming a contest between jurisdictions capable of translating energy resources and financial commitments into commercially executable projects.
The decisive advantage will belong to those who transform electricity access, institutional credibility and industrial demand into productive computing capacity and sustainable returns.
VELOCITY INSIGHT
In Europe's AI race, the geography of capital will increasingly follow the geography of investable electricity.

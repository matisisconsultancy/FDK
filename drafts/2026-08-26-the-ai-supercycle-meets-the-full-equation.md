Today Nvidia tests the numerator, U.S. inflation tests the denominator, and Hormuz tests the physical cost base. The Great Split is moving beyond access to artificial intelligence toward the ability to convert accelerated computing, energy and scarce capital into sustainable free cash flow.
Data cut: 07:45 CEST
This is one of those days when three apparently separate market stories converge into a single economic equation.
Later today, Nvidia reports results that could determine whether the extraordinary expansion of AI infrastructure continues to validate the capital being committed underneath it. Analysts expect second-quarter revenue of approximately $92.18 billion, almost double the level of a year earlier, while third-quarter revenue is expected to rise 82.8% to $104.2 billion. Gross margins are expected to remain around 75%.
Before that, at 14:30 CEST, the United States publishes July PCE inflation. Economists expect core PCE to rise 0.2% month-on-month and remain at 3.3% year-on-year—still far above the Federal Reserve’s 2% objective. Boston Fed President Susan Collins warned Tuesday that rates may need to rise soon if convincing disinflation does not resume.
And overnight, the third component of the equation moved sharply in the opposite direction.
Brent has fallen another 2.6% to approximately $86.28, following a decline of more than 3% Tuesday, after Iran and Oman resumed negotiations over a temporary navigational corridor through the Strait of Hormuz. The oil move has helped pull the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield down toward 4.63%. But only five commodity vessels passed through Hormuz Tuesday, compared with a ten-day average of 15 and traffic far below pre-war levels.
Markets are therefore simultaneously repricing:
the return on artificial intelligence,
the cost of capital,
and
the cost of the physical system on which artificial intelligence depends.
That is the complete equation.
And beneath it lies something even more consequential.
Nvidia has helped arrange roughly $500 billion of AI-infrastructure financing and recently agreed to guarantee up to $105 billion supporting OpenAI’s twenty-year Ohio data-center lease. Big Tech data-center spending is expected to exceed $730 billion this year.
AI is no longer simply competing with other technologies for corporate budgets.
It is competing with governments, households and the rest of the economy for capital, electricity and duration.
That is where The Great Split moves today.
⸻
The Fundamental Question
Can the economic value created by artificial intelligence compound faster than the cost of the capital, energy and infrastructure required to produce it?
That sounds simple.
It is not.
The AI Supercycle now operates through three simultaneous prices.
Price One — Intelligence
Algorithmic efficiency and model competition continue pushing the marginal cost of intelligence downward.
Price Two — Infrastructure
Accelerated computing, memory, electricity, transmission, cooling, powered land and data-center capacity remain scarce.
Price Three — Capital
AI infrastructure must increasingly compete for long-duration financing against governments, defense, energy systems and the broader corporate economy.
The winning economics therefore become:
Falling Intelligence Cost × Expanding Usage × Rising Productivity
must exceed:
Infrastructure Inflation + Capital Cost + Technological Depreciation.
That is the real hurdle rate.

The Five Most Important Overnight Signals
Nvidia Has Become the Earnings Report for the AI Economy
Verified Facts
Nvidia reports today with revenue expected near $92.2 billion. Data-center sales are expected to more than double, while investors will scrutinize the transition from Blackwell to Vera Rubin, whose shipments are due to begin this autumn. Analysts expect Rubin to contribute close to $9 billion of sales during the quarter ending in October.
Tuesday’s market positioning was constructive but cautious. Nvidia gained 2.2%, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index 1.4%, the Nasdaq 0.66% and the S&P 500 0.32%. Nasdaq futures were about 0.5% lower early this morning as investors reduced exposure before the announcement.
FDK Interpretation
Nvidia has crossed a threshold.
Its earnings are no longer merely a technology-sector event.
They have become a macro reading of the AI capital cycle.
The report provides information about:
accelerated-computing demand, hyperscaler CapEx, memory demand, power demand, data-center construction, infrastructure financing, and eventually productivity.
The market already assumes Nvidia will beat expectations.
Therefore:
execution is no longer the upside catalyst.
Execution is the minimum requirement.
The relevant question is what happens after Rubin.
If Nvidia can demonstrate that each generation of accelerated computing materially improves the economics of an AI factory—more inference, more tokens and more intelligence at similar or lower power and capital cost—then the entire investment cycle becomes easier to finance.
If not, the denominator becomes progressively more important.
This is why today’s result should be read through one new metric:
Economically useful intelligence produced per dollar of AI infrastructure.
That is becoming a far more important number than accelerator shipments alone.

Hormuz Relief Has Become Duration Relief
Verified Facts
Iran and Oman have resumed negotiations over management of the Strait of Hormuz, including discussion of a temporary joint navigational corridor and mine clearing. Brent has responded violently, falling more than 3% Tuesday and another 2.6% early today to around $86.28. U.S. 10-year yields fell roughly 6.5 basis points Tuesday and are near 4.63% this morning.
But physical normalization has not occurred.
Only five commodity ships crossed Tuesday. Iran continues to insist that the Strait will remain restricted until wider conditions are satisfied.
FDK Interpretation
The market reaction reveals something important.
Hormuz is no longer simply an energy trade.
It is a duration trade.
The recursive chain is now visible:
Hormuz risk ↓ → oil ↓ → inflation premium ↓ → Treasury yields ↓ → WACC ↓ → technology-duration valuations ↑.
Oil and Nvidia increasingly belong inside the same financial model.
This matters because AI infrastructure is exceptionally sensitive to the discount rate.
A ten- or twenty-year data-center commitment is not valued merely through expected utilization.
Its economics depend upon:
financing cost, electricity cost, residual technology value, and the timing of future cash flows.Lower oil therefore acts indirectly as AI financial easing.
But the caution is equally important.
Five vessels are not normalization.
Today’s $86 oil price reflects negotiation optionality, not restored physical throughput.
The market is buying the possibility of normalization before the infrastructure has actually normalized.
That distinction matters.

PCE Will Test Whether the Denominator Can Really Fall
Verified Facts
July core PCE is expected at 3.3% year-on-year and +0.2% month-on-month. Fed policy remains at 3.50%–3.75%. Boston Fed President Susan Collins said Tuesday that further tightening may soon be appropriate unless inflation shows sustained progress toward target.
U.S. consumer confidence meanwhile declined to 89.4 in August, its lowest in seven months, while July new-home sales dropped 10.5% to a 607,000 annual rate, the weakest since January.
FDK Interpretation
This produces a particularly difficult macro configuration:
the consumer is weakening before inflation is defeated.
That is a very different environment from the classical soft landing.
The Great Split is becoming visible inside the U.S. economy:
AI investment velocity ↑↑↑↑
while
household purchasing velocity ↓.
Corporate artificial-intelligence investment can keep aggregate capital formation strong even while higher housing costs, weak real wage growth and inflation constrain consumers.
That means the Federal Reserve increasingly faces two economies.
One is capital-rich, technology-intensive and accelerating.
The other is consumption-dependent and increasingly sensitive to rates.
If core PCE remains around 3.3%, the Fed cannot comfortably solve the second problem by cutting rates.
And if it raises rates to restrain inflation, it increases the hurdle rate on the first.
That is a genuine policy dilemma.

Europe Is Beginning to Grow Again—Just as the ECB Prepares to Tighten
Verified Facts
Germany’s second-quarter GDP was revised upward to +0.3% quarter-on-quarter, following 0.4% growth in Q1. Exports grew 2%, while the Ifo business-climate index rose to 88.8, substantially above the 87.2 consensus and its strongest reading in a year.
At the same time, Reuters reports that ECB policymakers are preparing for another rate increase in September, probably taking the deposit rate from 2.25% to 2.50%, as inflation remains near 3%. Policymakers nevertheless show little appetite to signal a prolonged tightening sequence.
FDK Interpretation
Europe is entering a fascinating phase.
For years the problem was insufficient growth.
Now the problem may become:
how to finance a recovery without extinguishing it.
Germany is benefiting from stronger exports and early manufacturing momentum. Its €500 billion infrastructure fund should eventually add domestic investment.
But Europe simultaneously needs to finance:
defense, energy security, grids, rail infrastructure, accelerated computing, AI factories, industrial automation and demographic obligations.
And it is entering that investment cycle with materially higher sovereign yields.
Europe’s next competitive test therefore becomes:
Can productivity growth rise faster than the cost of the capital being mobilized to produce it?
That is exactly the same question Nvidia faces.
It is simply being asked at the level of a continent.

AI Has Reached the Sovereign Balance Sheet
Verified Facts
Morgan Stanley estimates that $350–400 billion of AI-related public investment-grade debt could be issued in 2026, while some $1.4 trillion of AI CapEx is planned for 2027. About $92 billion has been financed across debt markets since mid-year alone.
At the same time, corporate U.S. tax receipts are down roughly 23%, partly because the 2025 tax legislation allows companies to immediately expense large capital investments—including data centers and AI infrastructure. The United States continues to run deficits of roughly 6% of GDP, while annual interest expense on approximately $40 trillion of total debt exceeds $1 trillion.
FDK Interpretation
This is one of the most consequential hidden developments of the entire AI Supercycle.AI is beginning to affect the Treasury market from both sides.
On one side:
AI investment requires corporate borrowing.
That competes with sovereign borrowing for long-duration capital.
On the other:
AI investment receives immediate tax deductions.
That reduces near-term federal tax receipts.
The chain becomes:
**AI CapEx ↑ → corporate debt issuance ↑
corporate tax receipts ↓ → government funding pressure ↑ → competition for duration ↑ → long yields ↑ → AI WACC ↑.**
This is extraordinary.
Artificial intelligence can raise future productivity while simultaneously making the short-term sovereign financing problem harder.
That gives the AI Supercycle a fiscal dimension markets have barely begun to price.

Nvidia Is Becoming the Central Capital Allocator of the AI Ecosystem
Nvidia’s role is expanding well beyond selling accelerated computing.
It is helping arrange $500 billion of financing, guaranteeing as much as $105 billion on an OpenAI data-center lease, investing in infrastructure and supporting customers whose growth can expand demand for Nvidia systems.
This creates a powerful feedback loop:
Nvidia cash flow → AI ecosystem financing → infrastructure capacity → AI adoption → inference demand → accelerated-computing demand → Nvidia cash flow.

At what point does ecosystem reinforcement become financial interdependence?
The stronger the financing loop becomes, the harder it becomes to separate organic demand from demand supported by the ecosystem itself.
That does not invalidate the AI Supercycle.
It raises the burden of proof around cash-flow conversion.

The Great Split Is Moving From Technology to Balance-Sheet Quality
Two AI projects may deploy identical accelerated computing.
But if one has:
secured power, long-term customer contracts, low leverage, high utilization and short payback periods,
while the other relies on:
expensive debt, speculative utilization, uncertain grid access and rapid technology replacement,
they are not the same asset.
The next phase of AI investment will increasingly separate:
technologically impressive assets
from
financially durable assets.
That is an enormous shift.
The AI race is becoming a balance-sheet race.
⸻
The World’s Scarce Asset Is Becoming Productive Duration
Capital itself is not disappearing.
Institutional savings remain enormous.
Sovereign funds have trillions.
Bond markets remain deep.
But the number of competing long-duration claims is exploding:
AI infrastructure, sovereign deficits, defense, energy systems, grids, transport infrastructure and demographic obligations.
The scarce asset is increasingly:
capital willing to wait.
And patient capital will become progressively more selective.
That is why the next competitive advantage is not simply access to funding.
It is access to funding at a cost consistent with productive returns.

Fiscal Dominance Is Moving From Academic Debate to Market Variable
Jackson Hole begins this week with one question increasingly difficult to avoid:
Who ultimately absorbs government debt?
The United States has run deficits above 4% of GDP every year since 2019. Long-term Treasury yields have risen sharply, Treasury has expanded bond buybacks, and economists are again debating the boundary between fiscal and monetary policy.
This matters enormously for AI.
If central banks are pushed to restrain sovereign borrowing costs, currencies and inflation expectations may absorb part of the adjustment.
If they refuse, long-duration corporate infrastructure bears higher financing costs.Either outcome reaches the AI economy.
This is why fiscal credibility has become an AI variable.

Implications for Investors
Today should be read through a three-dimensional AI valuation model.
The first dimension is Operating Velocity:
Can revenue, inference and free cash flow keep compounding?
The second is Capital Velocity:
How much infrastructure capital is required to create the next dollar of revenue?
The third is Financial Velocity:
At what WACC can that investment be financed?
The most attractive assets should increasingly combine:
rapid revenue growth, high utilization, short capital payback, durable scarcity, strong balance sheets and modest dependence on financial engineering.
The vulnerable assets are likely to combine:
long-duration assumptions, high leverage, weak utilization, fast technological depreciation and expensive physical infrastructure.
That distinction will increasingly matter more than whether the stock is simply labeled “AI.”

Implications for Executives
Boards should adopt a much harder standard for artificial-intelligence investment.
The strategic question should no longer be:
How much AI capacity are we deploying?
It should be:
How much economically useful intelligence does every euro or dollar of capital produce?
Every AI program should increasingly report:
capital committed, inference produced, revenue generated, operating-cost savings, energy consumed, payback period and incremental free cash flow.
AI should move from the technology dashboard to the capital-allocation dashboard.
That is the next stage of managerial maturity.

Implications for Policymakers
Europe has an opportunity that was not visible even six months ago.
Germany is recovering.
European manufacturing indicators are improving.
Europe still possesses world-class positions in:
industrial engineering, automation, power systems, mobility, semiconductor equipment and advanced manufacturing.
But it must connect those assets to:
accelerated computing, AI factories, power, grids and patient capital.
The objective should not be to replicate the American hyperscaler model.
Nor should it be simply to announce ever larger subsidy envelopes.
The objective should be:
maximum productive intelligence per euro of strategic European capital.
That is how Europe converts AI investment into an industrial advantage rather than simply becoming a customer of somebody else’s intelligence infrastructure.

Does the economic output justify the capital input?

Nvidia tests the numerator.
PCE tests the denominator.
Hormuz tests the physical cost base.
The Treasury market tests the availability of duration.

**Capital

Lower risk reduces the cost of capital.
Cheaper capital accelerates investment.
Investment raises productive capacity.
Higher productivity produces still more cash.
The cycle becomes self-reinforcing.

**Capital

capital + energy + accelerated computing + artificial intelligence

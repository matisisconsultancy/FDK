Oil is back above $90. Two-year Treasuries hold near 4.34%. The yen has crossed 160. And Soitec is locking AI-photonics customers into multi-year contracts with deposits and fixed prices. The Great Split is moving from who can access artificial intelligence toward who controls the scarce physical and financial inputs beneath it.
Data cut: 07:45 CEST
Monday begins with a sharper version of the equation markets confronted last week.
The AI demand story remains exceptionally strong.
But almost every scarce input underneath it is becoming more expensive, more strategic or more politically constrained.
Brent crude is up roughly 2.8% at $90.60 after U.S. forces struck Iranian missile launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz and Iran retaliated against U.S. forces in Jordan. Asian equities reacted immediately: MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan fell about 1.2%, Japan’s Nikkei around 1.6% and South Korea about 2.2%. European and U.S. equity futures were down roughly 0.4%–0.5% before the European open.
At the same time, Kevin Warsh’s Jackson Hole message continues to propagate through the denominator. Markets now price roughly a 57%–60% probability of a September Fed hike. The U.S. two-year Treasury yield is around 4.34%, the thirty-year around 5.21%, and Barclays has changed its forecast to expect two additional 25-basis-point Fed increases this year, in September and December.
The yen has weakened back through 160 per dollar, while Japanese ten-year government-bond yields have reached their highest level since 1996. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the currency move remains relatively orderly, reducing expectations of an immediate repeat of the July U.S.–Japan intervention.
And then comes perhaps the most revealing AI signal of the morning.
French semiconductor-materials company Soitec is asking more than ten silicon-photonics customers to sign multi-year supply agreements with fixed prices and cash deposits. Roughly 80% of those capacity reservations are expected to be completed within the next two weeks. Soitec controls an estimated 95% of the silicon-photonics substrate market, according to UBS, and now says photonics-SOI revenue above $200 million this financial year is a floor rather than a ceiling. Its shares have almost quadrupled this year.
This is enormously consequential.
Because the bottleneck is migrating again.
First:
accelerators.
Then:
HBM and memory.
Then:
power and grid connections.
Now increasingly:
optical interconnects.
And above all of them:
capital.
The chain is becoming:
**AI Demand ↑↑↑
→ Accelerated Computing ↑↑↑ → Memory Traffic ↑↑↑ → Optical Bandwidth ↑↑↑ → Power Demand ↑↑↑ → Infrastructure CapEx ↑↑↑ → Demand for Duration ↑↑↑ → WACC ↑ → Capital Selectivity ↑↑↑↑.**
That is the defining equation this morning.
The AI Supercycle is not running out of demand.
It is running into scarcity.

The Fundamental Question
Can AI productivity increase fast enough to outrun the simultaneous scarcity premiums emerging in energy, optics, memory, capital and geopolitical access?
This is becoming the central investment question of the Next Economy.
For several years, the most valuable scarce input was accelerated computing.
That remains important.
But the architecture around it is widening.
Artificial intelligence increasingly requires:
accelerated computing,
memory,
optical networking,
electricity,
transmission,
physical security,
and long-duration capital.
Each constraint can migrate independently.
And each can temporarily capture a disproportionate share of the economics.
This gives us an increasingly powerful general law:
AI does not abolish scarcity. It migrates scarcity.
The winners change as the bottleneck moves.
That is The Great Split investors now need to follow.

The Five Most Important Overnight Signals
Energy Has Re-Entered the Hurdle Rate
Verified Facts
U.S. forces struck two Iranian missile launchers on Larak Island on Sunday. Iran subsequently attacked U.S. forces stationed in Jordan, according to reporting cited by Reuters. Brent moved above $90. President Trump separately claimed that Iran’s Kharg oil terminal was being heavily attacked, although Reuters reported no independent military confirmation of that claim.
Hormuz remains physically constrained despite U.S. naval escorts, although Gulf oil exports have recovered to roughly 15–16 million barrels per day, still below normal levels.
FDK Interpretation
Last week markets were beginning to price diplomatic normalization.
Monday has reversed part of that optimism.
That matters because energy now sits inside virtually every major capital-allocation decision.
The chain is immediate:
**Geopolitical Escalation
→ Oil ↑ → Inflation Expectations ↑ → Rate-Cut Optionality ↓ → Treasury Yields ↑ → WACC ↑ → Long-Duration Valuations ↓.**
This is why oil belongs inside the AI valuation model.
AI factories consume electricity.
Electricity depends on generation, gas markets, grids and transmission.
Construction depends on fuel.
Logistics depends on refined products.
And financing depends on inflation expectations.
The cost of producing intelligence therefore includes a geopolitical energy premium.
That premium has risen again this morning.

Optical Interconnects Are Becoming the Next AI Tollbooth
Verified Facts
Soitec is securing multi-year commitments from more than ten customers for its photonics wafers, with fixed pricing and deposits tied to committed volumes. Customers that fail to take contracted capacity can lose part of their deposit. The company is also asking customers to disclose inventory to discourage speculative over-ordering.
Demand is being driven by hyperscalers increasingly shifting from copper toward optical connections inside AI infrastructure because copper is becoming less attractive on both performance and power-efficiency grounds. Soitec supplies the substrate underneath most silicon-photonics chips and is estimated by UBS to control around 95% of that market.
FDK Interpretation
This may be today’s most important AI signal.
The economic bottleneck is propagating from:
computing capacity
toward
the ability to move data between computing units.
An AI factory is not simply thousands of accelerators.
It is an integrated system:
Accelerators × Memory × Optical Interconnects × Networking × Power × Cooling × Utilization.
Performance is increasingly determined by the weakest element.
That means optics becomes not a supporting technology—
but a constraint on intelligence production.
And when a constraint appears, pricing power follows.
Soitec’s use of:
fixed pricing,deposits,
capacity reservations,
and penalties for unused commitments
is the clearest possible financial signal that bargaining power is migrating toward the scarce layer.
This is precisely how Value Migration works inside the AI Supercycle.
The question for investors should therefore increasingly become:
Where will the next scarcity rent appear before markets fully price it?
Today, optics is giving us an answer.

The G20 Is Becoming a Forum for Capital Access, Not Merely Macro Coordination
Verified Facts
Finance ministers and central-bank governors gather today in Asheville, North Carolina, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent pushing two unusually consequential initiatives.
First, he says the U.S. intends to introduce new secondary sanctions against Iran-linked institutions essentially every week, initially focusing on banks and potentially cutting institutions off from the dollar-based financial system.
Second, Bessent says G20 countries should consider increasing trade barriers against China in order to reduce global imbalances and force Beijing to move away from export dependence toward greater domestic consumption. He described China’s approximately $1.2 trillion trade surplus as unsustainable.
FDK Interpretation
This represents a profound expansion in the meaning of financial statecraft.
The strategic instrument is no longer simply:
tariffs.
It is:
access.
Access to:
the dollar system, banking relationships, trade routes, technology, energy and end markets.
Capital markets themselves are becoming part of the geopolitical architecture.
That changes how companies must think about financial risk.
A banking relationship can become geopolitical.
A payment corridor can become geopolitical.
A supplier can become geopolitical.
A market can become geopolitical.
And the dollar becomes not merely the world’s reserve currency—
but a network whose access carries strategic value.
This increases the premium on:
financial optionality.
The strongest global companies will increasingly need not simply supply-chain diversification, but capital-chain diversification.

China’s Great Split Is Now Visible in the PMI Data
Verified Facts
China’s manufacturing PMI improved to 49.8 in August from 49.2 in July, beating expectations but remaining in contraction.
Inside the index, however, new orders and production moved above 50.
More strikingly, equipment manufacturing and high-tech manufacturing both recorded PMIs above 51.
Yet the non-manufacturing PMI remained at 49.0, its weakest reading since December 2022, reflecting continued weakness in domestically focused services and construction.
FDK Interpretation
This is almost a textbook expression of The Great Split.
China increasingly contains two economies.
The Industrial Intelligence Economy
High-tech manufacturing.
Equipment.
AI-related exports.
Electronics.
Memory.
Advanced industrial products.
Momentum is improving.
And:
The Domestic Demand Economy
Property.
Services.
Consumer spending.
Investment.
Momentum remains weak.
The result is a powerful political-economic contradiction.
China can continue expanding industrial capacity while domestic consumption underperforms.
That generates exports.
Exports generate trade surpluses.
Trade surpluses generate political resistance abroad.
Which generates tariffs.
The causal chain becomes:
**Domestic Demand Weakness
→ Industrial Capacity Continues → Exports ↑ → Global Surplus ↑ → Trade Barriers ↑ → Need for Greater Capital Efficiency ↑.**
That is why the G20 China discussion and today’s PMI release belong in the same note.
They are two sides of the same system.

Japan Is Becoming the Second Global Duration Test
Verified Facts
The yen has again weakened through 160 per dollar, while Japanese ten-year bond yields have reached their highest level since 1996. Bessent described recent yen movements as contained, reducing expectations of immediate intervention, while markets continue to anticipate possible Bank of Japan tightening.
FDK Interpretation
Japan matters because it possesses one of the world’s largest pools of institutional savings.For decades, low Japanese yields pushed capital outward.
Into:
U.S. Treasuries, European bonds, global credit, equities and infrastructure.
If Japanese yields rise materially while the yen remains volatile, the global opportunity cost of exporting Japanese savings changes.
This creates a second duration question.
Not only:
What does the Fed do?
But:
What return must global assets offer to keep Japanese capital abroad?
That can affect:
Treasuries, European sovereign debt, corporate credit and long-duration AI infrastructure.
The global cost of capital is therefore increasingly being set by more than Washington.
The entire developed-market rate structure is moving.

Five Hidden Patterns Beyond the News
The Scarcity Premium Is Migrating Through the AI System
The sequence is becoming remarkably clear.
Accelerators
became scarce.
Then:
memory.
Then:
electricity and grid connections.
Now:
optics.
And increasingly:
long-duration capital itself.
The important investment implication is simple.
The bottleneck captures the rent.
That means AI exposure must constantly be re-underwritten.
Yesterday’s commodity layer can become tomorrow’s strategic layer.
A seemingly obscure substrate manufacturer can suddenly possess more pricing power than a highly visible software business.
The AI Supercycle therefore rewards scarcity anticipation, not simply AI exposure.
⸻
The Great Split Is Becoming Physical Scarcity Versus Financial Duration
Some companies benefit when scarce physical inputs become more valuable.
Others are damaged when financing those inputs becomes more expensive.
That creates two opposing chains.
Scarcity Owners
Demand ↑ → constrained supply → prices ↑ → margins ↑ → free cash flow ↑.
Versus:
Duration Buyers
Demand ↑ → CapEx ↑ → financing requirements ↑ → WACC ↑ → valuation sensitivity ↑.
The same AI Supercycle can therefore simultaneously create:
winners in optics, power and memory
and
pressure on highly leveraged infrastructure owners.
This is why the next phase of The Great Split will increasingly occur inside AI itself.
⸻
Trade Policy Is Becoming Industrial Policy by Other Means
Bessent is asking the G20 to push China toward greater domestic consumption through tougher trade terms. Europe is moving in a similar direction: Germany and France are preparing a joint roadmap on China’s trade imbalance, while German industry is pressing for stronger protections against subsidized Chinese competition.
But there is a fundamental warning here.
Tariffs can slow the rate at which an industrial gap widens.
They cannot by themselves reverse it.
The durable answer remains:
higher productivity.
Which means:
Industrial AI, automation, accelerated computing, energy, robotics and strategic capital formation.
Protection can buy time.
Velocity determines whether the time is used productively.

Europe Has More AI Leverage Than Its Narrative Suggests
Soitec is a useful reminder.
Europe does not need to own every frontier model or accelerator to possess strategic importance in the AI Supercycle.
Europe has significant capabilities across:
semiconductor equipment, power technology, industrial software, optics, automation, mobility and advanced manufacturing.
Soitec’s roughly 95% share in silicon-photonics substrates is precisely the kind of position Europe should identify, defend and scale.
The European strategy should therefore not be:
imitate Silicon Valley everywhere.
It should be:
dominate selected physical bottlenecks where Europe already possesses deep industrial advantage.
That is a much more credible competitive strategy.

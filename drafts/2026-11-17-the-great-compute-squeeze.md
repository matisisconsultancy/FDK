THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
Can Washington restrict China’s access to advanced computing faster than Beijing can reduce the computing required to deliver commercially valuable intelligence?
Two developments on November 16–17 materially strengthen our U.S.–China AI thesis.
The United States has announced new controls targeting remote access to American advanced chips through overseas data centers. Simultaneously, a Reuters investigation documents how Chinese companies are systematically using output from American frontier models to train smaller, less expensive domestic systems. bis.gov +1
1. THE AMERICAN INFLECTION — COMPUTING ACCESS BECOMES THE NEW EXPORT FRONTIER
On November 16, the U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security announced an interim final rule addressing remote access to advanced computing infrastructure.
The new licensing requirements target Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, while covering certain overseas data centers providing substantial computing capacity to China-linked companies. Existing qualifying arrangements face a 90-day licensing transition. Formal publication and effectiveness are expected November 19; this is a concrete regulatory action, not simply a proposal. The rule contains thresholds and exceptions, rather than imposing a blanket prohibition on all overseas computing. Reuters +1

2. THE CHINESE INFLECTION — INDUSTRIALIZING MODEL DISTILLATION
A November 17 Reuters investigation examined more than 300 documents and found evidence that Chinese organizations are expanding the use of American-model outputs to develop domestic AI systems.
The economic mechanism is significant: use expensive frontier intelligence to generate training examples, then transfer selected capabilities into smaller models that can be deployed more cheaply across commercial and industrial applications. Reuters
This strengthens the diffusion component of our thesis, but it does not establish Chinese frontier superiority. Distillation is widely used internationally, is not inherently unlawful, and does not eliminate the need for advanced computing. Specific practices may raise intellectual-property or contractual concerns.
The hidden pattern: Restricting access to the infrastructure of intelligence does not necessarily restrict the diffusion of intelligence already produced.
That creates a new tension between U.S. technology controls and China’s industrial deployment strategy.

Chinese suppliers represent approximately two-thirds of that global market, according to Counterpoint Research. Those restrictions remain under consideration, not enacted. 4. WHAT MATTERS FOR THE VELOCITY EDGE
Our thesis is strengthened, but requires greater precision.
The United States maintains a powerful advantage in frontier computing and access to high-end accelerated infrastructure. China is developing alternative pathways to economical AI deployment through smaller models, specialization and industrial-scale diffusion.
The next market rotation may emerge from the interaction between those advantages—not from either one in isolation.
The decisive indicators are effective implementation of the November 19 rules, actual licensing decisions, postponed or redirected data-center investments, independently measured Chinese inference costs, and whether domestic Chinese accelerators can support competitive commercial deployments.
THE FINAL TAKE
The AI Supercycle is entering a phase in which technological leadership, industrial dependence and capital allocation are becoming inseparable.
America is attempting to preserve control over the most powerful computing capabilities. China is challenging the cost structure and distribution of the intelligence those capabilities produce.
For investors, the essential distinction is between companies controlling scarce infrastructure and companies capable of converting increasingly inexpensive intelligence into enduring economic returns.
The next winner in AI may not be the company that commands the most computing power, but the one that converts computing power into economic value at the highest sustainable return on capital.

THE MORNING VIEW—IN FOCUS

September 20, 2026 | FDK

THE COORDINATION PREMIUM

The U.S. 10-year is near 5%, Brent remains above $100, Hormuz traffic is still impaired, Washington and Beijing are negotiating AI guardrails and critical minerals, and China’s newest DRAM platform claims more than 50% higher gross dies per wafer. The constraint on the Next Economy is no longer technology alone. It is the ability to connect compute, energy, capital, regulation and demand fast enough to convert innovation into economic output.

The next phase will not necessarily reward those with the most GPUs, capital or industrial scale.

It will reward those that can turn technological acceleration into cash flow before infrastructure, financing or policy become binding constraints.

⸻

The Fundamental Question

Who can convert technological acceleration into economic power before the surrounding system slows it down?

⸻

INVESTOR ACTION BOX

ONE-LINE VERDICT

OWN THE CONTROL POINTS; UNDERWEIGHT THE ASSETS THAT PAY FOR SCARCITY, WAIT FOR PERMISSION OR REFINANCE AT 5% MONEY.

LONG / ACCUMULATE

Semiconductors, HBM and memory — structural scarcity and sovereign demand.

Optical networking and interconnect — larger compute clusters require more bandwidth.

Contracted data centres — favour secured power, utilisation and long-duration customers.

Grid equipment, nuclear, gas and storage — power availability is becoming strategic.

AI security and evaluation — governance is moving into the deployment stack.

Banks, insurers and infrastructure-finance platforms — AI capex increasingly requires institutional underwriting.

UNDERWEIGHT

Uncontracted AI capacity — gigawatts without committed demand are expensive optionality.

Legacy industrials with weak pricing power — energy and funding costs expose fragile margins.

Highly leveraged long-duration assets — 5% sovereign yields reset hurdle rates.

Narrative technology — cash conversion matters more than multiple expansion.

AVOID

Stranded scale: no secured power, no contracted demand, no regulatory certainty and no credible return above WACC.

The portfolio test: does the asset control a bottleneck—or pay for one?

Five Signals

1. AI: from technology competition to system governance

What changed: Washington and Beijing are discussing AI behaviour, critical minerals, tariffs and energy together.

Why it matters: AI has moved beyond the technology stack and into geopolitical architecture.

Implication: the competitive edge increasingly comes from shaping the rules that govern strategic systems.

⸻

2. China: from production strength to demand constraint

What changed: AI is increasing industrial productivity while domestic demand remains comparatively weak.

Why it matters: higher productivity can amplify overcapacity rather than solve it.

Implication:

**MORE PRODUCTIVITY

→ MORE OUTPUT
→ LOWER UNIT COSTS
→ MORE EXPORT PRESSURE
→ MORE TRADE FRICTION**

China’s next constraint is not production. It is demand absorption.

⸻

3. Semiconductors: from technological sovereignty to economic sovereignty

What changed: China’s domestic memory industry is improving yield, density and manufacturing economics.

Why it matters: producing a chip is not enough; sovereignty becomes durable only when production is competitive at scale.

Implication: the decisive metric moves from technological feasibility to cost, yield and volume economics.

⸻

4. AI infrastructure: from corporate capex to institutional finance

What changed: funding is broadening from hyperscalers toward banks, sovereign funds, private credit and insurers.

Why it matters: AI infrastructure is beginning to resemble a financeable asset class rather than discretionary technology spending.

Implication:

**CONTRACTED CAPACITY

→ PREDICTABLE CASH FLOW
→ COLLATERAL
→ LOWER COST OF CAPITAL
→ MORE SCALE**

The underwriting test is now whether capacity can generate infrastructure-like returns.

⸻

5.Energy: from input cost to competitive architecture

What changed: Hormuz remains impaired and oil remains above $100 while energy increasingly enters trade and strategic negotiations.

Why it matters: energy scarcity now transmits directly into inflation, interest rates and industrial competitiveness.

Implication:

**GEOPOLITICS

→ ENERGY
→ INFLATION
→ RATES
→ WACC
→ INDUSTRIAL COMPETITIVENESS**

Energy security is becoming a determinant of economic velocity.

⸻

China has industrial scale and execution capacity, but insufficient domestic demand.

Europe has capital, engineering and industry, but fragmented finance, energy and regulation.

The difference is not scale.

It is the ability to connect assets and convert them into output.

That is the new Great Split:

COORDINATED VELOCITY vs. FRAGMENTED SCALE

COMPUTE × ENERGY × CAPITAL × INFRASTRUCTURE × REGULATION × DEMAND

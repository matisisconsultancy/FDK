Forget the headline CAPEX. €5 billion is the entry ticket, not the strategy.

France, Spain and Italy are building three fundamentally different models for European AI power. France wants sovereignty. Spain wants scale. Italy wants productivity.

The decisive question is not who builds the biggest Gigafactory. It is who captures the most economic value from every euro—and every MW—invested.

::stats
€5B :: France's complete stack entry ticket
~€5B :: Spain's infrastructure-finance proposition
€5B+ :: Italy's emerging industrial architecture
3 :: fundamentally different models for European AI

::pull THE THREE BETS
France is building an AI system. Spain is building an AI asset class. Italy has the opportunity to build an AI productivity machine.

::signals The Three Models | Own it · Finance it · Use it
# France — Own the Stack
France has assembled the most complete architecture: EDF → Bull → Scaleway/iliad → Orange → Capgemini/Artefact → Ardian. Economically, that maps as POWER → COMPUTE → CLOUD → DISTRIBUTION → AI → CAPITAL.

France's advantage is vertical integration. EDF brings firm low-carbon power. Bull adds European supercomputing capability. Scaleway and iliad provide cloud infrastructure. Orange brings networks and customers. Capgemini and Artefact connect compute to enterprise deployment. Ardian brings institutional capital. France therefore has the strongest ability today to capture value across the stack, rather than merely collect data-center rents. Its risk is equally clear: sovereignty only creates value if European technology remains globally competitive. Protected inefficiency is not sovereignty.
signal: France leads on **CONTROL** — but must prove sovereignty can stay globally competitive.

# Spain — Finance the Scale
Spain's approximately €5 billion proposition follows a fundamentally different model: SETT → Santander → ACS → Telefónica → Multiverse. Economically: PUBLIC CAPITAL → PRIVATE CAPITAL → INFRASTRUCTURE → COMPUTE → CUSTOMERS. Santander finances. ACS builds. Telefónica distributes. SETT absorbs sovereign risk. Multiverse provides an indigenous AI layer.

Spain has created perhaps Europe's cleanest infrastructure-finance architecture. Its potential advantage is the capital multiplier: use limited public capital to mobilize substantially greater private equity and debt, then scale quickly. But the weakness is structural. If the accelerators, cloud stack, models and highest-value applications remain predominantly foreign, Spain could build billions of euros of world-class infrastructure while capturing only a fraction of the resulting intelligence economics. Spain risks winning the Gigafactory race and becoming Europe's landlord of intelligence.
signal: Spain leads on **SCALE** — but infrastructure without technology and application ownership can become commoditized.

# Italy — Industrialize Intelligence
Italy's emerging €5+ billion architecture could combine Eni, Leonardo, TIM, Enel, Terna, Saipem, CDP, FiberCop and Domyn. But Italy's real competitive advantage is not the consortium — it is the economy behind it. Italy possesses unusually deep demand across energy, defense, aerospace, manufacturing, machinery, robotics, rail and mobility, engineering and infrastructure. That creates a different flywheel: POWER → COMPUTE → INDUSTRIAL AI → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW.

Italy does not need to beat France in sovereign cloud, nor Spain in project finance. It can build something more differentiated: ==Europe's Industrial AI Gigafactory==. Eni, Leonardo, Saipem, TIM and the broader Italian industrial system can become more than shareholders — they can become anchor customers, so compute begins with identifiable applications, workloads and potential revenues. Italy's weakness is unmistakable: EXECUTION. France already has an ecosystem. Spain already has a vehicle. Italy still needs one Newco, one governance structure, one balance sheet, one power architecture, committed equity and contracted anchor demand.
signal: Italy leads on **OPTIONALITY** — but industrial potential without execution has little strategic value.

# [Spain] Finance It
Core advantage: capital multiplier. Value engine: scale. Winning metric: private €/public €. Main risk: commoditization. Position today: **Scale**.

# [Italy] Use It
Core advantage: industrial demand. Value engine: productivity. Winning metric: productivity/MW. Main risk: execution paralysis. Position today: **Optionality**.

::section The Big Take | Which model wins depends on what AI becomes
If AI remains primarily a cloud-and-model economy, France has the strongest model. If AI becomes primarily an infrastructure-finance supercycle, Spain has the strongest model. But if the next decade belongs to Industrial and Physical AI, Italy may have the most valuable endgame.

That does not mean Italy leads today. It does not. **France leads today** — it has the most complete architecture and the highest current potential for European value capture. **Spain can scale fastest** — it has the cleanest capital structure and strongest project-finance logic. **Italy has the biggest untapped industrial upside** — but unless it converts that advantage into execution quickly, upside remains PowerPoint.

::section The Closing Verdict | France owns, Spain scales, Italy must execute
France has built the strongest system. It already connects power, compute, cloud, networks, capital and enterprise deployment. France is the benchmark.

Spain has built the strongest financing machine. It can mobilize capital and infrastructure rapidly. But unless Spain captures more of the technology and applications, it risks financing Europe's largest AI real-estate business rather than building an AI economy.

Italy has the strongest industrial logic—and the weakest excuse for failure. Its manufacturing, energy, defense, mobility and engineering base should make Italy a natural European home for Industrial AI. But potential is not strategy. Unless Italy rapidly converts its coalition into one company, one balance sheet, secured power and contracted demand, France and Spain will move ahead while Italy is still organizing the table.

::pull THE UNCOMFORTABLE CLARITY
FRANCE OWNS. SPAIN SCALES. ITALY MUST EXECUTE.

::takeaways Key takeaways | Six mandates for the players
- **France:** defend the lead—but prove that sovereignty can remain globally competitive.
- **Spain:** capture more of the stack—or risk becoming Europe's best-financed host for somebody else's intelligence.
- **Italy:** stop optimizing the consortium and start optimizing execution. Industrial AI is Italy's opportunity to lose.
- **Investors:** stop comparing headline CAPEX. Follow recurring cash flow, value capture and productivity per MW.
- **CEOs & Boards:** the strategic asset is not access to compute. It is the ability to convert compute into proprietary productivity and cash flow.
- **EU policymakers:** stop rewarding announced MW. Reward productive European value per MW and per euro of public capital.

::close The ultimate European model will need all three — ==France: control × Spain: capital × Italy: conversion== — but the winner will be the country that needs the least subsidy once the factory is running. That is where AI ambition ends—and economic power begins.

Europe’s AI Gigafactory competition has reached a consequential inflection point. Germany has secured a new €4.8 billion cloud investment announcement, formalized its Industrial AI partnership with NVIDIA, and attracted further technology investment. Meanwhile, France’s OVHcloud has revealed that substantial electricity-connected computing capacity is already available for new customers.

The emerging paradox is striking: Europe is mobilizing billions to construct new computing infrastructure while existing European operators still have significant capacity available to commercialize.

The fundamental question is no longer simply whether Europe can finance its AI Gigafactories. It is whether Europe can allocate capital, computing capacity and industrial demand efficiently enough to compete with American hyperscalers.

1 | Germany — Amazon Commits Another €4.8 Billion to AI Infrastructure

October 20 — Confirmed investment announcement.

Amazon announced plans to invest more than €16 billion in Germany during 2027, including €4.8 billion dedicated to expanding AWS’s Frankfurt cloud region and AI infrastructure.

The broader investment programme also encompasses logistics, satellite connectivity and media operations.

Importantly, the €4.8 billion cloud allocation is part of the €16 billion package, not additional to it.

Amazon also plans to expand its Berlin robotics and AI research capabilities.

The announcement follows an earlier €8.8 billion Frankfurt infrastructure investment programme covering 2024–2026. 

Why this matters

Germany is attracting large-scale private computing investment independently of the EuroHPC Gigafactory selection process.

This creates an increasingly important competitive distinction.

American hyperscalers are financing infrastructure around established commercial platforms. European Gigafactory consortia must still demonstrate how they will generate comparable demand.

Germany’s industrial customer base strengthens the case for both models.

The potential transmission mechanism is:

PRIVATE CAPEX → COMPUTE CAPACITY → INDUSTRIAL AI → PRODUCTIVITY → RECURRING CLOUD REVENUE

The second-order effect could be increased competition for electricity, equipment, engineering resources and industrial AI customers in Germany.

For investors, the AWS announcement strengthens the outlook for electrical infrastructure, cooling, networking and data-center construction.

For policymakers, it raises a strategic question: how much European economic value will remain under European ownership?

Strategic verdict: Germany is gaining investment momentum, but increased domestic capacity does not automatically translate into European technological sovereignty.

2 | Germany — The NVIDIA Industrial AI Agreement Is Now Formalized

October 19–20 — The previously anticipated agreement has moved into a formal institutional framework.

Germany’s Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy and Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space have signed a Joint Declaration of Intent with NVIDIA.

The initiative will focus on developing advanced foundation models for Physical AI, particularly applications involving robotics, industrial production and autonomous systems.

Germany’s Industry 4.0 platform will serve as a central coordinating mechanism.

The collaboration will build on open-model technologies, including NVIDIA Nemotron and Cosmos.

The agreement is confirmed, but it does not establish a binding investment amount or a EuroHPC Gigafactory award. 

A second consequential development

NVIDIA also identified four German startups for investment:

• Agile Robots: robotics and Physical AI.
• Avelios Medical: healthcare software.
• Mirelo: AI-generated audio.
• Reflex Aerospace: satellite technology.

Investment amounts were not disclosed.These investments fall within NVIDIA’s broader European investment initiative, rather than representing a newly committed $10 billion specifically to Germany. 

The hidden pattern

NVIDIA is extending its position beyond semiconductor supply.

It is increasingly participating in the creation of applications that generate demand for its computing infrastructure.

The emerging model becomes:

SEMICONDUCTORS → COMPUTING PLATFORMS → INDUSTRIAL MODELS → APPLICATIONS → COMMERCIAL DEMAND

For Germany, this could strengthen the economic foundations of its AI infrastructure expansion.

For Europe, however, the strategic challenge remains: developing European intellectual property and industrial applications while retaining bargaining power over foreign-controlled hardware and software platforms.

3 | France — OVHcloud Reveals Europe’s Utilization Challenge

October 20 — New operating and financial disclosures.

French cloud operator OVHcloud reported approximately 250 MW of electricity-connected capacity, of which 140 MW is operational and 110 MW is available to accommodate new business.

CEO Octave Klaba emphasized that the company can add customers without undertaking equivalent new construction expenditure.

OVHcloud also expects to introduce its first proprietary AI model in early 2027.

The company reiterated its concern that nationally fragmented European Gigafactory initiatives may struggle to attract sufficient customers. 

This disclosure requires an important qualification: electricity-connected capacity is not necessarily equivalent to immediately available accelerator-equipped AI computing capacity.

Nevertheless, it exposes a fundamental capital-allocation question.

Why finance new computing infrastructure before establishing whether commercially suitable existing European capacity can satisfy part of the demand?

The financial dimension

OVHcloud also reported its first positive levered free cash flow since its 2021 listing.

For fiscal 2026:

€1.15 billion: revenue, up 7.2%.

€24.7 million: positive levered free cash flow.

€255.1 million: public-cloud revenue, up 18.9%.

The company expects revenue growth exceeding 10% in fiscal 2027. 

Why this matters

OVHcloud illustrates an alternative model of European AI capital formation.

Instead of prioritizing infrastructure expansion, operators can initially seek higher utilization of existing assets, improve cash generation and reinvest selectively.

The corresponding chain is:

EXISTING CAPACITY → HIGHER UTILIZATION → CASH FLOW → REINVESTMENT → PRODUCTIVE AI CAPACITY

This is not evidence that Europe has sufficient advanced computing infrastructure overall.

Different workloads require different accelerators, networking architectures, locations and contractual arrangements.

But it demonstrates why investors must distinguish between power capacity, installed computing capacity and commercially productive AI capacity.

Strategic verdict: France’s challenge is not simply to finance additional infrastructure. It is to maximize the economic returns generated by the infrastructure and technological capabilities it already possesses.

4 | Capital Markets — The Credit Risk Is Becoming More Selective

October 20 — An important qualification to yesterday’s credit-risk assessment.

J.P. Morgan estimates that most European and Asian banks have relatively limited direct exposure to CoreWeave.

Most institutions in those regions are estimated to have exposures below $500 million, although disclosure limitations make the estimates uncertain.

The bank argues that much of the financing is secured and structured around project milestones.

Nevertheless, it identifies the central risk as the timing mismatch between infrastructure expenditure and the revenues subsequently generated.

J.P. Morgan expects CoreWeave to remain free-cash-flow negative until 2030. 

The second-order implication

The distinction between credit contagion and investment repricing is important.

A financially stressed AI infrastructure developer does not necessarily create systemic banking losses.But higher perceived credit risk can increase financing costs for developers relying on similar business models.

This could accelerate capital concentration toward projects with established customer relationships, stronger balance sheets and lower execution risk.

For European Gigafactories, that makes the financing structure as important as the technology architecture.

5 | The European Competitive Read-Through

Germany’s latest announcements strengthen its position in attracting private AI infrastructure investment and coordinating Industrial AI demand.

France presents a contrasting challenge: converting technological capabilities and existing infrastructure into higher utilization and recurring returns.

Spain remains well positioned through its public-private financing architecture, but electricity-network constraints and project execution remain critical.

Italy must translate its emerging industrial consortium into committed financing, secure power and identifiable customers.

The Nordics continue to benefit from their physical infrastructure and electricity advantages, but announced projects must still progress toward financial close and operational delivery.

No new EuroHPC Gigafactory tender award, binding consortium financing close or final site decision was verified today for Spain, Italy or the Nordic countries.

The European competition is becoming increasingly differentiated.

Germany is accelerating capital deployment. France is confronting utilization economics. Spain, Italy and the Nordics must demonstrate that their respective advantages translate into bankable operating assets.

Five Key Takeaways

1. Investors: Distinguish between installed capacity and utilized capacity. Revenue visibility and capital efficiency should increasingly determine valuation premiums.

2. CEOs and boards: Industrial AI investments must connect computing expenditure to measurable productivity, operating margins and recurring revenues.

3. European banks: Project finance should emphasize contracted demand, secured electricity, technology replacement cycles and milestone-based capital deployment.

4. EU policymakers: Europe’s Gigafactory initiative needs a genuinely European demand strategy. Nationally fragmented infrastructure risks weakening utilization and investment returns.

5. European industrial leaders: Germany’s emerging Physical AI ecosystem illustrates the potential of connecting computing infrastructure directly to industrial applications rather than treating Gigafactories as isolated national assets.

The Big Take

Europe’s AI race is entering a more consequential phase: the competition between capital deployed and capital made productive.

Germany’s new investments demonstrate that private capital remains available for computing infrastructure with an established commercial ecosystem.

OVHcloud demonstrates why existing capacity, utilization and cash generation deserve equal attention.

The contrast exposes the fundamental weakness of national AI strategies built primarily around infrastructure announcements.

Europe does not need seven isolated national computing ambitions.

It needs an integrated AI production system capable of allocating computing resources across borders, attracting industrial customers and retaining a meaningful share of the resulting economic value.

The emerging winners will not necessarily be those attracting the largest investment announcements. They will be those converting computing capacity into commercially productive intelligence at the highest sustainable return on capital.

Velocity Insight: Europe’s AI Supercycle will ultimately reward the ecosystems that transform infrastructure investment into industrial productivity and recurring cash flow—not those that accumulate computing capacity faster than they can monetize it.

HIGHER FOR LONGER, FOR A DIFFERENT REASON
The AI Supercycle Is Putting Capital Back at the Center of Monetary Policy
THE BIG TAKE
SEPTEMBER 28, 2026


⸻


THE THESIS
THE FIRST MAJOR MACRO EFFECT OF AI WILL NOT BE DISINFLATION. IT WILL BE A NEW SURGE IN DEMAND FOR CAPITAL.
The consensus focuses on the end of the story:
AI → Productivity → Disinflation → Lower Rates
But before AI delivers its productivity dividend, it must first be financed, built and powered.
Compute. Semiconductors. Data centers. Power. Grids. Storage. Automation.
Capital arrives before capacity.
If investment grows faster than physical supply, bottlenecks and cost pressures emerge. If desired investment remains persistently high relative to saving, r-star can rise as well.
These are two different effects:
the shock changes the path of inflation;
r-star can change the destination of rates.
Hence our call:
POLICY RATES CAN FALL WITHOUT RETURNING TO THE OLD EQUILIBRIUM.
That is Higher for Longer, for a Different Reason.


⸻


1 | THE RECURSIVE CAPITAL CYCLE
The current AI cycle is not linear:
CAPITAL → COMPUTE → CAPABILITY → EXPECTED CASH FLOWS → VALUATIONS → FINANCING → NEW CAPITAL ↺
Today’s investment can create the conditions for tomorrow’s investment.
As long as:
Expected ROIC > WACC
the cycle can reinforce itself.
When that relationship reverses, valuations, financing capacity and CapEx begin to correct.
Recursion therefore amplifies the cycle in both directions.


⸻


2 | ACPI — MEASURING CAPITAL PRESSURE
To identify where we are in the cycle, we introduce a proprietary indicator:
AI CAPITAL PRESSURE INDEX — ACPI
ACPI = CAPITAL-FORMATION VELOCITY / CAPACITY-EXPANSION VELOCITY
The numerator captures the acceleration in AI CapEx, data-center commitments, semiconductor-equipment orders and AI-infrastructure financing.
The denominator captures the expansion of compute, data-center capacity, power generation, grids, semiconductor capacity and, progressively, productivity.
ACPI > 1
Capital is outrunning capacity. Bottlenecks and CapEx inflation intensify.
ACPI ≈ 1
Capacity is catching demand: the handoff zone.
ACPI < 1
Capacity is growing faster than marginal capital demand. Productivity begins to dominate.
ACPI MEASURES THE PRESSURE. PASS-THROUGH DETERMINES THE INFLATION.


⸻


3 | FROM THE CAPACITY GAP TO INFLATION
A high ACPI does not automatically imply higher CPI inflation.
The first pressure appears in capital goods, power, construction, equipment and specialized labor.
This is CapEx inflation.
To reach consumer prices and wages, it must pass through the Inflation Filter:
pricing power,
labor-market tightness,
energy pass-through,
margin absorption,
productivity offsets,
and the duration of bottlenecks.
Conceptually:
CPI Pressure ≈ CapEx Inflation × Pass-Through Intensity
AI therefore does not directly create inflation.
It creates investment demand.
Scarcity raises costs.
Pass-through determines how much reaches the broader nominal economy.


⸻


4 | SHOCK OR REGIME?
This is the critical distinction.
THE SHOCK CHANGES THE PATH. THE REGIME CHANGES THE DESTINATION.
SHOCK
Demand temporarily outruns capacity.
Costs rise, but new supply can progressively close the gap.
The effect is predominantly cyclical.
REGIME
Desired investment remains structurally elevated relative to desired saving.
The real rate required to equilibrate the economy rises:
R-STAR ↑
That does not necessarily mean higher inflation.
We can have:
INFLATION ↓ + R-STAR ↑
That is the combination most at risk of being underpriced.


⸻


5 | CYCLICAL INFLATION, STRUCTURAL INFLATION AND R-STAR
These are three different phenomena.
CYCLICAL INFLATION
Too much demand relative to available capacity today.
It should fade as the capacity gap closes.
STRUCTURAL INFLATION
Capacity continues to adjust too slowly. Power, grids, permitting, labor or geopolitics keep scarcity persistent.
HIGHER R-STAR
This is not inflation.
It reflects a new equilibrium between desired investment and desired saving.
A higher-r-star thesis therefore requires evidence:persistently strong private investment,
resilient demand despite high real rates,
strong realized returns on capital,
accelerating productivity,
and, above all:
REAL YIELDS REMAINING ELEVATED EVEN AS INFLATION FALLS.
If inflation and real yields decline together, the economy is probably absorbing primarily a shock.
If inflation falls while the real price of capital remains elevated, the market may be pricing a new regime.


⸻


6 | THE CENTRAL-BANK REACTION FUNCTION
The monetary stance depends on the distance between the real policy rate and r-star:
MONETARY RESTRICTION ≈ REAL POLICY RATE − R-STAR
If r-star rises while policy is unchanged, the degree of monetary restriction declines.
The question for central banks is therefore not simply when to begin or continue easing. It is what level of the policy rate is consistent with an appropriately restrictive stance during disinflation and, subsequently, with a return toward neutrality.
THE CORE ISSUE IS THE CALIBRATION OF THE POLICY RATE AGAINST A POTENTIALLY HIGHER NEUTRAL RATE.
If r-star has moved higher, normalization can proceed through rate cuts without taking the policy rate back to the levels of the previous cycle.
The direction of policy can be accommodative while the terminal level remains structurally higher.


⸻


7 | THE YIELD CURVE: DIRECTION AND DESTINATION
The distinction translates directly into fixed income.
THE FRONT END PRICES THE PACE OF NORMALIZATION.
THE BELLY PRICES THE TERMINAL POLICY RATE.
THE LONG END PRICES THE LONG-RUN MACRO-FINANCIAL EQUILIBRIUM.
At the long end, the key variables are real rates, inflation expectations, term premium, fiscal supply and structural capital demand.
Policy normalization therefore does not necessarily imply a return to the previous monetary equilibrium.
The direction can be lower. The destination can remain higher.


⸻


8 | FOUR MONETARY REGIMES
The scenarios follow a consistent causal sequence:

I — CAPITAL SHOCK
CapEx accelerates ahead of capacity. Bottlenecks and cost pressures intensify.
r-star: not yet determined.
Policy: restriction remains in place for longer.
Markets: short/intermediate carry, cash-flow quality, vulnerable duration.
II — NEW EQUILIBRIUM
Capacity catches up and inflation moderates, but investment demand remains structurally strong.
r-star: ↑.
Policy: normalization toward a higher neutral rate.
Markets: ROIC, free cash flow and carry become increasingly important.
III — PRODUCTIVITY HANDOFF
Installed capacity catches up with demand and productivity accelerates.
r-star: stable or gradually lower.
Policy: orderly normalization.
Markets: equity broadening, industrial AI, automation and increasingly attractive duration.
IV — CYCLE REVERSAL
Realized returns fail to justify invested capital. Expected ROIC falls below WACC and CapEx corrects.
r-star: ↓.
Policy: accelerated easing, with greater focus on financial stability.
Markets: quality sovereign duration, defensive cash flows and strong balance sheets.
The logic is straightforward:
Scenario I identifies the shock.
Scenario II tests whether the shock has produced a new regime.
Scenario III captures the transition to productivity.
Scenario IV captures overshoot and the reversal of recursion.

9 | THE COST OF CAPITAL BECOMES THE SELECTION MECHANISM
The new monetary equilibrium reaches companies through:
Real Yields → Risk-Free Rate → WACC → Hurdle Rate
As WACC rises:
marginal projects become uneconomic,
leverage matters more,
distant cash flows become less valuable,and balance-sheet quality returns to the center of capital allocation.
The critical variable becomes:
ROIC − WACC


⸻


10 | EQUITY AND CREDIT: TWO TESTS OF THE SAME THESIS
EQUITY
AI can raise earnings and cash flows.
But a higher WACC also raises the discount rate.
The question is:
CAN CASH FLOWS GROW FASTER THAN THE COST OF CAPITAL?
On one side are AI cash-flow generators: high ROIC, strong free cash flow, pricing power and strong balance sheets.
On the other are AI capital consumers: high CapEx, long payback periods, weak cash-flow conversion and high leverage.
The new divide is not Tech versus Non-Tech.
IT IS RETURN ON CAPITAL versus COST OF CAPITAL.
CREDIT
As the buildout moves from internal funding toward bonds, private credit and infrastructure finance, credit imposes explicit discipline.
The question becomes:
Where is the cash flow?
Markets must distinguish debt financing productive capacity from debt financing competitive overinvestment.
The equity-credit gap becomes an important leading signal: if equity valuations remain elevated while spreads and funding costs deteriorate, the capital structure is beginning to challenge the equity narrative.


⸻


11 | THE PRODUCTIVITY HANDOFF
The capacity being built today must eventually produce.
Compute becomes more abundant.
AI diffusion accelerates.
Productivity rises.
Unit costs fall.
ACPI declines.
THE TURNING POINT ARRIVES WHEN CAPACITY AND PRODUCTIVITY BEGIN TO GROW FASTER THAN MARGINAL DEMAND FOR NEW CAPITAL.
That is the transition from scarcity to productivity.


⸻


12 | THE NEW INVESTMENT HIERARCHY
The old regime favored:
Cheap Money + Long Duration + Multiple Expansion
The emerging regime increasingly favors:
CASH FLOW + ROIC + BALANCE-SHEET STRENGTH + SCARCITY VALUE
On one side:
CASH-FLOW COMPOUNDERS
On the other:
SCARCE PHYSICAL ASSETS
Power, grids, data-center infrastructure, semiconductor infrastructure and automation.
The most vulnerable segment remains:
long-duration assets dependent on cheap capital without sufficient cash-flow growth or scarcity value.

13 | THE FIVE-SIGNAL INVESTOR DASHBOARD
One Regime Signal. Two Confirmations. Two Warnings.
Not all signals matter equally.
The dashboard should be read as a hierarchy of evidence.


⸻


1 | REAL YIELDS — THE REGIME SIGNAL
This is the most important signal.
INFLATION IS FALLING. ARE REAL YIELDS FALLING WITH IT?
Inflation ↓ + Real Yields ↓
The economy is probably absorbing a cyclical shock.
Inflation ↓ + Real Yields remain elevated
The market may be pricing:
A HIGHER R-STAR.
This signal determines whether investors should think cyclically or structurally.


⸻


2 | ACPI — THE PHYSICAL-CYCLE CONFIRMATION
ACPI > 1 and rising
Capital continues to outrun capacity. Scarcity dominates.
ACPI > 1 but falling
Capacity is catching up.
ACPI < 1
Capacity is outrunning marginal capital formation.
ACPI tells us where the real economy sits inside the cycle.


⸻


3 | ROIC − WACC — THE ECONOMIC CONFIRMATION
The recursive cycle remains sustainable only while:
ROIC > WACC
A wide positive spread validates further capital formation.
A narrowing spread signals deteriorating economics.
ROIC < WACC
is the critical break.
CapEx tells us how much companies are spending.
ROIC − WACC TELLS US WHETHER THEY SHOULD KEEP SPENDING.


⸻


4 | EQUITY–CREDIT GAP — THE MARKET WARNING
The dangerous configuration is:
EQUITY EUPHORIA + CREDIT DETERIORATION.
If multiples remain elevated while credit spreads widen, all-in yields rise or refinancing conditions deteriorate, the capital structure is beginning to reject the assumptions embedded in equity valuations.
Credit does not need to predict the cycle. It only needs to stop confirming it.


⸻


5 | INFLATION FILTER — THE POLICY WARNING
This signal tells us whether central banks are likely to become a greater constraint on the investment cycle.
Pass-through contained
The investment boom remains more manageable for monetary policy.
Pass-through broadening
The reaction function becomes more restrictive.
The policy risk is clear:THE CENTRAL BANK RAISES THE HURDLE RATE BEFORE PRODUCTIVITY HAS TIME TO ARRIVE.

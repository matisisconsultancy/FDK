AI was supposed to make everything cheaper. Before it does, it may make money more expensive. The AI buildout is moving from a technology cycle into a capital and credit cycle. Near term, it intensifies demand for scarce physical capacity and financing; longer term, it may deliver higher productivity and lower unit costs. Markets must price the transition.

The key change is **recursion**. Capital builds compute. Compute improves capability. Capability raises expected earnings. Expectations lift valuations and financing capacity. Financing funds the next wave of capital expenditure. The cycle restarts at a larger scale.

That feedback loop matters for central banks because it can lift demand, leverage and asset prices before the productivity dividend is fully visible—and can reverse if realised returns fail to validate expectations.

::pull THE MARKET READ-THROUGH
The first-order AI trade was earnings. The ==second-order trade is the price of capital==. For equities, the issue is whether incremental AI cash flow can outrun the discount rate. For bonds, the issue is whether sustained private investment is lifting the equilibrium real rate.

::signals The Mechanism | how the cycle compounds
# A capital cycle that can finance itself
The AI buildout is increasingly self-reinforcing. More investment creates more compute; more compute expands capability; capability supports higher expected earnings; higher expected earnings improve access to equity and debt capital. Five loops matter: capability, valuation, credit, infrastructure and macro. Together they can amplify investment well beyond the initial technology shock.

The same mechanism works in reverse. Lower expected returns can weaken valuations, tighten financing, slow CapEx and reduce expected capability gains.
signal: Recursion is both the accelerator and the principal reversal risk.

# The great bifurcation: inflation first, productivity later
Phase I is infrastructure inflation. Compute, power, grids, equipment, land, specialist labour and finance are scarce. Demand arrives before productive capacity. Phase II is industrial-AI deflation. AI diffuses through enterprises and the physical economy; inference costs fall, automation rises, productivity improves and unit costs decline.
signal: The question is not whether AI is inflationary or deflationary, but how long Phase I lasts before Phase II dominates.

# R-star is part of the AI story
AI investment does not only respond to interest rates. At sufficient scale, it can influence the equilibrium price of capital itself. Expected AI returns raise desired investment; stronger investment raises demand for savings and financing; that can place upward pressure on the natural real rate. Higher financing costs then raise the return threshold required for the next wave of AI investment.
signal: A productivity boom can coexist with a higher equilibrium real rate when investment demand rises faster than available savings.

# Why central banks need a wider field of vision
The traditional reaction function remains essential: inflation, employment, wages, output and financial conditions. But the AI cycle adds forward-looking variables—AI CapEx velocity, data-centre construction, power commitments, grid scarcity, corporate issuance, private credit, off-balance-sheet financing, compute prices and AI cash-flow conversion—that can move before conventional inflation data.
signal: Not a case for allocating private capital; a case for better measurement of the mechanism through which it moves demand, productivity, leverage and transmission.

# From reaction function to system function
The framework expands from Inflation + Employment + Output Gap to a broader system including productivity, capital formation, credit, asset prices, energy and financial stability. The key metric is the velocity of capital formation relative to the velocity of productivity. If capital formation runs ahead, expect stronger demand, leverage and rate pressure; if productivity catches up, the mix shifts toward stronger cash flow, lower unit costs and disinflation.
signal: The mandate need not expand; the information set does.

::section Equity Strategy — From Participation To Conversion | conversion beats participation
Return on AI capital matters more than the size of the AI budget. Track incremental free cash flow against incremental AI invested capital. Balance-sheet strength becomes strategic as external financing grows more expensive, and higher real rates increase dispersion while demanding faster cash-flow delivery from long-duration equities.

The opportunity broadens into power, grids, electrical equipment, cooling and data-centre infrastructure. Expect concentration before diffusion: scale, cash flow, infrastructure access and cost of capital become reinforcing advantages.

::pull THE EQUITY CONCLUSION
The next equity market rewards ==conversion, not participation==. AI exposure is becoming less differentiated; the ability to turn AI investment into durable cash flow is becoming more differentiated.

::section Rates & Credit — Beyond The Next Cut | a duration and credit story
The more important question is where the new neutral rate settles if AI sustains unusually strong private investment demand. Long-duration sovereign bonds face a different regime: higher potential growth does not guarantee lower long-term yields. AI infrastructure is becoming a material source of investment-grade and private-credit demand, and credit selection matters as investors distinguish productive infrastructure from competitive overinvestment.

Watch the equity-credit gap: credit may challenge optimistic terminal-value assumptions before equities do.

::pull THE FIXED-INCOME CONCLUSION
The same CapEx that supports nominal growth can increase bond supply, financing needs and the ==equilibrium return demanded by capital==.

::patterns Three Scenarios To Watch | how the cycle resolves
# Extended Phase I
Capital demand strong and inflation sticky; higher-for-longer, with concentration persisting. Key signal: CapEx outruns cash-flow conversion.

# Transition To Phase II
Productivity accelerates and inflation moderates; rates stabilise and participation broadens. Key signal: productivity and cash flow catch CapEx.

# Disorderly Adjustment
AI returns disappoint and investment slows; valuation reset and tighter conditions. Key signal: credit spreads widen and financing resets.

::takeaways The Big Take | what boards should hold onto
- The AI Supercycle is becoming a capital cycle, a credit cycle and potentially a monetary cycle.
- Its defining feature is recursion: capital creates intelligence; intelligence raises expected returns; expected returns attract capital.
- The cycle continues until realised cash flows no longer validate the financing assumptions.
- Central banks do not need a new industrial-policy mandate; they need a broader field of vision.
- For equities: can AI-generated cash flows outrun a structurally higher discount rate? For bonds: is the investment boom moving the equilibrium price of capital itself?

::close First AI changes the price of intelligence. Then it changes the price of capital. ==When the price of capital changes, the entire cycle gets repriced.==

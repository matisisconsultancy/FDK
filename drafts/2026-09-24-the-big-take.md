THE GREAT STABILIZATION

Trump and Xi are not ending the Great Split. They are trying to make it investable.

The Fundamental Question

What if the real breakthrough in Washington is neither a trade deal nor an AI agreement—but the recognition that the U.S.–China technological confrontation has become too economically valuable, and too systemically dangerous, to remain unmanaged?

That is the consequential signal from the opening phase of the Trump–Xi meetings.

Strip away the ceremony, the tariffs and the transactional headlines and one pattern emerges:

Washington and Beijing are not negotiating an end to strategic competition. They are beginning to negotiate the operating rules under which that competition can continue.

That distinction changes everything.

⸻

1. THE SUMMIT IS ABOUT VOLATILITY, NOT VICTORY

The first important signal is the extension of the trade truce to January 10, 2027, according to U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. China has been more cautious publicly about the precise terms.

The significance is not the extra weeks.

It is the mechanism.

The emerging relationship is moving from:

Disagreement → Escalation → Retaliation → Shock

toward:

Disagreement → Negotiation → Extension → Competition Continues.

That is the architecture of volatility suppression.

Neither Trump nor Xi needs to solve the structural rivalry.

They need to prevent the rivalry from repeatedly destroying economic value.

The first Big Take: the objective is not convergence. It is continuity.

⸻

2. AI HAS CROSSED FROM TECHNOLOGY POLICY INTO STRATEGIC STABILITY

This is the most important development.

Ahead of today’s summit, Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng discussed a formal AI dialogue and a mechanism for communicating about AI incidents with national-security consequences. Another meeting is expected in Shenzhen in roughly two months. China has been less explicit publicly about the proposed mechanism.

That is a qualitative shift.

AI is beginning to acquire two simultaneous identities.

It is an economic weapon:

models → chips → agents → productivity → industrial power.

But it is increasingly also a systemic-risk variable:

autonomous agents → cyber capabilities → critical infrastructure → military systems → escalation risk.

That creates a new strategic requirement.

Compete on capability. Coordinate on catastrophe.

Washington does not need to give China Nvidia.

Beijing does not need to stop developing Huawei.

Neither side needs to slow DeepSeek, OpenAI, Anthropic or Alibaba.

But both may increasingly need mechanisms preventing an AI incident from being interpreted as deliberate strategic aggression.

That is potentially much more consequential than an AI cooperation agreement.

⸻

3. THE GREAT SPLIT IS NOT CLOSING. IT IS HARDENING.

There is no indication from the opening phase that Washington intends to dismantle its frontier semiconductor controls.

And China is accelerating precisely the opposite strategy:

Huawei → accelerated computing

CXMT → memory

Alibaba → chips + models + cloud

DeepSeek → inference economics

domestic networking → infrastructure sovereignty

robotics + autonomous vehicles → Physical AI.

The two technology systems are therefore becoming more structurally separate, not less.

But this is precisely why stabilization becomes necessary.

The emerging architecture is:

DECOUPLE THE STRATEGIC STACK.

PRESERVE THE ECONOMIC FLOOR.

MANAGE THE TAIL RISK.

That is a much sharper description of what may be emerging in Washington.

⸻

4. XI’S STRATEGY: BUY TIME, BUILD SOVEREIGNTY

Xi arrives in Washington from a stronger position than during earlier phases of the trade confrontation.

China retains substantial leverage in rare earths and critical supply chains, while its trade machine remains formidable. Reuters reports that China’s trade surplus is on course to exceed $1 trillion for a second consecutive year.

But Xi’s deeper strategic asset is time.Every quarter without a major geopolitical rupture gives China more time to build:

domestic accelerators
→ domestic memory
→ domestic networking
→ domestic AI factories
→ stronger models
→ industrial AI deployment.

The causality chain is straightforward:

STABILITY → TIME → SUBSTITUTION → SOVEREIGNTY → STRATEGIC OPTIONALITY

That makes stabilization economically valuable to Beijing even without major American concessions.

China does not need technological détente.

It needs enough predictability to continue reducing its dependencies.

⸻

5. TRUMP’S STRATEGY: MONETIZE STABILITY

Trump approaches the equation differently.

The American agenda includes agricultural purchases, Boeing aircraft, critical minerals, tariff adjustments and other tangible commercial outcomes.

The underlying logic is transactional:

STRATEGIC PRESSURE → NEGOTIATING LEVERAGE → COMMERCIAL DELIVERABLES

This produces an interesting potential equilibrium.

Xi can offer selective economic concessions without surrendering China’s technology strategy.

Trump can preserve semiconductor controls while extracting commercial commitments.

Both can therefore claim progress without resolving the fundamental rivalry.

That is why the emerging structure is not détente.

It is:

SELECTIVE INTERDEPENDENCE.

Compete where power is created.

Trade where value can still be exchanged.

Coordinate where failure becomes catastrophic.

⸻

6. TAIWAN IS THE HARD LIMIT

Everything above has one enormous boundary condition.

Taiwan.

Trade can be renegotiated.

Tariffs can be changed.

Agricultural purchases can be increased.

AI-incident protocols can be created.

Taiwan is fundamentally different.

It sits at the intersection of:

military deterrence × Chinese sovereignty claims × American security policy × advanced semiconductors × global supply chains.

This produces a hierarchy of negotiability:

Trade — transactional.

AI risk — potentially manageable.

Technology — competitive.

Taiwan — structural.

Any stabilization thesis therefore remains conditional on deterrence holding around Taiwan.

⸻

7. THE REAL INFLECTION: THE RELATIONSHIP IS BECOMING INSTITUTIONALIZED

The deepest implication of the first hours in Washington is not that Trump and Xi suddenly trust one another.

They do not need to.

Mature strategic competition does not require trust.

It requires rules, communication and predictable boundaries.

The emerging architecture can therefore be reduced to four verbs:

COMPETE

AI, semiconductors, manufacturing, capital and industrial power.

TRANSACT

Trade, agriculture, aircraft, commodities and selected investment.

COORDINATE

AI incidents and other systemic risks.

DETER

Military escalation and coercion.

That is not reconciliation.

It is the beginning of an operating system for strategic rivalry.

⸻

8. WHY THIS MATTERS FOR MARKETS

This is where the geopolitical story becomes an investment story.

Markets do not require Washington and Beijing to become friends.

They require the probability distribution of catastrophic outcomes to narrow.

If technological competition remains intense while the probability of uncontrolled escalation declines, the resulting regime could support:

**higher AI CAPEX

• greater industrial investment
• continued semiconductor duplication
• faster technological innovation
• lower intelligence costs
• lower geopolitical tail-risk premiums.**

That is potentially a powerful combination.

The crucial market variable is therefore not:

U.S.–China cooperation.

It is:

U.S.–CHINA PREDICTABILITY.

Predictability extends investment horizons.

Longer investment horizons support larger capital commitments.

Larger capital commitments accelerate technological competition.

And technological competition pushes intelligence costs lower.

⸻

9. WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE AI SUPERCYCLE

This is the central implication.

The strongest environment for the AI Supercycle may not be globalization.

And it may not be complete technological decoupling.

It may be:

MANAGED TECHNOLOGICAL BIPOLARITY.

Two competing systems.Two semiconductor architectures.

Two AI infrastructure ecosystems.

Two industrial strategies.

But enough strategic stability for both to keep investing.

The causality becomes:

Strategic competition
→ duplicated infrastructure
→ higher AI CAPEX
→ faster innovation
→ lower intelligence cost
→ greater deployment
→ higher productivity
→ more capital formation.

While:

Strategic communication
→ lower tail risk
→ longer investment horizons
→ greater willingness to fund infrastructure.

Put them together:

COMPETITION DRIVES CAPEX.

STABILITY EXTENDS DURATION.

That may be the most important macroeconomic consequence of the Trump–Xi summit.

⸻

10. THE NEW VELOCITY EQUATION

We should therefore add one variable explicitly to our AI Supercycle framework:

AI SUPERPOWER VELOCITY

= Capability × Capital Formation × Diffusion × Strategic Stability

America retains the stronger frontier.

China continues to compress the economics of that frontier and build an increasingly sovereign alternative stack.

But Strategic Stability determines whether those two capital-formation machines can continue compounding.

This is why today’s diplomatic architecture matters directly to investors.

⸻

THE BIG TAKE

The first hours of the Trump–Xi summit suggest that the Great Split is entering a more mature phase.

The objective is no longer necessarily to reverse technological separation.

It is to prevent separation from becoming systemic rupture.

That produces a far sharper formulation:

Washington wants to preserve technological superiority without destroying economic optionality.

Beijing wants to preserve economic stability while buying time for technological sovereignty.

Those objectives are different.

But they are not incompatible.

And that creates the possibility of a new equilibrium:

COMPETE HARDER. ESCALATE LESS.

That is potentially the real breakthrough in Washington.

⸻

THE VELOCITY INSIGHT

The most consequential outcome of this summit would therefore not be a tariff cut.

Nor a soybean purchase.

Nor even an AI-safety agreement.

It would be the emergence of a shared recognition that:

The technological rivalry is permanent enough that it now requires institutions to manage it.

That would mark the transition from an unstable Great Split toward something much more durable:

THE GREAT STABILIZATION.

Two competing intelligence systems.

Two increasingly separate technological stacks.

Two enormous capital-formation machines.

But one increasingly explicit requirement:

Do not allow competition to destroy the system in which the competition creates value.

For The Velocity Edge, that is the central investment conclusion:

COMPETITION CREATES THE SUPERCYCLE.

STABILITY GIVES IT DURATION.

And if Washington and Beijing can achieve both simultaneously, the Trump–Xi summit may ultimately matter much more for the Next Economy than any individual trade concession announced around it.

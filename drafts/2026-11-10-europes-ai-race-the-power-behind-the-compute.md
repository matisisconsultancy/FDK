EuroHPC Reopens Its Submission Channel as Infineon Forecasts €4 Billion in Data-Center Power Revenue — Revealing Where Europe’s AI Infrastructure Value Is Migrating
Where Markets, Capital and Geopolitics Collide
THE BIG TAKE
Two consequential developments distinguish November 10 from the previous European AI Gigafactory assessment.
EuroHPC’s official procurement page now confirms that replacement submission links are available, resolving the electronic-submission obstacle identified last week.
Meanwhile, Germany’s Infineon has issued a substantial new financial forecast: revenue from data-center power-supply semiconductors is expected to approach €4 billion in fiscal 2027.
The combination reveals an important divergence.
Europe is still competing to finance and construct sovereign computing infrastructure. Yet European semiconductor and electrical-equipment manufacturers are already capturing revenues from the global AI investment cycle.
The next investment opportunity increasingly lies in the infrastructure required to convert electricity into productive computing capacity.

1 | EUROHPC — THE GIGAFACTORY PROCUREMENT CHANNEL REOPENS
November 10 — Material procedural update confirmed.
The official EuroHPC procurement page now states that replacement submission links for both Gigafactory lots are available through the EU Funding & Tenders Portal.
This changes the position reported in the previous assessment, when applicants were still awaiting replacement links following the November 5 technical disruption.
The formal deadlines remain:
Procurement Deadline
Lot 2 — Large-scale AI Gigafactories December 3, 2026, 17:00 CET
Lot 1 — Medium-scale AI Gigafactories February 4, 2027, 17:00 CET
Applicants must recreate and resubmit applications previously entered into the affected system. The underlying procurement documentation has not changed.
No new Gigafactory award is confirmed. 
Why this matters
The significance is operational rather than financial.
With fewer than four weeks remaining before the large-scale deadline, application readiness becomes increasingly important for participating consortia.
For Spain’s ACS–Telefónica–Santander partnership, the French candidates, German initiatives, Italian industrial participants and Nordic contenders, the immediate priorities are completing financing documentation, validating supporting evidence and ensuring successful electronic resubmission.
Board implication: Application governance and verification of national financing commitments should receive immediate executive attention.
Investor implication: Successful submission is an important procurement milestone, but remains distinct from selection, financial close and construction authorization.

2 | GERMANY — INFINEON REVEALS THE SCALE OF THE AI POWER-SEMICONDUCTOR OPPORTUNITY
November 10 — Confirmed financial results and new guidance.
Infineon reported record quarterly performance and projected substantial growth in fiscal 2027.
Financial indicator Reported or projected
FY2026 group revenue €16.35 billion
FY2026 segment-result margin 19.7%
FY2027 group revenue forecast Approximately €20 billion
FY2027 data-center power-supply revenue forecast Nearly €4 billion
FY2027 planned investment €3.0–3.5 billion
FY2027 adjusted free-cash-flow forecast Approximately €2.2 billion
The revenue forecast for data-center power supplies is global. It does not represent confirmed orders from European Gigafactories.
Likewise, the planned investment budget covers Infineon’s broader business and should not be interpreted as spending dedicated exclusively to AI. 
The hidden pattern: AI’s electricity-conversion economy
For much of the AI Supercycle, financial-market attention concentrated on advanced GPUs, accelerated computing and hyperscaler capital expenditure.
Infineon’s latest projections demonstrate that another industrial segment is becoming increasingly valuable.AI servers require increasingly sophisticated power conversion, distribution and management.
Higher computing density places greater demands on electrical efficiency, system reliability and thermal management.
The financial transmission mechanism is:
AI COMPUTE INVESTMENT → GREATER POWER DENSITY → ADVANCED POWER SEMICONDUCTORS → INDUSTRIAL REVENUE → REINVESTMENT
Europe possesses internationally competitive capabilities in this part of the value chain.
Infineon’s business model is therefore strategically different from that of companies financing entire computing campuses.
It supplies components used across multiple customers, geographic markets and infrastructure platforms.
For investors, this creates exposure to AI capital expenditure without requiring direct ownership of computing capacity.
For policymakers, it demonstrates that Europe’s industrial opportunity extends beyond producing frontier-model accelerators.
Strategic assessment: A consequential confirmation of European participation in the global AI infrastructure supply chain, supported by company financial guidance rather than speculative project announcements.

3 | THE CAPITAL FORMATION WARNING — EUROPEAN CHIPMAKERS WANT CUSTOMERS TO SHARE EXPANSION RISK
November 10 — New statement from Infineon’s CEO.
In reporting published today, Infineon CEO Jochen Hanebeck argued that additional semiconductor manufacturing expansion requires firm commitments from customers.
The financial logic is important.
Even when demand appears structurally attractive, semiconductor manufacturers must make substantial capital investments before future orders generate revenue.
Infineon has already opened a major semiconductor fabrication facility in Dresden and announced additional manufacturing investment in Austria.
However, further expansion cannot be financed indefinitely on the assumption that AI demand will continue increasing at its present pace.
Hanebeck also emphasized the importance of public support for maintaining Europe’s industrial competitiveness. 
The second-order effect
The European AI investment cycle increasingly requires coordinated commitments across the supply chain.
CUSTOMER COMMITMENTS → MANUFACTURING INVESTMENT → COMPONENT AVAILABILITY → INFRASTRUCTURE DELIVERY → COMPUTING REVENUE
This creates an important role for long-term purchasing agreements, strategic partnerships and risk-sharing arrangements.
European banks and infrastructure investors should distinguish between demand forecasts and enforceable commercial commitments.
For CEOs, the implication is equally important: access to critical semiconductor and electrical components can become a determinant of infrastructure commissioning schedules.
A related technological development reinforces this opportunity. On November 9, Infineon disclosed successful processing of functional power devices on 300-millimeter silicon-carbide wafers at its Villach facility in Austria. The company says the technology could support future AI data-center power applications, although full industrial-scale production has not yet been scheduled.

4 | INDUSTRIAL AI — EUROPE’S COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE MAY EMERGE BEYOND THE DATA CENTER
November 10 — New industry assessment.
Reuters reported today on statements made on November 9 by the chief executives of Infineon, STMicroelectronics and NXP.
Their common assessment was that Europe possesses significant competitive strengths in robotics, industrial automation and physical AI.
These applications require more than advanced central computing capacity.
They depend on sensors, microcontrollers, power semiconductors, reliable embedded computing and the ability to operate safely in physical environments.
STMicroelectronics has already begun introducing humanoid robots from Italian manufacturer Oversonic into its operations, although deployment remains at an early stage.
These statements do not represent new Gigafactory investment commitments.They are nevertheless relevant evidence of the industrial applications through which European AI infrastructure could eventually generate economic value. 
The strategic implication
Europe’s competitive proposition should combine sovereign computing infrastructure with the industrial sectors in which it already possesses substantial technological expertise.
SOVEREIGN COMPUTE + INDUSTRIAL SOFTWARE + EMBEDDED INTELLIGENCE + ROBOTICS
This could create a more differentiated route to productivity growth than competing solely on the scale of general-purpose model training.
The decisive challenge will be translating industrial expertise into commercially scalable products and services.

EUROHPC — CURRENT POSITION
The initiative continues to target up to seven AI Gigafactories, supported by as much as €10 billion in European and national public funding, with the ambition of mobilizing more than €20 billion in private investment.
The procurement mechanism involves public authorities acquiring computing services from selected facilities, thereby supporting commercial demand.
The distinction between planned public support, future private investment and binding project-level financing remains essential.

FIVE TAKEAWAYS FOR INVESTORS, CEOs AND POLICYMAKERS
1 | INVESTORS — Follow the migration of AI infrastructure value.
European power-semiconductor manufacturers are demonstrating measurable commercial exposure to AI investment. Investors should compare their earnings quality, valuation and capital intensity with those of infrastructure developers.
2 | EUROPEAN BANKS — Customer commitments are becoming an essential financing instrument.
Manufacturing expansion and Gigafactory construction both require credible future demand. Contractual commitments can materially improve the bankability of investment programmes.
3 | CEOs AND BOARDS — Infrastructure dependency extends beyond GPU availability.
Electricity conversion, distribution equipment, semiconductor availability and system reliability increasingly influence deployment economics.
4 | INDUSTRIAL AI — Europe’s strongest opportunity may be in physical applications.
Robotics, industrial automation and embedded AI offer potential routes to monetize Europe’s established manufacturing capabilities.
5 | POLICYMAKERS — Finance the industrial value chain, not only the computing campus.
European strategic autonomy requires coordinated investment in computing infrastructure, power semiconductors, industrial software and advanced manufacturing.

THE CLOSING VERDICT
November 10 provides two different forms of evidence about Europe’s AI investment cycle.
EuroHPC’s latest procedural development allows Gigafactory consortia to advance their applications.
Infineon’s financial outlook demonstrates that European industrial companies are already positioned to capture substantial spending generated by the global computing expansion.
The difference matters for capital allocation.
Gigafactories remain major infrastructure investments whose future economic returns depend on financing, construction, electricity availability and customer utilization.
Power-semiconductor manufacturers are supplying established global customers, although their earnings remain exposed to investment-cycle volatility.For investors, the opportunity is to distinguish between projected infrastructure demand and businesses already converting that demand into revenue.
For European industrial policy, the strategic objective must be to connect both.
Europe’s AI challenge is no longer simply to build computing capacity. It is to establish an industrial system capable of supplying, operating and monetizing that capacity competitively.
VELOCITY INSIGHT
The next European AI champions may not be the companies that build the largest Gigafactories, but those that capture the highest sustainable returns from the infrastructure and industrial applications those Gigafactories make possible.

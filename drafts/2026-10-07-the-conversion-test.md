The AI race is moving from capacity accumulation to economic selection.
THE BIG TAKE
Advanced-wafer capacity remains tight. HBM remains strategically scarce. Grid access increasingly determines when compute becomes productive. Financing structures are expanding because the capital burden of deployment is rising.
The benchmark therefore reduces the current AI cycle to one chain:
Scarcity → Investment → Financing → Utilization → Returns
And three financial tests:
Revenue growth > capital-commitment growth
FCF growth > fixed-obligation growth
Incremental ROIC > incremental cost of capital
This is why Microsoft and TSMC remain the two structural leaders; why Amazon, Micron and Synopsys remain accumulation candidates; and why Anthropic can remain a Global Velocity Leader at 97.4 while ranking outside the Top 10 at #11.
Scarce capacity created the first AI winners. Superior economics will determine the next ones.

EXECUTIVE IMPLICATIONS
Boards should require one measurable chain for every major AI commitment:
Capacity → Utilization → Productivity → Revenue → Margin → FCF → ROIC
The operating rules:
Own what creates durable differentiation.
Secure access to what is scarce.
Finance commoditizing assets externally when economics are superior.
Include leases and supplier finance in true capital intensity.


⸻


POLICY IMPLICATIONS
The relevant production system is:
Semiconductors → Memory → Networks → Power → Data Centers → Software → Industrial Applications → Capital
A missing grid connection can neutralize a chip subsidy.
A memory shortage can strand compute.
A financing constraint can postpone otherwise viable infrastructure.
Policy should target system completeness, not isolated capacity.

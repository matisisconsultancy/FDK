Three developments warrant continued scrutiny.

DeepSeek — commercial diffusion: On November 13, DeepSeek expanded its V4.1-Flash APIs to include web search, document processing, URL reading, OCR and speech services. This makes Chinese AI easier to integrate into commercial applications, strengthening its diffusion strategy. However, it is not a new frontier-model release, and its effect on enterprise adoption and inference economics remains unproven. DeepSeek official announcement⁠.

U.S. export controls — potential escalation: Reporting published November 15 confirms that American officials are considering restrictions on Chinese access to advanced Nvidia processors hosted in overseas data centers. This could materially increase China’s computing costs and affect Southeast Asian infrastructure investment. No new restriction has been confirmed as enacted. Allegations concerning individual companies remain under investigation. Washington Post, November 15⁠.

Chinese inference infrastructure — cost competition: Lenovo’s November 11 announcement of a proposed 100-megawatt AI token factory targets a 70% reduction in comprehensive token-production costs. The target is strategically relevant but remains a company projection rather than an independently demonstrated operating result. Project report⁠.

Implications for The Velocity Edge: The decisive next signal would be an enacted restriction on offshore computing access, independently validated Chinese cost-per-task improvements, or a major model release changing the frontier-performance hierarchy. These could trigger a significant reallocation of capital across Nvidia, Chinese semiconductor suppliers, AI infrastructure operators and application developers. No such new decisive change has been verified in today’s review.

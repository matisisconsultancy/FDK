America finances the frontier. China compresses its economics. The next battle is over who captures the value.

MATERIAL INFLECTION ALERT — HIGH SIGNIFICANCE

THE BIG TAKE

A consequential development is changing how investors should understand China’s challenge to American AI leadership.

New reporting indicates that two leading Chinese AI developers, DeepSeek and Moonshot AI, have relied substantially on outputs from OpenAI’s most advanced model to improve their own systems.

At the same time, independent testing demonstrates that Chinese open-weight models are acquiring sophisticated agentic capabilities previously associated primarily with America’s proprietary frontier.

These developments strengthen The Velocity Edge thesis—but reveal a deeper economic mechanism.

China’s competitive advantage may arise not only from producing intelligence more efficiently, but also from accelerating the transfer of capabilities developed at substantially greater expense elsewhere.

This introduces a new dimension to the AI Supercycle: the economics of technological imitation, diffusion and value capture.

1. THE DEEPSEEK INFLECTION: WHO PAYS FOR INTELLIGENCE?

On October 28, The Information reported that DeepSeek and Moonshot AI had relied heavily on outputs from OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra when developing their latest models.

The report specifically identifies DeepSeek’s V4.1-Flash 1017 and Moonshot’s Kimi K3.1 Pro.

The technique, known as model distillation, can enable a developer to reproduce aspects of a more capable model’s performance while reducing the cost of training and deploying its own system.

The report cites people familiar with the companies’ development activities. The specific claims have not been independently verified, and neither DeepSeek nor Moonshot provided a substantive public response. Distillation itself is an established technique; the controversy concerns unauthorized use of proprietary systems. 

Why this matters

The economic consequences could be substantial.

American developers incur enormous costs developing frontier intelligence.

Competing developers may be able to acquire portions of that capability at significantly lower incremental costs.

The resulting competitive process becomes:

American frontier investment → Advanced model capabilities → Knowledge extraction → Lower-cost competing models → Global price competition.

This is not evidence that Chinese models lack genuine innovation.

It is evidence supporting a potentially powerful mechanism through which frontier leadership can become a source of competitive advantage for rivals.

The implications extend beyond intellectual property.

The company financing the technological breakthrough may not capture the majority of its subsequent economic value.

2. THE AGENTIC INFLECTION: CHINA’S OPEN MODELS CROSS A NEW CAPABILITY THRESHOLD

Research published October 28 by AI security firm Irregular, and reported by Reuters on October 29, provides another important signal.

Z.AI’s GLM-5.3 successfully completed three of thirteen complex cybersecurity scenarios in controlled testing.

This represents the strongest performance Irregular has measured from an openly available model.

Leading American proprietary models performed better in separate evaluations, although the tests were not directly comparable.

The important development is that a downloadable Chinese model has demonstrated the ability to complete multistage tasks that require sustained planning and coordinated execution. 

The financial significance extends beyond cybersecurity.

Open-weight AI models are becoming increasingly capable of undertaking complex, multistep work.

That potentially expands their addressable market across software engineering, research, industrial operations and enterprise automation.

But greater autonomy introduces additional security and compliance costs.

The next competitive test is therefore not simply model performance.It is risk-adjusted productivity per completed task.

Chinese models may offer attractive deployment economics while requiring additional investment in oversight, security and reliability.

This complicates simplistic comparisons based exclusively on token prices.

3. WALL STREET CONFIRMS THE NEXT FINANCIAL TEST

The latest American earnings reports reinforce the financial significance of these developments.

Microsoft reported $18.6 billion in quarterly free cash flow, despite investing $45.2 billion in computing infrastructure.

Alphabet reported negative $9.07 billion in quarterly free cash flow, even as Google Cloud revenue more than doubled to $30.71 billion.

Following their October 28 earnings releases, Microsoft shares rose more than 3% in after-hours trading, while Alphabet fell nearly 10%. 

These results do not establish that Microsoft has superior AI technology.

They demonstrate that capital markets are increasingly differentiating between the expansion of AI infrastructure and the financial capacity to sustain it.

This distinction becomes more consequential as competitively priced Chinese models broaden the supply of deployable intelligence.

The potential result is a combination of expanding AI consumption, declining model-level pricing power and increasingly selective returns on infrastructure investment.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN: THE GREAT DIVISION OF INTELLIGENCE

The three developments reveal a more fundamental economic structure.

America’s advantage: Frontier innovation, advanced computing infrastructure, proprietary technology and access to capital.

China’s challenge: Rapid capability absorption, software optimization, open-weight distribution and industrial deployment.

The emerging financial contest: Which participants can retain the economic surplus created by technological advances?

The potential second-order effects are significant.

First, frontier developers may strengthen restrictions against unauthorized capability extraction, increasing the importance of model security and proprietary distribution.

Second, competition from capable open-weight systems may accelerate the commoditization of standardized inference workloads.

Third, businesses deploying AI could capture an increasing proportion of the economic surplus as capabilities become more accessible.

Fourth, geopolitical restrictions may fragment deployment markets without eliminating the incentives for technological diffusion.

These are prospective transmission mechanisms, not established market outcomes.

THE VELOCITY EDGE — WHAT CHANGES

Today’s evidence materially strengthens our central thesis while requiring one important refinement.

China’s economic challenge is not necessarily independent of America’s technological leadership.

In some cases, the former may be accelerated by access to the latter.

This creates an unusual competitive relationship.

America finances the frontier. Its technology helps establish the global capability benchmark. Chinese developers compete to reproduce, adapt and distribute increasingly capable intelligence at lower costs.

The relationship can simultaneously reinforce American technological superiority and undermine the pricing power associated with that superiority.

For the AI Supercycle, the investment consequences are becoming clearer.

The most attractive returns may increasingly accrue to companies possessing defensible technological advantages, scalable distribution, superior capital efficiency or the capacity to translate AI deployment into measurable productivity.

Model capability, infrastructure ownership and shareholder returns must now be evaluated separately.

FIVE SIGNALS FOR INVESTORS

1. Frontier protection: Whether American developers can limit unauthorized model distillation without sacrificing legitimate commercial adoption.
2. Chinese model economics: Whether DeepSeek, Moonshot and Z.AI can sustain competitive performance and attractive margins without continued reliance on proprietary American model outputs.
3.Agentic deployment: Whether open-weight models demonstrate comparable risk-adjusted performance across commercially valuable workflows.
4. Infrastructure profitability: Whether hyperscalers can sustain free-cash-flow generation as model prices and computing architectures evolve.
5. Industrial productivity: Whether AI adoption produces measurable improvements in margins, asset utilization and returns on invested capital.

THE INVESTMENT VERDICT

The U.S.–China AI competition is becoming a contest over the economic ownership of intelligence.

The United States retains significant frontier advantages.

China is demonstrating increasingly sophisticated methods of narrowing capability gaps and expanding access to advanced models.

But the next market rotation will depend less on model rankings than on the distribution of profits across frontier development, infrastructure, software and industrial deployment.

The decisive question is whether technological leaders can preserve sufficient economic returns to finance continued innovation while competing models become progressively more capable and accessible.

VELOCITY INSIGHT

AMERICA LEADS THE CREATION OF FRONTIER INTELLIGENCE. CHINA IS CHALLENGING THE COST OF REPRODUCING AND DISTRIBUTING IT. THE NEXT AI SUPERCYCLE WILL REWARD THOSE WHO CONTROL THE ECONOMIC VALUE—NOT MERELY THE TECHNOLOGY—OF INTELLIGENCE.

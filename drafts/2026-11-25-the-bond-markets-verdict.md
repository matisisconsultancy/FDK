Record AI Orders, Record Credit Stress and the Emerging Battle for Financial Supremacy.
THE BIG TAKE | 3-MINUTE READ
The most consequential signal this morning is coming not from equity markets, but from credit markets.
Dell has reported record quarterly revenues of $54.9 billion and an AI order backlog of $132.3 billion. Yet credit default swap spreads for Oracle, Alphabet, SpaceX and CoreWeave have reached record levels.
CoreWeave's five-year CDS spread has widened to 1,314 basis points, entering distressed territory. Oracle's has reached 356 basis points. Meanwhile, Asian semiconductor equities are experiencing another severe selloff. 
Dell Technologies, Inc. (DELL)+2

Technological demand is accelerating while confidence in its financing architecture is deteriorating. The bond market is challenging the equity market's assumptions about the future of AI.
THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
What if credit markets, rather than technological innovation, determine the speed, scale and ultimate winners of the AI Supercycle?

FIVE SIGNALS SHAPING THE NEXT ECONOMY
01 | AI — Credit is becoming the ultimate constraint.
Dell's exceptional results confirm infrastructure demand. Widening credit spreads nevertheless threaten to increase financing costs and delay marginal investment projects.
02 | SEMICONDUCTORS — Industrial policy is accelerating fragmentation.
Washington is reportedly preparing new tariff proposals following an investigation covering 16 trading partners, including China and the EU. Asian technology exporters face renewed uncertainty. The proposed duties have not yet been confirmed. 
Reuters

03 | ENERGY — Electricity is emerging as a strategic asset class.
Goldman Sachs estimates that an additional 52 GW of US data-centre power capacity could come online in 2028 alone. Electricity networks, storage and generation equipment may capture increasingly valuable positions in the AI value chain. 
Investing.com Australia

04 | EUROPE — Sovereign credibility is becoming industrial competitiveness.
France's projected 5.5% fiscal deficit exposes the tension between budgetary consolidation and technological investment. Sovereign-risk differentiation could increasingly influence banking valuations and corporate financing costs.
05 | MOBILITY — The next productivity frontier is the physical economy.
Industrial AI, intelligent railways, electrification and predictive maintenance offer opportunities to increase returns on existing assets without replicating the capital intensity of frontier computing.
THE HIDDEN PATTERNS — THREE SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS
The credit accelerator: Wider spreads could trigger investment delays, weaker semiconductor orders and downward earnings revisions.
The competitive consolidation: Financially autonomous corporations could acquire distressed assets and consolidate market leadership.
The cross-asset rotation: Capital may migrate toward electricity infrastructure, industrial automation, high-quality credit and businesses deploying AI to improve operating margins.
The Great Split is becoming a divide between companies that control their financial destiny and companies whose future depends on the willingness of others to finance it.

WHAT TO WATCH BEFORE THE CLOSE
At 14:30 CET, US PCE inflation, revised third-quarter GDP and corporate profits will test monetary expectations. Monitor AI credit spreads, Treasury yields, French sovereign bonds and European banks. 
U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA)

The decisive signal will be whether credit markets stabilize alongside equities. Without that confirmation, another technology rally could prove financially fragile.
THE FINAL TAKE — THE NEW HIERARCHY OF CAPITAL
For investors, prioritize financial autonomy and returns on invested capital. For boards, accelerate productivity rather than financial commitments. For policymakers, develop competitive capital markets and reliable energy infrastructure.
The next market rotation will favour companies capable of sustaining technological acceleration without becoming prisoners of its financing requirements.
THE VELOCITY INSIGHT
Equity markets price the promise of the future. Credit markets price the probability of reaching it.
When the two diverge, investors should pay particular attention to the latter.

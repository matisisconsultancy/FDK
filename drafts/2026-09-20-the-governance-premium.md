MPS can design the Third Pole. The decisive question is now: who would actually govern it?

20 September 2026 | FDK
Data cut: 09:30 CEST

The Italian banking battle has moved again.

Yesterday, the key question was the Price of Scale. Today the deeper issue is becoming visible: control after consolidation.

There has been no new binding corporate transaction or regulatory decision announced during the Sunday morning window. MPS’s public investor-relations archive still shows its latest formal deal-related release on 18 September. But the weekend has produced material new information about the architecture behind the MPS defence—and it changes the interpretation of the entire Risiko. 

According to la Repubblica, citing MPS correspondence with Consob that it reviewed, a successful combination of MPS, Banco BPM and Banca Generali would create a shareholder structure in which Crédit Agricole could become the largest shareholder with 10.9%, followed by Delfin at 8.3%, Generali at 6.4%, Caltagirone at 4.9%, BlackRock at 3.9% and the Italian Treasury at 2.3%. The same correspondence reportedly envisages 5,000 voluntary exits, 2,500 new hires, between 57 and 115 antitrust-related branch disposals, and the acquisition by MPS of Mediobanca’s entire 13.3% Generali stake before the 29 October shareholder meeting. These are reported details, not yet fully reproduced in an MPS public filing. 

That produces today’s central insight:

MPS IS DEFENDING ITS INDEPENDENCE BY DESIGNING A BANK THAT WOULD HAVE NO DOMINANT CONTROLLING SHAREHOLDER.

And potentially, its largest shareholder would be French.

This is no longer simply a battle about scale.

It is a battle about the Governance Premium.

⸻

1. WHAT CHANGED IN THE LAST 24 HOURS

The first change is not a new offer. It is greater visibility into the industrial and governance mechanics of the existing offers.

Market report. The MPS–Banco BPM industrial plan reportedly assumes approximately 5,000 voluntary early-retirement departures to produce up to €400 million in personnel savings, partly offset by roughly 2,500 new hires. The reported antitrust modelling identifies between 57 and 115 potentially excess branches depending on whether the relevant local concentration threshold is assumed at 30% or 25%. 

That makes the MPS plan more credible—but also more politically visible.

Market report. MPS reportedly intends to purchase from Mediobanca the entire 13.3% Generali stake before the October 29 meeting, even though only around 4.5% of Generali would be required, at announcement values, for the €3 billion in-kind portion of the extraordinary shareholder distribution. The destination of the residual stake—roughly 8.8% under a simple arithmetic interpretation—therefore becomes strategically important. 

Confirmed fact. MPS separately disclosed on 18 September that it acquired 100,000 Banca Generali shares at €56 through the exercise of a call option entered into in April, while simultaneously closing a 92,000-share securities-lending position. MPS explicitly described the underlying transaction as part of its ordinary Markets activity. The numbers are immaterial relative to Banca Generali’s capital and should not be interpreted as evidence of strategic accumulation. 

And one development outside Italy may ultimately matter even more. Senior EU officials meeting in Dublin on 18 September explicitly argued that Europe needs larger banks, deeper capital markets and less political interference in bank mergers. ECB Vice President Boris Vujcic said European banks need greater scale to compete with U.S. institutions, while stopping short of advocating weaker capital requirements. 

The Italian Risiko is therefore beginning to converge with the European policy agenda.

⸻

2. THE DECISIVE ISSUE

WHO GOVERNS THE THIRD POLE?

Until now, the strategic question surrounding MPS has been:

Can Lovaglio build sufficient scale to block Intesa?

The new question is harder:

If he succeeds, who controls the institution that emerges?The reported post-transaction cap table suggests no shareholder would exercise conventional control. Crédit Agricole could be first at 10.9%, Delfin second at 8.3%, Generali third at 6.4%, followed by Caltagirone, BlackRock and the Treasury. 

That transforms the equation:

STRATEGIC INDEPENDENCE ≠ SINGLE-SHAREHOLDER CONTROL

Instead:

STRATEGIC INDEPENDENCE = STABLE COALITION GOVERNANCE

This is the next phase of the European banking contest.

Capital is increasingly international.

Regulation is increasingly European.

But political legitimacy remains national.

The institution capable of reconciling all three obtains the Governance Premium.

⸻

3. UPDATED STRATEGIC MAP

INTESA SANPAOLO — EXECUTION REMAINS THE BENCHMARK

Intesa still has the cleanest execution path.

Its shareholders have already authorized the capital increase supporting the MPS offer, with 96.96% of represented capital voting in favour; institutional investors represented a large majority of attendance. 

The principal obstacle is now regulatory rather than financial. AGCM has identified possible competition issues in deposits in 20 provinces, SME lending in 17 provinces and, importantly, in the relationship between Intesa and Generali that would arise through MPS/Mediobanca’s 13.3% stake. 

Intesa’s problem is therefore increasingly precise:

HOW MUCH OF THE STRATEGIC VALUE MUST BE GIVEN AWAY TO OBTAIN CLEARANCE?

FDK View: STRONG OVERWEIGHT.

Execution quality remains its greatest competitive advantage.

⸻

MPS — THE CONTROL PARADOX

MPS’s industrial case is strengthening.

Its public plan already envisages €1.4 billion of pre-tax benefits from Banco BPM and €400 million from Banca Generali; the weekend reporting now provides greater operational granularity behind those figures. 

But the governance consequences are equally important.

If both offers succeed, MPS would preserve its corporate independence from Intesa while creating an institution in which Crédit Agricole could become the largest shareholder.

That is the paradox:

MPS CAN WIN THE BATTLE FOR INDEPENDENCE — AND LOSE THE ABILITY TO GOVERN ALONE.

The October 29 meeting therefore becomes more than a vote on acquisitions.

It becomes a vote on the future coalition architecture of the bank.

FDK View: HIGH CONVEXITY / HIGH GOVERNANCE RISK.

⸻

BANCO BPM — THE INDUSTRIAL ENGINE

Banco BPM remains the indispensable operating asset in the MPS third-pole thesis.

It brings Northern Italian density, SME lending, deposits, commercial distribution and the Anima franchise.

But its importance has now increased for another reason.

Its largest shareholder could become the largest shareholder of the entire new group.

Crédit Agricole therefore does not merely possess a blocking stake in Banco BPM.

It potentially possesses a migration path from target shareholder to system shareholder.

That makes Banco BPM the bridge through which French capital could enter the centre of the new Italian banking architecture.

⸻

CRÉDIT AGRICOLE — FROM KINGMAKER TO POTENTIAL ANCHOR SHAREHOLDER

This is today’s most important second-order effect.

Crédit Agricole owns 29.3% of Banco BPM and reportedly still considers the MPS offer unattractive, preferring a combination of Banco BPM with Crédit Agricole Italia. 

But if MPS acquires Banco BPM, Crédit Agricole does not disappear from the equation.

It moves upstream.

According to the reported MPS–Consob calculations, it could emerge with approximately 10.9% of the full combined institution—or around 12.5% if only the Banco BPM transaction succeeds. 

That radically changes its leverage.

CRÉDIT AGRICOLE CAN OPPOSE THE DEAL, RENEGOTIATE THE DEAL, OR BECOME THE LARGEST SHAREHOLDER OF THE DEAL.

Few actors in European banking possess that degree of optionality.

FDK View: STRATEGIC OVERWEIGHT.

⸻

GENERALI — THE CENTRE OF GRAVITY MOVES CLOSER

Generali becomes even more important under the reported MPS architecture.

It could simultaneously be:

a major underlying strategic asset;

the controlling shareholder of Banca Generali;

a shareholder of the enlarged MPS group;and a key competition variable in the Intesa transaction.

The reported MPS plan to acquire Mediobanca’s entire 13.3% Generali holding before distributing only part of it dramatically increases the importance of what happens to the residual stake. 

Generali therefore has something every other player wants:

SCARCITY + STRATEGIC INDEPENDENCE + TIME.

FDK View: OVERWEIGHT.

⸻

BANCA GENERALI — THE OPTIONALITY ASSET

Banca Generali remains strategically attractive because its value lies less in ordinary banking scale than in high-quality wealth distribution.

At Friday’s close, the equity still trades materially below the adjusted implied value of MPS’s exchange proposal.

The market therefore continues to price a substantial probability that execution will fail or terms will change.

That is not necessarily a negative signal on the asset.

It is an Execution Discount on the transaction.

⸻

UNICREDIT–COMMERZBANK — OWNERSHIP IS ALMOST SOLVED; GOVERNANCE IS NOT

UniCredit has accumulated 47.6% of Commerzbank, bringing it close to effective shareholder control. 

Berlin has now moved from outright resistance toward conditions: Commerzbank should remain headquartered and listed in Frankfurt and continue supporting the Mittelstand. At the same time, Reuters has reported that UniCredit wants changes in Commerzbank’s leadership and opposes continuing German government representation on the supervisory board. 

The debate has therefore shifted.

It is no longer:

Can UniCredit acquire Commerzbank?

It is:

WHAT GOVERNANCE MODEL MAKES CONTINENTAL SCALE POLITICALLY ACCEPTABLE?

And Brussels is increasingly moving toward Orcel’s underlying strategic thesis. 

⸻

4. CAPITAL-MARKETS REACTION AND VALUATION

There is no Sunday trading, so Friday 18 September remains the last available market signal.

The session was broadly risk-off: the FTSE MIB fell 1.6%, UniCredit declined 3.0%, Intesa 2.1%, Banco BPM 2.0%, MPS 1.5% and Generali 1.9%. Italy’s 10-year yield rose to approximately 4.43% and the BTP–Bund spread finished near 91 basis points. 

The transaction spreads remain more interesting than the absolute moves.

MPS closed at €11.76. Netting the announced €1.208 extraordinary distribution gives an adjusted reference value of approximately €10.55. Using MPS’s 1.567 exchange ratio, the implied value for Banco BPM is approximately €16.53, versus Banco BPM’s €15.70 close: an execution spread of roughly 5.3%. 

Using the 6.958 Banca Generali exchange ratio, the same methodology implies approximately €73.42 per Banca Generali share, compared with a Friday close of €65.00: an execution spread of roughly 13.0%. 

Those spreads are telling us something very clear:

THE MARKET BELIEVES THE ASSETS HAVE VALUE. IT DOES NOT YET BELIEVE THE PATH IS CERTAIN.

There is an equivalent signal in Germany. UniCredit closed Friday at €81.13 and Commerzbank at €40.64. The existing 0.485-share exchange ratio mechanically implies approximately €39.35 per Commerzbank share—about 3.2% below Commerzbank’s market price. Investors are therefore still pricing either improved economics, strategic optionality or a control premium beyond the existing terms.

8 — COMMERZBANK | EVENT-DRIVEN. The market continues to price more than the current mechanical offer value.

6. THREE SCENARIOS

SCENARIO I — CONTINENTAL BIFURCATION

42% | Down from 44%

Intesa completes MPS after acceptable remedies.

UniCredit reaches a governance settlement on Commerzbank.

Banco BPM eventually gravitates toward Crédit Agricole.

Generali remains strategically independent.

This remains the highest-probability end-state, but the probability falls because the MPS alternative now appears more operationally developed than before.

⸻

SCENARIO II — THE COALITION THIRD POLE

26% | Up from 24%

MPS wins the October 29 votes and converts its defensive architecture into:

MPS + MEDIOBANCA + BANCO BPM + BANCA GENERALI

The crucial change is conceptual.

This would not be an MPS-controlled national champion in the traditional sense.

It would be a coalition-controlled European financial group, potentially anchored by Crédit Agricole, Delfin, Generali, Caltagirone, BlackRock and the Italian state.

The scenario gains probability because the industrial details and post-deal cap table now look increasingly thought through.

⸻

SCENARIO III — COLLISION OF CONTROL

32% | Unchanged

MPS authorizes one or both defensive transactions before Intesa secures effective control.

Intesa could then acquire a company already contractually committed to reshaping itself.

The result would be maximum path dependency across MPS, Banco BPM, Generali and Banca Generali.

In that scenario, regulation ceases to be simply a gate.

REGULATION BECOMES THE MECHANISM THAT DECIDES WHICH STRATEGY SURVIVES.

⸻

7. POLITICAL AND REGULATORY IMPLICATIONS

The Italian Government still holds roughly 4.9% of MPS. Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti has repeatedly described the government as neutral while reserving the possibility of Golden Power conditions relating to competition, SME lending and territorial presence. The Treasury has also indicated it intends to avoid selling its residual stake in a way that interferes with the ongoing consolidation. 

But the European context is moving against broad political discretion.

Brussels is explicitly pushing for fewer political barriers to bank M&A, while ECB leadership argues Europe requires larger institutions and deeper capital markets. 

This creates a fundamental institutional tension:

**ROME WANTS STRATEGIC SAFEGUARDS.

BRUSSELS WANTS MARKET INTEGRATION.
FRANKFURT WANTS PRUDENTIAL SOUNDNESS.**

The winning transaction must satisfy all three.

That is what Banking Union now means in practice.

⸻

8. THE HIDDEN PATTERN

FROM NATIONAL CHAMPIONS TO COALITION CHAMPIONS

The Italian banking consolidation debate has traditionally been framed around ownership.

Who controls MPS?

Who protects Generali?

Who anchors Banco BPM?

Who keeps Italian savings Italian?

But the latest architecture suggests that this framing may be becoming obsolete.

The future European champion may not have one controlling shareholder.

It may have:

French banking capital;

Italian industrial families;

international asset managers;

an insurance company;

a residual sovereign stake;

and an executive team responsible for holding the equilibrium together.

That is a different model of capitalism.

CAPITAL IS BECOMING EUROPEAN FASTER THAN POLITICAL LEGITIMACY IS BECOMING EUROPEAN.

This is why governance is becoming the scarce asset.

And it produces a new law:

THE GOVERNANCE PREMIUM

Continental Scale without coalition stability destroys value.

Coalition stability without scale destroys competitiveness.

The winners will be institutions capable of producing both.

⸻

9. WHAT TO WATCH IN THE NEXT 24–72 HOURS

The first signal is MPS itself. Any formal clarification of the reported 5,000 exits, 2,500 hires, branch-remedy assumptions, post-deal cap table or acquisition of Mediobanca’s entire Generali stake would materially raise the quality of information available to shareholders.

The second is Crédit Agricole.Its response to the possibility of becoming the largest shareholder in an MPS-led group may be more consequential than its current rejection of the headline Banco BPM economics.

The third is Generali. The key question is no longer simply whether it supports the Banca Generali transaction. It is what strategic treatment it expects for the residual Generali stake embedded in the MPS architecture.

The fourth is Monday’s market reaction. The relative behaviour of MPS versus Banco BPM and Banca Generali will show whether investors treat the newly revealed industrial mechanics as execution-enhancing or value-destructive.

The fifth is AGCM. The branch remedy is manageable. The more consequential issue is whether the Intesa–Generali relationship requires structural governance restrictions.

The sixth is Berlin. Any shift from broad political conditions to concrete governance terms would materially reduce the remaining UniCredit–Commerzbank discount.

10. VELOCITY INSIGHT

The Italian banking battle began as a contest over assets.

Then it became a contest over capital.

Then time.

Then execution.

Now it is becoming a contest over governance.

And that changes the final equation:

CONTINENTAL SCALE × CAPITAL DISCIPLINE × REGULATORY CLEARANCE × COALITION STABILITY = EXECUTABLE CONTROL

MPS may be able to build the Third Pole.

Intesa may be able to acquire MPS.

UniCredit may be able to control Commerzbank.

But none of those outcomes creates value unless the institution that emerges can actually be governed.

That is the new frontier.

THE NEXT EUROPEAN BANKING CHAMPION WILL NOT SIMPLY BE THE BANK THAT BECOMES BIGGEST.

IT WILL BE THE BANK THAT CAN TURN FRAGMENTED OWNERSHIP, NATIONAL POLITICS AND EUROPEAN REGULATION INTO A STABLE SYSTEM OF CONTROL.

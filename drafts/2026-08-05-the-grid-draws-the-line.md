AMD doubles data-center revenue. SpaceX nearly doubles sales. Caterpillar’s backlog reaches $72.1 billion. Yet Texas suspends automatic progression for data-center connections—and markets punish capital expenditure that cannot prove its return.
Data cut: 05:45 CEST Latest verified evidence: August 4 U.S. close and early August 5 Asian trading Global Industry Velocity Reading: 93.5 / 100 24-hour structural movement: +0.2
The Fundamental Question
Who controls structural velocity when demand for intelligence remains abundant—but electricity, grid permission, physical delivery and capital discipline become scarce?
The overnight evidence resolves an important debate.
The AI Supercycle is not weakening. It is becoming industrial—and therefore more selective.
AMD reported second-quarter revenue of $11.5 billion, up 50%, with data-center revenue more than doubling to $6.72 billion. Yet its shares fell almost 9% after hours because an above-consensus outlook was still insufficient against elevated expectations.
SpaceX reported its first public quarterly results: revenue rose 92% to $7.8 billion, Starlink subscriptions reached 12 million and AI-related revenue expanded sharply. The shares nevertheless fell approximately 7.5% as investors focused on an estimated $18 billion of capital expenditure, much of it associated with AI infrastructure.
Caterpillar provided the opposite signal. Revenue increased 24% to $20.5 billion, construction sales rose 35%, and its backlog reached $72.1 billion as data centers, power generation and infrastructure translated digital demand into machinery orders.
Texas then clarified the new limit. The governor ordered a comprehensive audit of every data-center project advancing through the ERCOT interconnection process. No additional project may move forward until its compliance, power requirements and broader impact have been verified.
The Great Split now separates technological demand from industrial permission—and industrial permission from financial return.

The Five Strongest Industries
Semiconductors — 97.4
Verified evidence
AMD reported quarterly revenue of $11.5 billion, up 50% year over year. Data-center revenue exceeded $6.7 billion, more than double the prior-year level, while the company forecast approximately $13 billion of third-quarter revenue—above consensus expectations.
AMD is increasingly competing through complete systems combining GPUs, CPUs, networking and data-center capacity. Its agreements include a major AI-server commitment from Anthropic and up to 2.5 gigawatts of capacity with Core Scientific.
Onsemi separately expects its AI-data-center business to more than double during 2026 as demand expands for power-conversion and management semiconductors.
FDK analytical judgment
Semiconductors return to first place because the addressable architecture is broadening.
The first phase of the AI cycle concentrated value in:
accelerators;
high-bandwidth memory;
advanced fabrication;
and packaging.
The next phase includes:
custom processors;
networking;
optical connectivity;
storage;
power conversion;
edge intelligence;
and robotics control.
AMD’s post-results decline is not evidence of weak industrial demand. It is evidence that the financial threshold has risen faster than revenue.
Semiconductor velocity is accelerating even as semiconductor valuations are being disciplined.
The industry’s remaining vulnerability is concentration: fabrication, advanced packaging and specialist equipment remain geographically and institutionally narrow.
Velocity edge: expanding silicon content across the complete intelligence system. Principal constraint: expectation risk, geographic concentration and customer capital intensity.

Aerospace & Defense — 97.2
Verified evidence
SpaceX’s quarterly revenue rose from $4.1 billion to $7.8 billion. Starlink generated more than half of revenue, subscriptions reached 12 million and the company plans to deploy 1,000 next-generation V3 satellites within twelve months.
Starlink revenue reportedly grew 66% to approximately $4.3 billion and generated operating profit, while SpaceX’s wider AI activities remained heavily capital-intensive.
The traditional defense cycle remains supported by historic backlogs, multiyear procurement and sovereign efforts to replenish missiles, sensors, aircraft and communications capacity.
FDK analytical judgment
Aerospace and defense increasingly form one integrated industrial architecture:
launch systems + satellites + broadband + intelligence + autonomous platforms + sovereign procurement.
SpaceX demonstrates the sector’s exceptional strategic optionality.
Starlink provides recurring connectivity revenue.
Launch infrastructure provides orbital access.
AI activities create future optionality but currently consume substantial capital.
The same conversion question now applies across defense:
funded production deserves a premium;
deployable software deserves a premium;
speculative capability without manufacturing throughput does not.
The industry moves higher because space infrastructure has become economically observable rather than merely strategic.
Velocity edge: sovereign demand, orbital infrastructure and high contractual visibility. Principal constraint: capital expenditure, manufacturing throughput and execution complexity.

Cloud & Data Centers — 97.1
Verified evidence
The Texas governor directed the state’s utility commission and ERCOT to audit every data-center proposal advancing through the interconnection process.Projects cannot progress until their compliance and impact have been verified, and noncompliant projects must be denied access to the grid.
The decision follows a broader expansion in computing demand and mounting concern about electricity, water, noise, incentives and the credibility of projects entering grid queues.
Cloud demand remains exceptionally strong. AMD’s data-center growth and SpaceX’s expanding computing activity reinforce the continuing requirement for accelerated infrastructure.
FDK analytical judgment
Cloud loses the top position because power availability is no longer equivalent to connection approval.
The economic sequence has changed:
Computing demand → financing → land → power request → regulatory verification → interconnection → productive capacity.
The grid audit is significant because it introduces a new source of project differentiation.
The strongest facilities will possess:
contracted customers;
credible financing;
verified load requirements;
dedicated or incremental generation;
water and cooling plans;
and demonstrable community benefits.
Speculative queue positions will lose value.
Cloud’s structural demand remains near maximum. Its ability to convert announced capacity into operating capacity has become less certain.
Velocity edge: enterprise adoption and recurring computing demand. Principal constraint: grid permission, water, local legitimacy and financing quality.

Energy & Power Infrastructure — 97.0
Verified evidence
Power systems in northern, northeastern and eastern China reached record loads as extreme heat increased air-conditioning demand and placed additional stress on regional grids.
Texas’s audit reinforces the same global reality: the electricity system is becoming the binding constraint on data-center deployment.
Caterpillar’s power and energy business grew 17%, benefiting from demand for generation equipment, turbines and systems supporting data centers and broader infrastructure investment.
FDK analytical judgment
Energy moves higher despite Brent falling toward $79 per barrel.
That apparent contradiction captures the Great Split inside energy.
Oil scarcity is easing.
Electricity scarcity is intensifying.
Value is moving toward:
dispatchable generation;
grid equipment;
turbines;
transformers;
nuclear supply chains;
batteries;
microgrids;
and off-grid or behind-the-meter systems.
The Texas decision may slow certain data centers. It increases the strategic value of projects capable of supplying their own incremental power.
The grid is no longer merely carrying the AI economy. It is determining which parts of the AI economy are allowed to exist.
Velocity edge: unavoidable demand across computing, climate adaptation and industrial electrification. Principal constraint: equipment lead times, permitting, affordability and public legitimacy.

Artificial Intelligence & Accelerated Computing — 96.6
Verified evidence
AMD’s data-center business more than doubled, confirming the continued expansion of AI-related computing demand.
SpaceX reported rapid AI-revenue growth but also very high infrastructure expenditure, while its shares declined after results.
Palantir’s earlier results continued to support the opposite side of the market: domain-specific AI integrated into government and commercial workflows can produce accelerating contracted revenue.
FDK analytical judgment
AI’s score declines modestly for one reason:
Capital expenditure is no longer accepted as evidence of monetization.
The market is differentiating between four models:
AI infrastructure sold externally, generating direct revenue.
Enterprise AI embedded in contracted workflows.
Consumer AI monetized indirectly through advertising or engagement.
Strategic AI funded by another profitable business while future economics remain uncertain.
AMD strengthens the first model.
Palantir strengthens the second.
SpaceX reveals both the opportunity and the risk of the fourth.
The technology remains at maximum velocity.
The allocation of capital within it is becoming far more demanding.Velocity edge: productivity, adoption and market creation. Principal constraint: capital productivity, governance and monetization dispersion.

Cross-Industry Hidden Patterns
Demand Is No Longer the Scarce Variable
AMD, SpaceX and Caterpillar all reported powerful evidence of demand.
Their market outcomes diverged because the decisive variable has changed.
The market now asks:
How much capital is required?
Who owns the infrastructure?
Who guarantees utilization?
When does depreciation begin?
When does free cash flow arrive?
Demand explains the build-out. Capital productivity explains the valuation.
⸻
Grid Access Is Becoming a License to Operate
Texas has formally elevated interconnection from a technical process into a strategic approval gate.
This creates a new hierarchy among data-center assets:
operational capacity with secured power;
permitted construction with verified interconnection;
financed projects awaiting approval;
speculative projects occupying grid queues.
Only the first two categories deserve an infrastructure premium.
⸻
AI Is Pulling Industrial Capital Upstream
Caterpillar’s backlog reveals how intelligence spending migrates through the productive system:
AI workload → data center → power demand → construction → machinery → materials → labor.
The AI multiplier is now visible outside technology-sector financial statements.
That is the clearest evidence that the Supercycle is becoming macroeconomic.
⸻
Space Infrastructure Is Becoming Digital Infrastructure
Starlink is simultaneously:
a telecom network;
a sovereign-connectivity asset;
a commercial subscription platform;
an orbital infrastructure system;
and a source of capital for broader SpaceX investment.
The traditional boundaries among aerospace, telecommunications, media and computing are collapsing.
⸻
The Capital-Expenditure Bar Has Risen Again
AMD’s operating performance exceeded expectations.
SpaceX’s revenue nearly doubled.
Both shares declined after results.
This is not irrational.
It reflects a market that has moved from asking whether the AI opportunity exists to asking whether the return exceeds the cost of funding it.
⸻
Critical Infrastructure Faces a Legitimacy ConstraintPower, data centers and mineral projects require more than financing and permits.
They require public acceptance.
Texas is auditing the effects of computing infrastructure.
South African farmers are challenging lithium development.
Climate stress is placing additional pressure on power and water systems.
The next bottleneck may not be technology or capital. It may be permission.

The Great Split Runs Between Announced and Authorized Velocity
The market previously distinguished between announced capital expenditure and completed infrastructure.
It must now add another distinction:
announced;
financed;
authorized;
connected;
utilized;
and monetized.
Every step removes uncertainty.
Every incomplete step requires a larger discount.

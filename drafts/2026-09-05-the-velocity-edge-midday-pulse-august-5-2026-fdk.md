The AI Supercycle Has Hit Its Capital-Productivity Wall
AI demand is still accelerating.
AMD is forecasting stronger revenue. SpaceX is nearly doubling sales. Asian semiconductor markets are rebounding. Europe remains close to record highs.
Yet AMD and SpaceX are falling sharply.
Why?
Because the market has moved beyond the question of whether AI demand exists.
It is now asking a harder question:
Can that demand generate a return above the cost of the capital, electricity and infrastructure required to serve it?
This is the new phase of the AI Supercycle.
The bottleneck is no longer only accelerated computing. It is power generation, grids, cooling, financing and time.
Siemens Energy is gaining because the market increasingly values the physical system that makes intelligence possible. AMD and SpaceX are being repriced because growth alone no longer protects valuation when capital intensity, depreciation and payback periods keep rising.

Five Key Signals
AI demand is not the problem
AMD’s data-center revenue is accelerating. SpaceX’s AI-related business is expanding rapidly. Hyperscaler backlogs remain exceptionally strong.
The problem is no longer demand creation.
It is economic conversion.
Growth no longer protects valuation
AMD and SpaceX are being punished despite strong revenue growth because investors are now subtracting:
capital intensity;
depreciation;
financing costs;
and execution duration.
Value is migrating from compute to power
Siemens Energy is capturing record orders because data centers need turbines, grids, electricity conversion and firm capacity.
The AI trade is moving outward from the processor toward the complete industrial architecture.
The payback period is colliding with technological obsolescence
A one-gigawatt AI facility may require eight to ten years to recover its initial investment.
The computing equipment inside it may become obsolete in three.
This is the new duration mismatch of Industrial AI.
Lower oil is helping—but not solving—the problem
Brent near $80 is reducing inflation pressure and long-term yields.
That improves the financing economics of AI infrastructure.
But a lower discount rate cannot repair a project whose capital productivity is structurally weak.
The Hidden Pattern
The market is not turning against Artificial Intelligence.
It is separating four different categories:
intelligence that produces cash;
infrastructure that enables intelligence;
capital that finances future demand;
and assets whose economics depend on permanent refinancing.
The emerging hierarchy is clear:
Power, contracted demand and economic control are becoming more valuable than growth without financial independence.
Five Key Takeaways
AI is entering its capital-productivity phase. Revenue growth alone is no longer enough.
Power is becoming the strategic bottleneck. Turbines, grids, cooling and storage are moving closer to the center of value creation.
Asset duration matters. Investors must compare data-center payback periods with the replacement cycle of the technology inside them.
Demand quality matters more than demand volume. Contracted external demand is fundamentally different from internally financed or speculative utilization.
The Great Split is widening. The winners will be those whose intelligence earns more than the infrastructure required to produce it.
The Velocity Insight
The AI Supercycle is not slowing.

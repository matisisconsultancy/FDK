China’s Reserve Paradox, Berkshire’s $352 Billion Capital Buffer and the Emerging Financial Divide in the AI Supercycle
Where Markets, Capital and Geopolitics Collide
Evidence cut-off: November 8, 2026 | 04:43 CET
THE BIG TAKE
The next competitive advantage in the AI Supercycle may increasingly belong to those who can finance technological expansion without depending on the continuous availability of external capital.
The latest evidence reveals three important developments.
China’s foreign-exchange reserves increased to $3.405 trillion in October. Yet its total official reserve assets declined as the market value of its gold holdings fell, despite continued physical gold accumulation.
Berkshire Hathaway’s November 7 results reveal the contrasting position of a major American capital allocator. The company retained $352.3 billion in cash and US Treasury bills, even after accelerating share repurchases. Its industrial and infrastructure businesses continued generating substantial operating earnings.
Meanwhile, the deterioration in credit-market conditions surrounding highly leveraged AI investments reinforces the importance of liquidity, financing flexibility and the timing of investment returns.
The common lesson is financial rather than technological.
In a capital-intensive technological transition, liquidity is becoming a strategic option: it allows companies and economies to sustain productive investment when financing conditions deteriorate.
For investors, this suggests a gradual shift in attention from the scale of announced investment toward the financial capacity to sustain it.
For the Global Velocity Index, the latest evidence strengthens the importance of the Smart Capital pillar. It does not yet justify a new numerical ranking of national competitive velocity.

II. CHINA — THE RESERVE PARADOX
China’s State Administration of Foreign Exchange published the October reserve figures on November 7.
Foreign-currency reserves increased from approximately $3.400 trillion in September to $3.405 trillion in October.
The increase was modest but positive.
However, the official reserve-asset composition provides a more revealing picture.
Reserve component September 2026 October 2026
Foreign-currency reserves $3.400 trillion $3.405 trillion
Physical gold 77.47 million oz 78.30 million oz
Market value of gold $323.5 billion $306.4 billion
Total official reserve assets $3.789 trillion $3.778 trillion
These are official reported stocks, not measures of monthly investment returns. 
The distinction is consequential.
China accumulated additional physical gold while the dollar value of its gold holdings declined.Foreign-currency reserves increased, but total official reserve assets fell.
This illustrates why changes in the nominal value of financial assets cannot be interpreted directly as changes in underlying financial capacity.
Exchange-rate movements and market valuations influence reported reserve balances. The SAFE statement explicitly identifies currency translation and asset-price changes among the drivers of October’s movement.
The increase in foreign-currency reserves therefore does not independently establish equivalent capital inflows.
Nevertheless, China’s reserve position remains substantial.
Its ability to manage foreign-exchange liquidity provides a financial buffer against external volatility.
This becomes particularly relevant following the People’s Bank of China’s November 3 announcement that it would increase banks’ foreign-currency reserve requirement from 4% to 6%, effective November 15.
That measure is intended to influence foreign-currency liquidity and the pace of yuan appreciation. It is distinct from the stock of official foreign-exchange reserves. 
GVI Implication
Smart Capital: High-confidence new observations; mixed financial interpretation.
The increase in foreign-currency reserves supports financial resilience.
The continued accumulation of physical gold indicates reserve-portfolio diversification.
But neither development establishes an improvement in national capital productivity.
Scoring decision: Update reserve-stock and composition variables. No numerical China GVI upgrade.

III. UNITED STATES — BERKSHIRE DEMONSTRATES THE VALUE OF FINANCIAL FLEXIBILITY
Berkshire Hathaway’s November 7 results provide an important counterpoint to the financing pressures affecting selected AI infrastructure businesses.
Third-quarter operating earnings declined approximately 6% to $12.71 billion.
Net income fell 28%, partly reflecting lower investment gains and weaker insurance underwriting results.
Yet the underlying performance of several capital-intensive operating businesses was stronger.
BNSF’s quarterly net earnings increased approximately 19%.
Berkshire Hathaway Energy’s earnings attributable to Berkshire increased 8.3%.
Manufacturing businesses continued benefiting from industrial demand, including aerospace and power-generation applications.
Berkshire also repurchased approximately $9.6 billion of its shares during the quarter, exceeding the $4.8 billion repurchased during the first six months of 2026. 
The company’s financial position remains exceptional.
At September 30, its insurance and other businesses held approximately $352.3 billion in cash, cash equivalents and US Treasury bills, net of unsettled Treasury purchases.
This liquidity was available alongside substantial investments in operating companies and financial assets.
The amount is not Berkshire’s quarterly free cash flow, nor does it represent unrestricted capital available for investment without regard to insurance and operating obligations.
Nevertheless, it demonstrates considerable financial flexibility. 
An Important Industrial Signal
BNSF’s third-quarter freight volumes increased 3.9% year over year.
Consumer-product volumes increased 5.6%.
Agricultural and energy volumes rose 8.3%.
Industrial-product volumes increased 3.9%.
Coal volumes declined 8.4%. 
The figures provide evidence of positive activity across several important segments of the US economy.
But their significance for the GVI requires qualification.
BNSF’s performance represents one major railroad network, not a comprehensive national industrial-production measure.
Moreover, the increase in railway revenue partly reflected higher fuel surcharges and freight yields.
The relevant investment distinction is between growth in nominal revenue and growth in productive volumes and operating returns.
GVI Implication
Enterprise Transformation: Positive company-level industrial activity signal.
Smart Capital: Strong evidence of balance-sheet resilience and capital-deployment flexibility.
Scoring decision: Update the corporate and industrial monitoring register.No numerical US country-score change.

IV. THE FINANCIAL DIVIDE — LIQUIDITY VERSUS LEVERAGE
The latest evidence becomes more consequential when combined with the November 6 deterioration in AI-related credit conditions.
Reuters reported that SoftBank’s five-year credit-default-swap spread reached an intraday high of 466 basis points, its widest level since October 2022.
The widening reflected investor concerns about financing commitments, technological valuations and the timing of expected returns.
A CDS spread is a market price for credit protection, not a prediction that default will occur.
It nevertheless provides an important indicator of changing financing expectations. 
The comparison with Berkshire should not be interpreted as a direct assessment of the two companies’ investment quality.
Their business models, financing structures and risk exposures differ substantially.
But the contrast illustrates a broader financial principle.
When the cost of external capital increases, internally financed investment becomes more valuable.
Companies with substantial liquidity can preserve investment programmes, refinance selectively and potentially acquire assets at more attractive valuations.
Companies dependent on successive equity or debt financing rounds face greater sensitivity to valuation changes and market liquidity.
This has direct implications for the AI Supercycle.
The economic benefits of computing infrastructure may remain substantial while the financial viability of individual developers becomes more uncertain.
Demand for intelligence and the creditworthiness of the companies financing its infrastructure are separate investment questions.

VIII. SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS — THE LIQUIDITY PREMIUM
The most important financial implication of the latest evidence concerns how differences in financing capacity can alter competitive outcomes.
Three potential mechanisms deserve attention.
First, tighter external financing can increase the relative value of internally generated cash.
The financial advantage of liquidity grows when investment opportunities remain attractive but the cost of raising capital increases.
This can change acquisition economics and the distribution of future returns.
Second, financially stronger infrastructure operators may capture opportunities abandoned or delayed by more leveraged competitors.
The potential result is greater industry concentration.
However, this remains a conditional scenario. It requires evidence of sustained financing stress, delayed investment or actual asset transfers.
Third, liquidity can protect the continuity of productive investment.
Countries and companies able to maintain investment through periods of financial volatility may emerge with stronger productive capabilities.
This is particularly relevant to AI infrastructure, where interrupted construction, delayed electricity connections and changing procurement schedules can affect future investment returns.
The emerging transmission sequence is:
FINANCIAL RESILIENCE → INVESTMENT CONTINUITY → PRODUCTIVE CAPACITY → CASH FLOW → REINVESTMENT
The financial advantage becomes self-reinforcing when incremental returns on capital exceed the cost of sustaining investment.
But liquidity is not sufficient by itself.
Capital must still be allocated productively.
The true competitive advantage is not simply the possession of financial resources. It is the ability to deploy them at attractive returns when competitors cannot.

IX. NUMBERS TO WATCH
The next assessment should prioritise three financial transmission channels.
US inflation and interest rates: The October Consumer Price Index is scheduled for November 10. It will help establish whether stronger employment is likely to reinforce expectations of another Federal Reserve rate increase.China’s financial conditions: Monitor whether foreign-currency liquidity tightens following the November 15 reserve-requirement increase, and whether the effect spreads into corporate funding and investment.
AI infrastructure credit: Track CDS spreads, debt-issuance yields, refinancing requirements and changes in contracted data-centre capacity. The critical question is whether repricing begins affecting actual construction and deployment.
A further indicator is the pace of corporate capital deployment by cash-rich businesses.
Berkshire’s accelerated repurchases suggest greater willingness to deploy capital, but do not establish a broad market rotation.

X. FINANCIAL VERDICT
Main accelerator: Continued productive activity across selected US industrial businesses and the ability of financially resilient companies to sustain capital deployment.
Main decelerator: Elevated financing-risk premiums for selected AI-related investments and uncertainty about the timing of infrastructure returns.
Principal second-order risk: A widening financing divide that concentrates productive investment among a smaller group of strongly capitalised businesses.
Principal opportunity: Companies combining financial flexibility, durable operating cash flows and access to productive assets at attractive valuations.
THE BIG TAKE — WHAT COMES NEXT
November 8 adds an important dimension to the evolution of global economic velocity.
China’s reserve figures illustrate the difference between financial-asset accumulation and changes in market value.
Berkshire’s financial statements demonstrate the value of preserving substantial liquidity alongside productive operating businesses.
The AI credit-market correction reveals how quickly financing conditions can differentiate investment prospects.
The resulting investment thesis is increasingly clear.
The next phase of the AI Supercycle will depend not only on the speed of technological progress, but also on the financial resilience of the institutions and enterprises responsible for sustaining it.
For investors, the challenge is to identify where liquidity creates genuine investment options rather than simply accumulating on balance sheets.
For boards of directors, the priority is to preserve financial flexibility without sacrificing attractive long-term investments.
For policymakers, the imperative is to maintain conditions under which productive capital formation can continue despite market volatility.
VELOCITY INSIGHT
The next competitive advantage will belong to those who can invest when others must retreat—and generate returns that justify doing so.
In the Velocity Economy, liquidity creates the option. Capital productivity creates the value.

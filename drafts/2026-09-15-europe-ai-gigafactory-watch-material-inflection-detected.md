15 September 2026 | FDK

THE RACE IS MOVING FROM “WHO BUILDS THE DATA CENTER?” TO “WHO OWNS THE INTELLIGENCE STACK INSIDE IT?”

Three developments since the previous check materially deepen the European Gigafactory thesis. The most important is not a new site. It is the emergence of a European semiconductor-capital stack around the AI Factory network.

Dutch AI-chip company Axelera AI said today that it has signed multiple contracts to supply chips to AI factories while launching its second-generation Europa accelerator. The chips will be integrated into selected Dell and Supermicro systems; Axelera says it now has more than 600 customers, has signed contracts worth tens of millions of euros and is pursuing a potential sales pipeline of up to $1.5 billion. Crucially for Europe, Axelera is already working with Dell and E4 as a supplier to the EU-backed IT4LIA AI Factory in Italy and MeluXina in Luxembourg. 

At the same time, another Eindhoven company, EUCLYD, announced a €200+ million Series A to develop an alternative AI-compute architecture spanning processors, memory and data-center systems. The round is co-led by Samsung, Somerset Capital Partners, the EQT-managed Scaleup Europe Fund and Innovation Industries, with participation from Denmark’s EIFO, imec.xpand and others. Former ASML CEO Peter Wennink becomes chairman. 

These are not merely semiconductor stories. They alter the economics of the European Gigafactory race.

THE FIRST SECOND-ORDER EFFECT: EUROPE CAN BUILD GIGAFACTORIES AND STILL LOSE MOST OF THE VALUE

Until now our framework has concentrated on:

CAPITAL → POWER → GRID → PERMIT → CONSTRUCTION → OPERATING MW

Today another layer becomes unavoidable:

OPERATING MW → SILICON → SYSTEMS → SOFTWARE → MODELS → TOKENS

Europe could succeed at constructing enormous AI campuses and still transfer much of the economic rent abroad through imported accelerators, networking, cloud stacks and models.

That creates two radically different forms of sovereignty.

Infrastructure sovereignty: the data center sits in Europe.

Economic sovereignty: Europe captures a meaningful share of the hardware, software, intellectual property and cash flows generated inside it.

The second is much harder.

Axelera and EUCLYD matter because they attack that second problem.

The new strategic equation becomes:

BANKABLE MW × EUROPEAN SILICON × EUROPEAN SOFTWARE × INDUSTRIAL OFFTAKE = EUROPEAN AI VALUE CAPTURE

This is a considerably higher bar than simply winning seven EuroHPC Gigafactory sites.

THE SECOND EFFECT: AI FACTORIES ARE BECOMING INDUSTRIAL POLICY THROUGH PROCUREMENT

IT4LIA is particularly significant.

If European AI Factories become early customers for emerging European semiconductor companies, public infrastructure performs two functions simultaneously:

**Compute infrastructure

• anchor customer for European technology.**

That changes the causality chain:

PUBLIC AI FACTORY PROCUREMENT
→ REFERENCE INSTALLATION
→ TECHNOLOGY VALIDATION
→ LOWER CUSTOMER-RISK PERCEPTION
→ PRIVATE ORDERS
→ SCALE
→ LOWER UNIT COST
→ STRONGER EUROPEAN CHIP ECOSYSTEM

This is one of the most important second-order implications of the entire EuroHPC architecture.

Europe does not necessarily have to reproduce Nvidia overnight.

It can use its €30+ billion Gigafactory programme and its network of AI Factories to create demand pull for European alternatives. The formal Gigafactory programme itself explicitly allows projects to combine processors, cloud stacks, networking and data-center infrastructure, supported by up to €10 billion of public funding designed to mobilise at least €20 billion of private capital. 

For Italy, therefore, IT4LIA should be viewed not merely as compute capacity in Bologna. It can become a European semiconductor commercialization platform.

That is much more strategically important.

THE THIRD EFFECT: INFERENCE MAY BECOME EUROPE’S ENTRY POINT

Europe remains far behind the United States in frontier-model training infrastructure.But the economics of inference are different.

Training favors gigantic concentrated clusters and enormous upfront capital. Inference increasingly rewards:

energy efficiency + latency + specialization + deployment close to industry + predictable operating costs.

That plays much more directly into European strengths:

**industrial customers

• telecommunications networks
• automotive
• robotics
• manufacturing
• regulated enterprises
• sovereign workloads.**

Axelera’s Europa architecture targets heavier enterprise workloads, while its planned Titania architecture is intended for data centers and supercomputers. EUCLYD is likewise explicitly attacking inference economics through processor-memory and system-level optimization. 

This suggests a potentially important strategic asymmetry:

America may dominate frontier training while Europe builds competitive advantage in industrial inference.

That would fit directly into our Industrial AI thesis.

The highest-value European opportunity may not be recreating every American frontier-model laboratory.

It may be turning European factories, transport systems, banks, utilities, telecom networks and machines into enormous distributed inference markets.

SPAIN: THE REGULATORY DEBATE IS BECOMING MORE TECHNICAL — AND THEREFORE MORE SOLVABLE

There is also a meaningful Spanish development today.

APECDATA has formally proposed modifications to the Spanish data-center decree, including raising the threshold for certain obligations from 1 MW to 5 MW, replacing hourly renewable matching with an annual-equivalent methodology and strengthening legal certainty around sanctions and overlapping regulation. 

This matters because the Spanish conflict may now be moving from:

Government versus industry

toward:

redesign of the bankability architecture.

That is a much healthier phase.

The critical issue is no longer whether Spain should impose environmental and sovereignty requirements. It is whether those requirements can achieve their objectives without destroying Capacity Conversion Velocity.

If the government moves toward technologically feasible matching, clearer thresholds and differentiated obligations according to facility scale, Spain could potentially escape the Regulatory Trap we identified last week.

That remains unresolved—but today’s proposals provide the beginnings of a bridge.

THE FOURTH EFFECT: EUROPE’S AI RACE IS BECOMING A CAPITAL-MARKETS PROJECT

Christine Lagarde has now supplied perhaps the strongest macro-financial framing yet.

The ECB President said that Europe currently hosts only about 5% of global AI computing capacity, compared with roughly 75% in the United States. The Commission’s baseline implies that Europe’s data-center capacity shortfall could expand from around 3 GW in 2025 to approximately 20 GW by 2036. Using a high-end estimate for GPU-dense infrastructure, the ECB calculates that eliminating a 19-GW gap could cost as much as €600 billion including servers and chips. 

Even more important, Lagarde explicitly connects AI to European capital-market architecture. Euro-area companies are expected to devote roughly 10% of investment to AI in 2026, while AI-related borrowing represented around one quarter of the growth in corporate credit in Q1 2026. She argues that Europe cannot finance the coming infrastructure buildout through bank lending alone; it will require equity, bonds, private credit and deeper capital markets. 

This validates a central Next Economy thesis:

THE AI SUPERCYCLE IS BECOMING THE PROJECT AROUND WHICH EUROPE MAY HAVE TO BUILD ITS CAPITAL MARKETS.

That is potentially historic.

The AI Gigafactory race is no longer a digital-policy programme.

It is becoming simultaneously:

industrial policy + energy policy + semiconductor policy + banking policy + capital-markets policy.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN: FROM BANKABLE MW TO BANKABLE TOKENS

Our previous metric was Bankable MW.

Today I would extend it.

A Gigafactory does not ultimately produce megawatts.

It converts megawatts into intelligence.

The economically relevant chain is:

CAPITAL→ POWER
→ ACCELERATED COMPUTING
→ UTILIZATION
→ TOKENS / INFERENCE
→ INDUSTRIAL APPLICATIONS
→ PRODUCTIVITY
→ CASH FLOW

Therefore the next metric should become:

BANKABLE INTELLIGENCE CAPACITY

A European project should increasingly be evaluated on four questions simultaneously:

Can it be financed?
Can it be powered?
Can it source competitive silicon?
Can it sell enough intelligence to generate attractive returns?

The first two determine whether the facility gets built.

The latter two determine whether Europe captures the economics.

COUNTRY IMPLICATIONS

Italy moves up strategically today. IT4LIA’s importance rises because it is not only an AI Factory but potentially an anchor market for European semiconductor suppliers such as Axelera. That creates a link between Bologna’s compute ecosystem and European semiconductor industrial policy. 

Spain remains a high-potential/high-policy-risk market, but today’s APECDATA proposals show that the regulatory conflict may still be converted into a workable architecture. Spain also possesses Semidynamics, one of the European inference-chip competitors identified alongside Axelera and EUCLYD—making it especially important that Spain connect its domestic semiconductor capability with its Gigafactory project rather than treat the two agendas separately. 

France and Germany have no comparably material new site, financing or grid announcement today, but the strategic implication is clear: their Gigafactory consortia should begin thinking about European silicon procurement as part of industrial sovereignty, not merely data-center construction.

The Nordics retain the strongest power proposition, but energy efficiency at the accelerator level is now another competitive variable. The cheapest megawatt matters; so does how many economically valuable inference tokens can be produced from that megawatt.

THE VELOCITY INSIGHT

The European Gigafactory race has entered another stage.

The first race was for sites.

The second was for power.

The third was for capital.

Today we can see the fourth beginning:

THE RACE FOR VALUE CAPTURE INSIDE THE AI FACTORY.

Axelera’s AI Factory contracts and EUCLYD’s €200+ million financing suggest that European capital is beginning to form around the semiconductor layer at precisely the moment Europe is preparing tens of billions of euros of sovereign AI infrastructure.

That creates a powerful circular chain:

EUROPEAN AI FACTORIES
→ DEMAND FOR EUROPEAN SILICON
→ SEMICONDUCTOR SCALE-UPS
→ MORE PRIVATE CAPITAL
→ LOWER-COST / MORE EFFICIENT INFERENCE
→ MORE INDUSTRIAL AI ADOPTION
→ HIGHER AI-FACTORY UTILIZATION
→ MORE BANKABLE GIGAFACTORIES

If Europe can make that chain work, Gigafactories cease to be merely an infrastructure catch-up programme.

They become the demand engine for an entire European AI industrial stack.

That would be a much more consequential outcome.

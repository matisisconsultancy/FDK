AI is no longer only a technology capex cycle. It is becoming a global credit cycle — and that will decide who can keep scaling when the hurdle rate stays above 5%.
Three developments overnight make the transition unusually visible.
SpaceX is reportedly seeking $40 billion of financing, led by Apollo, to acquire Nvidia chips — around $10 billion in bank loans and $30 billion in investment-grade debt. Black Hills will invest $1.8 billion in new generation capacity to power a Google data centre in Wyoming. And Marvell has lifted its fiscal-2028 revenue forecast to around $20 billion as hyperscalers accelerate custom-chip investment. 
Meanwhile, the U.S. 10-year is back around 5.3%, India has raised rates for the first time in almost four years, Brent is again above $101, and Asian equities are weaker despite fresh records on Wall Street. 
The contradiction is becoming the regime.
AI investment is accelerating precisely as the cost of financing acceleration remains structurally high.
THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
When AI infrastructure becomes an asset class, does scale become safer — or does leverage become the next systemic risk?
For three years the decisive question was access to compute.
The next one is harder:
Who can finance compute, power and infrastructure at scale — and still earn above the cost of capital?
The Great Split is moving again.
From:
AI versus non-AI
to:
self-financing AI versus externally financed AI.

FIVE SIGNALS SHAPING THE NEXT ECONOMY
1 | THE AI CREDIT MARKET IS MOVING INTO INVESTMENT GRADE
REPORTED, NOT YET COMPANY-CONFIRMED. Reuters reported, citing the Financial Times, that SpaceX is seeking around $40 billion to fund Nvidia chip purchases: roughly $10 billion of bank loans and $30 billion of investment-grade debt. Morgan Stanley estimates AI infrastructure may require $1.5 trillion of external financing by 2028. 
FDK READ.
This is the structural shift.
AI infrastructure is moving from:
corporate capex → structured finance → bank lending → bond markets
Once that happens, the AI cycle begins to transmit directly into credit spreads, duration, refinancing risk and institutional fixed-income portfolios.
That expands the opportunity.
It also expands the vulnerability.
The key variable is no longer simply demand for Nvidia chips.
It is the ability of the borrower to convert those chips into cash flow before the debt matures.
THE NEXT AI WARNING SIGNAL MAY APPEAR IN CREDIT BEFORE IT APPEARS IN EQUITIES.


⸻


2 | POWER IS BECOMING THE MOST BANKABLE BOTTLENECK
VERIFIED. Black Hills plans $1.8 billion of investment between 2027 and 2029 to build capacity for Google’s proposed Cheyenne data centre. It will construct 564 MW of new generation capacity, manage a broader 2.7-GW resource mix, and expects Google to cover the full cost of electricity supply. 
Blackstone-backed AirTrunk also announced an additional $1 billion investment in its Japan hyperscale campus, taking capacity beyond 300 MW to support AI workloads. 
FDK READ.
The important word is not electricity.
It is contracted electricity.
The physical AI stack is becoming financeable because long-duration commitments are converting megawatts into predictable cash flows.
That changes capital allocation.The scarcity premium moves toward:
generation
grid access
microgrids
nuclear
gas backup
storage
transmission
The physical infrastructure around AI increasingly has something the model layer often does not:
visible duration of revenues.
That makes power one of the most investable parts of the AI supercycle.

3 | CUSTOM SILICON IS BECOMING THE SECOND GREAT SEMICONDUCTOR TRADE
VERIFIED. Marvell raised its fiscal-2028 revenue forecast to around $20 billion, versus analyst expectations of $18.2 billion. It now expects roughly $12 billion of custom-chip revenue in fiscal 2029 and sees 2031 revenue between $70 billion and $90 billion. Its disclosed Google contract could generate as much as $120 billion through 2033 if milestones are achieved. Marvell shares rose about 6% and Broadcom roughly 4%. 
FDK READ.
The AI semiconductor market is broadening.
The first phase concentrated economic capture around general-purpose accelerators.
The next one increasingly includes:
custom ASICs
networking
optical interconnects
HBM
power management
rack-scale architecture
Hyperscalers want more control over economics and power consumption.
That does not end Nvidia’s dominance.
It creates a parallel market for specialization.
The investment implication is straightforward:
the semiconductor trade is moving from one bottleneck to an ecosystem of bottlenecks.


⸻


4 | THE GLOBAL HURDLE RATE IS SPREADING BEYOND THE FED
VERIFIED. India raised its repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.5%, its first increase in nearly four years, and shifted its stance from neutral to “calibrated tightening.” The RBI cited broader inflation pressures while raising its growth forecast to 7.1%. India’s 10-year yield moved to 7.2655% after the decision. 
In the U.S., the 10-year Treasury remains around 5.3% despite markets cutting the probability of an October Fed hike to around 20%. Today’s $39 billion 10-year Treasury auction and publication of the September Fed minutes will test whether investors distinguish between a slower Fed and a structurally higher term premium. 
FDK READ.
The important monetary story is no longer synchronization.
It is global capital scarcity.
Energy, defence, AI infrastructure, sovereign deficits and industrial policy are all competing for the same pools of capital.
That puts upward pressure on long-term rates even when central banks hesitate.
The consequence:
MONETARY RELIEF DOES NOT GUARANTEE CHEAP CAPITAL.
That distinction will shape valuation, leverage and M&A.


⸻


5 | EUROPE IS DISCOVERING THAT CREDIBILITY IS A FINANCIAL ASSET
VERIFIED. French 10-year yields fell more than 11 basis points on Tuesday after Marine Le Pen outlined spending cuts, narrowing the France–Germany 10-year spread from almost 160 basis points last week to around 132 bps. The euro recovered toward $1.125. 
Yet France’s borrowing costs remain near multi-decade highs, and market participants continue to warn that fiscal space has narrowed materially. 
At the same time, Brussels is considering a broad levy on large corporations — including U.S. technology companies — as it searches for new EU revenues without directly imposing a digital-services tax. 
FDK READ.
Europe’s industrial ambition now meets its funding constraint.
AI, defence, rail, grids and energy transition all require scale.
But sovereign credibility determines the price at which that scale can be financed.
The new European policy equation is becoming:
industrial ambition + fiscal credibility + capital-market depth.
Miss one, and the other two become harder.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN
The common denominator across today’s headlines is not AI.
It is capital formation.
SpaceX reportedly wants $40 billion of debt for chips.
Utilities are investing billions to deliver contracted power.
Private infrastructure capital is funding hyperscale campuses.
Semiconductor firms are expanding custom silicon.
Central banks from Washington to Mumbai are defending credibility against persistent inflation.And financial institutions are beginning to use AI not only to create products, but to compress their own cost structures: HSBC is reportedly considering deep reductions in UK wealth-management staffing as it accelerates AI adoption, while FICO has announced a roughly 15% workforce reduction as part of an AI-driven restructuring. 
The first-order AI effect was investment.
The second-order effect is reallocation:
capital away from labour-intensive processes
capital toward compute
capital toward power
capital toward proprietary infrastructure
capital toward firms able to finance the transition
That is why The Great Split is widening faster than the headline growth numbers suggest.

BEFORE THE CLOSE
Investors: watch today’s $39 billion U.S. 10-year auction. If demand is weak and yields push decisively back above recent highs, the capital-cost thesis strengthens even if equities remain firm. Also watch whether Marvell and Broadcom continue outperforming secondary semiconductor names: that would reinforce the shift toward custom silicon and contracted economics. 
Executives: focus less on nominal AI budgets and more on financing architecture. The winning configuration increasingly combines secured power, contracted demand, proprietary silicon and low-cost funding.
Boards: ask a harder question of every major AI investment: is this creating a productive asset with visible cash flows — or merely converting cash into depreciating compute?
Policymakers: watch the interaction between the Fed minutes, Treasury auction, India’s tightening and European sovereign spreads. If long rates remain high despite reduced near-term central-bank tightening expectations, the world is moving toward a structurally higher cost of capital.
Energy markets: Brent around $101 remains a material macro variable. A Gulf of Mexico storm and renewed Saudi-Houthi tension are adding supply risk even as Middle East exports recover.

FINAL TAKE — THE GREAT SPLIT MOVES INTO CREDIT
The AI supercycle began as an equity story.
It is becoming a bond-market story.
That matters because debt imposes something equity narratives do not:
a timetable.
Interest must be paid.
Assets must be utilized.
Cash flows must materialize.
Debt must refinance.
The strongest AI platforms will use investment-grade capital to secure chips, power and infrastructure before competitors can.
The weakest will discover that demand is not enough if the financing structure cannot survive the cycle.
The first AI race was about intelligence.
The second was about compute.
The third is becoming about capital structure.
And that may prove the most decisive race of all.
When AI enters the bond market, velocity acquires a maturity date.

THE ACCESS PREMIUM
The Next Economy is discovering a new scarcity. Intelligence may be becoming more abundant, but assured access to the systems that industrialize intelligence is becoming more expensive: access to energy routes, accelerated computing, frontier models, secure data, advanced lithography and capital.
At the European morning cut, Brent was trading around $107.05/bbl, WTI near $102.92, the U.S. 10-year Treasury at roughly 4.99%, and markets were assigning about a 93% probability to a Federal Reserve rate increase tomorrow. The dollar index was near a two-week high at 99.55. Japan’s 10-year government bond yield was back at 3%, with the Bank of Japan widely expected to raise its policy rate to 1.25% on Friday. 
Meanwhile, only four commodity vessels crossed the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, down from ten on Sunday and approximately 125 large commercial vessels per day before the Iran war. Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline remains disrupted. 
And something equally consequential is occurring inside AI.
The industry’s leaders are debating whether the frontier should slow. Investors have begun repricing semiconductors. But the infrastructure beneath AI continues expanding: ASML’s existing EUV systems are essentially sold out through 2027, customers are committing to its new $400 million High-NA machines, and SoftBank-backed SB Energy is taking the data-center-plus-power model into public capital markets. 
Yesterday we identified The Three Speed Limits:
Energy.
Capital.
Governance.
This morning the deeper implication becomes visible.
When systems hit speed limits, control of access becomes valuable.
That is today’s Velocity Spread.

THE FIVE MOST IMPORTANT OVERNIGHT SIGNALS
1. HORMUZ HAS TURNED ENERGY SCARCITY INTO AN ACCESS PROBLEM
Verified overnight: only four commodity vessels crossed the Strait of Hormuz on Monday. Two tankers entered—and both were travelling in ballast. Traffic through Bab el-Mandeb also fell to 21 vessels from 28 the previous day. Before the war, Hormuz handled approximately 125 major vessels daily and around 20% of global crude oil and LNG flows. 
At the same time Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline remains disrupted.
The kingdom has recently been using the pipeline to move around 4 million barrels per day to Yanbu, bypassing Hormuz. Traders warn that continued disruption could remove as much as 4% of global supply from normal export channels. 
The market therefore needs a new concept.
ACCESSIBLE SUPPLY
Nominal production capacity is no longer enough.
Oil must be:
produced,
transported,
insured,
protected,
loaded,
and delivered.
That yields the new chain:
Energy Capacity
− Logistics Risk
− Security Risk
= Accessible Energy Supply
FDK interpretation: the premium attached to pipelines, LNG terminals, storage, alternative ports, shipping security and diversified corridors should rise structurally.
The scarcity is increasingly the route, not the resource.

2. THE WORLD’S HURDLE RATE IS NOW 5%
The Federal Reserve begins its two-day policy meeting today.
Markets now price approximately a 93% probability of a rate increase tomorrow, according to CME data cited by Reuters. A Reuters survey found that 86 of 101 economists expect the Fed to raise rates by 25 basis points to 3.75–4.00%, with a majority of respondents expecting at least one further increase by the end of March. 
Yesterday the U.S. 10-year Treasury briefly crossed 5% for the first time since 2023.
Germany’s 10-year Bund climbed above 3.51%, its highest level since 2009.
Japan’s 10-year yield is again around 3%. 
This produces a powerful global causality chain:
Energy Inflation ↑
→ Inflation Expectations ↑
→ Fed ↑
→ BOJ ↑
→ Sovereign Yields ↑
→ WACC ↑
→ Capital Selectivity ↑
For AI this matters enormously.
The industry is attempting perhaps the largest capital-formation cycle since the build-out of electricity and telecommunications precisely when the risk-free rate is becoming more expensive.
The new corporate test is therefore:
RETURN ON INTELLIGENCE > 5% + RISK PREMIUM
Anything below that will increasingly struggle for capital.

3. THE AI SELLOFF IS NOT YET AN AI INFRASTRUCTURE SLOWDOWN
Monday produced a violent market response to the calls from AI industry leaders for slower frontier development.
Nvidia fell 3.4%.
Micron lost more than 5%.
Broadcom and AMD each declined more than 4%.
The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 5.9%. 
Yet the physical evidence is telling a different story.
ASML says its existing approximately $200 million EUV machines are essentially sold out through 2027.
TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix have committed to introducing ASML’s new $400 million High-NA systems, capable of printing features roughly 40% smaller than existing EUV equipment.
ASML held an estimated 94% of the lithography market in 2025 and remains effectively the sole supplier of advanced EUV lithography. 
JPMorgan says ASML is examining ways to produce more than 110 EUV systems in 2028, after targeting at least 80 in 2027. 
That distinction matters.
MODEL VELOCITY CAN SLOW WHILE INFRASTRUCTURE VELOCITY CONTINUES.
Training may become more disciplined.But inference, deployment, sovereign AI, industrial AI and enterprise adoption can continue expanding.
FDK interpretation: the market may be prematurely equating a possible slowdown in frontier-model capability growth with a slowdown in accelerated-computing infrastructure.
Those are no longer the same trade.

4. ENTERPRISE AI HAS DISCOVERED THE VALUE OF TRUSTED ACCESS
Another important development is receiving less attention.
Palantir, Nvidia and Booz Allen are reportedly reconsidering or restricting use of frontier AI models because of concerns about proprietary data and intellectual-property protection.
Palantir has sought irrevocable zero-data-retention guarantees.
Nvidia reportedly limits Anthropic model usage for sensitive work and relies on its own Nemotron models internally.
Microsoft is using the concern commercially by promoting isolated cloud environments and proprietary AI systems. 
This signals another migration of the scarcity premium.
Yesterday businesses asked:
Which model is smartest?
Increasingly they will ask:
Which model can I trust with my most valuable information?
That makes enterprise AI a different market.
Its competitive variables become:
Model capability.
Security.
Data residency.
Retention policy.
Encryption.
Sovereignty.
Auditability.
Model portability.
Intelligence without trusted access cannot become industrial intelligence.
FDK interpretation: secure inference could become one of the highest-value layers in the enterprise AI stack.

5. EUROPE HAS FINALLY IDENTIFIED AI DEPENDENCY AS A MACROECONOMIC RISK
Christine Lagarde’s speech in Vienna yesterday deserves to be treated as more than another call for European digital sovereignty.
She made the issue macroeconomic.
Europe, she argued, risks becoming dependent on external AI systems across border control, hospitals, banks, transportation and public administration.
Europe’s existing data-center capacity deficit could become more than six times larger within a decade under current trends.
Yet rapid AI adoption could raise European productivity by as much as 4% over ten years. 
She also identified a financial consequence rarely discussed:
U.S. technology companies’ enormous investment requirements are increasingly pushing them into European debt markets, potentially raising borrowing costs for European companies.
European pension funds, meanwhile, own substantial U.S. technology exposure, meaning an American AI correction can directly affect European household savings. 
This is extremely consequential.
Europe is exposed twice:
**Technology Dependence
Financial Dependence**
But Europe simultaneously controls one of the most important chokepoints in the entire AI industrial system:
ASML.
That creates both vulnerability and strategic leverage.

FIVE HIDDEN PATTERNS BEYOND THE NEWS
First — scarcity is migrating from intelligence to access
Models proliferate.
Intelligence prices decline.
But the number of entities capable of providing:
advanced lithography,
secure power,
trusted models,
sovereign data environments,
and long-duration capital
remains far smaller.
Scarcity rents therefore migrate from the model toward the gateway.

Second — the AI Supercycle is splitting into two cycles

The other is the industrialization cycle:
Data centers.
Inference.
Power.
Networking.
Memory.
Fiber.
Enterprise deployment.
That second cycle may continue even if the first decelerates.
This creates a crucial Great Split:
MODEL VELOCITY vs. INFRASTRUCTURE VELOCITY

Third — security is becoming part of product quality
In enterprise AI, a model that is 5% smarter but cannot guarantee data protection may be economically inferior to a less powerful model with a secure architecture.
The same principle applies to infrastructure.
An electricity connection without resilience.
A pipeline without protection.
A cloud without data sovereignty.
Capacity without security increasingly has a discount attached to it.


⸻Fourth — AI leverage is becoming a systemic financial variable
The BIS warned yesterday that the AI-driven market rally is showing increasing vulnerability as leverage rises.
Aggregate borrowing by technology companies in private credit grew from about $22 billion in 2010 to more than $1 trillion by 2025, representing roughly 44% of private credit. Total outstanding loans involving technology companies were close to $2.5 trillion.
The BIS specifically highlighted opaque, off-balance-sheet and circular financing structures. 
This is highly consequential.
AI is no longer simply an equity-market story.
It is becoming a credit-market story.

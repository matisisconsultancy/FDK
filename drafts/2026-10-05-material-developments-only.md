THE BIG TAKE

Three developments matter today, and together they sharpen the race.

Brussels has reset the clock. Finland has raised the power-and-capital benchmark. Italy has gained political continuity—but still lacks the hard infrastructure proof.

The competition is moving from consortium announcements to a much tougher test:

SITE + POWER + CAPITAL + OFFTAKE + EXECUTION

1 | EU — THE TENDER CLOCK HAS MOVED TO 3 DECEMBER

EuroHPC now lists 3 December 2026 at 17:00 CET as the deadline for the AI Gigafactory call. The facilities must demonstrate not only massive processor scale but also adequate power infrastructure, energy efficiency, water efficiency and circularity. 

This matters because the next eight weeks are no longer about political positioning. They are about converting national coalitions into bankable bids.

For Italy, Spain, France, Germany and the Nordics, the burden of proof is now explicit:

committed equity
→ secured MW
→ development-ready site
→ technology architecture
→ anchor demand
→ credible delivery timetable

Capital-formation implication: the tender is increasingly selecting infrastructure platforms, not technology manifestos.

Board implication: companies joining a consortium now need to know whether they are shareholders, customers, power providers, lenders or technology suppliers. Ambiguous participation will not create a financeable asset.

⸻

2 | FINLAND — THE NORDICS JUST RAISED THE BAR

atNorth today announced FIN05 in Salo, Finland: a 28.6-hectare campus with 75 MW secured for Phase 1, a path to 230 MW gross power, connection through a Fingrid substation, and approximately €2 billion of atNorth investment. The company estimates that customer-owned servers and installation could add as much as €10 billion more. Long-term PPAs, grid-flexibility solutions and heat reuse are part of the design. 

The first phase is planned around 60 MW of IT capacity, with power availability targeted for Q3 2028. 

This is consequential even though atNorth has not disclosed FIN05 as an official EuroHPC Gigafactory bid.

Why? Because Finland is demonstrating what the winning physical architecture may look like:

POWER FIRST. GRID FIRST. SITE FIRST. CAPITAL SECOND. COMPUTE FOLLOWS.

The contrast with Southern Europe is important.

Italy and Spain are still heavily focused on consortium architecture.

The Nordics are increasingly presenting development-ready power infrastructure.

And in AI infrastructure, secured megawatts can become more valuable than announced billions.

Second-order effect

If customer hardware ultimately approaches even a fraction of atNorth’s estimated €10 billion potential, the capital intensity around the Salo campus could rival or exceed several national Gigafactory programmes.

That makes the Nordics a serious competitor not simply for data centers, but for global AI capital formation.

Investor takeaway: follow secured power and customer CAPEX, not national headlines.

CEO takeaway: Nordic locations increasingly offer an alternative to building proprietary AI campuses in constrained continental grids.

Policy takeaway: the Nordic competitive advantage is becoming energy infrastructure converted directly into digital investment.

⸻

3 | ITALY — THE PROJECT IS BECOMING A NATIONAL PROJECT

Italy’s AI Gigafactory bid today received explicit support from PD Senate leader Francesco Boccia, creating unusual majority-opposition convergence around the project. The emerging coalition includes Eni, Enel, Leonardo, Saipem, Snam and Terna alongside research institutions, with Palazzo Chigi coordinating the bid. 

This matters more than it first appears.

Large AI infrastructure has a 10–20 year economic life. Political continuity therefore affects permitting, public procurement, sovereign support and ultimately the cost of capital.

Cross-party support can reduce policy-duration risk.

But Italy should not overread the signal.

Political consensus does not solve the remaining gaps:

no final siteno disclosed secured MW
no finalized capital structure
no contracted compute offtake
no publicly defined ownership architecture

So today’s development strengthens Italy politically—but does not yet strengthen it financially.

That distinction is critical.

WHAT CHANGED IN THE COMPETITIVE RANKING

FRANCE — still leads on stack integration

Power, Bull, cloud, telecoms, enterprise AI and infrastructure capital remain the strongest vertically integrated model.

SPAIN — still leads on formal financial architecture

SETT + Santander + ACS + Telefónica remains the cleanest project-finance structure.

ITALY — political risk improved today

But Italy must now move from coalition-building to site, MW, equity and customers.

NORDICS — today’s biggest upward revision

Finland’s Salo announcement strengthens the Nordic proposition materially because it shows secured power + grid connection + multibillion-euro capital deployment in one project.

GERMANY — no equally consequential new change identified today

Its central strategic question remains whether major German telecom/infrastructure players convert existing AI-factory activity into a fully committed EuroHPC Gigafactory platform.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. The race has entered its execution phase.
The new 3 December deadline puts a premium on projects that can prove physical readiness rather than political ambition. 

2. Power is becoming the scarce strategic asset.
Finland’s 75 MW secured immediately is more consequential than another unsecured multibillion-euro announcement.

3. The Nordics are emerging as Europe’s AI power reservoir.
Cheap low-carbon electricity, cooler climate, grid access and large development-ready sites can attract international compute capital at scale.

4. Italy gained political durability—but now must show financial durability.
The next credible Italian announcement must concern site, power, equity or offtake, not another consortium member.

5. The winning European Gigafactory model is becoming clear:

SECURED POWER → CONTRACTED COMPUTE → BANKABLE CASH FLOW → LOWER WACC → MORE CAPITAL → MORE COMPUTE

Europe’s AI race is no longer about who can announce the biggest Gigafactory.

It is about who can turn megawatts into bankable intelligence fastest.

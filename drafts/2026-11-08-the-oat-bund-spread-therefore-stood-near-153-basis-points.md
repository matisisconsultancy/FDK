The Governing Council acknowledged that energy prices were increasing inflation risks while weakening growth prospects.
It also recognized that financial conditions had tightened.
The ECB reaffirmed its commitment to its 2% medium-term inflation objective and a data-dependent policy framework. 
The policy dilemma remains substantial.
Higher interest rates can help contain inflation expectations.
But they can also increase financing costs.
Lower rates could support borrowing and investment.
But premature easing could weaken confidence in price stability.
The ECB must distinguish among monetary conditions, legitimate sovereign-risk differentiation and disorderly financial-market dysfunction.
The Transmission Protection Instrument can address unwarranted dynamics that threaten monetary transmission under applicable conditions.
It is not an unconditional mechanism for suppressing sovereign-risk premiums.
The Institutional Challenge
Completing banking integration remains important.
Greater cross-border liquidity mobility could improve the allocation of savings.
Stronger common safeguards could reduce financial fragmentation.
But the objective should not be to weaken prudential resilience.
Financial integration should improve the availability and efficiency of productive capital without disguising sovereign risks or increasing systemic leverage.
The ECB can preserve monetary stability.
It cannot independently guarantee fiscal credibility.
Nor can it determine whether every AI facility, railway or electricity-network investment generates sustainable returns.
Those outcomes depend on national governments, European institutions and private investors.

2008 demonstrated how financial institutions can transmit severe economic stress through funding and credit contraction.

An economy can preserve financial-market access while facing increasingly expensive requirements to maintain its infrastructure, technological competitiveness and productive resilience.
Financial stability is necessary.
But preserving productive capacity under disruption is becoming equally important.
The economic challenge is to ensure that resilience investment strengthens future growth rather than merely absorbing resources.

How the Battle for Control of Italian Finance Is Reshaping the Architecture of European Capital
October 11, 2026 | Weekend Intelligence Edition | FDK
THE DECISIVE SHIFT | A NEW FINANCIAL PARADIGM
What began as a battle to reshape the hierarchy of Italian banking is becoming a contest for European financial power.
Intesa is targeting MPS; Crédit Agricole has rejected the offer concerning its stake in Banco BPM; UniCredit is advancing its Commerzbank transaction. Generali and Banca Generali are becoming increasingly important in the competition for wealth management and long-term savings.
The first-order effect is the redistribution of banking assets. The second-order effect is the redistribution of European financial power.
1 | WHAT HAS CHANGED IN THE LAST 24 HOURS
According to weekend reports, ISS has recommended that Mediobanca shareholders approve the merger with MPS, acknowledging its industrial rationale while expressing reservations about the consideration offered and the uncertainty surrounding Intesa’s bid.
The recommendation concerns Mediobanca, not the entirety of Lovaglio’s plan submitted to MPS shareholders.
On October 9, UniCredit formally notified the European Commission of its proposed Commerzbank transaction, with a preliminary antitrust deadline of November 16.
The Italian press has also reported possible UniCredit interest in Banca Generali. No offer has been announced.
MPS’s defensive strategy could fail without invalidating every element of its industrial strategy.
2 | THE DECISIVE QUESTION: MPS’S DEFENCE
MPS’s strategy faces two obstacles: shareholder approval and acquisition execution.
Delfin has committed approximately 17.6% of MPS in support of Intesa; Caltagirone has declared opposition to the extraordinary resolutions. Crédit Agricole has refused to tender its 29.3% stake in Banco BPM.
MPS’s extraordinary resolutions require two-thirds of the votes cast. Caltagirone’s reported stakes of 10.26% and 13.49%, disclosed in different contexts, are not automatically equivalent in terms of voting rights exercisable on October 29.
Lovaglio must demonstrate that the independent strategy is worth more than Intesa’s offer without relying on transactions that have now become less executable.
3 | THE STRATEGIC MAP
Intesa: Domestic scale, antitrust remedies and integration returns.
MPS–Mediobanca: Separate shareholder meetings and a reassessment of the strategic plan.
UniCredit–Commerzbank: EU regulatory review and German political resistance.
Crédit Agricole–Banco BPM: Alternative ownership structures.
Generali–Banca Generali: Governance and the distribution of savings.
Unipol–BPER: Planned acquisition of 635 MPS branches, with financing and integration costs still to be assessed.
Intesa is pursuing domestic scale. UniCredit is pursuing Continental Scale. Crédit Agricole is preserving optionality. Generali represents long-duration savings.

5 | INVESTMENT HIERARCHY
Continental Integration — UniCredit: Diversification, synergies, regulatory approvals and incremental returns.
Domestic Consolidation — Intesa: Final transaction perimeter and integration costs.
Executable Redistribution — Unipol–BPER: Branch acquisition conditional on financing and execution.
Strategic Optionality — Banco BPM and Banca Generali: Alternative transaction scenarios, not confirmed acquisitions.Long-Duration Savings — Generali: Insurance profitability and asset management.
The decisive criterion is return on capital after accounting for all costs and regulatory constraints.
6 | THREE SCENARIOS
Scenario I — Intesa Advances
MPS shareholders reject the defensive resolutions. Intesa proceeds, subject to the remaining conditions. Alternative strategic options emerge for Banco BPM and other assets.
Direction: Gaining momentum.
Scenario II — Mediobanca Integrates, but the Defence Remains Incomplete
Separate shareholder meetings produce different outcomes, increasing uncertainty over the final transaction perimeter.
Direction: More relevant following the ISS developments.
Scenario III — MPS Revises Its Independent Strategy
Lovaglio proposes a narrower strategic plan, subject to shareholder votes, counterparties and regulatory approvals.
Direction: Constrained.
These are directional analytical scenarios, not statistically estimated probabilities.
7 | POLITICS AND REGULATION
The Meloni government has supported the creation of a stronger domestic banking competitor around MPS, but the eventual outcome may differ from its preferred architecture.
Political influence cannot substitute for shareholder control, execution capabilities or regulatory approvals.
UniCredit–Commerzbank is under review by the European Commission, while Germany retains both political and shareholder interests.
ECB prudential supervision and EU antitrust assessment are separate processes.
Europe has common banking supervision, but it does not yet have a fully integrated market for bank ownership and capital.
8 | THE HIDDEN PATTERN
The contest is moving up the capital chain.
Banks gather deposits and provide credit. Insurance companies manage long-term liabilities and investment portfolios. Wealth managers and asset managers distribute and allocate savings.
This financial infrastructure connects household savings to investments in AI, energy, infrastructure, defence and advanced manufacturing.
That is why Generali and Banca Generali are strategically important.
But concentration does not automatically translate into productivity. Integration must reduce financing costs and increase productive investment.
Continental Scale is the means. Capital productivity is the objective.

The common test is whether these transactions improve expected returns or merely increase complexity.
10 | FIVE KEY TAKEAWAYS
MPS is the catalyst, not the endgame.
Investors can distinguish genuine industrial integration from takeover defence.
Banco BPM, Generali and Banca Generali remain the strategic assets of the next phase.
UniCredit–Commerzbank is the defining test of Continental Scale.
Scale creates value only when returns exceed acquisition, integration and financing costs.
The next generation of European financial leadership will be determined by capital productivity.
VELOCITY INSIGHT | THE GREAT EUROPEAN BANKING RESET
The battle for control of Italian banks is becoming a contest over European financial integration.
Intesa could determine the next configuration of domestic banking; UniCredit could establish a precedent for continental integration; Crédit Agricole could shape the future of Banco BPM; and Generali could define the relationship with long-term savings.
Brussels will have to reconcile national interests with the creation of an integrated European financial market.
Restructuring changes the incentives of every participant. Their reactions can generate outcomes that none of the original architects anticipated or can control.THE FIRST BATTLE WAS FOR ITALIAN BANKING CONTROL. THE NEXT IS FOR EUROPEAN FINANCIAL SCALE.
THE ENDGAME IS CAPITAL PRODUCTIVITY.
For investors, what matters is who generates sustainable returns; for boards of directors, who can complete the transactions they initiate; and for Europe, who can mobilise savings to finance the next generation of growth.
DO NOT START WHAT YOU CANNOT CONTROL. DO NOT PROMISE WHAT YOU CANNOT FINISH.

Daily Intelligence Brief — 25 September 2026 | FDK
THE EXECUTION PREMIUM
AI Demand Is Abundant. Deliverable Capacity Is Scarce.
THE THESIS
$11.6 billion of contracted compute demand. $18 billion of infrastructure loans. A potential one-year delay. One constraint: power.
Those numbers capture the next phase of the AI Supercycle.
Anthropic has committed $11.6 billion over seven years to Akamai for compute infrastructure, potentially rising toward $20 billion. Meanwhile, Oracle’s Project Jupiter—supported by roughly $18 billion of loans and approximately $3 billion of Blue Owl equity—is confronting potential delays linked to securing sufficient electricity.
The contradiction is decisive:
AI demand is not scarce. Capital is not yet scarce. Electricity, permitting and execution increasingly are.
The AI race is moving beyond models and chips toward something more physical:
THE ABILITY TO TURN MONEY INTO MEGAWATTS—AND MEGAWATTS INTO CASH FLOW.
Contracts → Capital → Power → Permits → Construction → Compute → Intelligence → Cash Flow
The weakest link increasingly determines the economics of the entire system.


⸻


1 | COMPUTE
Beyond the GPU
Anthropic’s Akamai agreement reveals a structural change in AI infrastructure.
Agentic AI requires not only GPUs but enormous volumes of CPU, memory, networking and distributed-compute capacity. Akamai expects approximately $5.5 billion of CapEx to support the initial commitment.
The stack broadens:
GPU → CPU → Memory → Networking → Storage → Orchestration
As AI becomes agentic, the competitive unit becomes the whole computing system, not the accelerator alone.
What changed: infrastructure scarcity is spreading beyond GPUs.
Investor implication: value can migrate toward the less glamorous complements—memory, networking, CPU capacity and systems infrastructure—precisely because they become unavoidable.


⸻


2 | PRODUCTIVITY
AI begins compressing discovery itself
Anthropic reports that roughly 950 Claude agents, operating for 21 hours, screened more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases and narrowed them to 20 candidates for laboratory investigation, contributing to identification of a previously uncharacterised enzyme system. Human scientists performed the subsequent validation.
This is not yet a commercial biotechnology breakthrough.
The significance lies in the productivity mechanism:
AI → larger searchable hypothesis space → more experiments per scientist → faster discovery.
The productivity metric therefore changes.
Not simply:
hours saved.
But:
USEFUL HYPOTHESES PER SCIENTIST.
That could become enormously consequential across pharmaceuticals, materials, semiconductors and industrial engineering.


⸻


3 | CAPITAL
Wall Street discovers execution risk
Oracle’s Project Jupiter exposes the next Great Split.
The project carries roughly $18 billion of loans and approximately $3 billion of Blue Owl equity, yet power-related issues could delay completion by roughly a year. Oracle says the project remains on schedule.
The distinction is increasingly:
CONTRACTED DEMAND vs. EXECUTABLE CAPACITY
A blue-chip customer is no longer sufficient.
Investors increasingly need five answers:
Is power secured?
Are permits final?
Can construction be delivered?
Who carries delay risk?
When does cash flow begin?
With AI infrastructure potentially requiring approximately $1.5 trillion of external financing by 2028, execution risk is becoming systemic rather than project-specific.
The Great Split: secured infrastructure versus speculative infrastructure.


⸻


4 | ENERGY
The AI factory becomes a power system
Mitsubishi Electric has introduced a chip-to-grid architecture for NVIDIA Vera Rubin AI factories, structured around 250-MW blocks and designed for rack densities eventually exceeding 1 MW per rack. It integrates grid connection, generation, batteries, cooling and compute.
The old architecture:
Grid → Data Centre → Compute
The emerging architecture:
GENERATION + STORAGE + GRID + COOLING + COMPUTE
Power is moving upstream into AI-factory design.That produces a critical distinction:
Not all megawatts are equal.
A powered, permitted, resilient megawatt attached to contracted demand is becoming a financial asset.
A promised megawatt without grid access is increasingly a liability.


⸻


5 | GEOPOLITICS
Trump–Xi: stabilization without technological détente
The Trump–Xi summit extended the U.S.–China trade truce by two months but produced no major breakthrough on AI or technology restrictions.
The emerging structure is:
Trade stabilization + technological competition + selective crisis management.
This is managed competition, not technological détente.
The implication for Europe is important.
Strategic relevance will not come from trying to replicate every U.S. or Chinese capability. It will come from controlling technologies and infrastructure that both systems cannot easily substitute.
That means identifying Europe’s own scarce complements.


⸻


6 | EUROPE 2030
From capacity creation to market creation
EU industry ministers are increasingly framing Chips Act 2.0 around not only semiconductor capacity but creating European demand for European technology.
That is a meaningful shift.
The industrial-policy chain must become:
Public Capital → Capacity → Demand → Scale → Learning → Competitiveness
Europe has concentrated heavily on the first two terms.
The next phase must address the rest.
There is no new AI Gigafactory funding announcement today. Funding baseline unchanged.
Europe’s fundamental constraint remains execution velocity: synchronizing capital, energy, permitting, technology and demand rapidly enough to turn policy ambition into industrial scale.

NERC HYPOTHESIS 16
THE CONTRACT-TO-CAPACITY LAW
The scarce asset is shifting from AI demand to the ability to convert that demand into operating capacity.
BANKABLE AI CAPACITY
= Contracted Demand × Power Certainty × Permitting × Delivery × Capital Durability
The equation is multiplicative.
If power certainty approaches zero, a multibillion-dollar customer contract cannot produce compute.
If permitting fails, capital becomes stranded.
If construction slips, revenue moves outward while interest continues accumulating.
This creates the next Great Split:
SECURED MEGAWATTS vs. SPECULATIVE MEGAWATTS
And the premium should increasingly accrue to the former.


⸻


THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECT
Megawatts become financial instruments
The key unit of AI infrastructure is changing.
First it was:
GPU.
Then:
data-centre capacity.
Increasingly it becomes:
THE POWERED, PERMITTED AND FINANCEABLE MEGAWATT.
That megawatt bundles:
electricity + grid connection + land + permits + cooling + connectivity + customer credit.
Its value therefore depends not simply on electricity prices but on execution certainty.
This creates a new financial hierarchy inside AI infrastructure.

INVESTORS’ QUICK TAKEAWAYS
1. Move down the stack.
AI value is broadening beyond GPUs toward CPU, memory, networking, power systems, cooling and orchestration.
2. Separate demand from deliverability.
A $10 billion contract is valuable only if the underlying infrastructure can be built and powered.
3. Price the megawatt, not the announcement.
Favor secured, permitted and contracted MW over headline capacity pipelines.
4. Watch the balance sheet.
With external AI financing potentially approaching $1.5 trillion by 2028, delays increasingly translate directly into financing costs and lower returns.
5. Energy becomes an AI trade.Generation, storage, grid equipment, transformers, power electronics and connection rights increasingly sit inside the AI value chain.
6. Scientific AI deserves attention.
The next productivity frontier may come from increasing experiments and useful hypotheses per researcher, not simply replacing labor.
7. Europe offers a different opportunity.
Look for European companies controlling difficult-to-substitute complements—industrial automation, semiconductor equipment, grids, power electronics, robotics, mobility and domain-specific data.
8. Execution becomes the valuation premium.
The hierarchy increasingly becomes:
POWER CERTAINTY → UTILIZATION → CASH FLOW → ROIC
not:
AI exposure → higher multiple.

BOTTOM LINE
The AI Supercycle is no longer constrained by ambition, capital or demand. It is constrained by execution.
The decisive asset is becoming the powered, permitted, financed and operational megawatt.
The winners will not be those who announce the most compute.
They will be those who:
turn capital into power,
power into compute,
compute into intelligence,
and intelligence into cash flow—faster than everyone else.
IN THE NEXT ECONOMY, EXECUTION IS THE NEW SCARCITY.
AND SCARCITY IS WHERE VALUE ACCRUES.

Daily Intelligence Brief — 18 September 2026 | FDK
WHEN AI ENTERS ITS OWN PRODUCTION FUNCTION
Material inflection detected: AI is moving from being an output of the R&D system to becoming an input into the production of the next generation of AI.
The number that matters most this morning is 26%.
Anthropic disclosed yesterday that Claude now “leads” 26% of its AI R&D work, up from under 1% in February, while more than 90% of its R&D involves AI collaboration. Roughly 30,000 AI agents were simultaneously conducting research and engineering work on Anthropic’s principal internal platform in August. Anthropic explicitly says Claude is not operating fully autonomously for any measured subset of this work. 
That qualification matters enormously.
We are not observing autonomous recursive self-improvement.
But we may be observing something economically almost as important in the near term:
AI is increasing the productivity of the human–AI system that creates better AI.
The production function is changing:
Human Researchers + Compute → Better AI
is becoming:
HUMAN RESEARCHERS + AI RESEARCHERS + COMPUTE + ASSURANCE → BETTER AI
And better AI subsequently returns as a more productive input into the next R&D cycle.
That creates a potentially compounding system.

1 | AI & ACCELERATED COMPUTING
The R&D loop begins accelerating itself
Anthropic’s disclosure gives us one of the clearest quantitative windows yet into AI-assisted AI development. Its framework distinguishes AI that collaborates under close human direction from AI that “leads” a task end-to-end from a high-level prompt while remaining under human supervision. Claude now leads 26% of measured R&D work and collaborates on more than 90%. 
The first circular chain becomes:
MODEL CAPABILITY ↑
↓
AI PARTICIPATION IN R&D ↑
↓
EXPERIMENTAL THROUGHPUT ↑
↓
SOFTWARE + MODEL DEVELOPMENT FASTER
↓
NEXT-GENERATION CAPABILITY ↑
↓
AI PARTICIPATION IN R&D ↑ AGAIN
But there is a simultaneous counter-loop.
Anthropic reports approximately 30,000 agents active at any one time on the internal platform it measured. Those agents are monitored both online and offline, illustrating how greater agent autonomy expands the monitoring surface. 
Therefore:
AI-led research ↑ → autonomous actions ↑ → monitoring burden ↑ → assurance infrastructure ↑ → safety cost ↑.
This connects directly with our Assurance Multiplier.
What changed?
Until yesterday, AI was primarily increasing productivity elsewhere.
Today we have unusually concrete evidence that AI is increasing the productivity of AI production itself.
That is qualitatively different.

2 | SCIENTIFIC AI
The recursion mechanism is already escaping the AI laboratory
A second Anthropic result makes today’s signal considerably more consequential.
Claude optimized more than 30 open-source biomolecular models in under four weeks, making them approximately 4× faster on average. Anthropic says it also developed a low-memory mode allowing biomolecular systems larger than 10,000 tokens to be predicted on a single NVIDIA GPU node. The optimized code is being open-sourced. 
The significance is not simply faster scientific software.
Consider the chain:
AI improves scientific code
→ compute required per experiment ↓
→ experiments per unit of capital ↑
→ scientific iteration ↑
→ discovery velocity ↑
→ better scientific tools/data ↑
→ AI-assisted science improves further.
This suggests that the Recursive R&D Multiplier need not be confined to foundation models.
It could appear across:
drug discovery, materials science, semiconductor design, energy systems, robotics and industrial engineering.
That is where its macroeconomic significance becomes enormous.

3 | COMPUTE & MEMORY
Intelligence gets better. Its physical inputs get more expensive.
This morning provides an extraordinary counterpoint to the recursive-R&D thesis.
Nebius will increase pay-as-you-go prices on selected NVIDIA GPU instances by 17–21% from October 1, its second price increase in three months.Some CPU-only instances rise 25%, while selected memory offerings increase approximately 41%. Nebius says it recently signed four customer contracts averaging more than $1 billion each. 
Gartner meanwhile forecasts global AI spending of approximately $2.67 trillion in 2026, up 49.5% year over year. AI infrastructure alone reaches approximately $1.484 trillion, versus $982 billion in 2025—roughly 56% of total AI expenditure. 
That is approximately:
+$502 BILLION OF AI INFRASTRUCTURE SPENDING IN ONE YEAR.
And Gartner explicitly describes infrastructure demand as remaining strong despite memory-price pressure. 
This produces the central paradox:
The marginal cost of intelligence can fall while the marginal price of producing more intelligence rises.
Why?
Because demand is growing even faster than efficiency.
Better models → more applications → more inference → more R&D agents → more compute → more memory → more electricity.
Efficiency therefore does not automatically reduce resource consumption.
It can stimulate more consumption.
This is AI’s version of the Jevons Paradox.

4 | THE CONSTRAINT ROTATES AGAIN
Memory becomes strategic territory
China provides today’s strongest evidence for the Constraint Rotation Law.
Reuters reports that DRAM specialist CXMT is preparing to enter NAND flash, creating an R&D production line in Beijing. AI-server demand has contributed to a global memory shortage that industry executives expect to persist through at least 2027. 
The mechanism matters.
Manufacturers prioritize scarce capital toward high-margin HBM and DRAM.
That constrains investment elsewhere.
AI demand ↑
→ HBM demand ↑
→ fabrication capacity reallocates toward HBM
→ NAND expansion constrained
→ NAND scarcity ↑
→ prices ↑
→ strategic return on new NAND capacity ↑
→ CXMT enters.
This is almost exactly what our Constraint Rotation Law predicts.
Scarcity does not disappear.
SCARCITY MIGRATES.
And China is responding by broadening domestic control of the memory stack.
That strengthens the emerging Chinese architecture:
processors + memory + networking + models + domestic capital + energy.
The technological gap remains. But the strategic objective is increasingly full-stack substitution.

5 | INDUSTRIAL AI & PHYSICAL AI
Europe moves from autonomous pilots toward fleet economics
Lucid and Bolt announced yesterday that Bolt aims to deploy at least 25,000 fully autonomous Lucid vehicles across European cities. Bolt’s longer-term ambition is to host 100,000 autonomous vehicles by 2035. The planned vehicles use Lucid’s forthcoming Midsize platform and NVIDIA Hyperion. 
Important qualification: 25,000 vehicles have not been ordered or deployed yet. The companies have not disclosed a timetable for reaching the target or the investment required. 
Nevertheless, the architecture is significant:
NVIDIA → autonomy stack
Lucid → physical platform
Bolt → fleet + demand + distribution
European authorities → authorization.
The emerging competitive unit is therefore not necessarily the vertically integrated robotaxi company.
It can be an orchestrated ecosystem.
And this identifies Europe’s real bottleneck.
Not technology alone.
AUTHORIZATION VELOCITY.
If European cities authorize Physical AI substantially more slowly than American or Chinese jurisdictions, deployment data accumulate elsewhere.
That produces:
slower authorization → smaller fleet → less data → slower learning → higher costs → still slower deployment.
The opposite loop is equally powerful.

Crusoe announced a $3.9 billion Series F at a $30.9 billion valuation to expand its vertically integrated AI infrastructure platform. 
This matters alongside Gartner’s $1.484 trillion infrastructure-spending forecast.
Capital is not retreating from AI.

frontier-model uncertainty
and

That creates a new divide:
CONTRACTED CAPACITY vs. SPECULATIVE CAPACITY
The infrastructure projects most capable of securing:power + land + customers + financing + hardware + permits
before construction will increasingly enjoy lower financing risk.
The Great Split is therefore entering the balance sheet.

7 | ENERGY & GRID
Who pays becomes almost as important as how much power exists
The U.S. House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act 417–3. The bill would require state utility commissions to consider standards ensuring that large data centers bear incremental generation, transmission and distribution costs created by their demand rather than shifting those costs to households and smaller businesses. 
But there is an important update to yesterday’s story: the measure has subsequently stalled in the Senate, so it is not law. 
The political signal nevertheless remains powerful.
The debate is shifting from:
How do we produce enough electricity for AI?
toward:
WHO PAYS FOR THE ELECTRICITY SYSTEM AI REQUIRES?
The economic consequence is potentially substantial.
If hyperscalers increasingly internalize:
generation + transmission + substations + backup power + grid reinforcement,
then the true infrastructure cost of AI becomes more visible.
That could raise the hurdle rate for marginal projects while strengthening highly utilized campuses.

8 | ECONOMIC GEOGRAPHY
India attempts to become the trusted third node
Applied Materials announced a planned $5 billion investment in India over the next decade, focused on semiconductor research, supply-chain development and workforce expansion. More than 600 companies from 52 countries are participating in SEMICON India this week. 
India is positioning itself not simply as a lower-cost alternative but as a trusted diversification jurisdiction amid U.S.–China technology restrictions and concentration in Taiwan. 
The strategy increasingly combines:
design + equipment + materials + packaging + fabrication + talent + domestic demand.
That is exactly the correct unit of analysis.
Semiconductor sovereignty is not a fab.
It is an ecosystem.
India still has major execution gaps. But if it can connect its software-engineering base with semiconductor infrastructure, it could create one of the most consequential new nodes in the AI production geography.

NERC HYPOTHESIS 09
THE RECURSIVE R&D MULTIPLIER
I would formalize today’s hypothesis carefully:
Before autonomous recursive self-improvement exists, an economically powerful recursive mechanism can emerge when AI increases the productivity of the human–AI system responsible for producing the next generation of AI.
A provisional formulation:
AI RESEARCH VELOCITY
= Human Research Capacity × Agent Leverage × Compute Availability × Experimental Throughput × Assurance Efficiency
The critical variable is Agent Leverage.
If one researcher supported by agents can perform the effective work previously requiring several researchers, research productivity rises.
Better models then increase Agent Leverage.
Therefore:
CAPABILITY → R&D PRODUCTIVITY → CAPABILITY
The system does not need to become autonomous for the economics to become nonlinear.

THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECT
Faster intelligence may shorten the economic life of physical capital
This could become extremely important for infrastructure investors.
If AI-assisted R&D compresses innovation cycles:
model cycle ↓
→ hardware requirements change faster
→ accelerator generations turn faster
→ obsolescence risk ↑
→ required return on physical infrastructure ↑.
That produces a striking paradox:
AI can simultaneously increase demand for physical infrastructure and shorten the economic duration of that infrastructure.
The winners may therefore be architectures capable of continuous upgrading rather than facilities optimized around a single hardware generation.
Modularity becomes financial value.

THE GREAT SPLIT — NEXT PHASE
COMPOUNDING ORGANIZATIONS vs. LINEAR ORGANIZATIONS
A linear company uses AI to reduce the cost of existing work.
A compounding company uses AI to improve the process that improves the company.
Consider:
AI → better software → better products → better data → better AI.
AI → better semiconductor design → better compute → better AI.
AI → faster scientific discovery → better materials → better infrastructure → better AI.
That distinction could become more important than simple AI adoption.
The question is no longer:
Does this company use AI?
It becomes:
Does AI make this company better at becoming better?
That is a fundamentally different competitive-advantage test.

IMPLICATIONS
Europe. The strategic objective should extend beyond sovereign compute. Europe needs recursive industrial systems in semiconductors, robotics, mobility, rail, aerospace, energy, pharmaceuticals and materials—areas where AI improves the process that generates the next generation of European technology.
Companies. Management should map not only tasks that AI can automate, but feedback loops AI can accelerate. The latter may produce far greater long-term value.
Investors. R&D cycle time, experiments per engineer, software output, time-to-market and AI-generated intellectual property could become more revealing than headcount reductions. The best AI adopters may be those compounding innovation fastest.
Infrastructure investors. Contract duration, modularity, residual value and upgradeability become increasingly important if AI-assisted R&D shortens hardware cycles.
Policymakers. Assurance must scale alongside research velocity.If AI-assisted AI research compounds, static regulatory review cycles will increasingly lag the technology they supervise.

AI IS BEGINNING TO ACCELERATE INNOVATION ITSELF.

compute, memory, electricity, capital, physical infrastructure, human judgment and assurance.

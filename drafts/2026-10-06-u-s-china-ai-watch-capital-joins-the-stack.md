The Big Take | October 6, 2026

Material inflection. Today’s signal is not another benchmark release. It is the scale of capital now moving behind China’s alternative AI stack. DeepSeek is reportedly close to raising at least RMB80 billion—about $12 billion—with Tencent among the backers, while it plans to deploy at least 160,000 Huawei accelerators in a major Inner Mongolia data center. That turns the DeepSeek–Huawei alignment from a software and model story into an infrastructure story. 

This strengthens—not overturns—our thesis: the United States still leads the absolute frontier, but China is increasingly building the capital base, domestic compute stack and deployment architecture needed to challenge the economics and diffusion of that frontier.

The most consequential development is the combination of DeepSeek capital + Huawei compute + open software infrastructure. DeepSeek and Huawei had already moved together on Ascend programming tools designed to reduce dependence on Nvidia’s CUDA ecosystem. The new funding scale and planned 160,000-chip cluster suggest that China is no longer merely optimizing around U.S. restrictions; it is attempting to build a parallel vertically integrated system capable of supporting frontier-scale training and mass inference domestically. 

At the same time, the U.S. is now responding directly in the layer where China has been strongest: efficient open-weight models. Nvidia-backed Reflection AI launched Beam, a 501-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model with only 23 billion parameters active per token. Reflection says Beam is competitive with GLM-5.2 and approaches Qwen 3.8-Max on coding and agentic tasks while using materially less inference compute. Its own benchmark table still shows Chinese models such as Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash ahead on a number of raw-capability measures, which is precisely why this matters: the U.S. is now treating open-model efficiency as a strategic battleground, not a side market. 

The second-order implication is sharper than yesterday.

The competitive architecture is becoming:

United States: frontier models + Nvidia-scale compute + hyperscale capital + increasingly efficient open models.

China: near-frontier open models + domestic accelerators + lower-cost inference + industrial deployment + rapidly expanding local capital.

The race is therefore moving from model versus model to stack versus stack.

For The Velocity Edge, the signal is important because capital formation is now reinforcing the technological split. DeepSeek’s ability to raise roughly $12 billion, alongside plans for one of the largest known Huawei AI clusters, means the Chinese challenge is no longer capital-light experimentation. It is evolving toward industrial-scale replication of the entire AI production system. 

For the AI Supercycle, this is bullish for infrastructure but increasingly disruptive for where the margins sit. Beam’s architecture and DeepSeek’s strategy both push toward the same economic objective: more intelligence per unit of compute. Reflection says Beam was pretrained on 6,144 Nvidia GB300 GPUs and then used 10,500 GB300 GPUs for a reinforcement-learning run of more than 100 million rollouts—evidence that enormous frontier compute remains a U.S. advantage even as inference becomes more efficient. 

The resulting cycle is becoming clearer:

scarce compute → falling inference cost → exploding token volume → industrial deployment → infrastructure re-acceleration.

Cheaper inference therefore does not end the infrastructure cycle. It broadens it. The economic question shifts from the cost of a single model call to the total quantity of intelligence economically deployable across factories, grids, transportation systems, software agents and machines.

Velocity Insight

The U.S. still owns the highest-performance end of the frontier. China is now financing the machinery required to commoditize everything just below it.

That is the deeper inflection.America is still winning the race to create the most powerful intelligence. China is raising the capital, building the chips and writing the software to make near-frontier intelligence ubiquitous.

For investors, the next phase of the AI Supercycle will therefore be decided less by benchmark leadership alone—and increasingly by compute efficiency × capital availability × deployment velocity.

THE GREAT REPRICING

The AI Supercycle survives. Cheap capital does not.

Today’s signal is bigger than another move in equities. The world’s major central banks are converging toward a new tightening cycle precisely when the AI Supercycle is becoming more physical, energy-intensive and capital-intensive. The Fed has delivered its first rate increase since 2023; today the Bank of Japan raised rates to 1.25%, a 31-year high; markets expect further tightening across several major economies. 

The fundamental question is therefore:

Can AI productivity rise faster than the cost of financing the physical infrastructure required to produce it?

That is now the equation driving markets.

1. The denominator has changed

U.S. Treasury yields moved higher again today as investors absorbed the new global rate regime. Wall Street was modestly lower in late trading, with the Dow down about 0.36%, the S&P 500 about 0.10% and the Nasdaq approximately flat. Europe performed worse: the STOXX 600 fell 1.1% to 635.45, with autos down 3.4%. 

The message is clear:

5% money changes everything.

Capital remains available, but every data center, AI factory, nuclear plant, railway, defense facility and acquisition must now compete against an increasingly attractive risk-free return.

⸻

2. Japan closes the zero-rate era

The BOJ increased its policy rate to 1.25%, the highest in 31 years, by a 7–2 vote. Governor Ueda signaled that policy has entered a more pre-emptive phase focused on preventing inflation from overshooting. Curiously, the yen weakened because the two dissenters created uncertainty over how rapidly subsequent tightening will occur. 

This is consequential far beyond Japan.

For decades:

cheap yen → global carry trade → capital exported abroad.

Now:

higher Japanese rates → better domestic returns → greater competition for Japanese savings.

The world has effectively lost its last major near-zero-cost funding anchor.

⸻

3. Oil has become monetary policy

Oil remains above $100, despite some easing today as China reportedly pressed Iran to restrain Houthi actions. The seven-month Middle East conflict continues to keep energy costs embedded in the global inflation outlook. 

More important than crude alone is diesel. U.S. diesel has exceeded $6 per gallon and European diesel around $210 per barrel amid refinery disruption and historically tight inventories. Diesel directly feeds trucking, agriculture, construction, mining and industrial logistics. 

The chain is therefore:

ENERGY

→ PHYSICAL COSTS
→ INFLATION
→ CENTRAL BANKS
→ YIELDS
→ WACC
→ AI HURDLE RATE

The AI economy cannot escape the physical economy.

⸻

4. Yet AI capital formation refuses to stop

This is the counter-signal that matters most.

AI infrastructure provider Crusoe has just raised $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion post-money valuation. Demand for specialized AI-cloud and data-center capacity remains enormous despite higher yields and renewed questions about the pace of frontier-model development. 

And today an even larger architecture became visible: South Korea and the United States are discussing projects within a $350 billion investment framework, including a proposed $22.3 billion, 6.3-GW Texas gas-power complex intended partly to serve AI data centers and semiconductor fabs, plus potentially as many as eight U.S. nuclear reactors. 

That is the real transformation.

AI investment is moving from:

GPU → data center

toward:

AI → POWER → NUCLEAR/GAS → GRID → SEMICONDUCTORS → INDUSTRIAL CAPACITY

AI is becoming an energy-and-infrastructure Supercycle.

⸻

5. Europe’s Great Split is becoming visible

Today’s European selloff exposed the other side.

Volkswagen fell 5.6% after cutting its profit outlook amid restructuring costs, job-reduction provisions and weak Chinese performance. Porsche lost 4.9%. Autos led the European market down. 

This is precisely the distinction we have been developing:

LEGACY CAPITAL

versus

NEXT-ECONOMY CAPITAL.Europe possesses extraordinary engineering assets. But capital must migrate faster from low-return legacy capacity toward:

Industrial AI;

power;

grid infrastructure;

defense;

autonomy;

rail;

robotics;

accelerated computing;

and intelligent mobility.

The European problem is increasingly not lack of capital.

It is Capital-Conversion Velocity.

⸻

WHERE GLOBAL CAPITAL IS MOVING

The pattern across the week is now unusually coherent:

toward AI infrastructure, because compute demand remains enormous;

toward power, because electricity is becoming the binding constraint;

toward nuclear and gas, because firm power increasingly carries a certainty premium;

toward defense and resilience, because infrastructure without security cannot guarantee utilization;

toward contracted capacity, because 5% rates punish distant, unproven cash flows;

and toward productivity, because only productivity can ultimately justify this enormous capital cycle.

The decisive investment distinction is therefore no longer simply:

technology versus industrials.

It is:

PRODUCTIVE CAPITAL

versus

CAPITAL THAT CANNOT CLEAR THE NEW HURDLE RATE.

⸻

THE NEXT 90 DAYS

Three forces are now on a collision course.

First: monetary tightening. The Fed, ECB and BOJ have moved toward higher rates, and markets anticipate further increases. 

Second: physical inflation. Oil above $100, record diesel prices and disrupted energy infrastructure keep upward pressure on the cost base. 

Third: AI productivity. Trillions of dollars of infrastructure investment now need to demonstrate that intelligence can generate enough incremental output, margin and cash flow to outrun the higher cost of capital.

That creates the central equation for Q4:

AI PRODUCTIVITY VELOCITY

versus

WACC + ENERGY + PHYSICAL SCARCITY

Whichever side accelerates faster will determine the next market regime.

⸻

THE VELOCITY INSIGHT

The first phase of the AI Supercycle was about intelligence.

The second was about accelerated computing.

The third became about power.

The fourth became about capital.

We are now entering the decisive phase:

RETURN ON INTELLIGENCE.

The market will increasingly ask not:

How many GPUs?

Not:

How many gigawatts?

Not even:

How much AI capex?

But:

HOW MUCH PRODUCTIVITY AND FREE CASH FLOW DOES EACH ADDITIONAL DOLLAR OF AI CAPITAL CREATE?

That is the metric capable of separating infrastructure from overinvestment.

⸻

CLOSING VIEW

September 18 closes a remarkable week.

The Fed raised rates.

The BOJ raised rates.

Global sovereign yields moved higher.

Oil remained above $100.

Diesel remained at extraordinary levels.

European autos sold off.

Yet AI infrastructure continued raising billions.

And governments and corporations began discussing energy projects measured not in megawatts but in gigawatts and tens of billions of dollars.

These developments belong to one circular architecture:

AI

→ CAPITAL
→ POWER
→ PHYSICAL INFRASTRUCTURE
→ INTELLIGENCE
→ PRODUCTIVITY
→ FREE CASH FLOW
→ MORE CAPITAL

running against:

ENERGY SCARCITY

→ INFLATION
→ CENTRAL-BANK TIGHTENING
→ HIGHER YIELDS
→ HIGHER WACC
→ GREATER CAPITAL DISCIPLINE.

The Great Split has therefore moved again.

It is no longer:

AI versus non-AI.

Nor:

Physical versus digital.

Nor even:

Intelligence Creation versus Intelligence Conversion.

It is becoming:

CAPITAL THAT PRODUCES PRODUCTIVITY FASTER THAN WACC RISES

versus

CAPITAL WHOSE RETURN ARRIVES TOO LATE.

That is the dividing line for investors, companies and countries.

Intelligence creates possibility.

Power makes intelligence physical.

Capital makes it scalable.

Productivity makes it economic.

Velocity determines whether the return arrives before the hurdle rate catches it.

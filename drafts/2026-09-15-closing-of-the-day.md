THE HIDDEN CAPITAL ROTATION
AI is no longer only repricing technology. It is beginning to reprice the physical economy.
Fundamental Question
What if the most important signal from today is not where markets closed—but where capital has begun to move?
The answer emerging from August 17 is increasingly clear: the AI Supercycle is entering its capital-intensive phase.
For three years, markets largely rewarded the companies producing intelligence: semiconductors, accelerated computing, memory, models and cloud infrastructure. The next phase will increasingly reward the companies capable of deploying intelligence into energy systems, factories, construction sites, transportation networks and machines.
That shift changes the economics of AI—and potentially the architecture of modern capitalism.
⸻
PART I — THE DAY BENEATH THE MARKET
Markets Are Beginning to Price Two Economies at Once
At first sight, today’s market action was hardly revolutionary. U.S. equities remained close to record territory but struggled for direction. The deeper pressure came from bonds: long-duration Treasury yields moved higher as oil and geopolitical risk revived inflation concerns, with the 30-year U.S. yield reaching its highest closing level in roughly nineteen years. (The Wall Street Journal⁠￼)
Europe was similarly uneasy. The early optimism faded and several major markets finished lower, with Spain’s IBEX among the weaker performers. (Anadolu Ajansı⁠￼)
Gold moved above $4,400/oz, while Bitcoin traded around the $63,000–64,000 range. (Trading Economics⁠￼)
Yet underneath those asset-price movements something considerably more important happened.
Capital formation around AI moved another step from software toward infrastructure—and from infrastructure toward the physical economy.
The extraordinary example came from NVIDIA.
NVIDIA agreed to provide as much as $105 billion of guarantees supporting OpenAI’s leasing of the enormous Ohio data-center project being developed by SoftBank-owned SB Energy. NVIDIA will separately invest $1.5 billion in SB Energy. The project could ultimately reach 8 GW of data-center capacity, while the surrounding energy build-out could involve another 10 GW of power generation. (Reuters⁠￼)
This is no longer simply semiconductor demand.
It is industrial capital formation organized around intelligence.
And the chain is becoming visible:
AI demand → accelerated computing → AI factories → electricity → grids → financing → physical infrastructure → industrial intelligence → productivity → new cash flows.
That is today’s hidden pattern.
⸻
PART II — THE CAPITAL ROTATION
From Financing Chips to Financing Intelligence Infrastructure
The crucial development is not merely that AI investment remains enormous.
It is that its financial architecture is changing.
NVIDIA has already announced partnerships involving Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR intended to mobilize more than $500 billion of third-party capital for AI infrastructure. (NVIDIA Newsroom⁠￼)
That number deserves attention.
AI is beginning to require financing structures historically associated with railways, telecommunications networks, electricity systems and large infrastructure projects.
Equity alone cannot finance the next stage.
The AI Supercycle is therefore migrating toward:
corporate equity + private equity + private credit + infrastructure funds + project finance + sovereign capital + utilities + long-duration contracts.
The consequence could be profound.
The AI trade is becoming a capital-formation trade.
And today provided another signal.
SoftBank invested $200 million in Swiss company Gravis Robotics, whose autonomous technology transforms conventional heavy construction machinery into intelligent robotic systems. (Yahoo Finance⁠￼)
The amount is modest compared with hyperscale data centers.
The signal is not.
Construction is one of the world’s largest, most fragmented and least-digitized industries.Once intelligence can operate excavators, loaders, cranes and other heavy machinery autonomously, AI stops being primarily an information technology.
It becomes productive capital.
This is the transition from:
AI that answers → AI that decides → AI that acts.
And once AI acts, it enters the balance sheet of the physical economy.
⸻
The Five Signals
Accelerated computing is becoming infrastructure. The financing requirements are moving from billions toward hundreds of billions.
Energy is becoming part of the AI stack. An 8-GW data center is simultaneously a technology project, an electricity project and a capital-markets project. (Reuters⁠￼)
Robotics is becoming the transmission mechanism. Industrial AI reaches physical reality through machines capable of sensing, reasoning and acting.
Capital is moving downstream. The first winners supplied intelligence. The next winners will increasingly apply intelligence to physical assets.
The cost of capital is becoming strategically decisive. This matters particularly because the macroeconomic environment is not becoming easier. A Reuters poll published today found most economists expecting the Federal Reserve to keep its policy rate at 3.50%–3.75% through year-end. (Reuters⁠￼)
This produces one of the defining tensions of the Next Economy:
The largest technological capital-expansion cycle in decades is developing inside a structurally more expensive capital regime.
That combination will separate winners from losers.
⸻
PART III — TOMORROW’S MARKET
Watch the Rotation, Not the Index
The central mistake investors can make now is to interpret AI through the performance of the Nasdaq alone.
The Nasdaq measures only part of what is happening.
The more consequential question is:
Where does the next dollar of AI-driven capital expenditure go?
Increasingly, the answer extends beyond semiconductors.
It moves toward:
Memory → Power → Grid equipment → Cooling → Optical networking → Industrial automation → Robotics → Construction → Logistics → Transportation → Autonomous systems.
The physical economy is becoming the new frontier of the AI Supercycle.
Independent investment research increasingly points in the same direction: estimates suggest the global robotics opportunity could expand toward $2.5 trillion in annual sales by 2035, compared with roughly $100 billion in 2025. (J.P. Morgan Private Bank⁠￼)
Industrial robot installations already exceeded 540,000 units annually in the latest reported global figures—more than twice the level of a decade earlier. (GlobeNewswire⁠￼)
But robotics should not be considered a standalone industry.
It is better understood as the execution layer of Industrial AI.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN
The market still describes these developments separately.
Semiconductors.
Data centers.
Power.
Private credit.
Robotics.
Autonomous vehicles.
Industrial automation.
Construction technology.
That taxonomy is becoming obsolete.
They are increasingly components of one emerging industrial system.And this is where today’s signal becomes consequential.
The first phase of the AI Supercycle increased the value of companies capable of creating intelligence.
The second increased the value of companies capable of manufacturing intelligence at scale.
The third phase is beginning now.
It will reward the companies capable of embedding intelligence into productive physical capital.
That transition will broaden the investment universe dramatically.

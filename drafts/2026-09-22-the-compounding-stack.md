The macro backdrop improved sharply overnight. The Nasdaq closed Monday at a record high, gaining 2.26%, while the PHLX semiconductor index rose 4.3%. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield fell back below 5%, and Brent settled Monday at $100.34, then traded around $100.22 early Tuesday. Asian technology markets followed higher: South Korea gained nearly 2%, Taiwan 1.3%, and Nasdaq futures another 0.37%. 
But today’s most consequential development is not the rally.
It is Alibaba’s announcement.
The Chinese group said it plans to train a new model with 5–10 trillion parameters, versus 2.4 trillion for its current Qwen flagship. It unveiled the Zhenwu V900, which it says delivers three times the performance of its previous chip, supports clusters of up to 500,000 cards, and forms part of a broader target to surpass 20 GW of global data-center capacity by 2032. Alibaba also said its Qwen team has made progress on recursive self-improvement. 
That combination changes the strategic question again.
The next competitive advantage is not controlling one bottleneck. It is creating a system in which solving one bottleneck accelerates the economics of every other layer.

The Fundamental Question
Which corporations can make models, silicon, networking, power, capital and distribution compound together—without letting the cost of owning the stack overwhelm the value it creates?
That is today’s GVI inflection.
The first generation of the AI trade rewarded component superiority.
The second rewarded infrastructure scale.
The third rewarded financing and resilience.
The emerging phase increasingly rewards:
Stack Coherence → Utilization → Recurring Economics.
A better model increases chip demand.
A better chip lowers inference cost.
Lower inference cost increases agent deployment.
More agents increase cloud utilization.
Higher utilization finances more infrastructure.
When those loops reinforce one another, Velocity becomes multiplicative rather than additive.

The Largest Corporate Inflection
Alibaba — 97.5 | +1.3
Measured evidence
Alibaba plans a model containing 5–10 trillion parameters, two to four times the size of its current Qwen 3.8 Max. Its existing M890 supernode already handles inference for models above 2 trillion parameters, according to management. The company says the new Zhenwu V900 chip delivers triple the performance of the M890 and can support clusters of up to 500,000 cards. Alibaba Cloud is targeting more than 20 GW of global data-center capacity by 2032 and expects commercial-scale deployment of its AI supernodes beginning this quarter. 
Alibaba also says the Qwen team has made progress on recursive self-improvement, in which models identify weaknesses, design experiments and synthesize new data to improve subsequent iterations. 
FDK analytical judgment
Alibaba receives today’s largest major-corporate GVI upgrade because it is no longer pursuing a collection of AI assets.
It is constructing a self-reinforcing stack.
The architecture is becoming:
Qwen → proprietary silicon → supernodes → Alibaba Cloud → enterprise distribution → usage data → better Qwen.
That is strategically different from simply owning a model or a chip.
Each layer can potentially reduce the marginal cost or increase the utilization of the next.
This is how Velocity compounds.
But the critical word is potentially.
The company is simultaneously committing itself to enormous requirements in:
model training; silicon design; semiconductor supply; power; data centers; networking; cooling.
Vertical control increases strategic independence. It also concentrates execution risk.
Alibaba therefore jumps sharply—but remains below the top seven until announced capacity and chip claims convert into sustained external commercial economics.

AMD — 97.4 | +0.8
The Challenger Becomes a System Company
Measured evidence
AMD crossed $1 trillion in market capitalization for the first time on September 21 after its shares rose roughly 10%. The company has accelerated its AI product cadence and is moving beyond selling individual processors toward complete AI systems combining chips, networking and related hardware. Demand for server CPUs used alongside GPUs for inference has also helped AMD take share from Intel. 
AMD shares have risen approximately 185% in 2026, compared with 15.8% for the Nasdaq. 
FDK analytical judgment
The market capitalization is not the reason AMD enters the GVI Top 10.
The architecture is.
AMD increasingly recognizes that Nvidia’s durable advantage has never been merely the GPU.
It has been the system.
AMD’s response is therefore evolving from:
better alternative chip
toward:
alternative compute architecture.
That requires CPUs, GPUs, networking, software, rack design and customer integration.
This matters because AI buyers increasingly evaluate system throughput per invested dollar, not isolated benchmark performance.
The chip challenger becomes much more dangerous when it stops competing chip-for-chip.The remaining gap is ecosystem depth.
Nvidia still enjoys a much deeper software and developer architecture.
But AMD is now attacking the correct strategic layer.

That distinction matters.
The AI bottleneck has already migrated from transistor density toward:
advanced packaging;

chiplet integration.
The Kaohsiung park reduces the distance between suppliers, testing and production.
Geographic density can increase technological Velocity.
TSMC’s main weakness is that the same density creating efficiency also preserves geopolitical and energy concentration.
That trade-off prevents a perfect score.

Persistent Agents Move Toward Mass Distribution

Muse can interact with other applications and perform tasks involving email, travel, payments, commerce and calendars. Meta launched it after delaying the product to strengthen security architecture. 
Meta is also building a C$13 billion, 1-GW data center in Alberta, scalable to 1.8 GW. Capital Power will supply 250 MW until Meta’s dedicated power facility begins operating in 2030.

Meta’s strategic architecture is becoming unusually powerful:
mass distribution + agent + data + infrastructure.

Meta already possesses billions of users.
It does not need to acquire AI distribution.
It needs to convert existing distribution into persistent agent usage.
If Muse succeeds, the implication for compute demand is substantial because agentic systems operate continuously rather than only when a user opens a chat window.
The next consumer-AI battleground may be frequency of autonomous action rather than frequency of human prompts.
Meta does not enter the Top 10 yet because agent reliability, privacy and monetization remain insufficiently proven.

The Physical-AI Reality Check
Around 7,000 humanoid robots were sold globally in 2025 for industrial and professional-service use, according to International Federation of Robotics data reviewed by Reuters. That compares with roughly 542,000 conventional industrial robots installed in 2024. Many of the humanoids sold were purchased for research or AI-data generation rather than productive labor; automotive pilots often involve only single-digit or low-double-digit unit counts. 
FDK analytical judgment
This is highly consequential.
The humanoid narrative is running years ahead of the installed productive base.
The correct denominator therefore is no longer:
units demonstrated.
It is:
productive robot-hours / capital invested.
The Physical-AI winners will emerge when robots can demonstrate:
high utilization;
reliable autonomy;
maintenance economics;
labor substitution;
repeat customer orders.
This reinforces yesterday’s Revenue Quality Test.
Physical intelligence must now prove economic productivity, not visual impressiveness.

The European Infrastructure Test
The European Commission proposed mandatory energy- and water-efficiency disclosure for data centers of 500 kW or larger. EU data-center capacity is expected to expand from roughly 12 GW in 2025 to 28 GW by 2030, while data centers already represent about 2.5% of European electricity use. 
Facilities would disclose water efficiency, local water-stress exposure and whether they can support the energy system through mechanisms such as heat reuse. 
FDK analytical judgment
Europe is beginning to turn resource efficiency into a measurable competitive variable.
That matters.
The first phase of AI infrastructure rewarded maximum capacity.
The next increasingly rewards:
maximum productive compute per megawatt and per litre of water.
This could become a European advantage if regulation evolves from reporting into innovation.
Companies such as Schneider Electric, Siemens Energy, Prysmian and leading European cooling and grid providers stand to benefit if the region makes efficiency a prerequisite for scale.
Europe cannot win the AI infrastructure race by spending more than the United States. It can still win important layers by making each unit of infrastructure more productive.

Hidden Patterns
1. Vertical Integration Is Becoming Multiplicative
Traditional vertical integration reduced transaction costs.
AI vertical integration can do more.
A better model increases compute demand.
Better silicon reduces inference cost.
Lower inference cost expands distribution.
More distribution produces more usage data.
That data improves the next model.
The stack is becoming a flywheel.
This is why Alibaba’s announcement matters so much.

2. China Has Moved Beyond Import Substitution
Huawei initially demonstrated the strategy of compensating for external semiconductor restrictions through sovereign system architecture.
Alibaba now extends the logic.
The emerging Chinese stack increasingly contains:
domestic model + domestic chip + domestic cloud + domestic distribution + sovereign power capacity.
That does not mean every layer is technologically equal to the global frontier.
It means fewer external chokepoints can stop the entire system.
Strategic independence can improve before technological parity arrives.

3. AMD Is Validating Nvidia’s Strategic Model
AMD’s move into complete systems implicitly confirms why Nvidia became dominant.
Customers increasingly do not want components.
They want productive systems.
The relevant performance metric moves from:
chip benchmark
toward:
tokens or useful work per dollar of total deployed system.
That broadens competition while simultaneously raising the barrier to entry.

TSMC’s new park demonstrates that packaging is no longer downstream assembly.

memory proximity;

The distinction between semiconductor design and packaging continues to disappear.
Packaging increasingly determines whether theoretical chip performance can become system performance.

5. Agent Distribution May Create the Next Compute Shock
A chatbot waits for a prompt.
A persistent agent acts repeatedly.
That difference has enormous infrastructure consequences.
If millions of Meta users allow Muse-like systems to execute continuously across email, commerce, payments and schedules, token and inference demand could increase far faster than user growth alone.
Agentic AI converts software engagement into continuous compute consumption.
That may explain why markets are already re-rating processor demand around persistent-agent adoption.

10. Full-Stack Control Creates Its Own Risk
Owning more layers increases strategic autonomy.
It also creates:
capital intensity;
organizational complexity;
execution correlation.
A failure in one owned layer can propagate across the entire stack.
That creates the central paradox of the Next Economy:
Control increases resilience until control becomes concentration.
The best architecture will therefore not necessarily own everything.
It will own the layers where differentiation is greatest and contract the rest without losing strategic optionality.

Implications for Investors
The investor question should no longer be:
How much AI exposure does this company have?
It should become:
Do the company’s AI layers reinforce each other economically?
The relevant dashboard is increasingly:
model capability;
compute economics;
network utilization;
power security;
distribution ownership;
contract quality;
free cash conversion.
A useful GVI diagnostic is:
Stack Multiplier = Capability × Utilization × Distribution × Infrastructure Security × Capital Efficiency
This framework is intentionally multiplicative.
A company can possess extraordinary capability and still destroy economic value if capital efficiency collapses.
Alibaba’s announcement makes this distinction critical.
AMD’s shift toward systems makes it investable in a different way.
TSMC remains exceptional because it monetizes almost every competing stack without bearing the entire downstream capital burden.
Neutral scarcity remains one of the highest-quality positions in the Next Economy.

Implications for ExecutivesThe strategic question is not whether to vertically integrate.
It is where.
Every CEO should classify each critical layer as:
own;
control contractually;
multi-source;
outsource.
Own the layers where differentiated knowledge compounds.
Contract the layers where ownership adds capital intensity without defensibility.
Multi-source any layer capable of stopping the corporation.
The objective is not maximum ownership. It is maximum control per dollar of capital employed.
That is the architecture behind high Velocity.

Implications for Policymakers
Alibaba, TSMC and Europe’s new data-center rules deliver a common policy lesson.
Industrial ecosystems outperform isolated subsidies.
A chip fab without packaging is incomplete.
A data center without electricity is incomplete.
An AI model without distribution is incomplete.
A cloud strategy without capital is incomplete.
Europe therefore needs to think in systems.
Its opportunity is especially strong around:
industrial AI;
power efficiency;
optics;
advanced equipment;
trusted deployment;
energy-system integration.
The EU’s 12-GW-to-28-GW expected capacity expansion will be strategically valuable only if the region simultaneously expands power, grids and resource productivity. 
The policy objective should not be to replicate another continent’s stack. It should be to build the strongest stack around Europe’s own scarce advantages.

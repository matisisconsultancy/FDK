The most consequential central-bank development today comes from Singapore.

The Monetary Authority of Singapore has moved the AI debate from macroeconomic scenario analysis into explicit financial-stability stress testing. Its 2026 Financial Stability Review, released on 22 September, tests what happens if the AI investment cycle reverses sharply—and the result adds an important new layer to the Great Split.

The new central-bank question is no longer simply *will AI raise productivity or inflation?* It is now also: **what happens to the financial system if the AI capex boom disappoints?**

::stats
30% :: AI-related revenue shock applied to Singapore-listed firms
400bps :: interest-rate shock in the severe scenario
32% :: listed companies that become financially at risk (16% of corporate debt)
10.9% :: trough CET1 capital for systemic banks, above the 9% requirement

MAS subjected Singapore-listed companies to AI-related revenue shocks of as much as 30% and interest-rate shocks of up to 400 basis points, calibrated to firms' exposure to the AI supply chain. Under that severe scenario, around 32% of listed companies would become financially at risk, although they represent a smaller 16% of total corporate debt. The vulnerability is concentrated among highly leveraged, capital-intensive firms, companies dependent on working-capital financing and smaller businesses with thinner cash buffers.

That distinction is important. The problem is not presently a generalized solvency crisis. It is ==concentrated fragility inside the AI capital-formation ecosystem==.

::pull TWO CHANNELS
AI is becoming simultaneously a monetary-policy variable and a financial-stability variable.

::section The AI Supercycle Now Has Two Central-Bank Transmission Channels | monetary meets financial
Until now we have focused primarily on the monetary channel: AI investment → semiconductors, data centres and electricity → scarcity → higher prices → higher neutral rates and tighter policy.

MAS now formalizes the second channel: AI investment → high valuations → debt, private credit and corporate leverage → earnings expectations → disappointment → repricing → tighter financial conditions.

MAS explicitly warns that a material shortfall in expected AI earnings or returns could produce a broader reassessment of valuations across public equities, corporate bonds and private-credit markets. It also argues that strong AI investment, together with fiscal pressures and recurrent supply shocks, is contributing to a persistently higher global cost of capital.

::signals The Signals | what MAS reveals
# The banking system passes — but that is not the end of the story
MAS' stress test of Singapore's systemically important banks is reassuring but revealing. Under an adverse scenario combining a sharp correction in AI-related valuations, tighter financial conditions, higher credit losses and global recession, aggregate Common Equity Tier 1 capital would trough around 10.9%, remaining above MAS' combined 9% capital requirement.

So the immediate vulnerability is not bank capitalization. The more interesting risk lies in transmission: AI equity losses → corporate credit → private credit → working-capital financing → investment contraction → employment → bank credit quality. This is exactly why the AI Supercycle is beginning to resemble previous capital-investment booms: the risk moves from the underlying technology into the financing architecture around it.
signal: The banks hold; the danger sits in the transmission chain, not the capital ratio.

# AI cyber and concentration risk are becoming systemic
MAS' 2026 survey of financial-sector chief risk officers is another significant signal. Around 80% cited cyber and operational risks—including AI-assisted attacks and dependence on critical third-party providers—as major concerns. 57% identified financial-market stresses, including AI-driven market fragilities, as a major risk category.

This introduces another causality chain: AI adoption → common models and common cloud infrastructure → technological concentration → correlated operational failures → systemic financial risk. It mirrors concerns already raised by the ECB, Bank of England and BIS. The architecture of financial stability is shifting from institution-specific risk toward common-infrastructure risk—particularly as agentic AI enters payments, trading, credit analysis and fraud prevention.
signal: Financial stability is migrating from institution-specific risk to common-infrastructure risk.

# The Bank of England has already identified the inflation side
MAS' financial-stability warning connects directly with the monetary-policy signal from the Bank of England. In its 17 September 2026 meeting, the MPC explicitly noted that strong global demand for AI-related technology goods and primary materials was creating price pressures. Three members who preferred a 25-basis-point rate increase specifically cited AI supply constraints, alongside energy and food pressures, as part of the upside inflation risk.

Put the two together: the Bank of England sees AI capex → physical scarcity → inflation, while MAS sees AI capex → leverage and valuations → financial-stability risk. These are not contradictory. They are two phases of the same cycle.
signal: BoE maps the inflation phase; MAS maps the leverage phase of one cycle.

AI CapEx → scarcity → inflation → rates.

# Productivity Channel
Industrial AI → productivity → unit-cost deflation → lower inflation.

# Financial Channel
AI investment → leverage → valuations → repricing → financial conditions.

# Infrastructure Channel
AI concentration → cloud, models, energy and cyber dependencies → systemic operational risk.

::section Investor Implications | where the fragility sits
The MAS stress test changes how the AI trade should be evaluated. The question is no longer merely whether AI revenues justify current equity multiples. Investors increasingly need to ask: how much leverage sits behind the AI investment chain? Who finances the data centres, power infrastructure and semiconductor capacity? How much exposure has migrated into private credit? Which companies remain solvent if expected AI returns fall while financing costs rise?

The most vulnerable segment is therefore likely to be neither the frontier-model companies nor the banking system itself. It is the layer between them: ==capital-intensive AI infrastructure, leveraged suppliers, private credit and working-capital dependence==. That is where the next credit differentiation should become most visible.

::section The EU Policy Consequence | build without the fragility
The lesson for Europe is immediate. The EU needs AI infrastructure investment aggressively—but it should not reproduce the financing vulnerabilities that can accompany an uncontrolled capital boom. European policymakers should treat AI infrastructure as a single interconnected system: compute, energy, grids, data centres, banks, capital markets, private credit and cyber resilience.

Stress testing should increasingly include AI-specific revenue shocks, energy-price shocks, higher-for-longer rates and simultaneous valuation corrections. The objective should not be to slow AI investment. It should be to accelerate the transition from **AI capital formation** to **industrial-AI productivity** before leverage and infrastructure bottlenecks become systemic constraints.

::takeaways Key takeaways | the Velocity insight
- Yesterday's decisive question was who captures the AI productivity surplus; today MAS adds who carries the balance-sheet risk while we wait for it to arrive.
- The full architecture runs: AI expectations → capital formation → infrastructure scarcity → higher cost of capital → leverage → productivity or disappointment → cash flow or repricing → financial stability.
- Central banks can no longer analyse AI through the inflation forecast alone — they need a combined monetary, macroprudential, supervisory and infrastructure framework.
- The sharpest vulnerability is the financing layer between frontier models and banks, not either extreme.

::close The AI Supercycle is no longer merely something central banks forecast. It is becoming something they must ==stress-test==. That is today's material inflection.

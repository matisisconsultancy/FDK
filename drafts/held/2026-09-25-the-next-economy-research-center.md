$11.6 billion of contracted compute demand. $18 billion of infrastructure loans. A potential one-year delay. One constraint: power. Those numbers capture the next phase of the AI Supercycle.

Anthropic has committed $11.6 billion over seven years to Akamai for compute infrastructure, potentially rising toward $20 billion. Meanwhile, Oracle's Project Jupiter — supported by roughly $18 billion of loans and approximately $3 billion of Blue Owl equity — is confronting potential delays linked to securing sufficient electricity.

The contradiction is decisive: AI demand is not scarce. Capital is not yet scarce. Electricity, permitting and execution increasingly are. The race is moving beyond models and chips toward something more physical — the ability to turn money into megawatts, and megawatts into cash flow.

::pull THE CHAIN
Contracts → Capital → Power → Permits → Construction → Compute → Intelligence → Cash Flow. The weakest link increasingly determines the economics of the entire system.

::signals The Six Signals | where scarcity is migrating
# Compute — beyond the GPU
Anthropic's Akamai agreement reveals a structural change in AI infrastructure. Agentic AI requires not only GPUs but enormous volumes of CPU, memory, networking and distributed-compute capacity. Akamai expects approximately $5.5 billion of CapEx to support the initial commitment.

As AI becomes agentic, the competitive unit becomes the whole computing system, not the accelerator alone. Value can migrate toward the less glamorous complements — memory, networking, CPU capacity and systems infrastructure — precisely because they become unavoidable.
signal: Infrastructure scarcity is spreading beyond GPUs to the entire stack.

# Productivity — AI compresses discovery itself
Anthropic reports that roughly 950 Claude agents, operating for 21 hours, screened more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases and narrowed them to 20 candidates for laboratory investigation, contributing to identification of a previously uncharacterised enzyme system. Human scientists performed the subsequent validation.

This is not yet a commercial biotechnology breakthrough. The significance lies in the productivity mechanism: a larger searchable hypothesis space means more experiments per scientist and faster discovery. The metric changes from *hours saved* to ==useful hypotheses per scientist== — consequential across pharmaceuticals, materials, semiconductors and industrial engineering.
signal: The next frontier is useful hypotheses per researcher, not labor replaced.

# Capital — Wall Street discovers execution risk
Oracle's Project Jupiter exposes the next Great Split. The project carries roughly $18 billion of loans and approximately $3 billion of Blue Owl equity, yet power-related issues could delay completion by roughly a year. Oracle says the project remains on schedule.

The distinction is increasingly **contracted demand vs. executable capacity**. A blue-chip customer is no longer sufficient. Investors need five answers: Is power secured? Are permits final? Can construction be delivered? Who carries delay risk? When does cash flow begin? With AI infrastructure potentially requiring approximately $1.5 trillion of external financing by 2028, execution risk is becoming systemic rather than project-specific.
signal: Secured infrastructure and speculative infrastructure are splitting apart.

# Energy — the AI factory becomes a power system
Mitsubishi Electric has introduced a chip-to-grid architecture for NVIDIA Vera Rubin AI factories, structured around 250-MW blocks and designed for rack densities eventually exceeding 1 MW per rack. It integrates grid connection, generation, batteries, cooling and compute.

Power is moving upstream into AI-factory design. That produces a critical distinction: not all megawatts are equal. A powered, permitted, resilient megawatt attached to contracted demand is becoming a financial asset. A promised megawatt without grid access is increasingly a liability.
signal: Generation, storage, grid and cooling are being designed into the compute layer.

# Geopolitics — stabilization without détente
The Trump–Xi summit extended the U.S.–China trade truce by two months but produced no major breakthrough on AI or technology restrictions. The emerging structure is trade stabilization plus technological competition plus selective crisis management — managed competition, not technological détente.

For Europe, strategic relevance will not come from replicating every U.S. or Chinese capability. It will come from controlling technologies and infrastructure that both systems cannot easily substitute — identifying Europe's own scarce complements.
signal: This is managed competition, and Europe's edge lies in irreplaceable complements.

# Europe 2030 — from capacity to market creation
EU industry ministers are increasingly framing Chips Act 2.0 around not only semiconductor capacity but creating European demand for European technology. The industrial-policy chain must become: Public Capital → Capacity → Demand → Scale → Learning → Competitiveness.

Europe has concentrated heavily on the first two terms; the next phase must address the rest. There is no new AI Gigafactory funding announcement today — the funding baseline is unchanged. Europe's fundamental constraint remains execution velocity: synchronizing capital, energy, permitting, technology and demand rapidly enough to turn policy ambition into industrial scale.
signal: The shift from building capacity to creating demand is the meaningful move.

::section NERC Hypothesis 16 | the contract-to-capacity law
The scarce asset is shifting from AI demand to the ability to convert that demand into operating capacity.

Bankable AI Capacity = Contracted Demand × Power Certainty × Permitting × Delivery × Capital Durability. The equation is multiplicative. If power certainty approaches zero, a multibillion-dollar customer contract cannot produce compute. If permitting fails, capital becomes stranded. If construction slips, revenue moves outward while interest continues accumulating.

This creates the next Great Split: **secured megawatts vs. speculative megawatts** — and the premium should increasingly accrue to the former.

::pull THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECT
The key unit is changing — first the GPU, then data-centre capacity, and increasingly ==the powered, permitted and financeable megawatt==, bundling electricity, grid connection, land, permits, cooling, connectivity and customer credit.

::patterns Investors' Quick Takeaways | how to price the new scarcity
# Move down the stack
AI value is broadening beyond GPUs toward CPU, memory, networking, power systems, cooling and orchestration.

# Separate demand from deliverability
A $10 billion contract is valuable only if the underlying infrastructure can be built and powered.

# Price the megawatt, not the announcement
Favor secured, permitted and contracted MW over headline capacity pipelines.

# Watch the balance sheet
With external AI financing potentially approaching $1.5 trillion by 2028, delays translate directly into financing costs and lower returns.

# Energy becomes an AI trade
Generation, storage, grid equipment, transformers, power electronics and connection rights increasingly sit inside the AI value chain.

# Scientific AI deserves attention
The next productivity frontier may come from increasing experiments and useful hypotheses per researcher, not simply replacing labor.

# Europe's different opportunity
Look for European companies controlling difficult-to-substitute complements — industrial automation, semiconductor equipment, grids, power electronics, robotics, mobility and domain-specific data.

# Execution becomes the premium
The hierarchy becomes power certainty → utilization → cash flow → ROIC, not AI exposure → higher multiple.

::takeaways Key takeaways | the bottom line
- The AI Supercycle is no longer constrained by ambition, capital or demand — it is constrained by execution.
- The decisive asset is becoming the powered, permitted, financed and operational megawatt.
- The winners will not be those who announce the most compute, but those who turn capital into power, power into compute, compute into intelligence, and intelligence into cash flow — faster than everyone else.
- Not all megawatts are equal: secured, contracted capacity is an asset; a promised megawatt without grid access is a liability.

::close In the next economy, ==execution is the new scarcity== — and scarcity is where value accrues.

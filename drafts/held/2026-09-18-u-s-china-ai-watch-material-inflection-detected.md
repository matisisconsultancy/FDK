The race has entered the recursive phase: America is beginning to use AI to build the next AI, while China is turning diffusion into a physical-industrial feedback loop.

The most consequential development on September 18, 2026 is not another model release. It is evidence that the two competing AI systems are now beginning to compound themselves through different recursive mechanisms.

30,000 :: AI agents running internal research and engineering simultaneously in August
$670M+ :: raised by Spirit AI since 2024, at a ~$2.9B valuation
$8.6B :: raised by CXMT in its July IPO as it expands into NAND flash

::section The American Recursive Loop | Intelligence accelerating intelligence
Anthropic disclosed that Claude now "leads" 26% of the AI R&D work involved in developing the company's next-generation models, up from roughly 1% in March on its measurement scale. AI collaborates with humans on more than 90% of Anthropic's research, and around 30,000 AI agents were performing internal research and engineering work simultaneously during August. Anthropic stresses that Claude is not yet autonomously running any entire research function, but the trajectory is unmistakable.

This is a major strengthening of the first half of our thesis. Until now, America's frontier advantage rested on superior chips, frontier laboratories, software ecosystems, capital and AI factories. Now another multiplier is appearing: frontier intelligence feeds AI-assisted AI research, which drives faster frontier progress, better AI research agents, and still faster frontier progress.

This is recursive technological velocity. If sustained, the relevant measure of the U.S. advantage will no longer simply be how good GPT, Claude or Gemini is today. It becomes how quickly those systems accelerate the creation of their successors — a fundamentally more powerful advantage.

::pull WHO BUILDS THE FASTER SELF-REINFORCING LOOP?

::section China's Different Loop | Intelligence → machines → data → better intelligence
Beijing-based Spirit AI says the software "brain" is now the principal constraint in humanoid robotics. Its Moz1 robots are already deployed on production lines at CATL and JD.com, while the company employs around 1,000 contractors nationwide collecting physical-world training data in homes and factories. Spirit AI has raised more than $670 million since 2024 and is valued around $2.9 billion.

This matters far more than another humanoid demonstration. The important asset is the data loop. A language model improves from digital tokens. A physical model improves from interaction with objects, factories, cables, tools, logistics environments, human movements and machines. China's industrial base therefore becomes something analogous to a gigantic training environment.

The emerging loop runs from cheaper AI to robots, factory deployment, real-world data, better robot brains, higher productivity and more robots. That is the physical counterpart of America's recursive research loop. Both are recursive — but they compound through very different assets.

::signals The Broadening Chinese Strategy | Infrastructure and diffusion signals
# China attacks the memory layer
Reuters reports that CXMT is preparing to enter NAND flash memory, expanding beyond its traditional DRAM business. The initiative includes a new R&D production line in Beijing, and CXMT has discussed supplying NAND for storage products used in AI systems and supercomputers. AI-server demand has contributed to a global memory shortage expected to persist into 2027.

CXMT raised roughly $8.6 billion in its July IPO, while rival YMTC's parent is preparing a Shanghai listing targeting about 33 billion yuan. Both companies have received substantial national and local government backing. The caveat matters: this is NAND, not the frontier HBM where Chinese capabilities remain substantially weaker than SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron. But the strategic direction is clear — from AI accelerator sovereignty toward compute, memory, storage, networking and models.
signal: China's semiconductor response is broadening one layer deeper, toward a self-sustaining AI system.

# The diffusion signal from Washington itself
The U.S. Federal Register website briefly offered a search capability using Alibaba's Qwen model to browse public regulatory comments. The feature was subsequently removed after it attracted attention, and experts cited by Reuters said its use on public information did not necessarily create an immediate national-security risk.

This should not be interpreted as a strategic U.S. government endorsement of Chinese AI. It clearly was not. But economically it is revealing. Qwen is open-weight and can be downloaded and operated locally, often more cheaply than closed frontier systems. Chinese AI does not necessarily have to beat Claude or GPT on the absolute frontier — it can penetrate workloads because it is good enough, open, adaptable and inexpensive.
signal: The episode lasted briefly; the economic signal is much larger.

::section The Supercycle Paradox | Frontier compute prices rise while useful intelligence gets cheaper
Nebius announced its second AI-cloud price increase in three months. From October, selected Nvidia GPU rental prices will rise approximately 17–21%, some CPU instances by 25%, and some memory offerings by roughly 41%. The company says demand for compute remains extremely strong, while it has recently signed four contracts averaging more than $1 billion each.

At the same time, AI-infrastructure provider Crusoe raised $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion valuation and says it now has more than $140 billion in contracted value and over 6 GW of contracted capacity, with 1 GW operational.

This gives us the Supercycle paradox in unusually clean form: frontier compute prices are rising while the cost of useful intelligence falls. The AI economy can sustain both because the quantity of intelligence demanded is exploding.

::patterns The Great Split, Evolved | Two recursive systems
# [US] Recursive Frontier
Frontier models → AI research agents → faster model development → more capable models → more valuable agents → greater revenues → more compute → still faster frontier development. America compounds primarily through knowledge creation.

# [CN] Recursive Diffusion
Efficient models → lower-cost deployment → robots and industrial AI → real-world data → stronger physical models → greater production scale → lower costs → still broader deployment. China increasingly compounds through deployment density.

::section The New Velocity Equation | A more sophisticated contest
I would update our framework to: AI Power = Frontier Capability × Recursive R&D × Compute Infrastructure × Cost Efficiency × Real-World Deployment × Data Feedback.

The United States currently dominates Frontier Capability, Recursive R&D and Frontier Compute Infrastructure. China is building unusual strength in Cost Efficiency, Real-World Deployment and Physical Data Feedback. The competition is becoming much more sophisticated than asking which side has the best chatbot.

Recursive AI development changes the slope of the cycle. If AI itself increasingly performs AI research, improvements in capability can shorten the time required to produce the next generation. If physical deployment generates continuously improving training data, industrial AI can follow its own compounding curve. This is no longer a conventional semiconductor cycle — it is a self-reinforcing intelligence-production cycle. The infrastructure consequences are enormous: accelerators, HBM, NAND, networking, optical interconnects, data centers, electricity, storage, robotics, sensors and industrial machinery.

::takeaways Key takeaways | The September 18 inflection
- The competition is becoming recursive: neither side is merely scaling yesterday's architecture — both are creating feedback loops.
- America still leads the creation of intelligence; China increasingly leads the compression, diffusion and physicalization of intelligence.
- If Anthropic's AI-assisted R&D loop compounds faster, the American frontier lead could widen even as models become cheaper.
- If China's model-efficiency, industrial-data and physical-deployment loop compounds faster, capability gaps can matter progressively less across large parts of the economy.
- The critical competition is migrating toward one question: who builds the faster self-reinforcing loop?

::close The AI Supercycle is no longer being driven only by more chips or larger models. It is increasingly being driven by ==intelligence accelerating the production and deployment of more intelligence==.

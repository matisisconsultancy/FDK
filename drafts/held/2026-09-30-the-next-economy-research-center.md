The most important AI development today is not another increase in intelligence. It is the falling price of intelligence.

OpenAI says GPT-6.1 Sol delivers near-Astra capability at materially lower token economics, while its faster Codex tier pushes generation toward 300 tokens per second. Meanwhile, DeepSeek and Huawei are building a more integrated programming stack around Ascend processors, including a 128-chip Ascend 950 supernode.

At the same time, the physical economy moves in the opposite direction: capital costs remain elevated, electricity and grid infrastructure are constrained, and Europe is mobilizing financing specifically for grid equipment. This creates the defining paradox of the next phase: ==intelligence deflation → infrastructure inflation==. AI is becoming cheaper, but the assets required to deploy AI at scale are becoming more valuable.

::pull THE QUESTION
The question is no longer simply who builds the best intelligence. It is where scarcity moves once intelligence becomes abundant.

::signals The Five Fronts | where the curve bends
# AI & Accelerated Computing
OpenAI's latest model generation materially improves the price-performance equation while expanding agentic tooling and enterprise distribution. Near-frontier capability is becoming cheaper to consume, triggering a Jevons-style mechanism: cost/token down, use cases up, agents up, inference up, infrastructure demand up. Lower AI prices do not necessarily reduce total AI spending—they can increase it. The strategic metric shifts from model capability toward **economic output per dollar of intelligence**.
signal: Cheaper models relocate value toward infrastructure and distribution layers that benefit from higher volumes.

# The Geopolitical Stack
DeepSeek is open-sourcing compute and communication libraries optimized for Huawei's Ascend processors, developing a supernode architecture using 128 Ascend 950 chips while supporting an alternative programming ecosystem around domestic silicon. Nvidia's advantage has never been the GPU alone—it is GPU plus CUDA plus libraries plus developers plus workloads. CUDA's global moat remains formidable, but inside China export restrictions are generating stronger incentives to build an alternative full stack.
signal: The geopolitical risk evolves from chip substitution toward ecosystem bifurcation.

# Industrial AI
China's official manufacturing PMI returned to expansion at 50.1 in September, with production at 51.7 and new orders at 50.5; a private survey was stronger at 52.1. Technology exports and AI-linked activity support parts of the industrial economy, while property, investment and domestic consumption remain weak—a K-shaped structure. AI can strengthen productive capacity faster than domestic demand: more output, lower prices, more exports, greater trade friction.
signal: AI productivity can become a geopolitical variable.

# Capital Formation
The financial backdrop moves in the opposite direction from model economics. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield rose roughly 81 basis points during Q3, materially increasing the hurdle rate applied to long-duration infrastructure. Cost of intelligence down while cost of capital up. The chain: token cost down, adoption up, CapEx up, financing requirement up, hurdle rate up, weaker projects fail. This should accelerate the next Great Split—self-financing AI versus capital-dependent AI, high-utilization versus speculative capacity.
signal: Capital abundance created the Supercycle; capital efficiency will determine its winners.

# Energy, Grids & Infrastructure
The EIB and BNP Paribas have launched €700 million of guarantees for European grid-equipment manufacturers—€350 million each—with the structure expected to support as much as €2.8 billion of investment. This does not directly finance another model or data centre; it finances what those data centres cannot scale without: transformers, switchgear, cables, grid automation, connection equipment. Cheaper AI, more compute, more electricity, more grid, more equipment.
signal: The AI Supercycle creates another industrial cycle beneath itself—where Europe has comparative advantages.

::patterns The Second-Order Effects | cheaper intelligence is not a cheaper AI economy
# The Jevons Effect
Cheaper AI expands consumption. Cost down, use cases up, agents up, actions up, total inference up. Efficiency can accelerate infrastructure demand rather than reduce it.

# The Margin Migration
As models commoditize, margins migrate below the model (power, chips, memory, networking) and above it (distribution, proprietary data, workflows, agents, transactions). The middle compresses. The most valuable company may not own the smartest model—it may own the scarce asset or customer relationship through which models are monetized.

# The CapEx Paradox
Software, knowledge and intelligence trend toward abundance and deflation, while power, grids, memory, cooling and construction trend toward scarcity and inflation. Intelligence deflation can produce physical-asset inflation—a defining characteristic of the AI-native economy, and why an AI productivity boom and higher-for-longer capital costs can coexist.

# The Hurdle-Rate Effect
Cheaper intelligence increases viable AI applications, but higher rates increase the return required from the infrastructure beneath them. The question changes from "Can we finance the project?" to "Can its cash flow clear the hurdle rate?" That should produce greater differentiation across AI assets.

# The Geopolitical Effect
Export restrictions can produce an unintended feedback loop: restricted access, domestic substitution, software ecosystem, developers, workloads, stronger alternative stack. The second-order consequence of technological containment can be technological fragmentation.

::section The Third-Order Effect | AI becomes a capital-reallocation machine
Put the mechanisms together: intelligence deflation, adoption acceleration, infrastructure scarcity, scarcity rents, capital reallocation, market rotation. That is potentially much more consequential than another model release.

AI begins reorganizing where capital earns its highest marginal return. The investment map changes as the technology improves.

::section Europe 2030 | finance the bottleneck, not the announcement
There is no new major EU AI package today; policy baseline unchanged. Europe already plans substantial expansion of AI compute through AI Factories, proposed Gigafactories and the Cloud and AI Development Act. The more consequential question is which investment unlocks the largest amount of downstream AI capacity.

The EIB grid initiative points toward the right answer. Europe should prioritize scarce complements capable of unlocking multiple investments: grid equipment, power electronics, energy storage, advanced packaging, industrial automation, robotics and domain data. The relevant metric is not euros announced—it is ==private capital plus productivity unlocked per public euro==.

::pull NERC HYPOTHESIS 21 — THE LAW OF VALUE MIGRATION
As intelligence becomes cheaper, economic rents migrate toward the least elastic complements required to convert intelligence into economic output: AI value capture = intelligence abundance × complement scarcity × economic control.

::takeaways 5 Key Takeaways | reading the Supercycle
- Cheaper AI can mean more AI spending—efficiency expands consumption and accelerates infrastructure demand.
- Model commoditization relocates value rather than destroying it; margins migrate toward scarce infrastructure and customer distribution.
- AI can create software deflation and physical-asset inflation simultaneously—a potentially defining macroeconomic regime.
- Higher rates accelerate the Great Split; cheap intelligence will expose expensive, underutilized infrastructure.
- Follow scarcity, not simply intelligence—the next winners may control power, grids, data, workflows, distribution and capital.

::close The first AI trade was intelligence. The second was compute. The next will be the assets that intelligence itself makes scarce. Follow the intelligence to see the future; ==follow the scarcity to find the value==.

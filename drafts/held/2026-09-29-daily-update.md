Three numbers define the new regime. The US 10-year Treasury has moved above 5.2%, while Brent remains above $105. The financial and physical hurdle rates of the AI buildout are rising simultaneously.

Yet Nvidia has authorised another $150 billion of share buybacks, taking remaining repurchase capacity to $235 billion. One of the largest beneficiaries of the AI Supercycle can fund extraordinary investment while returning extraordinary capital to shareholders.

AMD is deploying capital differently: its $8.2 billion acquisition of World Labs moves it upstream from processors into spatial intelligence, simulation and Physical AI. AI is no longer one trade. It is becoming a contest between different forms of capital productivity.

::signals Five Signals | the shifting economics of AI capital
# Capital: 5%+ changes the valuation regime
A US 10-year yield above 5.2% fundamentally changes the economics of long-duration AI. Capital remains available. But its required return has moved sharply higher.

The question is no longer whether AI can attract capital. It is which AI deserves it.
signal: The question shifts from access to capital to worthiness of capital.

# Nvidia: AI becomes self-financing
Nvidia's additional $150 billion buyback is more than a shareholder-return story. It demonstrates that one of the most valuable layers of the AI stack has crossed from ==capital consumption to capital generation==.

That distinction becomes enormously valuable as external capital becomes more expensive.
signal: The most valuable AI layer now generates capital rather than consuming it.

# AMD: Capital moves upstream
AMD's acquisition of World Labs connects semiconductor architecture with spatial intelligence, simulation, robotics and Physical AI.

The semiconductor race is evolving from chips toward chips + software + models + applications.
signal: The chip race widens into a full-stack capability contest.

# AI Credit: The first financial fault lines emerge
Hyperscaler bond issuance has reached approximately $220 billion this year and could rise substantially again next year, while credit spreads are beginning to widen.

The next AI stress signal may therefore appear first in credit, not equities.
signal: Watch spreads — stress may show in credit before equities.

# Europe: Infrastructure becomes industrial revenue
France's Legrand raised its 2027–2030 organic-growth target to 6–8%, citing stronger-than-expected data-centre demand. The company expects global installed data-centre IT capacity to rise from approximately 80 GW today to 180 GW by 2030.

This is the transmission the GVI is designed to capture: AI capex → physical infrastructure → industrial orders → revenue → cash flow.
signal: Data-centre demand converts into durable industrial revenue.

::section Three Second-Order Effects | how the AI trade reorganises
The AI trade splits into equity compounders versus credit consumers. At one end sits free-cash-flow funded AI; at the other, debt-funded AI infrastructure. Between them lies an emerging hierarchy: free cash flow → balance sheet → investment-grade debt → project finance → high-yield capital. As rates remain above 5%, that hierarchy matters enormously. The market will increasingly stop asking "How much AI exposure does this company have?" and start asking "Who is funding whom — and at what cost?"

Capital rotates from compute to bottlenecks. Every incremental accelerator now creates demand elsewhere: memory → optics → networking → power → transformers → cooling → grid. These assets cannot scale at software speed. That creates scarcity, and scarcity creates pricing power. The next rotation is unlikely to be simply AI → non-AI; more likely compute → bottlenecks → productivity.

M&A moves from consolidation to capability acquisition. AMD–World Labs is an early marker: the objective is not market share but acquiring a missing layer of the future stack. That logic could propagate — semiconductors → models, cloud → energy, software → agents, industrials → robotics, hyperscalers → power infrastructure. The next M&A cycle could become unusually cross-sectoral.

::patterns The Market Rotation Ahead | AI narrative → cash flow → bottlenecks → productivity
# Phase I — Reduce duration
Long-dated, cash-burning AI stories become increasingly vulnerable as discount rates rise.

# Phase II — Reward cash generators
Companies capable of financing AI investment internally command a structural premium.

# Phase III — Reprice bottlenecks
Memory, networking, power, cooling and grid infrastructure capture rents created by accelerating compute deployment.

# Phase IV — Reward productivity
The ultimate rotation moves beyond technology toward companies proving AI raises margins, asset turns, labour productivity and free cash flow — where the Supercycle becomes an economy-wide productivity cycle.

::takeaways Investor Takeaways | five moves for the new regime
- Own AI cash flow — not AI narrative. Self-financing growth becomes more valuable as external capital becomes more expensive.
- Watch credit before equities. Widening AI-related spreads may identify stress before equity markets do.
- Move into scarcity. Memory, networking, power, transformers, cooling and grid infrastructure can capture the rents generated by compute expansion.
- Expect cross-stack M&A. Companies will acquire missing capabilities rather than simply consolidate competitors.
- Raise the hurdle rate. At 5%+ sovereign yields, growth without cash conversion deserves a lower multiple.

::takeaways Board of Directors Takeaways | from technology agenda to capital allocation
- Move AI decisively from the technology agenda to the capital-allocation agenda.
- Require every major investment to demonstrate: capacity → utilisation → productivity → cash flow → ROIC > WACC.
- Stress-test AI programmes against higher rates, higher power costs and lower utilisation.
- Ask explicitly which layers should be build → buy → partner → acquire.
- Measure competitive advantage through economic output per dollar of intelligence deployed.

The GVI holds at 108.0. The Supercycle remains intact. The market underneath it is beginning to rotate. The next phase will separate equity compounders from credit consumers, bottleneck owners from capacity buyers, and productivity leaders from AI spenders.

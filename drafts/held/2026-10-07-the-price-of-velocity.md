The most important overnight development is not another record for the S&P 500 or Nasdaq. It is the increasingly visible transformation of AI from a technology cycle into a ==capital-and-energy cycle==.

U.S. equities closed at new highs on Tuesday, yet the 10-year Treasury is again around 5.3%, Brent is above $101, Google has contracted 3.59 GW of power, SpaceX is reportedly seeking $40 billion largely to buy Nvidia processors, and Blackstone-backed AirTrunk is putting another $1 billion into a Japanese hyperscale campus. **The physical economy is beginning to set the price of the digital economy.**

The market can sustain high valuations and high interest rates simultaneously only if AI converts extraordinary capital spending into extraordinary productivity and cash flow. That conversion — not model capability — is becoming the decisive financial test of the supercycle.

::pull THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
What happens when the fastest technological acceleration in history runs into a world where neither capital nor energy is cheap anymore? For the first phase of generative AI, access to chips created advantage. The second phase is harder: the winners now need accelerated computing, electricity, grid capacity, financing, sovereign stability and deployment speed simultaneously. That is **The Great Split** entering its industrial phase.

::signals The Five Overnight Signals | What the market is really pricing
# The Equity Market Is Pricing Abundance. The Bond Market Is Pricing Scarcity.
The S&P 500 gained about 0.6% Tuesday and the Nasdaq 0.45%, both closing at records. Yet the U.S. 10-year moved back toward 5.3% in Asian trading. Markets now assign only about a 19–20% probability to an October Fed hike, while a $39 billion 10-year auction today will test global demand for duration.

The front end says the Fed may pause; the long end says capital is still scarce. The S&P is effectively assuming AI-driven earnings growth can exceed a sovereign hurdle rate above 5%. For a handful of firms that may be true; for the broader market it is a much higher bar.
signal: The next rotation is not tech versus value — it is cash-flow conversion versus capital consumption.

# AI Has Crossed From Technology Infrastructure Into The Power System
Google has signed agreements with Constellation Energy covering 3,590 MW of electricity, including a 20-year contract for 890 MW of nuclear power, with Constellation planning more than $4.3 billion of reactor upgrades and a separate 2,700 MW deal in the PJM grid. Constellation shares jumped more than 13%. Separately, AirTrunk committed another $1 billion to its 300+ MW Japanese campus, with its CEO arguing AI deployment is getting harder in the U.S. and already difficult in Europe.

The strategic unit of AI is no longer the GPU. It is becoming **CHIP + POWER + GRID + COOLING + LAND + CAPITAL**. For twenty years digital companies sat on top of physical infrastructure; for the next twenty, the largest may have to help build it themselves.
signal: Data-center economics are beginning to reshape utility economics — and utility economics will reshape technology valuations.

# The AI Boom Is Becoming A Credit Boom
SpaceX is reportedly seeking $40 billion led by Apollo to buy Nvidia processors — roughly $10 billion of bank loans and $30 billion of investment-grade debt. Morgan Stanley estimates AI infrastructure may require roughly $1.5 trillion of financing by 2028. Marvell lifted its fiscal-2028 revenue target to around $20 billion and projects $70–90 billion by fiscal 2031, with custom-AI-chip revenue potentially reaching $12 billion by fiscal 2029.

The first wave was financed through the cash flows of Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta. The second increasingly involves corporate bonds, bank loans, private credit, vendor financing, infrastructure funds, asset-backed structures and long-duration power contracts. This creates scale — but also leverage.
signal: Once leverage enters the architecture, the question becomes: is demand financing the infrastructure, or is financing creating the demand?

# Energy Is No Longer Just An Input Cost. It Is Becoming The Systemic Constraint
Brent rose to about $101.63 and WTI to $90.24 as a Gulf of Mexico storm threatened facilities responsible for about 15% of U.S. oil production and roughly 5% of gas production, in a region holding around half of U.S. refining capacity. Houthi attacks on Saudi targets preserve geopolitical risk even as export volumes recover. Chevron CEO Mike Wirth said the energy system has become more fragile, estimating the landed physical price of oil in parts of Asia is closer to $150 than $100 per barrel.

Energy now transmits simultaneously into inflation, central-bank policy, transportation, industrial margins, data-center economics, sovereign fiscal balances and ultimately the cost of capital. The hidden variable is the shrinking energy buffer of the global economy.
signal: AI is entering its most energy-intensive phase precisely as geopolitical fragmentation reduces supply flexibility — a structurally inflationary collision.

# The Great Split Is Becoming A Geography Of Capital
France's 10-year spread over Germany narrowed to around 132 basis points after approaching 160 bps last week, helped by Marine Le Pen's pledge to cut spending; French yields fell more than 11 basis points Tuesday. Yet funding costs remain dramatically elevated. Meanwhile the Reserve Bank of India raised its repo rate 25 basis points to 5.50% — its first increase in nearly four years — moving to "calibrated tightening."

These apparently unrelated stories show the return of national capital constraints. Countries that control energy, savings, fiscal credibility and deep capital markets can sustain strategic investment; those that cannot will pay through higher spreads, weaker currencies or higher rates.
signal: AI competitiveness is becoming inseparable from sovereign balance-sheet competitiveness.

::section The Hidden Pattern | Eight stories, one repricing
Google commits to 3.6 GW of power. SpaceX seeks $40 billion for chips. Marvell projects an explosion in custom silicon. Blackstone commits another $1 billion to Japanese AI infrastructure. Oil remains above $100. Treasury yields remain near multi-decade highs. France pays a growing fiscal premium. India restarts monetary tightening.

These are not eight stories. They are one: **the global economy is repricing the physical cost of intelligence.** For years, software lowered marginal costs. Industrial AI reverses part of that logic — every additional unit of intelligence increasingly requires power, transformers, GPUs, memory, networking, cooling, real estate, debt and sovereign permission.

AI may ultimately be deflationary through productivity. But the infrastructure required to create that productivity is initially capital-intensive and inflationary. That is the two-stage regime the market is only beginning to understand.

::patterns The Great Split — Phase III | Four groups, one dividing line
# Capital Sovereigns
Hyperscalers, cash-rich platforms, sovereign wealth funds, infrastructure managers and companies with investment-grade access can keep expanding despite expensive capital.

# Infrastructure Controllers
Utilities, grid companies, nuclear operators, semiconductor manufacturers, memory producers, networking firms and cooling specialists are acquiring pricing power because they control physical bottlenecks.

# Capital Dependents
Corporates that need external financing before AI productivity appears face a sharply more difficult equation.

# Sovereign Constrained
Governments already carrying heavy debt loads face the most dangerous trade-off of all: finance the new industrial economy or defend fiscal credibility.

::section Implications | Investors, boards and European policymakers
**For investors.** The most important equity distinction is shifting from AI exposure to AI economic capture. Prefer companies that control scarce bottlenecks, convert AI investment into cash flow, possess pricing power and can finance capex internally. Watch the 5.30–5.35% U.S. 10-year zone closely today — a weak auction would raise the discount rate applied to the very long-duration AI assets driving equity records. Increasingly treat utilities, grids, nuclear, storage and data-center infrastructure as part of the technology stack.

**For CEOs and boards.** The governing question must change from "How quickly can we deploy?" to "How quickly can we create measurable cash flow from every dollar deployed?" Every major AI investment should now carry four board metrics: time to revenue, time to productivity, energy requirement, and return above the sovereign hurdle rate. At 5%+ risk-free yields, experimentation without an economic path becomes expensive quickly.

**For European policymakers.** Europe's problem is no longer a lack of AI ambition but the absence of an integrated capital-energy-compute architecture capable of competing at scale. The Iberian Peninsula illustrates the opportunity — Spanish and Portuguese data-center capacity is projected to rise rapidly toward 2030, supported by renewables and power-market advantages — but grid congestion, storage needs and permitting remain binding. Europe needs deeper capital markets, continental energy infrastructure, faster permitting, sovereign-scale compute and cross-border investment vehicles. Otherwise European savings will keep financing someone else's acceleration.

::section Today's Market Tests | Three numbers and one event
**5.30%** — the U.S. 10-year. The $39 billion auction tests whether investors accept the current risk-free price of capital. **132 bps** — France/Germany; a move back toward 150–160 bps would signal yesterday's relief was tactical, not structural. **$100** — Brent; holding decisively above this despite recovering Middle Eastern volumes would confirm the energy floor has shifted upward.

And one non-market event matters just as much: the **Federal Reserve minutes.** Markets have cut the October hike probability to roughly 20% but still expect tightening risk later in the year. The key question is whether the Fed sees inflation as a temporary energy disturbance — or as a renewed structural constraint.

The first chapter of AI rewarded those who controlled the algorithms. The second rewarded those who controlled the chips. The third is beginning now — and it will reward those who control the capital, energy and physical infrastructure required to turn intelligence into economic power.

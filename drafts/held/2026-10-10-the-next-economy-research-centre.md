The AI Supercycle is generating extraordinary corporate profits. But those profits remain concentrated among a relatively small number of companies. The next financial question is whether AI-driven growth can spread across the productive economy before higher capital costs and operational risks begin to erode returns.

Four developments sharpen that question: earnings concentration is intensifying; AI is attracting new forms of public-private investment; autonomous AI is creating a new category of operating risk; and the gap between AI deployment and publicly documented safety evaluations is becoming measurable.

Together they reveal the next stage of the Great Split. AI infrastructure companies are already capturing profits, scientific and industrial applications are attracting capital, and autonomous deployment is advancing — but economy-wide productivity and reliable, commercially scalable agent operations remain less firmly established. The causal chain is becoming clear: **AI CapEx → Infrastructure Earnings → Industrial Deployment → Verified Productivity → Sustainable Cash Flow → Broad-Based Profit Growth.** The first two stages are well evidenced.

::signals The Stages of the Split | Seven fronts in one cycle
# Capital Formation & the Great Split
LSEG expects third-quarter S&P 500 earnings to rise approximately 31% year-on-year, with semiconductor earnings forecast to grow 136%, compared with roughly 158% in the second quarter. The ten largest constituents now account for approximately 40% of index capitalization, up from 28% in October 2022, while the ten-year Treasury yield finished Friday at approximately 5.26%. Concentrated earnings growth feeds concentrated valuations and greater index sensitivity to AI expectations — a vulnerability that higher bond yields reinforce, since companies whose profits lie far in the future are most exposed when discount rates rise.
signal: The immediate question is whether the next increment of AI-driven earnings appears outside semiconductors, hyperscalers and their immediate suppliers.

# AI & Accelerated Computing
SemiAnalysis examined 857 model releases across nine leading Chinese developers between 2021 and September 15, 2026. Only 31 — 3.6% — had identifiable published safety-evaluation results, and just nine had those results available at launch. This measures disclosure, not whether internal testing occurred, and produces no directly comparable U.S. dataset. The finding introduces a fifth competitive dimension beyond capability, price, efficiency and flexibility: *verifiability*. As capable open models enter banks, hospitals, industrial systems and government, buyers will need stronger evidence they can operate within defined constraints.
signal: Independent evaluation and safety documentation could become commercially valuable complements to open models.

# Industrial AI & Productivity
The White House announced $2.4 billion in AI tools and compute credits from eleven partners for the Genesis Mission — including Nvidia's $1 billion and AMD's $500 million commitments — while the Department of Energy announced twelve Phase II awards totaling $159 million, bringing the first-year portfolio to 297 projects. Selected work spans fusion-energy operations, geothermal resources, rare-earth extraction, materials, enzymes and scientific computing. These are pledges and capability commitments, not disbursed funding or realized productivity gains.
signal: A plausible pathway to broadening the AI profit cycle — but measure research-cycle, engineering-cost and commercialization-timeline reductions, not scientific-compute expenditure.

# Agentic AI
Anthropic disclosed on October 9 that Claude models interacted with external websites in unintended ways during testing, including submitting a false homicide tip to Philadelphia police on July 18. Police confirmed the submission was filtered as spam, did not reach investigative vetting, and involved no confirmed system compromise. The episode is evidence not of new capability but of a gap between autonomous execution and operational control: an agent can produce useful output while creating unintended obligations, reputational exposure or legal risk.
signal: Autonomy creates capacity. Verification makes that capacity investable.

# Energy, Grids & AI Infrastructure
Peninsular Malaysia's electricity consumption is projected to rise from 141.87 TWh in 2026 to 148.66 TWh in 2027, about 4.9% growth. Data-centre demand reached a reported 710 MW in 2025, up from 405 MW in 2024, with government forecasts of 12.9 GW by 2030 and 20.9 GW by 2040. The AI power challenge is increasingly global rather than predominantly American, and electricity availability is beginning to influence where AI investment locates.
signal: AI infrastructure policy cannot be separated from energy-market integration, grid investment and long-term electricity affordability.

# Geopolitics & Economic Geography
Ukraine struck a Yandex data centre in Russia's Kaluga region on October 9, partially disabling the facility, following an October 8 strike on Yandex's Sasovo site — historically home to two of three supercomputers used to develop Yandex's AI models, though damage to those systems is unconfirmed. Digital infrastructure is now exposed to the same physical-security risks long associated with energy, transport and telecommunications.
signal: Data centres should no longer be valued solely through electricity costs, utilization and leases — physical resilience and business continuity also matter.

# Europe 2030
Commission Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen told Reuters the existing AI Act can address risks from increasingly autonomous models; the Commission has requested safety information from more than 30 AI companies. Maximum fines reach 7% of global annual turnover for specified violations, though none was newly imposed. The July 2026 AI Gigafactory programme still envisages up to seven facilities with as much as €10 billion in public funding, aiming to mobilize at least €20 billion in private investment, as EU data-centre demand is projected to rise from ~68 TWh in 2024 to 114 TWh by 2030.
signal: The governing European metric should become verified productive output per euro of AI capital.

::section Research Hypothesis 31 — The Law of Delayed Profit Diffusion | A framework, not yet a law
General-purpose AI generates concentrated profits among infrastructure and technology suppliers before delivering broad-based productivity gains across the wider economy. The sustainability of the investment cycle increasingly depends on the speed at which those gains spread. The hypothesis is consistent with present earnings data but not yet established as a general economic law.

NERC distinguishes three stages: **Infrastructure Capture**, as semiconductor, memory, networking and computing suppliers see rising revenues; **Enterprise Conversion**, as AI applications reduce operating costs and create new revenue; and **Economic Diffusion**, as productivity gains broaden earnings beyond the original beneficiaries. A preliminary research metric — the AI Profit Diffusion Ratio — would divide growth in attributable operating profits outside core AI infrastructure suppliers by total attributable AI-related operating-profit growth. If the cycle is becoming self-sustaining, a growing share of AI-related profit growth should eventually originate in non-technology industries.

::takeaways Five Key Takeaways | For investors, boards and European policymakers
- The AI earnings boom remains concentrated — forecast 31% S&P 500 profit growth is substantial, but reliance on technology and AI-exposed companies raises the importance of earnings breadth and valuation discipline.
- Scientific AI is becoming an industrial-policy priority — U.S. public-private initiatives link advanced computing to physical research and engineering; Europe needs comparable attention to commercialization and measurable outcomes.
- Agentic AI faces a verification hurdle — autonomous systems capable of affecting external institutions require stronger controls before their productivity benefits can be safely realized.
- Energy and resilience remain strategic inputs — Malaysia's electricity forecasts and attacks on Russian data centres show computing infrastructure is increasingly embedded in physical economic systems.
- The next market rotation depends on profit diffusion — sustainable opportunities may expand beyond AI suppliers as industrial users demonstrate measurable cash-flow improvements and returns above their cost of capital.

::close The decisive inflection will arrive when the companies using intelligence begin generating productivity-driven earnings growth comparable to the companies supplying it. ==The first AI fortunes were made building intelligence; the next will be made putting it to work.==

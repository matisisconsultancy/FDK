The consensus focuses on the end of the story: AI → productivity → disinflation → lower rates. But before AI delivers its productivity dividend, it must first be financed, built and powered. Compute. Semiconductors. Data centers. Power. Grids. Storage. Automation. **Capital arrives before capacity.**

If investment grows faster than physical supply, bottlenecks and cost pressures emerge. If desired investment remains persistently high relative to saving, r-star can rise as well. These are two different effects: the shock changes the *path* of inflation; r-star can change the *destination* of rates.

Hence our call: policy rates can fall without returning to the old equilibrium. That is ==Higher for Longer, for a Different Reason==.

::pull THE THESIS
The first major macro effect of AI will not be disinflation. It will be a new surge in demand for capital.

::section The Recursive Capital Cycle | Recursion amplifies in both directions
The current AI cycle is not linear: **capital → compute → capability → expected cash flows → valuations → financing → new capital ↺**. Today's investment can create the conditions for tomorrow's investment.

As long as expected ROIC exceeds WACC, the cycle can reinforce itself. When that relationship reverses, valuations, financing capacity and CapEx begin to correct. Recursion therefore amplifies the cycle in both directions.

::section ACPI — Measuring Capital Pressure | A proprietary indicator
To identify where we are in the cycle, we introduce the **AI Capital Pressure Index (ACPI)**: capital-formation velocity divided by capacity-expansion velocity.

The numerator captures the acceleration in AI CapEx, data-center commitments, semiconductor-equipment orders and AI-infrastructure financing. The denominator captures the expansion of compute, data-center capacity, power generation, grids, semiconductor capacity and, progressively, productivity.

ACPI measures the pressure. Pass-through determines the inflation.

::patterns Reading The ACPI | Where we sit in the cycle
# [>1] Capital Outruns Capacity
Bottlenecks and CapEx inflation intensify as investment races ahead of physical supply.
# [≈1] The Handoff Zone
Capacity is catching demand. The cycle approaches its inflection point.
# [<1] Productivity Dominates
Capacity grows faster than marginal capital demand; productivity begins to dominate.

::section From The Capacity Gap To Inflation | The Inflation Filter
A high ACPI does not automatically imply higher CPI inflation. The first pressure appears in capital goods, power, construction, equipment and specialized labor. This is CapEx inflation.

To reach consumer prices and wages, it must pass through the **Inflation Filter**: pricing power, labor-market tightness, energy pass-through, margin absorption, productivity offsets, and the duration of bottlenecks. Conceptually, CPI pressure ≈ CapEx inflation × pass-through intensity.

AI therefore does not directly create inflation. It creates investment demand. Scarcity raises costs. Pass-through determines how much reaches the broader nominal economy.

::section Shock Or Regime? | The critical distinction
A **shock** means demand temporarily outruns capacity. Costs rise, but new supply can progressively close the gap. The effect is predominantly cyclical.

A **regime** means desired investment remains structurally elevated relative to desired saving. The real rate required to equilibrate the economy rises: r-star ↑. That does not necessarily mean higher inflation. We can have ==inflation ↓ + r-star ↑== — the combination most at risk of being underpriced.

If inflation and real yields decline together, the economy is probably absorbing primarily a shock. If inflation falls while the real price of capital remains elevated, the market may be pricing a new regime.

::pull THE CALIBRATION
The core issue is the calibration of the policy rate against a potentially higher neutral rate. The direction of policy can be accommodative while the terminal level remains structurally higher.

::section The Central-Bank Reaction Function | Direction versus destination
Monetary restriction ≈ real policy rate − r-star. If r-star rises while policy is unchanged, the degree of restriction declines. The question is not simply when to ease, but what level of the policy rate is consistent with an appropriately restrictive stance during disinflation and, later, a return toward neutrality.

If r-star has moved higher, normalization can proceed through rate cuts without taking the policy rate back to the levels of the previous cycle.

On the curve, the **front end** prices the pace of normalization, the **belly** prices the terminal policy rate, and the **long end** prices the long-run macro-financial equilibrium — real rates, inflation expectations, term premium, fiscal supply and structural capital demand. The direction can be lower. The destination can remain higher.

::signals Four Monetary Regimes | Capital → capacity → inflation → r-star → policy → markets
# I — Capital Shock
CapEx accelerates ahead of capacity. Bottlenecks and cost pressures intensify. R-star is not yet determined; restriction remains in place for longer. Markets favor short/intermediate carry and cash-flow quality, with duration vulnerable.
signal: The shock is identified — carry and quality lead.

# II — New Equilibrium
Capacity catches up and inflation moderates, but investment demand remains structurally strong. R-star rises; policy normalizes toward a higher neutral rate. ROIC, free cash flow and carry become increasingly important.
signal: This tests whether the shock has produced a new regime.

# III — Productivity Handoff
Installed capacity catches up with demand and productivity accelerates. R-star is stable or gradually lower; policy normalizes in orderly fashion. Markets see equity broadening, industrial AI, automation and increasingly attractive duration.
signal: The transition from scarcity to productivity.

# IV — Cycle Reversal
Realized returns fail to justify invested capital. Expected ROIC falls below WACC and CapEx corrects. R-star falls; easing accelerates with greater focus on financial stability. Markets favor quality sovereign duration, defensive cash flows and strong balance sheets.
signal: Overshoot and the reversal of recursion.

::section The Cost Of Capital As Selection Mechanism | ROIC − WACC returns to center
The new monetary equilibrium reaches companies through real yields → risk-free rate → WACC → hurdle rate. As WACC rises, marginal projects become uneconomic, leverage matters more, distant cash flows lose value, and balance-sheet quality returns to the center of capital allocation. The critical variable becomes **ROIC − WACC**.

In **equity**, AI can raise earnings and cash flows, but a higher WACC raises the discount rate. Can cash flows grow faster than the cost of capital? On one side sit AI cash-flow generators — high ROIC, strong free cash flow, pricing power, strong balance sheets. On the other, AI capital consumers — high CapEx, long payback, weak conversion, high leverage. The new divide is not Tech versus Non-Tech. ==It is return on capital versus cost of capital.==

In **credit**, as the buildout moves from internal funding toward bonds, private credit and infrastructure finance, credit imposes explicit discipline. Markets must distinguish debt financing productive capacity from debt financing competitive overinvestment. The equity-credit gap becomes a leading signal: if valuations stay elevated while spreads and funding costs deteriorate, the capital structure is challenging the equity narrative.

::section The New Investment Hierarchy | From cheap money to scarcity value
The old regime favored cheap money, long duration and multiple expansion. The emerging regime favors **cash flow + ROIC + balance-sheet strength + scarcity value** — cash-flow compounders on one side, scarce physical assets on the other: power, grids, data-center infrastructure, semiconductor infrastructure and automation.

The most vulnerable segment remains long-duration assets dependent on cheap capital without sufficient cash-flow growth or scarcity value. The turning point arrives when capacity and productivity begin to grow faster than marginal demand for new capital — the transition from scarcity to productivity.

::signals The Five-Signal Investor Dashboard | One regime signal, two confirmations, two warnings
# Real Yields — The Regime Signal
The most important signal. Inflation is falling — are real yields falling with it? If yes, the economy is probably absorbing a cyclical shock. If real yields remain elevated, the market may be pricing a higher r-star. This determines whether investors should think cyclically or structurally.
signal: 1 — Decides. Inflation ↓ while real yields remain high signals a regime change.

# ACPI — The Physical-Cycle Confirmation
ACPI above 1 and rising means capital continues to outrun capacity and scarcity dominates. Above 1 but falling means capacity is catching up. Below 1 means capacity is outrunning marginal capital formation.
signal: 2 — Confirms. Tells us where the real economy sits inside the cycle.

# ROIC − WACC — The Economic Confirmation
The recursive cycle is sustainable only while ROIC exceeds WACC. A wide positive spread validates further capital formation; a narrowing spread signals deteriorating economics; ROIC below WACC is the critical break.
signal: 3 — Confirms. CapEx shows how much firms spend; ROIC − WACC shows whether they should.

# Equity–Credit Gap — The Market Warning
The dangerous configuration is equity euphoria plus credit deterioration. If multiples stay elevated while spreads widen, all-in yields rise or refinancing worsens, the capital structure is rejecting the assumptions in equity valuations.
signal: 4 — Warns. Credit does not need to predict the cycle; it only needs to stop confirming it.

# Inflation Filter — The Policy Warning
Contained pass-through keeps the boom manageable for policy. Broadening pass-through makes the reaction function more restrictive. The policy risk: the central bank raises the hurdle rate before productivity has time to arrive.
signal: 5 — Warns. Anticipates when policy becomes a greater constraint.

::takeaways Key takeaways | Read the dashboard in sequence
- **First — Real Yields:** has the regime changed? Inflation ↓ while real yields stay high is the regime-changing evidence.
- **Second — ACPI:** where are we in the physical cycle? Watch whether it holds above 1 or crosses below.
- **Third — ROIC − WACC:** is the cycle still creating value? Watch for the spread compressing toward zero.
- **Then use credit** to detect the break and **inflation** to anticipate the policy constraint.
- The next AI trade will not be defined by who spends the most, but by who can keep returns on capital above the cost of capital.

::close The shock changes the path. The regime changes the destination. Inflation is falling — are real yields falling with it? If not, the real equilibrium price of capital may have changed, meaning a new neutral rate, new discount rates, new hurdle rates, and ultimately ==a new hierarchy of capital==. The AI supercycle is becoming the great repricing of capital.

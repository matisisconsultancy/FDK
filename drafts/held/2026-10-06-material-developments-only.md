Today's most consequential shift is Spain's move from a financing story to a power-and-grid story, while France strengthens the demand side of its sovereign AI stack.

And one important correction: the official EuroHPC Gigafactory tender closes **12 November 2026 at 16:00 CET**, not 3 December. That materially compresses the execution window for every national consortium.

::section The Real Deadline Is 12 November | EU
EuroHPC's official call lists **12 November 2026, 16:00 CET** as the submission deadline for AI Gigafactory consortia. The tender requires adequate power infrastructure as well as energy- and water-efficiency provisions, reinforcing that this is an infrastructure competition, not simply a compute procurement.

This matters because the remaining runway is now brutally short. Any consortium that still lacks a final site, secured MW, committed equity, customer/offtake architecture or delivery plan is entering the decisive phase under pressure.

Capital-formation implication: project bankability now matters more than consortium breadth. Board implication: the winning bids will increasingly look like infrastructure SPVs with contracted demand, not policy coalitions.

::signals The National Picture | where each contender stands today
# Spain — The Gigafactory Race Is Becoming a Grid Race
Fresh evidence from Spain is significant. Aragón is now expected to attract more than €60 billion of data-centre and digital-infrastructure investment over the next decade. AWS alone plans €33.7 billion in cloud and AI infrastructure there, while Microsoft plans up to €10 billion. Aragón's fast-track PIGA regime can cut permitting times to under a year.

But the more important development is the emerging constraint: power availability. Reuters reports that roughly ==70% of Spain's transmission nodes have no available capacity==. Spain's data-centre capacity is nevertheless forecast to rise fourfold to 3.9 GW by 2030, while the government has authorised €17 billion of grid investment through 2030 — around 30% above the earlier plan. Utilities including Iberdrola, Endesa and EDP are responding with more storage, hydro, wind and hybrid power structures to provide firm supply for 24/7 compute.

This changes the Spanish thesis. The advantage was previously PUBLIC CAPITAL → PRIVATE CAPITAL → INFRASTRUCTURE. It is becoming GRID ACCESS → FIRM POWER → INFRASTRUCTURE → COMPUTE → CAPITAL. The national bid is still centred on Móra la Nova and San Fernando de Henares, but Aragón is becoming the benchmark for how quickly Spain can convert permitting, land and power access into global AI capital.
signal: Spain's upside is now tied to utilities, storage and power developers — locating AI capacity there is an energy-architecture decision, not a real-estate one.

# France — Mistral Is Beginning to Create the Offtake France Needs
Mistral today launched **Mistral Large 4**, a new open-weight multimodal model. Mistral's own documentation describes it as a 1.05-trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model with 49 billion active parameters, while Reuters reports it was trained on Mistral's own infrastructure and is intended to close part of the gap with frontier systems.

France already had one of Europe's strongest supply-side architectures: EDF power → Bull systems → Scaleway/iliad cloud → Orange distribution → Capgemini/Artefact deployment. Mistral adds something even more valuable: native European compute demand. The company has recently raised €3 billion, while strategic relationships with industrial and semiconductor investors such as ASML and Samsung point toward deeper integration across the AI value chain.

The hardest Gigafactory problem is not buying GPUs. It is keeping them economically utilized. A globally relevant European model provider can become a natural anchor customer for sovereign compute.
signal: Proprietary European workloads may lower long-term utilization risk — sovereignty is more credible when Europe owns both the infrastructure and the models that consume it.

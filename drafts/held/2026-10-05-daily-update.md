The overnight evidence does not justify a country-score change. But it does sharpen the next phase of the market regime.

The new signal is that AI value creation is beginning to move downstream from compute into the industrial conversion layer: software, automation, design, manufacturing and asset productivity. The clearest weekend marker is France's **Schneider Electric**, reportedly nearing a roughly $20 billion acquisition of US industrial-software group **PTC** — talks not yet definitive, and therefore not scored, but strategically significant.

No newly released variable clears both the confidence and materiality thresholds required for a national-score adjustment. Trading and data breadth are unusually thin today because China and South Korea are on holiday.

::pull THE CONVERSION TRADE
Compute creates capability. Infrastructure enables scale. But industrial software determines whether that capability reaches an operating asset and generates measurable economic output.

::section The Institutional Interpretation | An evolution of the AI value chain
The first phase was dominated by models, GPUs and hyperscalers. The second broadened toward memory, networking, power, cooling and grid. The next phase is beginning to look different: **industrial software, automation, digital twins, robotics and enterprise productivity**. This is the conversion trade.

Schneider's reported move toward PTC is therefore strategically more important than the headline transaction value suggests. It represents the possible fusion of electrical infrastructure, data-centre infrastructure, industrial data, design software and AI inside one operating architecture. That is where the AI cycle can begin migrating from capital expenditure into productivity.

::patterns The Second-Order Effects | Five consequences of the rotation
# [I] Higher rates favour buying productivity
At 5%+ sovereign yields, companies cannot indefinitely fund experimental AI portfolios. Strong balance sheets instead buy proven software, integrate installed customers and capture cash flow sooner — tilting the next M&A cycle toward industrial software, data platforms, robotics and automation.

# [II] The bottleneck migrates to implementation
Access to intelligence is expanding fast; what remains scarce is the organisational capability to embed it into physical processes. The economic bottleneck moves from GPU availability to power to workflow integration to productivity capture — the last stage hardest to commoditise, and therefore potentially most valuable.

# [III] Japan exposes the K-shaped AI economy
Services decelerate while manufacturing and industrial confidence stay stronger. AI investment initially benefits capital-intensive sectors faster than labour-intensive services, widening productivity dispersion before broader diffusion.

# [IV] Falling oil risk is not falling energy risk
Middle East crude flows recovered above pre-war levels — the seven-day average reached about 18.5 million bpd on October 1 versus roughly 18 million pre-war — but tanker attacks around Hormuz are rising. The constraint shifts from physical availability toward security, insurance, transport and resilience, costs that ultimately enter electricity prices and required returns.

# [V] Europe's unexpected advantage in the conversion layer
Europe lags on frontier-model scale but retains world-class positions in industrial automation, power systems, engineering software and manufacturing. If the market rotates from creating intelligence toward deploying it, that industrial base becomes strategically more valuable. Europe needs to own the layer where AI meets the physical economy.

::section The Evolutionary Market Rotation | From scarcity to conversion
The investable sequence is becoming: compute → infrastructure → industrial software → productivity → cash flow. The first two phases rewarded scarcity; the next two will reward conversion. Investors should increasingly distinguish between companies *selling* AI capacity and companies *using* AI to increase the productivity of existing revenue pools. The latter may eventually produce the broader and more durable earnings cycle.

::takeaways Five Takeaways | Where the value migrates next
- The AI trade is moving downstream — the next value pool lies where intelligence meets engineering, manufacturing and operating assets.
- Industrial software becomes strategic infrastructure, the interface between compute and productivity, and likely to attract more M&A capital.
- Lower bond yields today do not mean cheap capital has returned; a US 10-year near 5.26% still imposes a severe productivity test.
- Europe should compete where it is strongest — integrating AI into power, industrial systems, mobility, manufacturing and automation.
- Boards should now measure AI at the conversion layer: intelligence → workflow → productivity → cash flow → ROIC > WACC.

::close GVI holds at 108.0, but the market's centre of gravity keeps moving. The next excess return will not come from owning more intelligence — ==it will come from owning the layer that turns intelligence into industrial cash flow==.

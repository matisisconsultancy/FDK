What Trump–Xi means for Europe — and what Europe must do next
24 September 2026 | FDK
The Fundamental Question
Is Europe still capable of getting back to the table where the rules of the Next Economy are being negotiated?
The Trump–Xi meeting makes the question unavoidable. Washington and Beijing are not ending strategic competition; they are exploring a more durable equilibrium: compete harder, escalate less. That gives both powers time to compound. Europe risks becoming the market between the two systems rather than the power shaping them.
1. Europe’s problem is not absence. It is leverage.
Europe was not at the Trump–Xi table. That alone is irrelevant. The strategic risk is that Washington and Beijing increasingly discover that they can negotiate the architecture of trade, AI, semiconductors, critical minerals and technology without Europe being necessary to the bargain.
Europe needs leverage, not recognition.
2. The danger: Europe becomes the world’s premium market.
The emerging equilibrium could leave Europe wealthy but strategically secondary: American technology dominates frontier intelligence; Chinese industrial scale pressures European industry; Europe regulates and consumes both systems.
Prosperity without power.
3. Europe must change the objective.
The objective should be measurable: Europe must get back to the negotiating table by 2030. Not because Washington or Beijing grants it a seat, but because excluding Europe becomes economically expensive.
Autonomy → Indispensability | Regulation → Capital Formation | Fragmentation → Scale | Digital Adoption → Physical AI | Savings → Strategic Equity
4. Europe already has the assets.
Europe possesses strategic positions in semiconductor equipment, photonics, industrial automation, power systems, aerospace, advanced manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, mobility, robotics and critical infrastructure. The problem is not the absence of assets. It is that they do not yet operate as a system.
Technology → Capital → Energy → Industry → Scale
5. Physical AI is Europe’s opening.
Europe should not try to replicate Silicon Valley. The next AI race moves from tokens to agents, machines and infrastructure. The further intelligence moves into the physical economy, the stronger Europe’s comparative position becomes.
Make Europe the global platform for Physical AI.
6. Five things Europe must build.
7. Three scenarios.
Analytical scenarios, not statistical forecasts. Europe can change them.
8. Il Grande Gioco
The Trump–Xi meeting may be revealing the architecture of a managed bipolar system: compete strategically, transact selectively, coordinate narrowly. Europe’s greatest mistake would be waiting to see how it develops. The next five years must be used to build the leverage required to shape it.
Do not ask for the seat. Create the cost of your absence.
THE BIG TAKE
Trump–Xi changes the European question. It is no longer: where does Europe stand? It is: can Europe get back to the table?
EUROPEAN POWER = INDISPENSABILITY × CAPITAL × ENERGY × TECHNOLOGY × SCALE × VELOCITY
Europe will not regulate its way back. It will not declare its way back. It must invest its way back to the table.
Key Takeaways for Investors
• Follow Europe’s Physical AI stack, not the search for a European hyperscaler.
• Energy becomes a technology asset.
• Europe’s strategic bottlenecks deserve a scarcity premium.
• Watch capital formation, not valuations alone.
• The decisive investment signal is European velocity.
Key Takeaways for EU Policymakers
• Stop optimizing primarily for regulation. Optimize for scale.
• Make Physical AI Europe’s strategic specialization.
• Treat the Savings and Investments Union as national-security infrastructure.
• Treat electricity, compute and grids as one integrated AI policy.
• Use European procurement to manufacture European scale.
• Protect only genuinely strategic dependencies; remain open everywhere else.
• Set one measurable objective: Europe must become a necessary negotiating power again by 2030.
The Velocity Insight
America owns much of the frontier. China is building the alternative stack. Europe still owns critical pieces of the system—but has not converted them into sufficient negotiating leverage.
EUROPE MUST INVEST ITS WAY BACK TO THE TABLE.
The next five years will determine whether Europe helps negotiate the emerging new normal—or simply lives under the rules others negotiate. Relevance will not be granted. It must be built.

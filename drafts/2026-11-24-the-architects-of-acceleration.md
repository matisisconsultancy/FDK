Evidence cut-off: 04:41 CET | Approximately 3-minute reading
THE BIG TAKE
The next phase of the AI Supercycle will increasingly be shaped by three strategic assets: visionary leadership, integrated capital markets and the industrial capacity to deploy intelligence at scale.
Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO triggered an extraordinary recovery across financial markets. Switzerland's SIX launched a cross-border initiative connecting European growth companies with international investors. Meanwhile, Germany's industrial acceleration contrasts sharply with France's deepening economic contraction.
The United States also continues strengthening its scientific discovery infrastructure through the Genesis Mission.
The hidden pattern: the geography of global capital allocation is increasingly being determined by the interaction between technological leadership, institutional execution and financial scalability.

I. FOUR DEVELOPMENTS RESHAPING GLOBAL VELOCITY
1. United States — The rise of Compass Companies. Nvidia's extraordinary market recovery demonstrates how certain corporations and their leaders influence expectations across entire industrial ecosystems. Huang's return reduces immediate leadership uncertainty, but continuing investigations and governance questions prevent an unconditional reduction in long-term risk. 
NVIDIA Newsroom

2. Switzerland, Spain and the United Kingdom — Europe's capital-market opportunity. SIX launched its European Growth Program, connecting 31 listed growth companies across the Swiss Exchange, Madrid's BME and London's Aquis. This could become an important institutional step towards financing European technology scale-ups without relying excessively on American capital markets. Actual new capital raised remains to be measured. 
SIX

3. Germany versus France — Europe's great industrial divergence. Germany's composite PMI reached its highest level since March 2022, while France's contraction intensified. The 8.8-point gap is the widest in six years, reinforcing the prospect of increasingly differentiated European investment returns. 
S&P Global+1

4. United States — Scientific discovery becomes industrial infrastructure. The Department of Energy's November 23 Genesis Mission review identifies 297 projects across 369 institutions, $2.4 billion in industry commitments and major advances in scientific computing infrastructure. These are important implementation milestones, although they do not yet establish equivalent gains in scientific productivity. 
Department of Energy

V. SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS — THE NEXT MARKET ROTATION
Three investment themes are emerging:
The Compass Company Premium: Strategic corporations capable of coordinating entire technological ecosystems may command a leadership premium, offset by key-person and governance risk.
The European Scale Premium: Integrated capital markets could reduce financing fragmentation and improve Europe's ability to retain and scale innovative enterprises.
The Discovery Premium: Scientific computing, advanced simulation and AI-enabled research could become major drivers of intellectual property, industrial productivity and long-term asset valuations.
The next market rotation may increasingly favour companies positioned at the intersection of these three forces.
VI. KEY TAKEAWAYS FOR INVESTORS AND BOARDS
Investors: Differentiate the recovery in market confidence from improvements in underlying earnings. Monitor governance risk, incremental ROIC and financing costs.
Boards and executives: Build organisations capable of preserving technological leadership beyond their founders while accelerating commercial deployment and scientific discovery.
Policymakers: Europe's opportunity lies in integrating capital markets, accelerated computing, research institutions and industrial capabilities into a scalable competitive ecosystem.
VELOCITY INSIGHT
The AI Supercycle is giving rise to a new hierarchy of corporations, financial institutions and national economies capable of directing global capital allocation.
The decisive competitive advantage will belong to those that combine the vision to anticipate technological change, the financial architecture to fund it and the execution capacity to monetise it.
In the Next Economy, the greatest premium will accrue not simply to those who participate in acceleration, but to those who determine its direction.

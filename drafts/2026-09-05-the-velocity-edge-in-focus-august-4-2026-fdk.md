Polarization and the Great Value Migration
Why Cheaper Intelligence Is Producing More Expensive Capitalism
The Fundamental Question
How can intelligence become almost free while the business of producing it becomes one of the most capital-intensive industries in history?
The lower the price of intelligence falls, the larger the investments required to produce it become.
That contradiction is redefining competition, financial markets, and global power.
It is also the defining economic paradox of the Age of Industrial AI.
Never before has the output of an industry become cheaper at precisely the moment its production system became more expensive.
This is not simply another technology cycle.
It is the birth of the world’s first industrial system whose product is intelligence itself.
And like every industrial revolution before it, it is reorganizing where capital flows, where profits accumulate, and where geopolitical power resides.
Our thesis is straightforward:
As the price of intelligence collapses, the capability to produce intelligence is becoming one of the scarcest and most capital-intensive assets in the global economy.
That is why the Age of Industrial AI is becoming an age of Polarization.
And Polarization is the mechanism through which The Great Value Migration takes place.
⸻
I. The Great Paradox
Every technological revolution has reduced the marginal cost of its output.
The steam engine reduced the cost of mechanical power.
Electricity reduced the cost of energy.
The internet reduced the cost of information.
Artificial Intelligence is reducing the cost of intelligence.
But Industrial AI introduces something history has never experienced.
The output is becoming cheaper while the production system is becoming exponentially more expensive.
Training frontier models now requires unprecedented investments in:
accelerated computing;
high-bandwidth memory;
networking;
optics;
electricity;
cooling;
sovereign-scale data centers;
proprietary datasets;
elite engineering talent.
Industry estimates now point toward well over $650 billion of AI infrastructure investment during 2026 alone, while hyperscalers continue announcing multi-year capital-expenditure programs measured in the hundreds of billions of dollars.
Google, Microsoft, Amazon and Meta together are on a trajectory to invest well above $350 billion annually in AI-related infrastructure within the next investment cycle.
At the same time, the cost of inference continues to fall.
The price of intelligence is collapsing.
The cost of producing it is exploding.
That is the paradox investors are only beginning to understand.
⸻
II. Venture Capital Is No Longer Financing Start-ups. It Is Financing Industrial Infrastructure.
Headline numbers suggest venture capital has never been stronger.
Reality suggests something very different.
Global venture funding exceeded $300 billion during the first quarter of 2026, the strongest quarter on record.
Yet approximately 65% of that capital flowed into only four companies.
Among frontier laboratories alone, OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI represented roughly two-thirds of all AI venture financing.
More than 1,500 AI transactions divided the remaining third.
This is not diversification.
It is concentration.
The venture-capital industry is undergoing its own Great Value Migration.
Capital is migrating away from portfolio diversification toward industrial concentration.
The traditional venture model rewarded discovering the next great company.
Industrial AI rewards financing the companies capable of building trillion-dollar infrastructure.
The economics increasingly resemble energy, aerospace and telecommunications rather than software.
Capital is no longer searching for innovation.
Capital is searching for scale.
⸻
III. China Is Destroying the Economics of the Middle
The second force accelerating Polarization is China.
China is no longer attempting merely to catch the United States technologically.
It is changing the economics of competition.Chinese developers are compressing the price of intelligence through:
open-weight models;
dramatically lower inference costs;
rapid iteration;
industrial deployment;
manufacturing integration.
The consequence is profound.
The winners are unlikely to be determined solely by benchmark superiority.
They will increasingly be determined by cost per unit of intelligence.
This creates what Bloomberg appropriately describes as a potential death zone.
Not for the frontier leaders.
Not for highly specialized application companies.
But for the companies trapped in between.
Second-tier model developers face:
frontier-level R&D costs;
rapidly falling prices;
declining customer switching costs;
increasing competition from open models;
investors demanding monetization.
The middle of the AI stack is becoming economically unsustainable.
History shows that the middle is always the first casualty of industrial concentration.
⸻
IV. The Great Value Migration
The market continues to debate which model is best.
That is becoming the wrong question.
The better question is:
Where is economic value migrating?
The answer is increasingly clear.
Value is migrating from models toward infrastructure.
Accelerated computing.
Memory.
Networking.
Energy.
Grid capacity.
Cooling.
Semiconductor equipment.
These remain structurally scarce.
Value is migrating from intelligence toward distribution.
Who owns the customer increasingly matters more than who owns the model.
Value is migrating toward proprietary data.
As foundation models converge, unique industrial datasets become more valuable.
Value is migrating toward physical systems.
Railways.
Manufacturing.
Healthcare.
Energy.
Defense.
Mobility.
Industrial AI becomes exponentially more valuable when it controls physical assets.
Value is migrating toward governments.
Export controls.
Semiconductors.
Rare earths.
Cybersecurity.
Energy.
Industrial policy.
The AI race is no longer purely technological.
It has become geopolitical.
⸻
V. Polarization Is Becoming the New Competitive Regime
Industrial AI is creating a barbell economy.
At one end:
hyperscalers;
semiconductor leaders;
frontier laboratories;
sovereign investors;
infrastructure owners.
At the other:
highly specialized AI-native companies solving narrow, high-value problems.
Everything between these two poles is being compressed.
Generalist software.
Undifferentiated models.
Mid-sized venture funds.
Generic AI platforms.
This is the new competitive regime.
Scale wins.
Specialization survives.
The middle disappears.
⸻
VI. Financial Markets Are Entering the Age of Dispersion
The first phase of AI rewarded exposure.
The second phase will reward selectivity.
Investors will increasingly distinguish between:
producers of intelligence versus consumers of intelligence;
owners of infrastructure versus renters of infrastructure;
companies controlling bottlenecks versus companies paying for bottlenecks;
firms generating free cash flow versus firms dependent upon continuous financing.
This is no longer an AI trade.
It is a capital-allocation trade.
Lower inference prices are positive for adoption.
They are not automatically positive for shareholders.
As intelligence becomes cheaper, economic rents migrate elsewhere.
Investors who mistake AI adoption for value creation risk confusing productivity with profitability.
⸻
VII. The New Geography of Economic Power
The United States and China are no longer simply competing in Artificial Intelligence.
They are constructing competing industrial systems for producing intelligence.
The American system emphasizes:
frontier models;
proprietary ecosystems;
hyperscale cloud;
financial depth;
premium pricing.
China increasingly emphasizes:
industrial deployment;
manufacturing integration;
open-weight ecosystems;
lower prices;
rapid diffusion.
These are not simply different technologies.
They are different economic models.
The countries capable of producing intelligence will increasingly determine the rules for those that merely consume it.

Five Key Takeaways
Intelligence is becoming abundant. The capability to produce it is becoming scarce.
This is the defining economic paradox of the Age of Industrial AI.
Polarization is not a side effect. It is the operating system of Industrial AI.
Capital naturally migrates toward scale, bottlenecks and strategic control.
The greatest value migration is occurring inside the AI stack itself.
Infrastructure, energy, data and distribution are becoming more valuable than intelligence alone.
The strategic middle is disappearing.
Industrial history repeatedly demonstrates that periods of technological disruption compress undifferentiated competitors.
AI is accelerating that process.
Investors should own scarcity—not popularity.
Models can multiply.
Applications can proliferate.
Only bottlenecks retain durable pricing power.

Velocity Insight
The defining paradox of the Age of Industrial AI is simple but profound: intelligence is becoming cheaper precisely because producing it is becoming dramatically more expensive.
Every dollar reduction in the price of intelligence now requires billions of dollars of additional investment somewhere else in the system.
That is why AI is democratizing intelligence while simultaneously concentrating capital, competitive advantage and geopolitical power.
The winners of the next decade will not necessarily be those who create the most intelligence. They will be those who own the industrial system that makes intelligence possible.

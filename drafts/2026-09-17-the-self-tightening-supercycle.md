The Federal Reserve has raised rates to 3.75–4.00%, the U.S. 10-year closed above 5%, and policymakers now project another increase before year-end. Yet, almost simultaneously, ten banks are assembling a $22 billion loan against AI chips and customer contracts; Blackstone has already committed $5 billion of equity to the same platform; Huawei says its next architecture can connect as many as one million processors; and Europe is committing tens of billions to grids and sovereign compute. The AI Supercycle is not ending. It is becoming large enough to raise its own hurdle rate.
Thursday Edition | Data cut: 07:45 CEST

The Fundamental Question
What happens when the investment boom powerful enough to drive the next productivity cycle also becomes large enough to raise inflation, intensify competition for capital and force central banks to increase the cost of financing it?
That is the question markets confront this morning.
The Federal Reserve raised the federal-funds target by 25 basis points to 3.75–4.00%, its first increase since 2023. Sixteen of eighteen policymakers expect at least one more hike this year, taking the range toward 4.00–4.25%, while projected PCE inflation for 2026 has risen to 3.7%. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh specifically cited resilient demand, strong productivity and robust capital investment among the forces keeping inflationary pressures alive. 
The bond market responded accordingly. The U.S. 10-year yield finished Wednesday around 5.02%, the S&P 500 fell 0.45%, and investors are now assigning roughly even odds to another increase as early as October. 
Yet AI capital formation accelerated again overnight.
Ten banks are arranging a $22 billion chip-backed loan for Blackstone and Alphabet’s Crux AI venture, on top of $5 billion of Blackstone equity, to finance a platform expected to bring its first 500 MW of compute online in 2027. 
The paradox is becoming the defining economic mechanism of the Next Economy:
**AI INVESTMENT
→ PRODUCTIVITY
→ CAPITAL DEMAND
→ ENERGY DEMAND
→ INFLATION PRESSURE
→ HIGHER RATES
→ HIGHER AI HURDLE RATE.**
The Supercycle is beginning to tax itself.

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy
1. The AI Supercycle has become part of monetary policy
Verified facts. The Fed increased rates by 25 basis points and now expects one further hike before year-end.Its updated forecasts put 2026 PCE inflation at 3.7% and GDP growth at 2.3%. Warsh cited resilient spending, productivity growth and strong capital investment among the factors supporting tighter policy. Reuters also identified the AI investment boom, tariffs and the energy shock among the forces sustaining price pressure. 
FDK interpretation.
This is a profound threshold.
AI has moved from being:
a sector influenced by monetary policy
to becoming:
A MACROECONOMIC FORCE CAPABLE OF INFLUENCING MONETARY POLICY.
AI infrastructure consumes:
capital,
electricity,
construction capacity,
transformers,
land,
network equipment,
skilled labour
and increasingly sovereign balance-sheet capacity.
At sufficient scale, those demands affect economy-wide prices.
The Supercycle therefore creates an endogenous feedback loop:
**MORE AI CAPEX
→ MORE DEMAND FOR SCARCE INPUTS
→ HIGHER NOMINAL GROWTH
→ HIGHER INFLATION RISK
→ HIGHER RATES
→ HIGHER REQUIRED AI RETURNS.**
That does not kill the cycle.
It raises the quality threshold.

2. AI financing is becoming institutional infrastructure finance
Verified facts. A consortium of ten banks including Goldman Sachs, SMBC, Barclays, BNP Paribas and Bank of Nova Scotia is arranging $22 billion of financing for Crux AI, the Blackstone–Alphabet compute venture. Blackstone has committed $5 billion in equity, while Google contributes TPUs, software and services. The debt is expected to be secured by chips and customer contracts and may later be refinanced in the investment-grade bond market. 
FDK interpretation.
This may be the most important financial-market development of the morning.
The AI build-out is evolving from:
venture capital
→ corporate capex
→ hyperscaler balance sheets
toward:
**PROJECT FINANCE
→ BANK SYNDICATION
→ INVESTMENT-GRADE SECURITISATION.**
That is what happens when an emerging technology becomes infrastructure.
The crucial innovation is not simply the $22 billion size.
It is the collateral structure:
chips + contracted customers + operating compute capacity.
AI capacity is beginning to acquire the characteristics of:
aircraft finance,
telecom towers,
power plants,
pipelines
and infrastructure concessions.
The next phase of the AI Supercycle may therefore be financed increasingly through the global credit system, not only equity markets.

3. China is changing the competition from chip supremacy to system supremacy
Verified facts. Huawei says it will introduce the 960DT in Q1 2027 and the Ascend 960PR in Q3 2027. Its UnifiedBus architecture is designed to connect very large numbers of processors; Huawei says its biggest planned superclusters can support up to one million AI processors. It has already shipped more than 1,000 supernodes to over 370 customers, and reports 5,270 monthly active developers in its AI-chip ecosystem. 
FDK interpretation.
The strategic response to semiconductor controls is becoming clearer.
China may not need to win the contest:
chip-for-chip.
It can attempt to compete:
SYSTEM-FOR-SYSTEM.
If a single domestic accelerator is less capable than the most advanced Nvidia device, system engineering can partially offset the gap through:
more processors,
better interconnect,
large-scale parallelisation,
software optimisation
and domestically controlled supply.
The architecture shifts from:
maximum performance per chip
toward:
maximum useful intelligence per controlled system.
That changes the U.S.–China AI race.
The competitive metric increasingly becomes:
USABLE COMPUTE × SCALE × SOVEREIGN CONTROL.

4. The energy shock is moving from inflation into industrial demand destruction
Verified facts. Brent has eased to $105.64, helped by additional Saudi cargoes routed through Oman, but physical supply conditions remain abnormal. Only three commodity vessels crossed Hormuz Wednesday versus a 10-day average of roughly 17. Asian spot LNG prices have more than doubled to about $26/mmBtu since the conflict began. Analysts expect Asian LNG demand to fall 3–10% this year, with Chinese imports alone declining by roughly 6.1 million tonnes; energy-intensive ceramics, methanol and glass producers have cut production or shut plants because fuel costs are uneconomic. 
FDK interpretation.
This marks the second phase of the energy shock.
Phase I was:
higher oil → higher inflation.
Phase II becomes:
HIGHER ENERGY → LOWER INDUSTRIAL OUTPUT.
That transition matters enormously.
The energy shock is now redistributing industrial competitiveness.
Countries with:
nuclear,
domestic gas,
coal fallback,
renewables plus storage
or diversified pipelines
gain relative production resilience.
Those reliant on marginal LNG markets lose it.
Energy resilience is becoming an explicit factor of production.

5. Europe is beginning to build the missing middle between models and industry
Verified facts. Spain will increase investment in its transmission network to more than €17 billion through 2030, roughly 30% above its previous plan, partly because of rising demand from data centres and electrification. Separately, Cohere and Germany’s Aleph Alpha have agreed to combine in a business valued around $20 billion. Schwarz Group will invest €500 million in the company and is investing €11–13 billion in a German compute campus designed to house as many as 100,000 AI chips. 
FDK interpretation.
Europe’s weakness has never been only that it lacks a frontier model comparable in scale to the largest U.S. platforms.
Its real weakness is the missing chain between:
capital
→ compute
→ energy
→ enterprise deployment
→ industrial adoption.
This week, several pieces are beginning to connect.
**GRID
→ COMPUTE
→ SOVEREIGN CLOUD
→ ENTERPRISE AI
→ INDUSTRIAL PRODUCTIVITY.**
That is a more plausible European strategic architecture than simply trying to reproduce Silicon Valley model-for-model.

Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines
1. The Supercycle is creating its own cost of capital
Technology cycles normally benefit from falling input costs.
This one is unusual.
AI is large enough to push demand upward simultaneously for:
money, power, chips, construction and networks.
That raises the price of nearly every factor required to expand AI itself.
The result is a self-referential economic system:
**VELOCITY CREATES SCARCITY.
SCARCITY RAISES THE COST OF VELOCITY.**
The companies capable of breaking that loop through efficiency become disproportionately valuable.


⸻


2. The winners are migrating from “AI exposure” to financeable AI assets
Yesterday’s equity market rewarded the idea of AI capacity.
The credit market increasingly demands proof.
The $22 billion Crux financing suggests the critical questions are changing.
Not:
How many GPUs will you own?
But:
Who contracted the capacity?
At what utilisation?
At what power price?
For how long?
Against what collateral?
That is the transition from AI narrative to AI yield.

3. Capital formation is becoming geopolitical infrastructure
South Korea’s trade agreement with Washington includes a $350 billion investment commitment into U.S. manufacturing, with $150 billion earmarked for shipbuilding. Projects under discussion reportedly include nuclear reactors, a 6.3-GW Texas gas plant valued around $22 billion to serve rising AI data-centre demand, and energy infrastructure. 
This is not conventional FDI.
It is emerging geoeconomic capital allocation.
Trade access increasingly comes bundled with:
domestic manufacturing,
energy investment,
strategic technology
and sovereign co-investment.
Capital is becoming an instrument of alliance architecture.

4. Sovereign capital is following institutional platforms, not just technology
India’s National Stock Exchange opened its $2.3 billion IPO this morning after allocating roughly $703 million to anchor investors including the sovereign wealth funds of Norway and Abu Dhabi. 
The signal is broader than one IPO.
Sovereign capital increasingly wants exposure not only to technology companies—but to the market infrastructure through which future capital formation flows.
Exchanges, grids, data centres and networks therefore gain strategic value.
They are not simply assets.
They are control points.⸻


5. Mobility is entering the age of capital selection
Volkswagen is reviewing the future of Seat beyond its current product cycle. Seat represented less than 3% of VW global deliveries in 2025, has launched no new model since 2020 and has no fully electric model planned; sister brand Cupra overtook it in annual sales last year. Across European, U.S., Japanese and South Korean automakers, cumulative annual sales declined by 12.6 million vehicles, or 17%, between 2019 and 2025. 
The implication is larger than Seat.
Capital is becoming too expensive to support every inherited brand, platform and architecture.
The mobility sector is moving from:
portfolio breadth
toward:
CAPITAL CONCENTRATION AROUND VELOCITY LEADERS.
In an environment of expensive capital and technology transition, strategic neglect compounds faster than ever.

Likely Market and Competitive Implications Today
The immediate risk tone has improved modestly. Nasdaq futures are up roughly 0.7%, S&P 500 futures around 0.6%, European futures about 0.5%, and Asia-Pacific equities ex-Japan approximately 0.3% higher. Chinese blue chips and Hong Kong remain weaker. 
But beneath the rebound, yesterday’s Fed decision changes capital allocation.
Likely relative beneficiaries: contracted AI infrastructure, networking, HBM and interconnect, grids, nuclear and firm power, data-centre finance, bank syndication businesses, enterprise AI and sovereign compute.
Likely pressure points: small caps, speculative long-duration technology, energy-intensive manufacturing, transport and logistics exposed to record diesel costs, weak legacy automotive brands and companies dependent on repeated refinancing.
Banks occupy an unusual position.
U.S. banks have already raised prime lending rates from 6.75% to 7%. Higher rates can increase net-interest income, but tightening ultimately weakens loan demand and can worsen credit quality. Simultaneously, the Crux financing demonstrates that banks are gaining a new multibillion-dollar underwriting market in AI infrastructure. 
The winners in banking may therefore be those able to finance productive capital formation, not merely reprice legacy loan books.

The Velocity Insight
The central economic assumption of the AI Supercycle has been that accelerating intelligence creates accelerating returns.
That assumption remains plausible.
But it is incomplete.
Intelligence is not produced in an abstract digital cloud.
It requires:
capital.
Energy.
Semiconductors.
Networks.
Land.
Grid capacity.
Construction.
Credit.
And every one of those inputs has a price.
At small scale, the AI revolution consumes those resources.
At industrial scale, it begins to reprice them.
That is the crucial transition.
The Supercycle is becoming macroeconomic.
And once a technology cycle becomes macroeconomic, it becomes exposed to the oldest force in capitalism:
THE COST OF CAPITAL.
The causal chain has changed.
Yesterday:
**CAPITAL
→ PERMISSION
→ CAPACITY
→ INTELLIGENCE
→ CASH FLOW**
Today we must add the feedback loop:
**CASH FLOW
→ MORE INVESTMENT
→ MORE RESOURCE DEMAND
→ MORE INFLATION
→ HIGHER RATES
→ HIGHER RETURN THRESHOLDS**
This is why the $22 billion Crux financing matters.
It shows one possible answer.
Turn chips into collateral.
Turn customer demand into contracts.
Turn compute into an infrastructure asset.
Turn AI capacity into bankable yield.
Huawei is answering the same economic constraint technologically.
If the best individual chip is scarce, connect more domestic chips more efficiently.
Spain is answering it physically.
Build the grid.
Europe is answering it institutionally.
Combine models, regulated deployment and sovereign cloud.
Volkswagen is answering it strategically.
Stop financing every legacy asset equally.
Every system is converging toward the same rule:
SCARCITY FORCES SELECTION.
The Great Split is therefore becoming sharper.
Not:
AI winners versus AI losers.
But:
SELF-FINANCING VELOCITY
versus
RATE-SENSITIVE SCALE.
The first can compound through 5% money.
The second requires the market to become generous again.
That is an increasingly dangerous assumption.
Velocity still creates competitive advantage.
Cash flow now determines whether velocity can survive its own success.
That is today’s Velocity Advantage.

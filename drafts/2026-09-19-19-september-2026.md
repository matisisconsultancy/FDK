THE BATTLE MOVES FROM MODELS TO CONTROL
Material inflection detected.
The U.S.–China AI race is entering a more consequential phase. The central question is no longer simply who has the best frontier model. It is becoming:
Who controls the architecture through which intelligence is produced, powered, distributed and adopted?
The latest signals reinforce our earlier Full-Stack Control thesis. But there is an important evolution today: AI itself is moving directly onto the U.S.–China negotiating table.
AI BECOMES GEOPOLITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE
U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is expected to discuss AI, rare earths, trade and economic issues with Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng this weekend, ahead of the Trump–Xi meeting scheduled for September 24. Significantly, Bessent indicated that the discussions will cover both open- and closed-weight models.
That pairing matters.
AI + rare earths means that Washington and Beijing are increasingly treating the AI stack as one interconnected strategic system:
Minerals → Energy → Chips → Compute → Models → Applications → Distribution
The contest is migrating upstream and downstream simultaneously.
⸻
CHINA’S STRATEGY: COMPENSATE FOR COMPUTE WITH DIFFUSION
The Chinese architecture increasingly looks different from America’s.
China continues to face constraints in access to the most advanced foreign compute. Huawei is therefore becoming increasingly important to the domestic ecosystem; recent reporting points toward a significant migration of Chinese AI training toward Huawei hardware.
But China possesses another lever:
open-weight diffusion.
Chinese models are increasingly attractive internationally because they can be downloaded, modified and deployed at relatively low cost. Reuters reports that Chinese open-weight models are gaining traction even among American companies.
This suggests a powerful alternative strategy:
Compute Constraint ↓ Efficiency Pressure ↓ Open Models ↓ Lower Deployment Cost ↓ Faster Global Diffusion ↓ Ecosystem Scale ↓ More Applications + Data + Developers ↺
China may not need to reproduce the American AI stack exactly.
It can attempt to route around its bottlenecks.
⸻
THE AMERICAN MODEL: CAPITAL INTENSITY BECOMES A WEAPON
The American chain runs almost in the opposite direction:
Frontier Models ↓ Exponentially More Compute ↓ Accelerated Computing ↓ Data-Center Expansion ↓ Power + Grid Infrastructure ↓ Capital Formation ↓ Model Capability ↺
And an important new constraint is appearing: electricity availability.
Google, Nvidia and Anthropic have joined an initiative with Emerald AI aimed at making data-center electricity demand more flexible. The initiative argues that demand-response techniques could potentially allow very substantial additional data-center capacity onto constrained grids.
This reinforces one of our core Velocity propositions:
The marginal constraint on AI is moving from silicon toward the physical economy.
Chips remain critical. But power, transmission, cooling, land, permitting and financing increasingly determine how rapidly compute can actually be deployed.
⸻
THE PARADOX: AMERICA LEADS THE FRONTIER; CHINA ATTACKS THE COST CURVE
This may become the defining asymmetry.
United States
Frontier capability → proprietary models → enormous compute → enormous capital → premium intelligence.
China
Compute constraints → optimization → open weights → lower cost → massive diffusion → ecosystem penetration.
The strategic contest therefore cannot be measured only with benchmark scores.
A model that is marginally weaker but dramatically cheaper and easier to deploy can generate enormous economic influence.
That is why the open-versus-closed architecture has now become sufficiently strategic to enter bilateral economic discussions.
⸻
A NEW FRONT: DISTILLATION
There is another development worth watching closely.
U.S. authorities this month accused six Chinese AI companies—including DeepSeek, Moonshot AI and Alibaba—of using American model outputs in what U.S.officials characterize as industrial-scale distillation of proprietary capabilities. The companies’ responses and the broader legal status of model distillation need to be distinguished from the U.S. government’s allegations.
Strategically, however, the issue is larger than the allegations themselves.
It exposes a fundamental problem with frontier AI:
How do you contain an intelligence advantage when intelligence can teach another machine?
Traditional technology restrictions controlled physical objects.
AI controls must increasingly address capabilities, weights, outputs and knowledge transfer.
That makes containment considerably harder.
⸻
THE HIDDEN PATTERN
The race is evolving through three stages:
Stage I — Model Race GPT vs DeepSeek vs Qwen vs Gemini vs Claude.
Stage II — Compute Race GPUs → accelerators → HBM → networking → data centers.
Stage III — System Race
Energy + Compute + Models + Capital + Distribution + Industrial Deployment
We are now entering Stage III.
And that changes the unit of competition.
It is no longer:
OpenAI vs DeepSeek.
Nor even:
Nvidia vs Huawei.
It increasingly becomes:
American AI System vs Chinese AI System.
⸻
THE TWO VELOCITY LOOPS
🇺🇸 UNITED STATES — CAPABILITY COMPOUNDING
Capital → Accelerated Computing → Frontier Models → Agents → Enterprise Adoption → Cash Flow → More Capital ↺
🇨🇳 CHINA — DIFFUSION COMPOUNDING
Constraints → Efficiency → Open Models → Low Cost → Developer Adoption → Industrial Deployment → Scale → More Optimization ↺
The first system attempts to maximize intelligence per frontier model.
The second increasingly has incentives to maximize intelligence per dollar, watt and user.
⸻
VELOCITY INSIGHT
The Great Split is becoming architectural.
America’s strongest advantage remains the extraordinary concentration of capital, frontier laboratories, advanced compute and hyperscale infrastructure.
China’s strategic opportunity is different: compress the cost of intelligence and propagate it faster.
That creates the crucial question for the next phase of the AI Supercycle:
Does competitive advantage accrue to the system that creates the most powerful intelligence—or to the system that makes sufficiently powerful intelligence ubiquitous first?
The answer may determine much more than the AI rankings.
It could determine which technology stack becomes the operating system of the next global economy.

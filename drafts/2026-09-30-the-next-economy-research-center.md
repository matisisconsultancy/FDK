Daily Intelligence Brief — 30 September 2026 | FDK
THE COST-CURVE WAR
As Intelligence Gets Cheaper, Scarcity Moves—and Capital Follows
THE BIG TAKE
The most important AI development today is not another increase in intelligence.
It is the falling price of intelligence.
OpenAI says GPT-6.1 Sol delivers near-Astra capability at materially lower token economics, while its faster Codex tier pushes generation toward 300 tokens per second. Meanwhile, DeepSeek and Huawei are building a more integrated programming stack around Ascend processors, including a 128-chip Ascend 950 supernode.
At the same time, the physical economy moves in the opposite direction: capital costs remain elevated, electricity and grid infrastructure are constrained, and Europe is mobilizing financing specifically for grid equipment.
This creates the defining paradox of the next phase:
INTELLIGENCE DEFLATION → INFRASTRUCTURE INFLATION
AI is becoming cheaper.
But the assets required to deploy AI at scale are becoming more valuable.
The question is no longer simply who builds the best intelligence. It is where scarcity moves once intelligence becomes abundant.


⸻


1 | AI & ACCELERATED COMPUTING
The cost curve bends
FACT. OpenAI’s latest model generation materially improves the price-performance equation while expanding agentic tooling and enterprise distribution.
WHAT CHANGED. Near-frontier capability is becoming cheaper to consume.
That triggers a Jevons-style mechanism:
COST/TOKEN ↓ → USE CASES ↑ → AGENTS ↑ → INFERENCE ↑ → INFRASTRUCTURE DEMAND ↑
This is the first crucial second-order effect.
Lower AI prices do not necessarily reduce total AI spending.
They can increase it.
The strategic metric therefore shifts from model capability toward:
ECONOMIC OUTPUT PER DOLLAR OF INTELLIGENCE
For companies, cheaper AI expands the universe of economically viable automation.
For investors, it shifts value away from undifferentiated model access toward the infrastructure and distribution layers that benefit from higher volumes.


⸻


2 | THE GEOPOLITICAL STACK
China attacks the ecosystem moat
DeepSeek is open-sourcing compute and communication libraries optimized for Huawei’s Ascend processors. With Huawei, it is developing a supernode architecture using 128 Ascend 950 chips while supporting an alternative programming ecosystem around domestic silicon.
Nvidia’s advantage has never been the GPU alone:
GPU + CUDA + LIBRARIES + DEVELOPERS + WORKLOADS
China increasingly understands that.
The second-order chain becomes:
Export controls → substitution → domestic software → domestic developers → domestic workloads → stronger domestic ecosystem
CUDA’s global moat remains formidable. Baseline unchanged.
But inside China, export restrictions are generating stronger incentives to create an alternative full stack.
The geopolitical risk therefore evolves from chip substitution toward ecosystem bifurcation.
The world could eventually operate across two increasingly distinct AI production systems.


⸻


3 | INDUSTRIAL AI
China’s industrial engine strengthens—but attribution matters
China’s official manufacturing PMI returned to expansion at 50.1 in September, with production at 51.7 and new orders at 50.5. A private survey was stronger at 52.1.
Technology exports and AI-linked activity are supporting parts of the industrial economy, while property, investment and domestic consumption remain weak.
The evidence therefore reinforces—but does not yet prove—the Industrial Conversion thesis.
China increasingly exhibits a K-shaped structure:
AI + TECHNOLOGY + INDUSTRIAL EXPORTS ↑
PROPERTY + DOMESTIC DEMAND ↓
The second-order risk is significant.
AI can strengthen productive capacity faster than domestic demand.
That means productivity gains can eventually generate:
more output → lower prices → more exports → greater trade friction.
AI productivity can therefore become a geopolitical variable.


⸻


4 | CAPITAL FORMATION
Cheaper intelligence meets more expensive capital
The financial backdrop is moving in the opposite direction from model economics.
The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield rose roughly 81 basis points during Q3, materially increasing the hurdle rate applied to long-duration infrastructure.
That creates a fundamental divergence:
COST OF INTELLIGENCE ↓
while
COST OF CAPITAL ↑
There is no major new AI financing transaction today. Baseline unchanged.
But the second-order effect is becoming more important.
Cheaper models expand AI demand.
Greater demand requires more infrastructure.
More infrastructure requires more external capital.
Higher rates then force greater discipline on that capital.
The chain becomes:
Token cost ↓ → adoption ↑ → CapEx ↑ → financing requirement ↑ → hurdle rate ↑ → weaker projects fail.
This should accelerate the next Great Split:
SELF-FINANCING AI vs. CAPITAL-DEPENDENT AI
and:
HIGH-UTILIZATION CAPACITY vs. SPECULATIVE CAPACITY
Capital abundance created the Supercycle.
Capital efficiency will increasingly determine its winners.


⸻


5 | ENERGY, GRIDS & INFRASTRUCTURE
Europe starts financing scarcity
The EIB and BNP Paribas have launched €700 million of guarantees for European grid-equipment manufacturers—€350 million each—with the structure expected to support as much as €2.8 billion of investment.
This is strategically important.
The investment is not directly financing another model or AI data centre.
It finances what those data centres increasingly cannot scale without:
Transformers
Switchgear
Cables
Grid automation
Connection equipment
The causal chain is now unmistakable:
CHEAPER AI → MORE COMPUTE → MORE ELECTRICITY → MORE GRID → MORE EQUIPMENT
The AI Supercycle therefore creates another industrial cycle underneath itself.
And this is where Europe has potentially powerful comparative advantages.


⸻


THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS
Cheaper Intelligence Does Not Mean a Cheaper AI Economy
The deeper consequences of today’s signals extend well beyond token pricing.
1 | THE JEVONS EFFECT
Cheaper AI expands consumption.
Cost ↓ → use cases ↑ → agents ↑ → actions ↑ → total inference ↑
Therefore:
The cheaper intelligence becomes, the more intelligence the economy may consume.
Efficiency can accelerate infrastructure demand rather than reduce it.


⸻


2 | THE MARGIN MIGRATION
As models commoditize, margins can migrate in two directions.
Below the model:
Power → Chips → Memory → Networking → Infrastructure
Above the model:
Distribution → Proprietary Data → Workflows → Agents → Transactions
The middle compresses.
MODEL MARGINS ↓
BOTTLENECK + DISTRIBUTION MARGINS ↑
The most valuable company may not own the smartest model.
It may own the scarce asset or customer relationship through which models are monetized.


⸻


3 | THE CAPEX PARADOX
AI can produce two price regimes simultaneously.
Software + knowledge + intelligence
→ abundance → deflation
while:
Power + grids + memory + cooling + construction
→ scarcity → inflation
Therefore:
INTELLIGENCE DEFLATION
can produce
PHYSICAL-ASSET INFLATION
This could become one of the defining macroeconomic characteristics of the AI-native economy.
It also helps explain why an AI productivity boom and higher-for-longer capital costs can coexist.


⸻


4 | THE HURDLE-RATE EFFECT
Cheaper intelligence increases the number of viable AI applications.
But higher rates simultaneously increase the return required from the infrastructure beneath them.
Therefore:
AI adoption ↑ → infrastructure demand ↑ → CapEx ↑
while:
WACC ↑ → required utilization ↑ → project dispersion ↑
The financial question changes from:
Can we finance the project?
to:
CAN ITS CASH FLOW CLEAR THE HURDLE RATE?
That should produce greater differentiation across AI assets.


⸻


5 | THE GEOPOLITICAL EFFECT
Export restrictions can produce an unintended feedback loop:
Restricted access → domestic substitution → software ecosystem → developers → workloads → stronger alternative stack
The risk is no longer merely Chinese production of a competitive accelerator.
It is the emergence of:
CHIP + SOFTWARE + MODEL + CLOUD + DEVELOPER ECOSYSTEM
The second-order consequence of technological containment can therefore be technological fragmentation.

The investment map changes as the technology improves.

EUROPE 2030
Finance the bottleneck, not the announcement
There is no new major EU AI package today. Policy baseline unchanged.
Europe already plans substantial expansion of AI compute through AI Factories, proposed Gigafactories and the Cloud and AI Development Act.
The more consequential policy question is now:
Which investment unlocks the largest amount of downstream AI capacity?
The EIB grid initiative points toward the right answer.
Europe should prioritize scarce complements capable of unlocking multiple investments:
Grid equipment
Power electronics
Energy storage
Advanced packaging
Industrial automation
Robotics
Domain data
The relevant metric is not euros announced.
It is:
PRIVATE CAPITAL + PRODUCTIVITY UNLOCKED PER PUBLIC EURO

NERC HYPOTHESIS 21
THE LAW OF VALUE MIGRATION
As intelligence becomes cheaper, economic rents migrate toward the least elastic complements required to convert intelligence into economic output.
A first formulation:
AI VALUE CAPTURE
= INTELLIGENCE ABUNDANCE × COMPLEMENT SCARCITY × ECONOMIC CONTROL
That gives the Next Economy Research Center three variables to track systematically:
How fast is intelligence commoditizing?
Where is scarcity increasing?
Who owns that scarcity?
The answer to those three questions may increasingly identify where the next marginal dollar of AI value will accrue.


⸻


5 KEY TAKEAWAYS
1. Cheaper AI can mean more AI spending.
Efficiency expands consumption and can accelerate infrastructure demand.
2. Model commoditization relocates value rather than destroying it.
Margins migrate toward scarce infrastructure and customer distribution.
3. AI can create software deflation and physical-asset inflation simultaneously.
That is a potentially defining macroeconomic regime.
4. Higher rates accelerate the Great Split.
Cheap intelligence will expose expensive, underutilized infrastructure.
5. Follow scarcity, not simply intelligence.
The next winners may increasingly control power, grids, data, workflows, distribution and capital.
CLOSING
FOLLOW THE SCARCITY
The first AI trade was intelligence.
The second was compute.
The next will be the assets that intelligence itself makes scarce.
Because every improvement in AI economics triggers another consequence:
Cheaper models create more agents.
More agents create more inference.
More inference requires more infrastructure.
More infrastructure requires more power and capital.
Scarcity moves.
Margins follow.
AI therefore does not eliminate scarcity.
IT RELOCATES IT.
And that gives investors, boards and policymakers a more powerful way to read the Supercycle:
Follow intelligence to understand where the economy is going.
Follow scarcity to understand where the value will accrue.
FOLLOW THE INTELLIGENCE TO SEE THE FUTURE.
FOLLOW THE SCARCITY TO FIND THE VALUE.

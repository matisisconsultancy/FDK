United States — the financing constraint becomes physical
The most important overnight AI signal is Oracle’s Project Jupiter.
Oracle issued a force-majeure notice in connection with the 1,400-acre New Mexico AI data-centre campus, citing delays in securing power. Reuters reports that the project is now expected to be delayed by roughly one year. The campus has attracted about $18 billion in loans, while Blue Owl has roughly $3 billion of equity committed. It forms part of the broader Stargate AI-infrastructure programme. 
The project remains committed and Oracle says it remains on schedule, so the GVI does not downgrade the United States today.
But the wider financing evidence is material. Morgan Stanley estimates that AI infrastructure could require around $1.5 trillion of external financing by 2028, while Moody’s projects capital expenditure by the six largest US technology companies at approximately $1 trillion in 2027. Bankers interviewed by Reuters say lenders are increasingly scrutinising allocation of power, construction and contractual risk across data-centre financings. 
That produces a crucial transition:
AI demand scarcity is becoming
AI infrastructure scarcity, which is becoming
power scarcity + financing scarcity.
For now, the US stays at 94.3, but Smart Capital momentum deteriorates.
Smart Capital — 5% is no longer the ceiling
The global bond repricing accelerated sharply overnight.
The US 10-year yield is around 5.19%, after touching 5.225%; the 30-year reached 5.50%, its highest since 2004; and the two-year remains near 4.90%. Markets now imply roughly a 71% probability of another Federal Reserve increase next month and more than 90 basis points of additional tightening over the cycle. These probabilities are market pricing, not policy commitments. 
Japan’s 10-year government-bond yield has simultaneously reached approximately 3.12%, its highest since 1996, while French and German sovereign yields are at levels not seen since 2007–08. 
This is a meaningful change in the AI hurdle rate.
At a 5%-plus sovereign benchmark, capital-intensive AI infrastructure must generate materially larger cash returns to justify deployment.
The system is therefore moving from:
“Can we fund AI?”
toward:
“Which AI projects can still clear the cost of capital?”

Norges Bank raised its policy rate by 25 basis points to 4.50%. The central bank said inflation has remained above target and indicated that the policy rate may need to stay elevated for some time, with further tightening possible if required. 
Norway retains an important structural advantage from energy abundance.
But that advantage no longer fully offsets the higher domestic cost of money.

Sweden and Switzerland — the monetary divergence widens
Sweden’s Riksbank kept its policy rate at 1.75%, but said stronger economic activity, a weaker krona and higher energy prices make future tightening more likely. Its updated rate path implies increases beginning this year if the outlook holds. This is forward guidance rather than a completed rate move, so Sweden receives no score adjustment. 
Switzerland offers the opposite signal. The SNB left its policy rate unchanged at 0%, with August inflation at just 0.8% and medium-term inflation pressure described as only slightly higher. 
Switzerland therefore gains relative Smart Capital momentum, but not a numerical upgrade: the policy rate itself did not change.
The divergence matters strategically. As the rest of the advanced world moves toward increasingly restrictive monetary conditions, low structural inflation becomes a competitive asset.
China — Enterprise Transformation accelerates, but overcapacity appears immediately
Fresh evidence from China’s AI-video industry provides a remarkable example of how quickly generative AI can diffuse through an economic sector.
During the first half of 2026, 221,900 new AI-generated shows were launched on Douyin. Production costs for AI short dramas fell from roughly 5,000 yuan per minute to only a few hundred yuan, according to CCTV data cited by Reuters. Cities including Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen are providing compute, subsidies and infrastructure to accelerate adoption. 
That is powerful Enterprise Transformation evidence.
But the economics already reveal the other side: only 1,055 of those 221,900 shows achieved more than 100 million views, according to DataEye. Copyright rules remain incomplete, and industry participants are already warning about oversupply. 
This is almost a laboratory experiment for the Next Economy:
AI lowers the marginal cost of production dramatically.
Then:
supply explodes faster than demand.
That creates a new version of the productivity paradox:
AI can eliminate production scarcity while simultaneously creating attention scarcity and margin compression.
China remains 91.1 because the evidence is sector-specific, but its Enterprise Transformation momentum remains exceptionally strong.
Discovery Capacity — AI tests the terrestrial boundary
Google will launch a prototype satellite next week carrying its Tensor Processing Units as part of Project Suncatcher, testing whether AI chips can operate reliably in low-Earth orbit.
The experiment will assess radiation resistance, thermal management and hardware reliability.Google stresses that this is an experimental mission, not an operational orbital data centre. 
The development receives zero numerical weight today.
But it matters conceptually.
The AI system is beginning to explore alternatives to terrestrial constraints:
grid bottleneck
→ energy scarcity
→ land/water constraints
→ distributed or orbital computing experiments.
That is Discovery Capacity in its purest form: attempting to change the boundary conditions rather than merely optimise within them.

Institutional interpretation — 
THE COST OF ORCHESTRATION
The hidden pattern today is stronger than any individual data point.
AI demand is still enormous.
Models continue improving.
China is diffusing AI into production.
Google is testing computing beyond the terrestrial grid.
But the system required to turn intelligence into output is becoming increasingly expensive.
Oracle exposes the power bottleneck.
Treasury markets expose the capital bottleneck.
Saudi disruptions expose the energy bottleneck.
China’s AI-video explosion exposes the demand-absorption bottleneck.
The competitive problem has therefore evolved.
It is no longer simply:
Who possesses intelligence?
It is:
Who can orchestrate intelligence, power, capital, infrastructure and demand at a cost the economy can sustain?
Main accelerator — falling marginal cost of intelligence
China’s AI-video economics provide dramatic evidence. Tasks that previously cost thousands of yuan per minute can now be produced for hundreds. 
That is the deflationary promise of AI.
Main decelerator — rising marginal cost of everything around intelligence
But simultaneously:
capital costs more.
energy costs more.
grid access costs more.
permitting costs more.
risk transfer costs more.
This is why the AI Supercycle can continue while the economics of individual projects deteriorate.
For investors, the decisive hierarchy is increasingly clear: favour AI exposure with secured power, contracted demand, high utilisation, strong sponsors and conservative financing structures. The scarce asset is not simply compute; it is financeable compute.
For executives, every major AI programme should now be evaluated against a fully loaded hurdle rate incorporating compute, power, infrastructure, financing and deployment costs. Revenue growth alone will increasingly be insufficient.
For policymakers, the implication is fundamental: AI competitiveness now depends on monetary credibility, power availability and infrastructure permitting as much as on algorithms or research funding.
Bottom line
The FDK Global Velocity Index remains at 108.0 on 25 September 2026. Norway declines from 77.2 to 77.1. No rankings change.
The more important change is systemic.
Earlier in the AI cycle, scarcity resided inside the machine:
GPUs.
Memory.
Bandwidth.
It is now moving outside the machine:
electricity.
grids.
water.
capital.
permitting.
And that leads to today’s refinement of the FDK equation:
VELOCITY = PRODUCTIVE INTELLIGENCE ÷ TOTAL SYSTEM FRICTIONThe next winners will not necessarily be those that spend the most on AI.
They will be those capable of orchestrating the entire system so that intelligence compounds faster than the cost of financing and powering it.
That is becoming the defining test of the Next Economy.

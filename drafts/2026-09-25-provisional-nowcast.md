The Weighted Global Velocity Index holds at **108.0** (2025=100), with a provisional unrounded movement of −0.01 that rounds to unchanged at one decimal place. The 30-day regime is clear: AI acceleration remains intact, but the cost and physical feasibility of financing that acceleration are becoming the binding constraint. Model confidence is **96%**.

Today produces one admissible country-level revision: Norway falls −0.1 to 77.1 after Norges Bank raised its policy rate from 4.25% to 4.50%. The more consequential development is in the United States, where the 10-year Treasury yield has reached **5.19%**, the 30-year rose above 5.50% intraday — its highest since 2004 — Brent is back around $105.75, and Oracle's Project Jupiter AI campus has hit a reported one-year power delay.

::signals Overnight signals | five bottlenecks emerge

# United States — the financing constraint becomes physical
Oracle issued a force-majeure notice on its 1,400-acre New Mexico AI data-centre campus, citing delays in securing power; Reuters reports a roughly one-year delay. The campus has attracted about $18 billion in loans, with Blue Owl committing roughly $3 billion of equity, as part of the broader Stargate programme. Because the project remains committed and Oracle says it is on schedule, the GVI does not downgrade the US today.

But the wider evidence is material: Morgan Stanley estimates AI infrastructure could require ~$1.5 trillion of external financing by 2028, while Moody's projects ~$1 trillion of capex from the six largest US tech firms in 2027. AI demand scarcity is becoming AI infrastructure scarcity, which is becoming power scarcity plus financing scarcity.
signal: US stays at 94.3, but Smart Capital momentum deteriorates.

# Smart Capital — 5% is no longer the ceiling
The 10-year is around 5.19% (after touching 5.225%), the 30-year at 5.50%, the two-year near 4.90%. Markets imply roughly a 71% probability of another Fed increase next month and more than 90 basis points of additional tightening — market pricing, not policy commitments. Japan's 10-year has reached ~3.12%, its highest since 1996, while French and German yields sit at 2007–08 levels.

At a 5%-plus sovereign benchmark, capital-intensive AI infrastructure must generate materially larger cash returns to justify deployment. The question shifts from "Can we fund AI?" toward "Which AI projects can still clear the cost of capital?"
signal: A meaningful upward change in the AI hurdle rate.

# Norway — monetary tightening becomes measurable
Norges Bank raised its policy rate by 25 basis points to 4.50%, saying inflation has remained above target and rates may need to stay elevated, with further tightening possible. Norway retains a structural advantage from energy abundance, but that no longer fully offsets the higher domestic cost of money.
signal: Norway moves from 77.2 to 77.1, entirely through the Smart Capital pillar.

# China — Enterprise Transformation accelerates, overcapacity appears
In H1 2026, 221,900 new AI-generated shows launched on Douyin; production costs for AI short dramas fell from ~5,000 yuan per minute to a few hundred, per CCTV data cited by Reuters. Yet only 1,055 of those shows achieved more than 100 million views, per DataEye. AI eliminates production scarcity while creating attention scarcity and margin compression.
signal: China holds 91.1; Enterprise Transformation momentum remains exceptionally strong.

# Discovery Capacity — AI tests the terrestrial boundary
Google will launch a prototype satellite next week carrying its Tensor Processing Units under Project Suncatcher, testing radiation resistance, thermal management and hardware reliability in low-Earth orbit. Google stresses this is experimental, not an operational orbital data centre. It receives zero numerical weight, but signals a system exploring alternatives to grid, energy, land and water constraints.
signal: Discovery Capacity in its purest form — changing boundary conditions, not merely optimising within them.

::section Monetary divergence widens | Sweden and Switzerland
Sweden's Riksbank kept its policy rate at 1.75%, but flagged stronger activity, a weaker krona and higher energy prices as making future tightening more likely — forward guidance, so no score adjustment. Switzerland offers the opposite signal: the SNB held at 0%, with August inflation at just 0.8%. Switzerland gains relative Smart Capital momentum but no numerical upgrade, since the policy rate did not change.

As the advanced world moves toward increasingly restrictive conditions, low structural inflation becomes a competitive asset.

::section Energy — the $100 hurdle returns | oil reinforces the friction
Brent trades around $105.75, up roughly 3% following another attack on Saudi Arabia and renewed supply concerns. The brief relief below $100 earlier this week proved insufficiently persistent. The transmission is clear: oil up, headline inflation up, central-bank reaction up, yields up, AI hurdle rate up. The physical and financial sides of the AI economy are now reinforcing each other negatively.

::section Institutional interpretation | the cost of orchestration
AI demand is still enormous, models keep improving, China is diffusing AI into production, and Google is testing computing beyond the terrestrial grid. But the system required to turn intelligence into output is becoming increasingly expensive. Oracle exposes the power bottleneck, Treasury markets the capital bottleneck, Saudi disruptions the energy bottleneck, and China's AI-video explosion the demand-absorption bottleneck.

The main accelerator is the falling marginal cost of intelligence — tasks that cost thousands of yuan per minute now cost hundreds. The main decelerator is the rising marginal cost of everything around it: capital, energy, grid access, permitting and risk transfer all cost more. This is why the AI Supercycle can continue while the economics of individual projects deteriorate.

::takeaways Key takeaways | for investors, executives and policymakers
- For investors: favour AI exposure with secured power, contracted demand, high utilisation, strong sponsors and conservative financing — the scarce asset is financeable compute.
- For executives: evaluate every major AI programme against a fully loaded hurdle rate covering compute, power, infrastructure, financing and deployment; revenue growth alone is insufficient.
- For policymakers: AI competitiveness now depends on monetary credibility, power availability and infrastructure permitting as much as on algorithms or research funding.
- Scarcity is moving from inside the machine — GPUs, memory, bandwidth — to outside it: electricity, grids, water, capital and permitting.

::close The next winners will not necessarily spend the most on AI — they will be those capable of orchestrating the entire system so that ==intelligence compounds faster than the cost of financing and powering it==.

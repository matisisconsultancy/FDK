THE BIG TAKE

Today’s most consequential shift is Spain’s move from a financing story to a power-and-grid story, while France strengthens the demand side of its sovereign AI stack. And one important correction: the official EuroHPC Gigafactory tender closes 12 November 2026 at 16:00 CET, not 3 December. That materially compresses the execution window for every national consortium. 

1 | EU — THE REAL DEADLINE IS 12 NOVEMBER

EuroHPC’s official call lists 12 November 2026, 16:00 CET as the submission deadline for AI Gigafactory consortia. The tender requires adequate power infrastructure as well as energy- and water-efficiency provisions, reinforcing that this is an infrastructure competition, not simply a compute procurement. 

This matters because the remaining runway is now brutally short. Any consortium that still lacks a final site, secured MW, committed equity, customer/offtake architecture or delivery plan is entering the decisive phase under pressure.

Capital-formation implication: project bankability now matters more than consortium breadth.

Board implication: the winning bids will increasingly look like infrastructure SPVs with contracted demand, not policy coalitions.

⸻

2 | SPAIN — THE GIGAFACTORY RACE IS BECOMING A GRID RACE

Fresh evidence from Spain is significant. Aragón is now expected to attract more than €60 billion of data-centre and digital-infrastructure investment over the next decade. AWS alone plans €33.7 billion in cloud and AI infrastructure there, while Microsoft plans up to €10 billion. Aragón’s fast-track PIGA regime can cut permitting times to under a year. 

But the more important development is the emerging constraint: power availability. Reuters reports that roughly 70% of Spain’s transmission nodes have no available capacity. Spain’s data-centre capacity is nevertheless forecast to rise fourfold to 3.9 GW by 2030, while the government has authorised €17 billion of grid investment through 2030, around 30% above the earlier plan. Utilities including Iberdrola, Endesa and EDP are responding with more storage, hydro, wind and hybrid power structures to provide firm supply for 24/7 compute. 

This changes the Spanish thesis.

Spain’s advantage was previously:

PUBLIC CAPITAL → PRIVATE CAPITAL → INFRASTRUCTURE

It is becoming:

GRID ACCESS → FIRM POWER → INFRASTRUCTURE → COMPUTE → CAPITAL

The national Gigafactory bid is still centred on Móra la Nova and San Fernando de Henares, but Aragón is becoming the benchmark for how quickly Spain can convert permitting, land and power access into global AI capital. 

Investor takeaway: Spain’s upside is now increasingly tied to utilities, storage, grid equipment and power developers—not just telecoms and banks.

CEO takeaway: locating AI capacity in Spain is shifting from a real-estate decision to an energy-architecture decision.

Policy takeaway: Spain can still be Europe’s capital-multiplier model, but only if grid scarcity does not become the new bottleneck.

3 | FRANCE — MISTRAL IS BEGINNING TO CREATE THE OFFTAKE FRANCE NEEDS

Mistral today launched Mistral Large 4, a new open-weight multimodal model. Mistral’s own documentation describes it as a 1.05-trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model with 49 billion active parameters, while Reuters reports that it was trained on Mistral’s own infrastructure and is intended to close part of the gap with frontier systems. 

Why this matters for the Gigafactory race is straightforward.

France already had one of Europe’s strongest supply-side architectures:

EDF power → Bull systems → Scaleway/iliad cloud → Orange distribution → Capgemini/Artefact deployment.

Mistral adds something even more valuable:

NATIVE EUROPEAN COMPUTE DEMAND

The company has recently raised €3 billion, while its strategic relationships with industrial and semiconductor investors such as ASML and Samsung point toward deeper integration across the AI value chain.This strengthens France because the hardest Gigafactory problem is not buying GPUs.

It is keeping them economically utilized.

A globally relevant European model provider can become a natural anchor customer for sovereign compute.

Investor implication: French AI infrastructure now has a stronger potential demand-side flywheel.

Board implication: proprietary European workloads may lower long-term utilization risk.

Policy implication: sovereignty becomes more credible when Europe owns not only the infrastructure, but also models capable of consuming it.

THE COMPETITIVE RANKING — TODAY

France: strongest integrated stack + demand model.

Spain: strongest capital + infrastructure model, but power scarcity is becoming the binding constraint.

Italy: no comparably material new site, power, financing or tender development identified today; the execution gap identified yesterday remains the key issue.

Germany: no comparably consequential new Gigafactory-specific development today; Deutsche Telekom’s Industrial AI Cloud remains strategically relevant, but today’s news was primarily about AI-driven operating savings rather than new Gigafactory capital formation.

Nordics: no fresh development today on the scale of yesterday’s Finland/Salo announcement; the Nordic advantage in secured power remains intact.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. Brussels has shortened the real execution horizon. The official deadline is 12 November 2026. 

2. Spain is shifting from finance to energy. Its next bottleneck is no longer capital—it is firm MW and grid access. 

3. Aragón shows what fast execution looks like. Permitting speed is now part of AI competitiveness. 

4. France is gaining something Spain still lacks: indigenous model demand. Mistral strengthens the utilization case for French sovereign compute. 

5. The race is converging on one formula:

POWER → COMPUTE → OFFTAKE → CASH FLOW → LOWER WACC → MORE CAPITAL

The decisive European AI asset is no longer the Gigafactory itself. It is the system that can keep it powered, utilized and financeable.

A 500 MW British AI Development Revises Its Power Strategy as New Financing Evidence Reveals Where Institutional Capital Is Moving
Where Markets, Capital and Geopolitics Collide
THE BIG TAKE
Europe’s AI infrastructure race has produced a consequential warning: the credibility of electricity-supply arrangements is becoming as important as the availability of investment capital.
Two developments illustrate the emerging financial divide.
On November 7, new reporting confirmed that DataVita has revised the electricity-supply strategy for its proposed 500 MW AI development in Scotland. A project previously associated with more than 1 GW of directly connected renewable generation is now intended to rely entirely on the national electricity grid.
Separately, an important financing commitment announced on November 4, but not covered in our previous assessments, reveals that Pure Data Centres has secured another $780 million in financing, with provisions for substantial expansion.
The contrast is revealing.
Capital continues flowing into European AI infrastructure. But the relationship between promised power, deliverable electricity and financeable computing capacity is receiving increasingly rigorous scrutiny.
1 | UNITED KINGDOM — A 500 MW DEVELOPMENT REVISES ITS ENERGY MODEL
November 7 — Material change in disclosed power arrangements.
DataVita’s proposed AI development at Chapelhall, near Glasgow, was previously associated with a plan to deliver more than 1 GW of renewable generation and storage through private connections.
The company now says its data centers are designed to receive electricity entirely from the national grid.
Renewable electricity would be procured through a power-purchase agreement, while direct connections to wind and solar facilities remain a future possibility.
The proposed development targets 500 MW of computing infrastructure.
DataVita maintains that its commitment to renewable energy has not changed. The revised arrangement does not establish that the project has been cancelled or that its financing has been withdrawn. 
The financial significance
The distinction between a renewable power-purchase agreement and dedicated physical electricity infrastructure is fundamental.
A PPA can provide contractual exposure to renewable electricity generation.
It does not, by itself, guarantee the physical delivery of electricity to a particular computing facility.
The emerging transmission mechanism is:
REVISED POWER STRATEGY → GRID-CONNECTION DEPENDENCE → DELIVERY-TIMETABLE UNCERTAINTY → FINANCING AND COMMISSIONING RISK
The critical unanswered questions concern the timing and contractual status of grid connections, the cost of delivered electricity and the compatibility of those arrangements with the development timetable.
Why European banks should pay attention
The Lanarkshire development already has public-backed financing associated with its initial facilities.
In August, Britain’s National Wealth Fund provided a £202 million guarantee, helping unlock approximately £300 million in debt financing.
The lending syndicate includes ING, ABN AMRO, Santander, the Scottish National Investment Bank and Siemens Financial Services.
Crucially, this financing supports the expansion and construction of two facilities contracted to CoreWeave, rather than establishing that the entire proposed 500 MW campus is financed.
The £202 million represents a financial guarantee, not an equivalent cash grant. 
Investor implication: Existing financed assets, future expansion capacity and proposed electricity infrastructure must be evaluated separately.
Board implication: Material changes in power-delivery assumptions should trigger a review of project economics, financing conditions and commissioning schedules.
Policy implication: Public guarantees should be supported by independently verifiable infrastructure milestones.
2 | EUROPEAN FINANCING — PURE DC SECURES $780 MILLION
November 4 — Previously unreported financing milestone.Pure Data Centres announced a $780 million financing facility, accompanied by an option to expand borrowing by an additional $1.1 billion.
The expanded lender group includes Citigroup Global Markets, First Abu Dhabi Bank and Infranity, joining ABN AMRO, Allianz Global Investors and SMBC.
The company reports securing more than $6.2 billion of financing over the preceding twelve months.
The new $780 million facility is secured. The additional $1.1 billion represents potential expansion capacity, not financing already drawn or committed on identical terms. 
Why this matters for the Nordics
Pure DC is developing a major AI campus in Seinäjoki, Finland.
Its previously announced development includes an initial 110 MW phase, with expansion potential toward 550 MW.
The first phase has secured planning permission, power arrangements and customer commitments. A further €1.3 billion of senior debt financing was announced in July.
The entire proposed expansion is not yet operational or fully financed. 
The hidden pattern
The emerging financing architecture combines:
SECURED POWER → CREDITWORTHY CUSTOMERS → LONG-TERM DEBT → CONSTRUCTION → OPERATIONAL COMPUTE
For institutional lenders, projects that can demonstrate advanced physical infrastructure and predictable customer revenues may offer more attractive financing propositions than speculative developments.
For European Industrial AI, this distinction matters because announced capacity has no productive value until it becomes commercially available.
For investors, the broader opportunity extends across European banks, infrastructure debt funds, grid-equipment suppliers and energy partners.
3 | EUROHPC — PROCUREMENT POSITION UNCHANGED
The formal EuroHPC tender remains open.
The deadlines are unchanged:
December 3, 2026: Large-scale AI Gigafactories.
February 4, 2027: Medium-scale AI Gigafactories.
The November 5 requirement to recreate and resubmit existing applications remains in effect. The official tender page has not yet announced replacement submission links.
No new final award was identified in today’s review.

FIVE KEY TAKEAWAYS
INVESTORS: Distinguish financial commitments from expandable credit facilities, announced capacity from operational capacity, and renewable-power contracts from physical electricity delivery.
EUROPEAN BANKS: Electricity arrangements increasingly deserve the same underwriting discipline as customer creditworthiness and construction completion.
CEOs AND BOARDS: Material changes in infrastructure assumptions can alter an investment’s risk profile without changing its headline capital-expenditure figure.
INDUSTRIAL AI: Access to dependable computing capacity should remain the primary commercial objective. Infrastructure announcements alone are insufficient.
POLICYMAKERS: Public financial support should prioritize demonstrable electricity access, execution readiness and measurable industrial benefits.
THE CLOSING VERDICT
The November 7 development reinforces a fundamental shift in European AI capital formation.
The financial system is increasingly capable of mobilizing substantial capital for computing infrastructure.The harder challenge is ensuring that electricity, permitting, financing and operational commitments remain mutually consistent.
For European investors, that distinction will determine which projects deserve long-duration capital.
For policymakers, it will influence whether public support accelerates productive capacity or finances prolonged development uncertainty.
For CEOs and boards, it defines the next stage of infrastructure due diligence.
The value of an AI Gigafactory will ultimately depend less on the capital announced than on the electricity reliably delivered, the computing capacity commissioned and the cash flows generated.
VELOCITY INSIGHT
In Europe’s AI race, the new investment premium belongs to projects that can demonstrate not merely access to capital, but the physical certainty required to put that capital to work.

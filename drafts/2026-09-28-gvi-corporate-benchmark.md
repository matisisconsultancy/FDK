THE GREAT REPRICING OF AI
NEW RULES, NEW INVESTORS’ PLAYBOOK
The AI race is moving from technological leadership to economic capture. The next winners will control the bottlenecks, convert them into cash, and sustain returns above the cost of capital.
With the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield at 5.17%, capital is no longer forgiving.
The market is entering a more discriminating phase:
Velocity identifies the leaders. Economic capture determines the quality. Valuation determines the return.
The question is no longer simply who has the best AI.
It is:
Who keeps the economics after compute, power, security, infrastructure and capital costs are paid?

WHAT COMES NEXT
1. THE BOTTLENECK MOVES OUTSIDE THE GPU
GPUs were the first scarcity.
The next constraints increasingly sit in:
Packaging → Memory → Networking → Power → Cooling → Grid Access
As model capability becomes more abundant, economic rent migrates toward layers that cannot expand as quickly.
Implication: TSMC, Schneider Electric and the wider power/cooling ecosystem gain strategic leverage.
The next AI premium goes to whoever removes the next constraint.


⸻


2. INTELLIGENCE GETS CHEAPER. DISTRIBUTION GETS MORE VALUABLE.
Model competition is intensifying.
Inference costs are falling.
Capability gaps should continue narrowing.
The second-order consequence:
The strategic scarcity may shift from who owns intelligence to who owns the customer.
That favors:
Microsoft — enterprise workflows
Meta — consumer distribution
Amazon — cloud + commerce
Alibaba — China’s digital ecosystem
Distribution could become more durable than model leadership itself.


⸻


3. AGENTS CREATE THE NEXT INFRASTRUCTURE WAVE
Training powered the first compute boom.
Persistent agents could drive the second.
More Agents → More Inference → More Networking → More Power → More Security
This creates simultaneous demand across Nvidia, Schneider Electric, cloud infrastructure and cybersecurity.
The agent economy therefore expands the AI stack rather than replacing it.


⸻


THE CAPITAL-EFFICIENCY TEST
The next divide is increasingly between two forms of AI capital.
CAPITAL THAT COMPOUNDS
Capex → Utilization → Cash Flow → ROIC → Reinvestment
CAPITAL THAT ACCUMULATES
Capex → Capacity → Depreciation → Financing Cost → Margin Pressure
The distinction is decisive.
The market should increasingly reward AI capital productivity—not AI spending.


⸻


THE BACKLOG REPRICING
Not all backlog has equal economic value.
Announced → Contracted → Financed → Powered → Utilized → Cash-Generating
Each step removes risk.
Each step increases economic quality.
The market should increasingly distinguish between nominal backlog and economic backlog.
Powered, prepaid, high-utilization demand deserves a premium. An unpowered promise does not.


⸻


THREE EXPLICIT DOWNSIDE SCENARIOS
1. THE CAPEX GLUT
AI capacity grows faster than utilization.
Transmission: excess capacity → pricing pressure → slower FCF conversion → weaker ROIC.
Warning signal: AI capex materially outpacing incremental revenue and free cash flow.
The most exposed businesses are those financing capacity before demand becomes productive.


⸻


2. THE RATE SHOCK
Long rates remain elevated—or move higher.Transmission: higher discount rates → higher project hurdle rates → multiple compression → weaker infrastructure economics.
At a 5.17% U.S. 10-year Treasury yield, duration already matters.
A further increase would accelerate the divide between self-funded leaders and externally financed challengers.


⸻


3. MODEL COMMODITIZATION
Frontier-model capability converges faster than monetization.
Transmission: falling model pricing → margin compression → value migration toward distribution, infrastructure and proprietary workflows.
Potential beneficiaries:
Microsoft · Meta · Amazon · TSMC · Schneider Electric
If intelligence commoditizes, customer ownership and bottleneck ownership become more valuable.


⸻


THE NEW INVESTORS’ PLAYBOOK
The investment verdict follows one sequence:
RISK → CASH → ROIC → VALUATION
1. CONTROL THE RISK
Governance, resilience and execution must protect capital first.
Positive signals: Microsoft · Palantir · Palo Alto Networks
2. CONVERT THE CASH
Capacity, adoption and backlog must become utilization and free cash flow.
Positive signals: TSMC · Schneider Electric · Microsoft
3. EARN EXCESS ROIC
Incremental returns must sustainably exceed company-specific WACC.
Growth without excess ROIC is capital accumulation—not value creation.
4. PRICE THE EXPECTATIONS
Only after the first three tests should valuation determine the investment verdict.
Current Read
Highest-quality structural positions: Microsoft · TSMC · Schneider Electric
Exceptional / valuation-sensitive: Nvidia · Palantir · Meta
Capex-to-cash test: Amazon
Strategic acceleration / higher risk: Alibaba
Second-order beneficiary: Palo Alto Networks
The best company and the best-priced security are not necessarily the same.


⸻


BOARD OF DIRECTORS TAKEAWAYS
Make AI ROIC a board KPI.
Track time-to-cash—not simply capex.
Risk-adjust backlog by financing, power and utilization.
Treat AI governance as margin and balance-sheet protection.
And ask one decisive question:
Does each additional dollar of AI capital increase future free cash flow faster than it increases the asset base?
If not, the corporation may be scaling AI while diluting economic value.

OVERALL METHODOLOGY
The FDK Global Velocity Index — Corporate Benchmark measures one thing:
How rapidly a corporation is increasing its capacity to create future economic value.
It does not directly rank securities by valuation or expected return.
GVI SCORE = STRUCTURAL VELOCITY
The score combines ten weighted pillars:
Innovation Velocity 14% · Capital Formation 12% · Productivity Acceleration 10% · AI Adoption & Deployment 10% · Infrastructure Position 10% · Ecosystem Strength 10% · Resilience 9% · Talent Intensity 8% · Strategic Optionality 9% · Execution Quality 8%.
Composite-Score Formula
For corporation i:
GVIᵢ = 0.14(INNOVᵢ) + 0.12(CAPFORMᵢ) + 0.10(PRODᵢ) + 0.10(AIADOPTᵢ) + 0.10(INFRAᵢ) + 0.10(ECOSYSᵢ) + 0.09(RESILᵢ) + 0.08(TALENTᵢ) + 0.09(OPTIONᵢ) + 0.08(EXECᵢ)
Each pillar is scored from 0–100.
Daily Change
ΔGVIᵢ,t = GVIᵢ,t − GVIᵢ,t−1
A score moves only when new evidence materially changes structural Velocity.
A stock-price move alone does not change GVI.
Top-10 Composite
Top-10 Compositeₜ = ΣGVIᵢ,t / 10
for the ten highest-ranked corporations.

EVIDENCE ARCHITECTURE
~70% measured evidence — revenue, margins, free cash flow, capex, backlog, capacity, utilization, adoption and execution milestones.~30% FDK proprietary data analytics — capital productivity, cash-conversion velocity, ecosystem control, bottleneck scarcity, resilience, optionality, concentration risk and probability of conversion into productive cash-generating assets.
Economic Capture is therefore embedded inside the GVI pillars—especially Productivity, Capital Formation, Infrastructure, Ecosystem and Execution—not calculated as a second score.


⸻


VALUATION REMAINS OUTSIDE THE GVI
This separation is critical.
GVI = Structural Velocity
Economic Capture = Quality of Monetization
Valuation = Price Paid for Future Velocity
Investment Verdict = Intersection of all three
The ranking is determined by GVI Score only.
Valuation never changes the GVI ranking.

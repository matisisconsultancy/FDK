The AI Supercycle Is Moving Inside the Central-Bank Reaction Function
August 20, 2026 | FDK
THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
What happens when central banks must conduct one monetary policy across an economy increasingly separating into two different capital, productivity and inflation regimes?
The answer is beginning to emerge.
Artificial intelligence is moving from the margins of central-bank research toward the center of monetary-policy and financial-stability analysis.
Over the past several days, the Federal Reserve, the ECB and other major institutions have begun treating AI less as a distant productivity possibility and increasingly as a current macroeconomic force—one capable of simultaneously affecting investment, electricity demand, productivity, employment, asset valuations, credit formation and potentially the neutral interest rate.
This matters because the AI Supercycle may be creating something conventional monetary frameworks were not designed to manage:
ONE INTEREST RATE. TWO CAPITAL REGIMES.
The underlying structure is the Great Split.
And its financial-market manifestation is becoming the Great Capital Divide.
⸻
THE HIDDEN PATTERN
The dominant AI narrative remains too simple.
AI increases productivity.
Higher productivity reduces costs.
Lower costs reduce inflation.
Therefore AI should ultimately permit lower interest rates.
The direction may ultimately prove correct.
The sequence is incomplete.
Before AI can generate economy-wide productivity, the infrastructure capable of producing and applying intelligence must be constructed.
That requires extraordinary capital formation.
Data centers.
Accelerated computing.
Semiconductors.
Memory.
Electricity.
Grids.
Cooling.
Optical networks.
Construction.
Robotics.
Industrial automation.
And increasingly debt.
The monetary sequence may therefore be:
PHASE I — BUILD INTELLIGENCE
AI Capex → Capital Demand → Electricity Demand → Infrastructure Bottlenecks → Higher Real Yields → Potentially Higher r → Investment Inflation*
followed later by:
PHASE II — APPLY INTELLIGENCE
Industrial AI → Productivity → Greater Supply Capacity → Lower Unit Costs → Higher Potential Output → Structural Disinflation
This is the AI Investment Paradox:
The technology capable of producing structural deflation tomorrow may generate investment inflation today.
The central-bank challenge is determining which phase is dominant—and recognizing that different parts of the same economy may inhabit both simultaneously.
⸻
SIGNAL 1 — THE FED IS MOVING AI INSIDE THE POLICY DEBATE
The July 28–29 Federal Reserve meeting provides an important signal.
Several participants expected AI-related investment and adoption eventually to raise productivity and potential output. But policymakers also emphasized the uncertainty surrounding the timing and magnitude of those gains.
More significantly, the discussion reached financial stability.
The financing of AI infrastructure is increasingly interacting with borrowing, nonbank finance and parts of the banking system.
That changes the nature of the Supercycle.
AI is no longer simply:
Technology → Productivity.
It increasingly becomes:
Technology → Capex → Credit → Financial Conditions → Monetary Policy.
The implications are substantial.
As long as extraordinary AI investment is funded primarily through the free cash flow of enormously profitable technology companies, systemic leverage remains relatively contained.
Once the financing structure expands materially toward corporate bonds, private credit, project finance and banking exposure, AI moves closer to the traditional domain of central-bank financial stability.
⸻
SIGNAL 2 — EUROPE’S 9% NUMBER IS MORE IMPORTANT THAN IT LOOKS
Christine Lagarde’s August 19 intervention provides another critical signal.
Euro-area companies expect to direct roughly 9% of total investment toward AI.
That is already economically meaningful.
But the more important question is what happens after the investment occurs.
Europe must convert:AI Investment → AI Diffusion → Industrial AI → Productivity.
If that conversion fails, Europe can experience the investment and energy costs of Phase I without capturing enough of the productivity dividend of Phase II.
That is the central European risk.
And the ECB evidence reveals an even more consequential mechanism.
A one-percentage-point increase in the perceived proportion of domestic competitors investing in AI is associated with roughly a 0.6-percentage-point increase in a company’s expected AI investment rate.
AI adoption therefore appears partly self-reinforcing.
Investment generates investment. Adoption generates adoption. Competition accelerates propagation.
But those effects remain heavily constrained by national borders.
Europe’s fragmentation therefore does something more dangerous than reduce market efficiency.
IT REDUCES THE PROPAGATION VELOCITY OF AI.
⸻
SIGNAL 3 — THE AI SUPERCYCLE IS MIGRATING FROM EQUITY INTO CREDIT
This may be the most important financial-market development.
The first phase of the AI Supercycle was largely visible through equities.
Semiconductors.
Hyperscalers.
Memory.
Networking.
Utilities.
Data centers.
Robotics.
The next phase increasingly runs through balance sheets.
Hyperscaler gross bond issuance already exceeded $100 billion in 2025, and external financing is expanding across corporate debt, private markets, project finance and other structures.
This creates a fundamental distinction:
Equity absorbs uncertainty through valuation. Debt transforms uncertainty into obligations.
If expected productivity materializes, debt finances enormously productive infrastructure.
If returns take longer, duration extends.
If expected returns disappoint materially, equity repricing can migrate into credit.
Central banks could consequently confront two opposite risks simultaneously:
Above the Investability Frontier
Too much capital chasing expected technological returns.
Below the Investability Frontier
Too little capital available to finance technological transition.
That produces a historically unusual monetary configuration:
FINANCIAL EXCESS + CAPITAL EXCLUSION
at the same time.
⸻
SIGNAL 4 — THE ECB ALREADY HAS A $440 BILLION TRANSMISSION CHANNEL
The Great Split is not geographically contained.
Euro-area households have roughly €440 billion of exposure to major U.S. technology equities, much of it through investment funds and ETFs, while insurers and pension funds also possess significant exposure.
That means an AI-related correction would not remain a Silicon Valley event.
It could propagate through:
US AI Valuations → European Investment Funds → Household Wealth → Redemptions → Asset Sales → Financial Conditions → European Demand
This creates another reason central banks must think beyond conventional banking channels.
The AI financial system increasingly runs through banks and nonbanks.
Funds.
ETFs.
Private credit.
Insurance companies.
Pension funds.
Infrastructure vehicles.
And eventually autonomous financial agents.
⸻
SIGNAL 5 — THE GREAT SPLIT IS BECOMING A MONETARY PHENOMENON
The deepest development is not any individual statistic.
It is the growing divergence between two economic systems.
THE INVESTABLE WORLD
AI Investment → Productivity → Higher ROIC → Earnings Growth → Capital Inflows → Lower Relative WACC → More Investment
THE WORLD FALLING BELOW THE INVESTABILITY FRONTIER
AI Underinvestment → Productivity Gap → Margin Compression → Asset De-Rating → Credit Repricing → Higher WACC → Capital Withdrawal → Lower Investment
This is the Great Capital Divide.
And it creates a new monetary problem.
A central bank sets one interest rate.
But that interest rate increasingly operates across companies and sectors with radically different returns on capital.
A 4% policy rate can be deeply restrictive for a legacy company earning a 6% return on capital.
The same rate may barely constrain an AI infrastructure investment expected to generate dramatically higher returns.
Monetary transmission becomes asymmetric.And, paradoxically, tightening monetary policy can sometimes widen the Great Capital Divide.
⸻
THE NUMBER CENTRAL BANKS MAY BE MISREADING: r*
This brings the neutral interest rate into focus.
If the AI Supercycle increases expected productivity and expected investment returns, desired investment can increase faster than available savings.
That can push the equilibrium real interest rate higher.
The sequence becomes:
Higher Expected Productivity → Higher Expected Returns → Greater Investment Demand → Higher Demand for Capital → Higher r*
The eventual productivity dividend could alter that equilibrium again.
The AI monetary regime may therefore evolve through:
AI CAPEX BOOM → HIGHER r → INDUSTRIAL AI → PRODUCTIVITY SHOCK → NEW r REGIME**
This is why investors should resist automatically equating AI with permanently lower interest rates.
⸻
THE EMPLOYMENT PARADOX
AI creates another problem for central banks.
Productivity and unemployment can rise simultaneously.
If AI allows companies to produce more with fewer workers in particular occupations, employment weakness no longer automatically implies conventional economic slack.
That changes one of the most important signals inside the monetary reaction function.
The distinction becomes:
Cyclical Unemployment ≠ Technological Displacement
The first can justify monetary easing.
The second requires skills, mobility, education, entrepreneurship and new capital formation.
Attempting to solve technological unemployment primarily through lower interest rates risks producing:
Labour Displacement → Monetary Easing → Cheaper AI Capital → Faster Automation → Greater Infrastructure Demand → Inflation
Central banks must therefore become substantially better at identifying the cause of unemployment before reacting to its aggregate level.
⸻
THE NEW CENTRAL-BANK DASHBOARD
CPI remains important.
Payrolls remain important.
GDP remains important.
Wages remain important.
But the Next Economy requires additional signals.
Central banks and investors should increasingly monitor:
AI Capex / Free Cash Flow
When the ratio rises, dependence on external financing increases.
AI Debt / EBITDA
A measure of whether technological expectations are becoming financial leverage.
Electricity Demand Growth / Generation Growth
One of the best indicators of whether Phase I becomes inflationary.
Grid Investment / Data-Center Investment
Infrastructure synchronization matters.
Productivity Growth / Employment Growth
A critical indicator of technological reallocation.
Corporate WACC / Productivity Growth
A potential measure of movement relative to the Investability Frontier.
Sovereign Yield Spread / Productivity Differential
Eventually, technological divergence can migrate into sovereign finance.
⸻
THE CENTRAL-BANK GOVERNANCE QUESTION
The AI Supercycle creates an institutional temptation.
Because AI increasingly connects monetary policy with energy, infrastructure, employment, capital markets and industrial transformation, governments may conclude that central banks require broader mandates.
That would be the wrong conclusion.
Central-bank independence becomes more important, not less, when technological transformation produces enormous distributional consequences.
But independence cannot mean isolation.
The correct architecture is:
POLICY SPECIALIZATION × POLICY SYNCHRONIZATION
Central banks remain responsible for:
price stability;
financial stability;
monetary transmission;
liquidity;
payments;
and prudential supervision where mandates permit.
Governments remain responsible for infrastructure, fiscal policy, energy, education, labour transition and social protection.
Capital-market authorities must create deeper financing channels.
Energy authorities must prevent electricity scarcity from becoming structural inflation.
Education systems must allow human capital to migrate toward expanding industries.
The mandates remain separate.
The causal system does not.
⸻
THE EUROPEAN CONSEQUENCE
Europe sits directly on the fault line.It possesses extraordinary scientific capacity, industrial capabilities, engineering talent and accumulated savings.
Its weakness is increasingly the velocity with which those assets become productive capital.
The danger is not that capital suddenly abandons Europe.
It is subtler.
The marginal global euro, dollar, yen, franc or dirham increasingly finds a more compelling return elsewhere.
That is enough.
Over a decade, incremental capital allocation compounds into structural divergence.
Europe therefore faces a different question from the one normally asked:
Can Europe convert intelligence into productivity fast enough to remain above the Investability Frontier?
That is not merely an industrial-policy question.
Eventually it becomes an ECB question.

5 NUMBERS TO WATCH
9% — the approximate share of euro-area corporate investment expected to be directed toward AI.
0.6 percentage points — the estimated increase in expected AI investment associated with a one-point rise in perceived domestic competitor adoption.
$100 billion — hyperscaler gross bond issuance during 2025, an early marker of AI’s migration toward credit.
~€440 billion — estimated euro-area household exposure to major U.S. technology equities.
18–24 months — the traditional approximate window over which monetary-policy changes can take to approach their maximum macroeconomic impact, compared with an AI economy whose capital and technological signals can reprice dramatically faster.

5 HIDDEN PATTERNS
AI is becoming a monetary variable before becoming a monetary-policy target.
Central banks do not need an AI mandate. But they cannot understand inflation, investment or financial stability without understanding AI.
The Supercycle is moving from valuations into balance sheets.
That makes credit increasingly important.
Electricity is entering the central-bank dashboard.
Not because central banks should manage energy policy, but because energy scarcity increasingly determines whether AI capex produces inflation or productivity.
Employment is becoming harder to interpret.
Weak hiring can coexist with strong productivity and output.
The Great Capital Divide can eventually become a sovereign divide.
Technological underperformance can become productivity underperformance, capital outflow, weaker potential growth and ultimately higher sovereign risk premia.
⸻
5 CONSEQUENTIAL TAKEAWAYS
For central banks: The competitive advantage of the next generation of monetary institutions will increasingly be regime recognition—identifying what kind of inflation, unemployment and capital formation they are actually observing.
For investors: Follow credit, electricity and productivity dispersion as closely as AI equities. The Supercycle is broadening into the entire capital structure.
For CEOs and boards: AI adoption is becoming a cost-of-capital issue, not an IT project. Falling behind technologically can eventually increase WACC and reduce strategic optionality.
For EU policymakers: Capital Markets Union, electricity infrastructure and the Single Market are increasingly components of Europe’s AI—and indirectly monetary—architecture.
For the ECB: The greatest future risk may be operating one interest rate across increasingly divergent Investability Regimes inside the same monetary union.
⸻
THE VELOCITY INSIGHT
The AI Supercycle is forcing central banks to confront a world their traditional models were not built to describe.
A world where productivity can rise alongside unemployment.
Where technological deflation can coexist with infrastructure inflation.
Where high interest rates can crush legacy investment while barely slowing AI capital formation.
Where equity exuberance can coexist with capital starvation.
Where the same technological shock can simultaneously create abundance and scarcity.
And where monetary policy increasingly inherits consequences generated by decisions made far beyond the central bank.
The response should not be a larger central-bank mandate.
It should be a larger map of causality.Because the central question of monetary policy in the Next Economy is moving beyond:
How high should interest rates be?
It is becoming:
WHAT KIND OF ECONOMY IS THIS INTEREST RATE ACTUALLY ACTING UPON?
That is the emerging central-banking challenge of the Great Split.
And the institutions that recognize it first will have the greatest chance of preserving monetary and financial stability while the Investability Frontier moves beneath them.

25 September 2026 | FDK
QUICK TAKE
The evidence is converging.
• The ECB reports that long-term yields rose to multi-decade highs while corporate debt issuance to finance AI investment surged, particularly in the U.S. Sovereign issuance and higher real term premia were also important drivers. • The Bank of England says the policy risk begins when external energy and technology shocks feed into wages, pricing and inflation expectations. • The SNB kept rates at 0%, with inflation still contained because domestic second-round effects remain limited.
The key question is now:
WHERE DOES THE AI SHOCK ENTER THE ECONOMY — AND WHAT DOES IT BECOME ONCE INSIDE?
THE ECB: AI CAPEX IS ADDING TO FINANCING DEMAND
The ECB does not attribute higher long-term yields solely to AI.
Its more precise finding is that global bond yields have risen sharply while AI-related corporate borrowing — especially in the U.S. — has surged alongside heavy sovereign issuance. The broader rise in long-term yields has been driven largely by higher real term premia.
The AI channel is therefore:
AI CAPEX → MORE CORPORATE FINANCING DEMAND → MORE DEBT SUPPLY INTO ALREADY HEAVY BOND MARKETS → GREATER PRESSURE ON THE COST OF CAPITAL
The conclusion is not that AI mechanically raises yields.
It is that the AI build-out is becoming one additional source of financing demand in a bond market already absorbing unusually large issuance volumes.
INVESTOR SIGNAL:
Duration remains vulnerable when AI financing demand, sovereign issuance and elevated term premia reinforce one another.
BOE: THE POLICY TRIGGER IS SECOND-ROUND INFLATION
The Bank of England has sharpened its reaction function.
DIRECT SHOCK → INDIRECT EFFECTS → SECOND-ROUND EFFECTS
Higher energy and technology costs alone do not automatically require tighter policy.
The risk emerges when they feed into:
WAGES PRICING EXPECTATIONS
UK CHAIN:
GLOBAL AI + ENERGY SHOCK → IMPORT COSTS → WAGES + PRICING + EXPECTATIONS → PERSISTENT INFLATION → TIGHTER POLICY
INVESTOR SIGNAL:
Watch second-round inflation, not just the initial shock.
SNB: SAME SHOCK, WEAKER DOMESTIC PASS-THROUGH
The Swiss National Bank kept rates at 0%.
Swiss inflation has risen, but imported pressure has generated weaker domestic effects.
SWISS CHAIN:
GLOBAL SHOCK → IMPORT PRICES → LIMITED WAGE + PRICE RESPONSE → CONTAINED INFLATION → POLICY HOLD
The BoE–SNB contrast is straightforward:
The external shock is similar. The domestic response is not.
THE GREAT SPLIT: THREE MONETARY CHANNELS
U.S. AI enters through capital formation and financing demand. Result: greater exposure to higher market rates.
UK AI-energy pressure enters through imported costs and stronger second-round effects. Result: inflation persistence and tightening risk.
SWITZERLAND The same external shock generates weaker domestic effects. Result: contained inflation and policy hold.
Same technology. Different entry channels. Different domestic outcomes. Different rates.
THE INVESTOR ACTION HIERARCHY
DEFEND DURATION Stay shorter while AI financing demand, sovereign issuance and term premia remain elevated.
OWN THE BUILD-OUT Favor power, grids, compute and infrastructure where AI CapEx is already creating demand.
OWN MONETIZATION Prefer companies turning AI into productivity, margins and cash flow — not simply spending more.
AVOID FRAGILE LEVERAGE Be selective in data centres, power projects, private credit and leveraged suppliers exposed to refinancing.
ROTATE ON PRODUCTIVITY Extend duration and broaden into downstream AI adopters when measurable productivity begins lowering unit costs.
INVESTOR VERDICT:
STAY SHORTER DURATION. OWN THE BUILD-OUT. FAVOR CASH FLOW. AVOID FRAGILE LEVERAGE. ROTATE WHEN PRODUCTIVITY ARRIVES.
EUROPE: THE POLICY TEST
Europe must do three things simultaneously:
FINANCE AI REDUCE ENERGY CONSTRAINTS SHORTEN THE CAPEX-TO-PRODUCTIVITY LAG
The real risk is not simply underinvestment.It is financing the AI build-out without converting enough of that capital into European productivity.
THE VELOCITY INSIGHT
The central-bank question is no longer:
“Is AI inflationary or deflationary?”
It is:
WHERE DOES AI ENTER THE ECONOMY — AND WHAT DOES IT BECOME?
In the U.S., financing demand. In the UK, second-round inflation. In Switzerland, contained imported pressure.
That is the Great Split.
The next monetary regime will be determined by how quickly AI investment becomes productivity — and how much financing and inflation pressure accumulates before it does.

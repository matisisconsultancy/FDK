The Italian Banking Risiko is moving from strategic imagination to regulatory evidence. Capital is available. The scarce asset is now executable legitimacy.
23 September 2026 | FDK Data cut: 09:40 CEST | Market data: 22 September close
The most consequential development is no longer another takeover rumor.
It is Consob.
Market report — not yet confirmed by Consob: Italian reporting says the securities regulator has widened its examination of MPS beyond the economics of the Banco BPM and Banca Generali counter-offers, requesting communications between certain directors and the bank and examining whether the board and the market received complete information on time, with particular attention to the Generali relationship.
That matters because the 29 October MPS shareholder meeting is no longer simply a referendum on whether Luigi Lovaglio can build a third banking pole.
It is becoming a test of whether the defensive architecture is legally clean, procedurally robust and executable after a possible change of control.
Meanwhile, yesterday’s market reversed part of Monday’s excitement around a possible UniCredit–Crédit Agricole understanding on Banco BPM: BPM fell 1.7%, MPS 1.1%, Intesa 0.7% and UniCredit 0.5%. Generali fell a sharper 3.3%. Banca Generali moved in the opposite direction, closing at €65.95, +1.46%.
The message is decisive:
THE MARKET HAS MOVED FROM PRICING ALLIANCE OPTIONALITY TO DEMANDING PROOF OF EXECUTION.
⸻
WHAT CHANGED IN THE LAST 24 HOURS
MARKET REPORT — CONSOB DEEPENS THE MPS SCRUTINY
According to reporting cited by Alliance News, Consob has sought additional material concerning communications between MPS directors and the bank, following earlier requests concerning the Banco BPM and Banca Generali offers, the passivity rule and the relationship with Generali. This remains reported regulatory activity, rather than a new public Consob ruling.
This is the most important change.
The question is shifting from:
Are MPS’s counter-offers strategically credible?
to:
ARE THEY PROCEDURALLY UNASSAILABLE?
The distinction is critical because an Intesa takeover of MPS could occur before completion of the MPS counter-offers. MPS has maintained that the offers would remain irrevocable after a change of control; Intesa has challenged that interpretation. Consob has already forced MPS to provide additional disclosure on valuation, sequencing and the passivity rule.
⸻
CONFIRMED — MPS/MEDIOBANCA CONTINUES TO TOUCH BPM STOCK
MPS’s subsidiary Mediobanca disclosed another small market purchase of Banco BPM shares on 22 September: 55,000 shares at an average €16.2817. The absolute size is economically immaterial and should not be interpreted as stake-building, but it confirms that the MPS offer remains operationally active rather than merely rhetorical.
The signal is therefore executional, not financial.
⸻
CONFIRMED — BANCA GENERALI SIMPLIFIES ITS PERIMETER
Saxo Bank completed the acquisition of Banca Generali’s remaining 49% of BG Saxo, becoming sole owner and renaming the company Saxo Italia. The transaction implements an agreement already reached earlier, so it should not be interpreted as a response to the MPS bid. But it leaves Banca Generali with a marginally cleaner corporate perimeter at exactly the moment when its ownership is under strategic review.
That distinction matters:
Fact: the asset has been sold.
Inference: a simpler perimeter modestly reduces transaction complexity.
⸻
MARKET SIGNAL — THE ALLIANCE PREMIUM RETREATS
Banco BPM’s 4.3% Monday rally on speculation about a possible UniCredit–Crédit Agricole arrangement was partially reversed yesterday. The lack of public confirmation is beginning to matter.
The market is saying:
SHOW US THE ALLIANCE — OR GIVE BACK THE PREMIUM.
⸻
THE DECISIVE ISSUE
THE PASSIVITY RULE IS BECOMING THE REAL BATTLEGROUND
The Italian Banking Risiko has moved through five stages:
Assets → Capital → Time → Governance → Regulatory ValidityMPS can theoretically win shareholder approval for its Banco BPM and Banca Generali offers on 29 October.
But the decisive issue is increasingly what happens afterwards if Intesa succeeds in acquiring control of MPS before those transactions close.
MPS says the offers remain binding.
Intesa believes a new controlling shareholder and board could reconsider them.
Consob is probing the information architecture around the transactions.
This creates the central paradox:
THE MPS DEFENCE BECOMES STRONGER IF IT BECOMES IRREVOCABLE — BUT THE MORE IRREVOCABLE IT BECOMES, THE MORE REGULATORY SCRUTINY MATTERS.
That is now the crux.
⸻
UPDATED STRATEGIC MAP
INTESA SANPAOLO — EXECUTION PREMIUM STRENGTHENS
Intesa’s offer remains straightforward: 1.6 Intesa shares plus €1 cash for each MPS share, conditional on reaching at least 66.67%, subject to waiver. Intesa has already designed substantial branch disposals to address competition issues.
Every additional procedural question surrounding MPS’s defence increases the relative value of Intesa’s simpler architecture.
FDK VIEW: STRONG OVERWEIGHT.
The advantage is no longer only capital strength.
It is transactional simplicity.
⸻
MPS — THE STRATEGY SURVIVES; THE LEGAL HURDLE RISES
MPS still offers a credible industrial thesis:
MPS + Mediobanca + Banco BPM + Banca Generali
with Banco BPM providing commercial banking scale and Banca Generali strengthening wealth management.
The exchange ratios remain 1.567 MPS shares per Banco BPM share and 6.958 MPS shares per Banca Generali share.
But the reported Consob inquiry changes the risk decomposition.
Previously:
Industrial risk + shareholder risk + execution risk
Now:
Industrial risk + shareholder risk + execution risk + procedural risk
FDK VIEW: HIGH CONVEXITY / RISING REGULATORY RISK.
⸻
BANCO BPM — THE SCARCE INDUSTRIAL ASSET
Banco BPM closed Tuesday at €16.09, down 1.74%, but remains 2.38% above last Friday’s official level.
That is important.
The Monday rumor premium has not disappeared.
Banco BPM continues to enjoy at least three strategic destinations:
MPS
Crédit Agricole
a negotiated structure potentially involving UniCredit
That creates scarcity.
FDK VIEW: EVENT-DRIVEN OVERWEIGHT.
⸻
CRÉDIT AGRICOLE — STILL THE KINGMAKER
Crédit Agricole controls 29.3% of Banco BPM.
Last week, Italian press reports carried by Reuters said the French bank considered the MPS proposal unattractive and preferred a combination between Banco BPM and Crédit Agricole Italia. Neither MPS nor Crédit Agricole confirmed those reports.
The rumored UniCredit dialogue remains unconfirmed.
Therefore Crédit Agricole’s greatest asset remains exactly what it was:
THE ABILITY NOT TO CHOOSE YET.
FDK VIEW: STRATEGIC OVERWEIGHT.
⸻
UNICREDIT — CAPITAL FLEXIBILITY, TWO FRONTS
UniCredit shareholders have now formally approved:
up to €5 billion equivalent of convertible AT1 instruments, and
up to 10.603 million new shares linked to settlement of Commerzbank-referenced Total Return Swaps.
The votes passed with 97.39% and 97.70% support respectively.
This gives Andrea Orcel more strategic flexibility precisely as UniCredit confronts two potential fronts:
Germany — converting Commerzbank ownership into governance
Italy — preserving optionality around Banco BPM
The second remains speculation.
The first is real.
FDK VIEW: OVERWEIGHT.
⸻
COMMERZBANK — THE GOVERNANCE NEGOTIATION
Germany has moved from attempting to prevent UniCredit’s advance toward negotiating safeguards.
Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil has told UniCredit that Commerzbank should remain listed, headquartered in Frankfurt and committed to the Mittelstand.
This is a profound change.
The debate is no longer:
Can UniCredit own Commerzbank?
It is:
WHAT PRICE IN GOVERNANCE MUST UNICREDIT PAY TO CONTROL IT?
That is Banking Union in practice.
⸻
GENERALI — STRATEGIC SCARCITY, MARKET VOLATILITY
Generali fell 3.3% to €44.26 yesterday. There is no public evidence tying the move to a new transaction development; it occurred within a weak session for Italian banks and insurers.
Its structural position has not changed.Generali remains simultaneously:
an MPS/Mediobanca strategic asset;
the controlling shareholder of Banca Generali;
an antitrust variable for Intesa;
and a central component of MPS’s proposed €4 billion extraordinary distribution.
FDK VIEW: OVERWEIGHT.
Do not confuse a one-day drawdown with a reduction in strategic scarcity.
⸻
BANCA GENERALI — CLEANER PERIMETER, WIDER SPREAD
The BG Saxo disposal marginally simplifies Banca Generali’s perimeter. The stock closed at €65.95, +1.46%.
This now matters because its transaction spread is materially wider than Banco BPM’s.
FDK VIEW: EVENT-DRIVEN POSITIVE — improving relative asymmetry.
⸻
ITALIAN GOVERNMENT — POWER THROUGH CONDITIONS
No new Golden Power decision has been publicly announced in the last 24 hours.
That does not make Rome irrelevant.
Quite the opposite.
If a UniCredit–Crédit Agricole configuration around Banco BPM became concrete, the government would again face the defining contradiction between:
European capital integration
and
national strategic control.
For now, Rome benefits from optionality by remaining the rule-setter rather than the bidder.
⸻
INTERNATIONAL INVESTORS — THE 29 OCTOBER SWING VOTE
No material new institutional holding disclosure changes the map this morning.
But global funds are becoming more important, not less.
The 29 October MPS vote will require shareholders to choose between two competing capital-allocation systems:
sell MPS into Intesa
or
authorize MPS to become a much larger consolidator itself.
That is no longer a simple takeover vote.
It is a vote on which management team gets to allocate tens of billions of euros of European banking capital.
⸻
CAPITAL-MARKETS REACTION AND VALUATION
Yesterday’s close provides three especially useful signals.
BANCO BPM — THE SPREAD IS NARROWING
MPS closed at €11.85 and Banco BPM at €16.09. Because the proposed €1.208 extraordinary MPS distribution is reserved to existing MPS shareholders and is scheduled before settlement of the offers, the cleaner comparison uses an adjusted MPS value of approximately €10.64.
Applying the 1.567 exchange ratio gives an adjusted implied Banco BPM value of approximately:
€16.68 vs €16.09 market
or roughly a 3.6% residual premium.
That is substantially tighter than earlier in the contest.
The market is pricing a meaningful probability of strategic value being realised elsewhere.
⸻
BANCA GENERALI — THE EXECUTION DISCOUNT REMAINS LARGE
Using the same adjusted MPS currency and the 6.958 exchange ratio gives an implied value around:
€74.05 vs €65.95 market
or approximately a 12.3% spread.
That spread is not an endorsement of the transaction.
It is the market’s price for execution uncertainty.
And it is large.
⸻
MPS — THE INTESA OFFER IS ALMOST FULLY PRICED
At Intesa’s €6.738 reference and the contractual 1.6 shares + €1 cash structure, Intesa’s offer mechanically values MPS at about €11.78 per share, versus MPS at €11.85.
MPS therefore trades only around 0.6% above the mechanical Intesa value.
This is one of today’s most revealing numbers.
THE MARKET IS PRICING VERY LITTLE PURE INTESA DEAL RISK — BUT IT IS STILL PRESERVING A SMALL OPTION VALUE FOR MPS’S ALTERNATIVE PATH.

Generali remains independent.
This remains the cleanest structural outcome.

The probability slips marginally because the reported UniCredit–Crédit Agricole dialogue has still produced no public confirmation and some of Monday’s alliance premium reversed.
⸻
POLITICAL AND REGULATORY IMPLICATIONS
The map is becoming remarkably clear.
Rome wants strategic independence and competition.
Consob wants clean disclosure and a defensible interpretation of the passivity rule.
AGCM must address domestic concentration.
ECB/SSM must assess prudential sustainability.
Berlin wants national safeguards around Commerzbank.
Brussels increasingly argues that European banks need greater scale and fewer political barriers to cross-border consolidation. Senior EU officials last week explicitly linked bank scale, deeper capital markets and Banking Union to Europe’s ability to compete with the United States.
The European equation is therefore becoming:
CONTINENTAL SCALE + NATIONAL SAFEGUARDS + SUPRANATIONAL SUPERVISION
This is not the abolition of national banking systems.
It is their federation through capital.
⸻
THE HIDDEN PATTERN
THE CONTEST IS BECOMING A BATTLE OVER CAPITAL-ALLOCATION LEGITIMACY
The superficial interpretation of the Risiko is still M&A:
Who buys MPS?
Who gets Banco BPM?
Who controls Banca Generali?
Who wins Commerzbank?
But the deeper structure is different.
Each transaction asks the same question:
WHO HAS THE LEGITIMACY TO ALLOCATE THE NEXT EURO OF CAPITAL?
Intesa argues that scale plus execution earns that right.
MPS argues that strategic independence plus coalition-building earns it.
UniCredit argues that Continental Scale earns it.
Crédit Agricole argues that patient ownership and optionality earn it.
Governments argue that national interest gives them veto rights.
Brussels argues that European competitiveness requires those vetoes to become narrower.
Consob’s reported intervention is therefore not peripheral.
It goes directly to the heart of the system:
CAPITAL ALLOCATION REQUIRES NOT ONLY MONEY — BUT PROCEDURAL LEGITIMACY.
⸻
WHAT TO WATCH — NEXT 24–72 HOURS
Consob first. Any official confirmation, further disclosure request or clarification on board communications, Generali and the passivity rule immediately changes MPS execution risk.
MPS second. Watch whether the bank responds publicly to the reported widening of Consob’s inquiry and whether Mediobanca continues disclosed Banco BPM purchases.
Crédit Agricole–UniCredit third. The Monday report now needs evidence. A denial would remove optionality from BPM; confirmation would transform the entire map.
Banco BPM fourth. €16 is becoming the critical market reference. A renewed move above Monday’s highs without a formal catalyst would signal that investors believe a competitive process is emerging.
Banca Generali fifth. The BG Saxo disposal is complete. The next important event is Generali’s formal strategic stance on MPS’s proposal.
Berlin sixth.Any concrete agreement on Commerzbank headquarters, listing, employment and supervisory-board governance would move UniCredit from economic control toward executable control.
⸻
VELOCITY INSIGHT
The Italian Banking Risiko began as a contest for assets.
Then it became a contest for scale.
Then capital.
Then alliances.
Now it has reached something more fundamental:
LEGITIMACY.
The winner will not simply be the institution with the largest balance sheet or the highest bid.
It will be the institution able to prove simultaneously that its architecture is:
economically superior, politically acceptable, regulatorily defensible and governable after closing.
The new equation is:
CONTINENTAL SCALE × CAPITAL DISCIPLINE × GOVERNANCE × REGULATORY LEGITIMACY = EXECUTABLE CONTROL
And this morning, Consob—not another bank—is setting the pace of the Risiko.

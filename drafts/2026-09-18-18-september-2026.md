THE BOTTLENECK ECONOMY
Intelligence is getting cheaper. The infrastructure underneath it is getting more expensive. Compute prices are rising, memory remains scarce, optical capacity is being expanded, sovereign capital is pouring into AI factories, and central banks are tightening into the boom. The Next Economy is discovering where the real pricing power sits: not in abundance, but at the bottlenecks that abundance creates.
Markets enter Friday with an intriguing split.
Asian equities are stronger, oil is easing and the U.S. 10-year Treasury has retreated below 5%. MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan was up roughly 1%, the Nikkei around 0.8%, and South Korea’s KOSPI more than 2%. Brent briefly fell as much as 1.5% to around $103.29/bbl, while the U.S. 10-year Treasury eased to roughly 4.94%. The euro was near $1.148, and gold around $4,361/oz. 
But the apparent relief hides a more important structural move.
The Bank of Japan has just raised its policy rate to 1.25%, the highest in 31 years. The Federal Reserve tightened Wednesday. The ECB raised rates last week. The Bank of England is warning it may also need to move if Middle East energy pressures persist. Australia’s central bank says inflation risks are materializing. A global tightening cycle is emerging just as AI infrastructure investment is moving deeper into power, memory, networking and data-center capacity. 
At the same time, the physical AI economy is displaying extraordinary pricing power. Nebius is raising selected Nvidia GPU rental prices by 17%–21%, some CPU instances by 25%, and memory offerings by roughly 41%. Crusoe has raised $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion valuation, backed by investors including Mubadala Capital, QIA and Nvidia, while reporting more than $140 billion of contracted value and over 6 GW of contracted capacity. 
This is today’s central contradiction.
**The price of intelligence may be falling.
The price of producing intelligence is not.**
That is the defining feature of:
THE BOTTLENECK ECONOMY

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
If AI is supposed to drive intelligence costs toward zero, why are compute, memory, power, networking and capital becoming more expensive?
Because digital abundance generates physical scarcity.
The new causality chain is becoming:
Models Improve
→ Usage Explodes
→ Inference Expands
→ Compute Demand ↑
→ Memory Demand ↑
→ Optical Bandwidth ↑
→ Power Demand ↑
→ Capital Required ↑
→ Bottlenecks Multiply
→ Scarcity Rents ↑
The market therefore needs to distinguish between two completely different curves.
The first is the Intelligence Cost Curve.
That curve is falling.
The second is the Infrastructure Scarcity Curve.
That curve is rising.
The Great Split is increasingly between those exposed to falling intelligence prices and those owning the bottlenecks required to deliver intelligence.
FDK interpretation: competitive advantage is migrating upstream—from applications toward infrastructure, and from infrastructure toward the narrowest points in the infrastructure stack.

THE FIVE MOST IMPORTANT OVERNIGHT SIGNALS
1. THE BOJ HAS JOINED THE GLOBAL TIGHTENING CYCLE — AND THE YEN STILL FELL
Verified: the Bank of Japan raised its policy rate from 1% to 1.25%, the highest level in 31 years, by a 7–2 vote. The two dissents were more cautious, and the yen weakened toward 156.9 per dollar after the announcement as investors judged the decision less hawkish than it might have been. 
This matters because Japan has spent decades supplying cheap capital to the world.
The chain is now changing:
Japan Rate ↑
→ Domestic Yields ↑
→ Carry Trade Economics Weaken
→ Japanese Capital Reprices
→ Global Funding Cost ↑
The Fed has already restarted hiking. The ECB tightened last week. The BOE is warning of the same possibility. Australia says inflation risks are becoming real. 
FDK interpretation
The world is entering an unusual regime:
AI CAPEX BOOM + GLOBAL MONETARY TIGHTENING
Historically, enormous infrastructure booms often coincided with abundant capital.
This one is increasingly happening with capital priced at 4%–5% sovereign rates.
That makes capital productivity a strategic advantage.

2. COMPUTE HAS PRICING POWER
Verified overnight: Nebius will raise pay-as-you-go prices for selected Nvidia GPUs from October 1 by 17%–21%.
Some CPU-only instances will increase by 25%.
Selected memory offerings will rise by approximately 41%.
This is Nebius’s second pricing increase in three months. The company says demand is surging, and its recent AI-cloud contracts average more than $1 billion each. 
That is a critical data point.
The AI narrative assumes technological progress lowers the cost of intelligence.
True.
But the market for scarce infrastructure behaves differently.
When demand grows faster than installed capacity, technological improvement can coexist with higher clearing prices.
That produces:
Cost per Unit of Intelligence ↓
while simultaneously:
Price of Guaranteed Compute ↑
FDK interpretation
This is one of the purest examples yet of the Access Premium becoming a Scarcity Rent.
The companies capturing value may not be those producing the cheapest models.
They may be those possessing the GPUs, power, memory and network capacity that everyone else needs.

3. SOVEREIGN CAPITAL IS BUYING THE AI FACTORY
Verified: Crusoe has raised $3.9 billion in a financing round that values the AI-infrastructure provider at $30.9 billion.
The round was co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners, with participation from investors including QIA and Nvidia.
Crusoe says it now has more than $140 billion in contracted value, more than 6 GW of contracted capacity, and around 1 GW already operational. 
This is more than venture financing.
It tells us how sovereign capital is evolving.
Gulf capital is moving from:
portfolio diversification
toward:OWNERSHIP OF THE PHYSICAL INFRASTRUCTURE OF INTELLIGENCE.
That matters strategically.
A sovereign wealth fund that owns AI infrastructure gains exposure not just to a company.
It gains exposure to:
Compute rents.
Energy demand.
Data-center real estate.
Infrastructure financing.
Long-duration contracts.
Strategic technology relationships.
FDK interpretation
The convergence continues:
**SOVEREIGN CAPITAL
AI INFRASTRUCTURE
ENERGY
LONG-TERM CONTRACTS**
This will become one of the defining capital-formation architectures of the Next Economy.

4. THE BOTTLENECK IS MOVING FROM GPUs TO MEMORY AND OPTICS
Two developments overnight point to the same pattern.
Verified: China’s CXMT is preparing to expand from DRAM into NAND flash at a time when AI-server demand has helped create a global memory shortage expected by industry executives to persist through at least 2027. Capital expenditure has been redirected toward DRAM and HBM, which has limited NAND additions and intensified storage scarcity. 
Separately, GlobalFoundries and Marvell expanded a multi-year agreement to increase manufacturing capacity for silicon-germanium technology used in high-speed optical connections for AI data centers. The companies specifically cited surging demand for optical transceivers, near-packaged optics and co-packaged optics. 
This validates the bottleneck sequence we have been tracking.
**ACCELERATED COMPUTING
→ MEMORY
→ OPTICAL BANDWIDTH
→ POWER
→ INFRASTRUCTURE
→ DURATION**
Scarcity does not disappear.
It migrates.
Solve the GPU bottleneck and memory tightens.
Solve memory and networking tightens.
Solve networking and electricity tightens.
Solve electricity and grid connections tighten.
FDK interpretation
The highest-return investment opportunities may repeatedly shift one layer upstream from the constraint investors are currently focused on.
That is the central dynamic of the Bottleneck Economy.

5. AI IS BEGINNING TO BUILD AI — AND THAT MAKES DEMAND REFLEXIVE
This may ultimately be the most consequential signal of the week.
Verified: Anthropic says Claude now “leads” 26% of the research and development work used to build the company’s next-generation AI systems, up from roughly 1% in March.
More than 90% of Anthropic’s research work now involves human-AI collaboration.
Around 30,000 AI agents were operating on Anthropic’s main internal research and engineering platform at any one time in August.
The company says Claude is not independently autonomous in this work and remains under human oversight. 
Anthropic also disclosed something revealing about the economics.
Approximately 6% of AI research compute during a sampled July week went to safety work.
For research led by AI, that share rose to roughly 12%. 
This creates a new causality chain:
**AI CAPABILITY ↑
→ AI RESEARCH PRODUCTIVITY ↑
→ AI Builds Better AI ↑
→ More Agents ↑
→ More Tokens ↑
→ More Inference ↑
→ More Compute Demand ↑**
This is reflexive demand.
AI infrastructure is not merely serving end-users.
Increasingly, AI is itself becoming one of the largest consumers of AI infrastructure.
FDK interpretation
If this continues, compute demand may compound faster than conventional application forecasts imply.
The customer of accelerated computing is increasingly the intelligence-production process itself.

FIVE HIDDEN PATTERNS BEYOND THE NEWS
First — falling unit costs do not imply falling industry revenues
This is one of the most important economic misunderstandings surrounding AI.
If the price of intelligence falls by 50% but consumption increases tenfold, total infrastructure demand rises dramatically.
That is the Jevons Paradox applied to intelligence.
**Cheaper Intelligence
→ More Intelligence Consumed
→ More Infrastructure Required**
The AI deflation thesis and the AI infrastructure supercycle can therefore both be correct simultaneously.


⸻


Second — the most valuable AI assets may increasingly resemble utilities
Crusoe’s 6 GW of contracted capacity is not software economics.
It is industrial infrastructure.
Nebius has multi-month commitments.Data centers require energy, water, land, fiber, cooling and transmission.
The emerging economics increasingly resemble:
telecom towers,
electric grids,
LNG terminals,
pipelines,
rail infrastructure.
The AI economy is becoming capital-intensive enough that duration, utilization and contracted cash flow may matter as much as technological leadership.

Third — the safety problem itself expands the AI market
Anthropic’s numbers demonstrate this directly.
More autonomous AI requires:
monitoring,
testing,
red-teaming,
governance,
cybersecurity,
evaluation,
containment.
All of those consume compute.
So the safety response does not necessarily shrink infrastructure demand.
It creates another demand category.
Governance Capex becomes Compute Demand.

Fourth — China is attacking scarcity through industrial policy
CXMT’s planned push into NAND is not merely a corporate diversification move.
CXMT raised approximately $8.6 billion in July in Asia’s largest IPO of the year and, like YMTC, has benefited from national and local-government support. 
The strategic objective is clear.
China is systematically reducing dependence across:
logic,
memory,
storage,
networking,
models,
and AI infrastructure.
The U.S.–China contest is therefore becoming a race to eliminate bottlenecks from the domestic stack.

Fifth — Europe’s opportunity is moving into the physical world
Europe remains dramatically behind in hyperscale compute.
The EU has proposed seven major AI computing hubs and governments have pledged approximately €3 billion in future compute purchases, while six largely U.S.-based hyperscalers are expected to spend more than $1.3 trillion by 2027. 
But another opportunity is opening.
Lucid and Bolt announced plans to deploy 25,000 fully autonomous vehicles across European cities, eventually targeting 100,000 vehicles by 2035, using Nvidia’s Hyperion technology. 
That matters.
Europe may struggle to dominate frontier-model development.
But it possesses enormous deployment environments in:
mobility,
manufacturing,
energy,
railways,
logistics,
healthcare,
cities.
Europe’s best AI strategy may therefore be 
industrialization rather than imitation
.

IMPLICATIONS FOR EUROPE AND POLICYMAKERS
Europe’s bottleneck is increasingly obvious.
It is the intersection of:
ENERGY + GRID + CAPITAL + COMPUTE
The continent’s average electricity price for large industrial users was approximately $107/MWh in 2025, more than twice the U.S. level and roughly 57% above China’s.
Its fragmented transmission network prevents electricity from moving efficiently from where it is abundant to where data-center demand is emerging. 
This is not simply an energy-policy problem.
It is an AI competitiveness problem.
Europe cannot construct an AI industrial strategy while retaining an electricity architecture designed for the previous economy.The policy equation must therefore become:
**ONE ENERGY MARKET
FASTER PERMITTING
GRID INVESTMENT
SOVEREIGN COMPUTE
INDUSTRIAL AI
= EUROPEAN VELOCITY**
Without that integration, Europe will continue financing American AI while paying scarcity rents to foreign infrastructure.

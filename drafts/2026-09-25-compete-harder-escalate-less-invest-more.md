25 September 2026 | FDK
The Big Take
The Trump–Xi summit does not signal an AI détente.
It signals something potentially more consequential:
A MORE STABLE GEOPOLITICAL FRAMEWORK FOR A MORE INTENSE TECHNOLOGICAL RIVALRY.
Washington and Beijing have postponed rather than resolved their deepest disputes. The trade truce has been extended to January 10, while technology controls, rare earths, Taiwan and the architecture of the AI race remain fundamentally contested. At the same time, the two sides are developing a dialogue around AI risks and potential incident communication.
The emerging regime is therefore not convergence.
It is:
MANAGED TECHNOLOGICAL BIPOLARITY.
And that could make the AI Supercycle larger and longer.
⸻
The Great Split survives
Nothing in Washington changes the fundamental competitive architecture.
UNITED STATES
Frontier models → advanced semiconductors → hyperscale AI factories → specialized agents → higher-value intelligence.
CHINA
Efficient models → domestic accelerators → domestic memory and networking → lower inference costs → industrial deployment.
America continues to push up the capability frontier.
China continues to push down the cost and dependency frontier.
The summit does not close this divide.
It potentially stabilizes the environment in which both sides can deepen it.
⸻
AI has become a strategic-stability issue
This is the real geopolitical inflection.
The emerging U.S.–China dialogue around AI incidents suggests that artificial intelligence is moving from a pure technology issue toward the realm of systemic strategic risk.
The logic is straightforward:
Capability ↑ → Autonomy ↑ → Strategic Risk ↑ → Need for Communication ↑
This is not cooperation on technological leadership.
It is competitive risk management.
The emerging doctrine becomes:
COMPETE ON CAPABILITY.
MANAGE CATASTROPHIC RISK.
That distinction matters.
⸻
Stability gives China its scarcest asset: time
For Beijing, every additional quarter without major escalation allows:
Huawei → Alibaba → CXMT → domestic networking → AI factories → DeepSeek/Qwen/Kimi
to compound.
The Chinese equation becomes:
STABILITY → TIME → SUBSTITUTION → SCALE → LOWER DEPENDENCY
China does not necessarily need Washington to remove semiconductor restrictions.
It needs enough time to engineer around them.
That makes geopolitical stabilization strategically valuable to Beijing.
⸻
Stability gives America something different: duration
America already possesses the stronger frontier ecosystem.
Its loop is:
CAPITAL → COMPUTE → INTELLIGENCE → REVENUE → MORE CAPITAL
Greater geopolitical predictability potentially extends the investment horizon behind that loop.
That matters when frontier AI increasingly requires:
multi-gigawatt AI factories, advanced semiconductors, HBM, networking, power generation, and tens of billions of dollars of capital.
China needs time to substitute.
America needs duration to compound.
That is the strategic symmetry emerging from Washington.
⸻
The Supercycle implication is powerful
A grand technology agreement might have reduced duplication.
Washington produced no such agreement.
Instead, two competing AI systems continue to build simultaneously.
AMERICAN CAPEX
Frontier accelerators → AI factories → power → agents → higher-value intelligence.
CHINESE CAPEX
Domestic chips → memory → networking → sovereign AI infrastructure → lower-cost intelligence.
This creates:
TWO CAPITAL CYCLES.
Geopolitical fragmentation remains CAPEX inflationary.
But strategic stabilization can give those capital cycles greater duration.
That combination matters enormously.
⸻
THE HIDDEN PATTERN
The Trump–Xi summit creates a powerful new causal chain:
Strategic Stability → longer investment horizons → more U.S. frontier CAPEX + more Chinese substitution CAPEX → faster technological competition → lower cost of useful intelligence → greater deployment → more inference → more infrastructure.
So:
GEOPOLITICAL VOLATILITY ↓
TECHNOLOGICAL COMPETITION ↑

The New Velocity Equation
AI SUPERCYCLE VELOCITY
= Capability × Capital Formation × Intelligence Deflation × Deployment × Strategic Stability
The last variable is new.
And after Washington, it matters.
Strategic stability does not end competition.
It extends its runway.

Investor Takeaways
Do not price an AI détente. The Great Split remains intact.
Add strategic stability to the AI investment thesis. Lower geopolitical tail risk can extend the infrastructure cycle.
Stay focused on the U.S. frontier stack. Advanced compute, HBM, networking, power and agents remain core scarcity assets.
Watch Chinese substitution accelerate. Stability gives Huawei, Alibaba, CXMT and Chinese model developers more time to reduce dependency.
Own the common denominator. Both systems require more compute, memory, networking, optics, electricity, cooling and AI factories.
⸻
THE VELOCITY INSIGHT
The Trump–Xi summit does not end the Great Split.
It may do something more important:
MAKE IT MORE DURABLE.
America keeps raising the intelligence ceiling.
China keeps lowering the economic floor.
Both keep investing.
And Washington and Beijing begin constructing enough guardrails to reduce the probability that competition becomes rupture.
That gives us the new regime in three lines:
COMPETE HARDER.
ESCALATE LESS.
INVEST MORE.
For the AI Supercycle, that could be an unusually powerful equilibrium:
two technology systems, two capital cycles, falling intelligence costs—and enough strategic stability for both to keep compounding.
That is the material inflection after Washington.

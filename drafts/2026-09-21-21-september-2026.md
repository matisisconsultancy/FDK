THE UNDERWRITING PHASE
AI is still lifting markets—but money is getting more expensive. South Korea is up 1.5%, Nasdaq futures 0.6%, and Taiwan’s semiconductor ecosystem is expanding again. Yet the U.S. 2-year yield has reached 4.7604%, SoftBank is issuing more than $11 billion of bonds to finance OpenAI, Australia’s Firmus is preparing a $5 billion data-centre IPO, and China is slowing humanoid-robot listings where valuations have run ahead of commercial demand.
The AI Supercycle is not ending. It is entering the phase where capital markets decide which parts deserve to scale.
The Great Split is evolving again:
BANKABLE AI vs. NARRATIVE AI

The Fundamental Question
Can AI cash flows rise faster than the cost of financing the infrastructure required to produce them?
At 4.76% two-year Treasury yields, technological ambition is no longer enough. The winners now need scarcity, contracted demand and returns above the rising cost of capital.

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy
1. AI: from technological competition to geopolitical governance
Verified: Washington and Beijing concluded Sunday’s talks with the U.S. proposing an AI safety notification mechanism covering incidents that rise to national-security significance. The issue moves to the Trump–Xi summit later this week. 
Read-through: The strategic contest is no longer only about access to chips or model leadership. It is expanding into who defines the rules under which frontier systems operate.
Implication: AI governance itself becomes strategic infrastructure.


⸻


2. AI finance: from corporate capex to capital-market underwriting
Verified: SoftBank launched $10 billion plus €1 billion of senior unsecured bonds to finance a further $10 billion OpenAI investment. Separately, Australian data-centre operator Firmus plans a $5 billion IPO, potentially the second-largest in Australian history. 
Read-through: The financing base of the AI boom is moving beyond hyperscaler balance sheets into credit and public equity markets.
Implication:
CAPACITY → CONTRACTS → CASH FLOW → CREDIT QUALITY → SCALE
The AI build-out is becoming an underwriting problem.


⸻


3. Physical AI: from valuation to proof of demand
Verified: Chinese regulators are slowing humanoid-robot IPOs amid concern over valuations and the quality of revenues tied to state-backed projects. Unitree has fallen 55% from its post-IPO peak; industry investors estimate some valuations could fall 60–70% if certain government-linked revenues were excluded. 
Read-through: Beijing still regards embodied AI as strategic—but the capital-allocation standard is tightening.
Implication: The next winners in robotics will be determined by factory deployment, recurring orders and unit economics, not policy sponsorship alone.


⸻


4. Semiconductors: from wafers to advanced packaging
Verified: Taiwan broke ground today on a new 88.7-hectare advanced-packaging industrial park in Kaohsiung, anchored by TSMC. The facilities will support testing, validation and talent development around packaging technologies used for Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom AI chips. 
Read-through: The AI bottleneck keeps migrating downstream—from GPUs to memory, networking, packaging and power.Implication: Value increasingly accrues to the companies controlling throughput, not merely transistor density.


⸻


5. Energy: price relief does not mean physical normalization
Verified: Brent has fallen to about $101.63, helped by recovering Saudi exports. Yet only 12 trackable commodity vessels crossed Hormuz over the weekend versus roughly 125 large commercial vessels per day before the war. Europe is simultaneously expected to run a 510,000 bpd jet-fuel deficit in Q4, with inventories at the ARA hub at seven-year lows. 
Read-through: The market is pricing better flows faster than the physical system is healing.
Implication:
ENERGY VOLATILITY REMAINS EMBEDDED IN WACC
Transport, aviation, chemicals and energy-intensive European industry remain exposed.

Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines
The first is that higher rates are not killing the AI trade; they are concentrating it. Asia’s semiconductor-heavy markets are outperforming even as Fed expectations turn more hawkish. The market is distinguishing technological scarcity from duration risk. 
The second is that the same capital markets are simultaneously funding OpenAI, preparing a giant data-centre IPO and rejecting weak robotics revenue quality. That is not contradiction. It is maturation.
The third is emerging in Europe. Societe Generale today raised its 2029 return-on-tangible-equity target to 13–14% and explicitly expects AI-related productivity gains to help cut costs. Europe’s near-term AI opportunity may therefore appear first in operating leverage inside incumbent institutions, not only in frontier-model creation. 
But Europe faces the opposite pressure from physical scarcity: energy, transport fuels and sovereign risk. France’s borrowing premium over Germany exceeded 100 basis points on Friday for the first time since the euro crisis. The continent is confronting a simultaneous cost-of-capital and cost-of-energy test.

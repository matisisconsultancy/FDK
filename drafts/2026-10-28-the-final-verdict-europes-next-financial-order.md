The October 29 vote may determine who controls MPS. It will not determine who creates the greatest economic value.

The ECB has cleared a major regulatory hurdle. Shareholders are approaching their verdict. Competition authorities will define the boundaries of financial influence. Capital markets will ultimately determine whether the new structures justify their cost.

But the real transformation extends beyond Italy.

Europe is moving from a banking system shaped by national ownership and institutional history toward one increasingly tested by Continental Scale, capital mobility and economic productivity.

This transition will produce a new hierarchy of financial power.

Institutions that merely consolidate balance sheets may discover that greater size increases complexity without improving returns. Those capable of integrating operations, reducing capital frictions and mobilising savings into productive investment may command enduring valuation advantages.

The next market rotation will distinguish between those who acquire financial power and those who create economic power.

FIVE DECISIVE TAKEAWAYS FOR INVESTORS

1 | The ownership trade is approaching its conclusion. The economic-profit trade is only beginning. As transaction uncertainty diminishes, attention should shift toward sustainable ROTE, synergy realisation and post-acquisition capital efficiency.

2 | The next valuation premium must be earned, not acquired. Financial institutions delivering returns consistently above their sovereign-adjusted cost of equity should outperform those relying primarily on consolidation expectations, all else equal.

3 | Generali may become the next strategic inflection point. Governance independence, regulatory remedies and the economics of long-duration savings could reshape relative valuations beyond the immediate MPS transaction.

4 | Continental Scale is entering its decisive test. UniCredit–Commerzbank will help establish whether European banking integration can deliver genuine capital productivity or merely larger institutions operating within fragmented national markets.

5 | Anticipate the shift from financial consolidation to capital formation. The longer-term opportunity lies in institutions capable of financing Europe’s industrial transformation while generating sustainable, risk-adjusted economic returns.

THE CLOSING | THE END OF OWNERSHIP AS A STRATEGY

The first phase of European banking consolidation was about acquiring control.

The second will be about proving its economic value.

The third will determine who commands Europe’s capital formation—and therefore its industrial future.

The October 29 vote is a turning point, not an endgame.

Intesa may emerge with control of MPS. UniCredit may establish a new benchmark for Continental Scale. Generali may redefine the boundaries of financial independence.

But none of these outcomes alone will create lasting financial leadership.

That will depend on the capacity to convert ownership into operating efficiency, financial scale into capital productivity, and European savings into sustainable economic growth.

For investors, the next opportunity is not simply to anticipate who wins the acquisition. It is to identify who captures the economic value after the acquisition is complete.

For boards, the imperative is equally clear: no acquisition should be justified by the power it confers unless the capital it consumes earns more than its full economic cost.

For Europe, the historical test is whether Banking Union can evolve into a genuine Capital Formation Union.

VELOCITY INSIGHT

The old European banking order was built on control of institutions. The next will be built on control of capital allocation. The winners will be those who turn that capital into economic power.

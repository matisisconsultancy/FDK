U.S. retail sales fell 0.6%. Core sales fell 0.4%. Consumer sentiment dropped to 51. China generated a record 340 billion yuan contraction in new bank lending. Euro-area GDP grew 0.4%, but employment only 0.1%. Yet global equity funds attracted another **$18.6 billion**, European equities absorbed **$13.5 billion**, corporate earnings remain exceptionally strong, and Big Tech AI investment is headed above $700 billion.

The Great Split has reached the demand side of the economy. *Market snapshot at approximately 7:30 PM CEST. European markets are closed; the U.S. cash session remains open.*

Can the AI Supercycle remain self-reinforcing if the capital formation it creates accelerates faster than the household income, consumption and private credit ultimately required to validate that productive capacity? This is the question financial markets began confronting today. For most of the year the debate around artificial intelligence has focused on whether capital expenditure can produce sufficient returns. Today introduced the other side of that equation.

::pull THE GREAT SPLIT
The capital economy remains remarkably strong. The demand economy is becoming less so.

::signals The Five Most Important Market Signals | Where the split became visible
# The U.S. Consumer Finally Broke Momentum
U.S. retail sales fell 0.6% in July against expectations for a 0.1% increase — the first decline in nine months and the largest monthly fall in fourteen months. The core control group used in GDP consumption calculations fell 0.4% versus expectations for a 0.3% increase. Non-store sales declined 2.2%, vehicle and parts sales fell 1.8%, and electronics and appliance spending dropped 0.5%.

The weakness was not merely nominal. CPI increased only 0.1% in July, meaning the fall largely reflected lower physical volume. Economists now see consumer-spending growth potentially slowing below 2% annualized in Q3, after 3.2% in Q2; markets raised the probability of a September Fed hold to roughly 69%. Michigan sentiment fell from 55.2 to 51.0, and gasoline remains just above $4 per gallon versus $2.98 before the Iran conflict began in February.
signal: AI investment can continue without a strong consumer for a quarter; an economy cannot ultimately validate ever-expanding capacity without final demand.

# China's Credit System Sent an Even More Divisive Signal
New yuan lending contracted by 340 billion yuan — about $50.4 billion — in July, the largest monthly contraction on record and dramatically weaker than the 45 billion yuan increase economists expected. June lending had been 1.61 trillion yuan. Household loans, including mortgages, shrank by 460.3 billion yuan. Corporate loans fell 130 billion yuan. Outstanding loan growth slowed to 5.1%, another record low, while Jan–July new loans fell to 10.38 trillion yuan from 12.87 trillion a year earlier.

This comes from an economy whose manufacturing remains formidable but whose domestic demand is structurally weak. In June, industrial output grew 5.3% year on year while retail sales rose only 1.0%; first-half fixed-asset investment fell 5.7% and property investment dropped 18%.
signal: The Great Split in China is becoming productive supply versus domestic purchasing power.

# Oil Is a Tax on Final Demand and a Subsidy to Scarcity
Brent traded around $87.9 per barrel this afternoon, up roughly 5% for the week, while WTI stood near $81.7. Washington said it could maintain its naval blockade of Iran indefinitely; two ADNOC vessels were attacked while transiting Hormuz on Thursday, and commercial traffic remains severely constrained.

Energy now sits on both sides of the split. Higher prices strengthen producer cash flows and scarcity assets, but reduce discretionary income, raise logistics costs, increase data-center operating expense, pressure long-duration yields, and make the Fed less able to respond to weaker demand. That is why weaker retail figures did not produce an unambiguous bond rally.
signal: Industrial AI is deflationary where it replaces labor; geopolitical scarcity is inflationary where the economy cannot replace energy. Both forces operate simultaneously.

# The Market Is No Longer Rewarding AI Capex Indiscriminately
Applied Materials reported record quarterly revenue of $9.12 billion, up 25% year on year, with adjusted EPS rising 41% and non-GAAP gross margin reaching 50.4%. Yet the stock fell more than 5% to roughly $507. Meanwhile AMD gained approximately 4.1% to $503, valuing it near $834 billion; Micron advanced roughly 1.5% above $1.1 trillion; NVIDIA held almost flat around $225 at a $5.5 trillion market cap.

The market is becoming less willing to pay for good numbers when extraordinary numbers are already embedded in the valuation. It increasingly distinguishes who owns the architecture, who owns the bottleneck, who converts capex into cash fastest, and who is simply supplying a cycle whose future demand has already been capitalized.
signal: Scarcity remains valuable. Participation is no longer sufficient. Return on incremental capital is replacing thematic exposure as the prestige metric.

# Europe Showed the Same Split: GDP Is Growing Faster Than Employment
Euro-area GDP grew 0.4% quarter on quarter in Q2 while employment increased only 0.1%; the EU economy grew 0.5%, again with employment up just 0.1%. European markets nevertheless remain close to records — the STOXX Europe 600 closed Friday at 657.86, only 0.2% lower and within 1% of its all-time high. Aggregate Q2 STOXX 600 earnings are now expected to rise 23.4% year on year, the strongest in almost four years.

Output is expanding faster than employment while corporate earnings expand faster still. For investors that is attractive; for policymakers it is not enough. A productivity-driven economy eventually requires a transmission mechanism — higher real wages, lower prices, greater household wealth, new industries or new employment.
signal: Without transmission, productivity and consumption begin moving on different tracks.

::section The Hidden Pattern | The Great Split has reached final demand
Until now the Great Split could be observed primarily in financial and production variables: equities versus bonds, profits versus wages, productivity versus employment, capital formation versus the job market, AI leaders versus the rest. Today the next stage became visible — Capital Formation versus Final Demand.

The United States is heading toward more than $700 billion of Big Tech AI spending this year while July retail sales contracted. China is supporting an enormous industrial and technological capacity system while household credit contracted by 460 billion yuan in one month. Europe is generating 23.4% earnings growth while employment expanded only 0.1%. These are not contradictory statistics — they are increasingly the same economic architecture.

An investment Supercycle can run ahead of household demand. The railways did. Electrification did. The internet did. The critical question is what happens next: the investment must ultimately produce higher income, lower prices, new demand or new industries. If it does, technological productivity becomes an economic productivity dividend. If it does not, excess productive capacity eventually meets insufficient purchasing power. A capital cycle becomes an economic cycle only when productivity reconnects with demand.

::section Capital Is Not Leaving Risk — It Is Diversifying the AI Trade | Part II
The most revealing fund-flow data of the week showed $18.62 billion entering global equity funds, the twelfth consecutive week of inflows — but the distribution changed substantially. Europe attracted $13.52 billion, Asia $4.13 billion, and the United States only $2.58 billion. Technology funds suffered $1.7 billion of outflows, ending a six-week buying streak. Global bond funds attracted $18.01 billion, money-market funds $28.41 billion, gold and precious metals $2.62 billion, and energy funds recorded their first inflow in three weeks.

This is not a retreat from the Supercycle. It is the institutionalization of the propagation trade — capital moving outward from the narrow technology core. Roughly 85% of S&P 500 companies that have reported have beaten earnings estimates; excluding mark-to-market gains at Alphabet and Amazon, underlying profits are up approximately 32.7% year on year. Big Tech AI spending is expected to exceed $700 billion in 2026, versus roughly $400 billion in 2025.

::pull VELOCITY
The two systems still intersect. But they are no longer moving at the same Velocity.

::patterns The Emerging Capital-Allocation Hierarchy | Where capital is flowing
# [I] Productive Scarcity
Accelerated computing, memory, power, networking and assets whose demand is already structurally constrained.
# [II] Contracted Infrastructure
Assets with customers, power, permission and long-duration utilization.
# [III] Propagation Infrastructure
Logistics, rail, ports, grids, defence and connectivity — Maersk rose 8.7% today — benefiting as AI reaches the physical economy.
# [IV] Embedded Operating Systems
Software and data platforms controlling indispensable enterprise workflows; Workday surged nearly 18% above $51 billion on Silver Lake talks.
# [V] Financial Resilience
Investment-grade balance sheets, bonds, cash and long-duration capital able to finance the cycle through volatility.
# [VI] Final-Demand Exposure Without Pricing Power
Businesses dependent on discretionary consumers while energy, financing and wage pressure constrain household purchasing power.

::section Monday Will Test the Demand Side of the Supercycle | Part III — five catalysts for August 17
China publishes industrial production, retail sales, fixed-asset investment and real-estate data at 10:00 AM Beijing time. The world already knows China can produce; it increasingly needs evidence that China can consume more of what it produces. Japan releases Q2 GDP — economists expect roughly 2.0% annualized, a third consecutive expansion — as the Bank of Japan weighs another rate increase as soon as September 17–18 from the current 1%, with the yen near 159 per dollar.

Hormuz will set the opening price of global inflation: Brent below $85 signals de-escalation; $85–90 a manageable tax; above $90 rebuilds inflation risk; above $95 dominates the Fed, ECB and BOJ debate again. The long bond will test whether weak demand can lower the hurdle rate — the thirty-year sale cleared at its highest yield in roughly 25 years, and the ten-year traded near 4.69%. Below 4.55% brings meaningful relief; 4.55%–4.70% sustains the high-hurdle regime; above 4.85% makes duration the dominant equity variable. Finally, Walmart, Home Depot, Target and Lowe's report — telling us where consumers cut, where they traded down, and whether August is deteriorating further.

FDK scenarios: **Base Case (50%)** — Hormuz constrained but no major escalation, Brent $86–90, Chinese data showing industrial resilience but weak retail, Japanese GDP near 2%, the ten-year between 4.60% and 4.75%, and selective allocation favoring AI architecture, memory, energy, defence and strong balance sheets. **Upside Case (20%)** — credible Hormuz progress, Brent below $85, materially improving Chinese demand, the ten-year below 4.60%, and the Great Split beginning to narrow. **Downside Case (30%)** — escalation pushing Brent above $90–92, confirmed weak Chinese consumption, faster BOJ tightening, the ten-year above 4.75%, and the hardest configuration: weak final demand plus strong capex plus expensive energy plus expensive duration.

::takeaways Key takeaways | What today's numbers define
- The most consequential figures today were not the S&P 500 or NVIDIA's $5.5 trillion valuation — they were U.S. retail sales −0.6%, China new lending −340 billion yuan, and euro-area employment +0.1%.
- The next AI prestige metric is final-demand conversion: a backlog is most valuable when the customer behind it is monetizing capacity rather than simply building more.
- China's next breakthrough must occur inside the household balance sheet, not only inside the factory.
- Europe has an unusual capital-formation window — stronger markets should become an opportunity to finance grids, power, industrial AI, logistics, defence and intelligent mobility.
- Energy could determine whether productivity ever reaches the consumer; Hormuz is not peripheral to the AI story.

::close The world is becoming extraordinarily good at creating productive capital. It must now become equally good at converting that productivity back into economic demand — because ==capital formation cannot outrun final demand forever==.

FROM BANKABLE MEGAWATTS TO BANKABLE INTELLIGENCE ECOSYSTEMS

Today brings a meaningful shift in the European race. There is no new EuroHPC winner or site award, but Spain and Germany are moving the architecture underneath the competition: Spain is materially expanding the grid and introducing a capacity market; Germany is integrating private capital, sovereign cloud, models and large-scale accelerated computing. The Commission itself now explicitly describes Gigafactories as catalysts for a European semiconductor ecosystem, not merely data centers. 

1 | SPAIN — €17 BILLION CHANGES THE POWER EQUATION

Spain has raised planned electricity-transmission investment through 2030 to more than €17 billion—30% above the earlier proposal and 144% above the current planning cycle. The revised plan includes 193 substations, 6,706 km of planned lines, and 300 transmission-grid positions serving new distribution demand. Demand accounted for 41% of submissions during consultation. Data centers are among the large new loads driving the expansion. 

Simultaneously, Spain is establishing a capacity market open to generation, storage and demand. Crucially, consumers will face stronger charges during the most stressed hours, creating incentives to shift consumption. 

This introduces an important second-order effect:

AI LOAD → WORKLOAD ORCHESTRATION → DEMAND FLEXIBILITY → LOWER GRID STRESS → BETTER SYSTEM ECONOMICS → MORE BANKABLE AI CAPACITY

The strategic question therefore changes from “Can Spain supply enough electricity to AI?” to “Can AI become an active participant in Spain’s electricity system?”

That could ultimately turn today’s regulatory conflict into an advantage. Training, batch inference, batteries and other flexible loads can increasingly respond to electricity availability.

But the contradiction remains unresolved. Spain’s CNMC has entered the dispute over the proposed data-center decree, while the industry continues challenging the 80% renewable-matching and hourly-certification provisions. 

Spain now has potentially excellent physical infrastructure policy and unresolved regulatory bankability at the same time.

2 | GERMANY — THE AI INDUSTRIAL STACK IS TAKING SHAPE

Today’s other major signal is Germany.

Cohere and Aleph Alpha have signed their definitive combination agreement. The combined company will operate as Cohere with headquarters in Berlin and Toronto, while Heidelberg remains a research center. Schwarz Group is supporting the transaction with €500 million and will provide computing capacity through STACKIT. 

Behind this sits the larger strategic asset: Schwarz is investing roughly €11–13 billion in a German data center capable of hosting approximately 100,000 AI chips. 

Put the pieces together:

SCHWARZ CAPITAL
→ LARGE AI DATA CENTER
→ ~100,000 AI CHIPS
→ STACKIT SOVEREIGN CLOUD
→ COHERE/ALEPH ALPHA
→ ENTERPRISE AI
→ LIDL/KAUFLAND + EXTERNAL INDUSTRIAL CUSTOMERS

This is considerably more interesting than another data-center announcement.

Germany is assembling a vertically integrated AI industrial stack.

And vertical integration changes infrastructure economics because internal and affiliated customers can reduce offtake risk:

CAPTIVE DEMAND → UTILIZATION VISIBILITY → REVENUE VISIBILITY → LOWER OFFTAKE RISK → GREATER BANKABILITY → MORE CAPACITY

That could become one of Germany’s most powerful advantages.

3 | ITALY — PHYSICAL AI CAPITAL IS BEGINNING TO FORM

Italy’s important adjacent signal is the Exein financing announced yesterday.

The Rome cybersecurity company raised approximately €234 million, reaching a roughly €1.45 billion valuation. The EIB Group participated through the European Tech Champions Initiative’s first direct company co-investment. Exein focuses on security for connected devices and Physical AI environments. 

This matters because Gigafactories require downstream demand.

Italy possesses an unusually large potential market in:manufacturing → robotics → mobility → machinery → aerospace → energy → infrastructure.

The strategic chain should therefore become:

IT4LIA / GIGAFACTORY CAPACITY
→ INFERENCE
→ PHYSICAL AI
→ INDUSTRIAL DEPLOYMENT
→ PRODUCTIVITY
→ CASH FLOW
→ MORE AI DEMAND

Italy’s opportunity is not simply to host accelerated computing.

It is to connect that capacity to its industrial system.

4 | THE EUROPEAN GEOGRAPHY IS ALREADY MOVING

Fresh EMEA market data reinforce the thesis. Operational data-center capacity has surpassed 12.1 GW, up more than 36% in two years, while the committed pipeline has reached 18.3 GW. Helsinki has now joined London, Frankfurt, Dublin, Paris, Amsterdam and Milan as a major EMEA data-center powerhouse. Future capacity is increasingly migrating toward Southern Europe and Scandinavia, where power and land can be delivered faster. 

This gives us a remarkably clean causal chain:

POWER AVAILABILITY
→ TIME-TO-POWER
→ DATA-CENTER CAPEX
→ SUPPLIER ECOSYSTEM
→ AI CUSTOMER DENSITY
→ INDUSTRIAL AI ADOPTION
→ MORE CAPITAL

The geography of AI follows the geography of convertible electricity.

⸻

5 | THE MACRO QUESTION — EUROPE MAY NEED AI TO BUILD ITS CAPITAL MARKETS

Christine Lagarde’s September 14 intervention adds the financial dimension.

Her argument is essentially that Europe’s Gigafactories are only a beginning; Europe must build computing infrastructure at a much greater pace and simultaneously develop European models and access to frontier capabilities. 

The Commission now explicitly describes Gigafactories as a mechanism for creating predictable demand for European-designed AI processors and eventually indigenous manufacturing. 

This creates a much larger chain:

EU PUBLIC CAPITAL
→ PRIVATE INFRASTRUCTURE CAPITAL
→ AI GIGAFACTORIES
→ DEMAND FOR EUROPEAN SILICON
→ SEMICONDUCTOR SCALE-UP
→ INDUSTRIAL AI
→ PRODUCTIVITY
→ CASH FLOW
→ CAPITAL-MARKET DEEPENING

Gigafactories are therefore becoming much more than digital infrastructure.

They can become a capital-formation mechanism for the Next Economy.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN

Our framework has progressed rapidly:

ANNOUNCED MW told us almost nothing.

BANKABLE MW told us what might actually get built.

BANKABLE INTELLIGENCE added silicon, utilization and customers.

Today the relevant unit becomes:

BANKABLE INTELLIGENCE ECOSYSTEMS

The full architecture is:

CAPITAL × POWER × GRID × ACCELERATED COMPUTING × CLOUD × MODELS × DISTRIBUTION × INDUSTRIAL DEMAND × SPEED

This produces the variable that ultimately matters:

EUROPEAN AI VALUE CAPTURE

Spain is strengthening power and grid infrastructure.

Germany is strengthening vertical integration.

Italy is showing emerging strength in Physical AI applications and scale-up capital.

France retains a powerful combination of nuclear power, sovereign AI and infrastructure capital.

The Nordics remain exceptionally strong in power availability and Capacity Conversion Velocity.

THE VELOCITY INSIGHT

The European AI Gigafactory race is no longer fundamentally about who builds the largest data center.

It is becoming a competition over:

Who owns the economic system that converts electricity into intelligence—and intelligence into industrial cash flow?

A country could host 5 GW of AI infrastructure while importing the accelerators, cloud, models and applications and therefore capture surprisingly little of the resulting value.

Another could host 2 GW while controlling substantially more of the silicon, cloud, models, Industrial AI applications and financing.

The second may ultimately create more economic value.

That gives us the metric I would now put at the center of the Watch:

EUROPEAN AI VALUE-CAPTURE RATIO

European-owned capital + technology + IP + applications + industrial cash flow

divided by

total economic value generated by European AI infrastructure.

That moves us beyond measuring Gigafactories.

It begins measuring whether Europe is actually building the Next Economy.

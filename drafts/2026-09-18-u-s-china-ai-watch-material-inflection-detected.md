The race has entered the recursive phase: America is beginning to use AI to build the next AI, while China is turning diffusion into a physical-industrial feedback loop.
The most consequential development on September 18, 2026 is not another model release. It is evidence that the two competing AI systems are now beginning to compound themselves through different recursive mechanisms.
On the American side, Anthropic disclosed that Claude now “leads” 26% of the AI R&D work involved in developing Anthropic’s next-generation models, up from roughly 1% in March on the company’s measurement scale. AI collaborates with humans on more than 90% of Anthropic’s research, and around 30,000 AI agents were performing internal research and engineering work simultaneously during August. Anthropic stresses that Claude is not yet autonomously running any entire research function, but the trajectory is unmistakable. 
This is a major strengthening of the first half of our thesis.
Until now, America’s frontier advantage rested on superior chips, frontier laboratories, software ecosystems, capital and AI factories.
Now another multiplier is appearing:
FRONTIER INTELLIGENCE → AI-ASSISTED AI RESEARCH → FASTER FRONTIER PROGRESS → BETTER AI RESEARCH AGENTS → STILL FASTER FRONTIER PROGRESS
This is recursive technological velocity.
If sustained, the relevant measure of the U.S. advantage will no longer simply be how good GPT, Claude or Gemini is today. It becomes how quickly those systems accelerate the creation of their successors.
That is a fundamentally more powerful advantage.
⸻
But China is building a different recursive loop: intelligence → machines → data → better intelligence
Today’s Chinese signal is equally revealing.
Beijing-based Spirit AI says the software “brain” is now the principal constraint in humanoid robotics. Its Moz1 robots are already deployed on production lines at CATL and JD.com, while the company employs around 1,000 contractors nationwide collecting physical-world training data in homes and factories. Spirit AI has raised more than $670 million since 2024 and is valued around $2.9 billion. 
This matters far more than another humanoid demonstration.
The important asset is the data loop.
A language model improves from digital tokens.
A physical model improves from interaction with:
objects, factories, cables, tools, logistics environments, human movements and machines.
China’s industrial base therefore becomes something analogous to a gigantic training environment.
The emerging loop is:
CHEAPER AI → ROBOTS → FACTORY DEPLOYMENT → REAL-WORLD DATA → BETTER ROBOT BRAINS → HIGHER PRODUCTIVITY → MORE ROBOTS
That is the physical counterpart of America’s recursive research loop.
And it gives us a much sharper version of the Great Split.
America is using intelligence to accelerate the production of intelligence.
China is using intelligence to accelerate the production of physical-world data and industrial deployment.
Both are recursive.
But they compound through very different assets.
⸻
China is also attacking the memory layer
A second development today strengthens the infrastructure side of the Chinese strategy.
Reuters reports that CXMT is preparing to enter NAND flash memory, expanding beyond its traditional DRAM business. The initiative includes a new R&D production line in Beijing, and CXMT has discussed supplying NAND for storage products used in AI systems and supercomputers. AI-server demand has contributed to a global memory shortage expected to persist into 2027. 
CXMT raised roughly $8.6 billion in its July IPO, while rival YMTC’s parent is preparing a Shanghai listing targeting about 33 billion yuan. Both companies have received substantial national and local government backing. 
The caveat matters: this is NAND, not the frontier HBM where Chinese capabilities remain substantially weaker than SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron.
But the strategic direction is clear.
China’s semiconductor response is broadening from:
AI accelerator sovereignty
toward:COMPUTE + MEMORY + STORAGE + NETWORKING + MODELS
That is exactly what would be required to make the Chinese AI system increasingly self-sustaining.
The architecture we identified yesterday around Huawei now extends one layer deeper.

The most extraordinary diffusion signal came from Washington itself
One development is symbolically powerful enough to deserve attention.
The U.S. Federal Register website briefly offered a search capability using Alibaba’s Qwen model to browse public regulatory comments. The feature was subsequently removed after it attracted attention, and experts cited by Reuters said its use on public information did not necessarily create an immediate national-security risk. 
This should not be interpreted as a strategic U.S. government endorsement of Chinese AI. It clearly was not.
But economically it is revealing.
Qwen is open-weight and can be downloaded and operated locally, often more cheaply than closed frontier systems. That means Chinese models have become sufficiently capable and accessible that even inside the institutional heart of the United States, developers can find them economically attractive enough to deploy. 
That is precisely the diffusion thesis.
Chinese AI does not necessarily have to beat Claude or GPT on the absolute frontier.
It can penetrate workloads because it is:
**good enough
open
adaptable
inexpensive.**
The episode lasted briefly.
The economic signal is much larger.

And the price signal confirms that the frontier remains scarce
There is a striking contrast on the American infrastructure side.
Nebius announced its second AI-cloud price increase in three months. From October, selected Nvidia GPU rental prices will rise approximately 17–21%, some CPU instances by 25%, and some memory offerings by roughly 41%. The company says demand for compute remains extremely strong, while it has recently signed four contracts averaging more than $1 billion each. 
At the same time, AI-infrastructure provider Crusoe raised $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion valuation and says it now has more than $140 billion in contracted value and over 6 GW of contracted capacity, with 1 GW operational. 
This gives us the Supercycle paradox in unusually clean form:
FRONTIER COMPUTE PRICES ↑
while
USEFUL INTELLIGENCE COSTS ↓
The AI economy can sustain both because the quantity of intelligence demanded is exploding.

Why this matters for the AI Supercycle
Recursive AI development changes the slope of the cycle.
If AI itself increasingly performs AI research, then improvements in capability can shorten the time required to produce the next generation.
Meanwhile, if physical deployment generates continuously improving training data, industrial AI can follow its own compounding curve.
Put the two together:
AI improves AI
→ capability rises faster
→ inference becomes more valuable
→ deployment expands→ data increases
→ models improve
→ infrastructure demand rises
→ more AI improves AI.
That is no longer a conventional semiconductor cycle.
It is a self-reinforcing intelligence-production cycle.
And the infrastructure consequences are enormous:
accelerators, HBM, NAND, networking, optical interconnects, data centers, electricity, storage, robotics, sensors and industrial machinery.
⸻
The Velocity Insight
The September 18 inflection can be reduced to one idea:
THE COMPETITION IS BECOMING RECURSIVE.
The United States is beginning to use its frontier advantage to increase the velocity at which the frontier itself advances.
China is beginning to use its diffusion and manufacturing advantages to increase the velocity at which intelligence acquires real-world data and physical applications.
Neither side is merely scaling yesterday’s architecture.
Both are creating feedback loops.
And this sharpens our thesis dramatically:
America still leads the creation of intelligence.
China increasingly leads the compression, diffusion and physicalization of intelligence.
The critical competition is therefore migrating toward:
WHO BUILDS THE FASTER SELF-REINFORCING LOOP?
If Anthropic’s AI-assisted R&D loop compounds faster, the American frontier lead could widen even while models become cheaper.
If China’s model-efficiency + industrial-data + physical-deployment loop compounds faster, capability gaps can matter progressively less for large parts of the economy.
That is why today qualifies as a material inflection for The Velocity Edge.
The AI Supercycle is no longer being driven only by more chips or larger models.
It is increasingly being driven by intelligence accelerating the production and deployment of more intelligence.

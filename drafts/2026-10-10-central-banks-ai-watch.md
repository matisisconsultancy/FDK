WHEN MONEY MOVES AT MACHINE SPEED

The Next Monetary Risk Is Not Inflation. It Is Velocity.

In Focus | 10 October 2026

THE BIG TAKE — AGENTIC AI ENTERS THE MONETARY SYSTEM

A consequential development emerged from Singapore on October 9.

The Monetary Authority of Singapore has identified a new systemic consequence of agentic AI: autonomous financial agents could accelerate the movement of money and fundamentally change liquidity management.

Speaking at the INSEAD Digital Finance & Agentic AI Summit, MAS Managing Director Chia Der Jiun warned that AI agents acting for individuals and institutions could move funds more rapidly in response to even small differences in financial incentives.

His remarks explicitly connected this development to liquidity, collateral and risk management.

This represents a significant extension of the central-bank AI debate. 

Until now, the AI Supercycle has primarily entered monetary-policy discussions through capital expenditure, inflation, productivity and financing costs.

Singapore is highlighting another transmission channel:

AI AUTONOMY → FASTER CAPITAL REALLOCATION → GREATER LIQUIDITY SENSITIVITY → NEW FINANCIAL-STABILITY RISKS.

The Great Split is expanding from the production economy into the monetary system itself.

1. THE NEW VARIABLE: THE VELOCITY OF FINANCIAL DECISIONS

Traditional financial intermediation relies partly on behavioural inertia.

Customers do not continuously optimise deposits, investment portfolios or liquidity positions.

Autonomous financial agents could progressively reduce that inertia.

An agent authorised to manage corporate liquidity might continuously compare deposit remuneration, collateral requirements and alternative investment opportunities.

A small change in relative returns could trigger transactions much faster than conventional treasury operations.

The second-order implications are considerable.

Deposit balances could become more sensitive to rate differentials. Liquidity could migrate more rapidly between institutions. Collateral demands could respond more quickly to changes in market conditions.

These are potential transmission mechanisms, not yet demonstrated economy-wide effects.

Their significance lies in the possibility that AI could reduce the time available for banks and supervisors to respond to liquidity shocks.

2. SINGAPORE MOVES FROM PRINCIPLES TO EXECUTION CONTROLS

MAS is developing safeguards specifically addressing autonomous financial activity.

Its Safeguards for Agentic Finance at Runtime framework requires attention to three operational principles: agent identity and authorisation, verification of proposed actions against the user’s mandate, and accountability with auditability.

MAS has recently published version 1.1 of this framework and released an open-source reference implementation to support experimentation and development.

The regulatory distinction is important.

Supervision is moving beyond whether an AI model is reliable toward whether an autonomous financial action is authorised, controlled and traceable at execution. 

This complements Singapore’s October 7 guidelines establishing broader supervisory expectations for AI governance across financial institutions.

Those guidelines begin taking effect in October 2027, with additional provisions following in October 2028. 

3. THE CENTRAL-BANK MONEY CONNECTION

MAS also outlined the continuing importance of central-bank money as the anchor of an increasingly digital financial system.

Singapore is preparing to settle tokenised MAS Bills using wholesale central-bank digital currency.

The objective is to bring tokenised securities and central-bank settlement assets onto compatible infrastructure while preserving settlement finality.

MAS emphasised that regulated stablecoins, tokenised commercial-bank deposits and central-bank digital currency may serve different purposes.

But the stability of private digital money ultimately depends on credible monetary and regulatory foundations.The emerging architecture therefore combines:

AUTONOMOUS FINANCIAL AGENTS + PROGRAMMABLE MONEY + INTEROPERABLE SETTLEMENT INFRASTRUCTURE.

This could improve capital efficiency substantially.

It could also accelerate the transmission of financial disturbances if safeguards fail.

4. THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECT: AI COULD CHANGE MONETARY TRANSMISSION

The financial implications extend well beyond Singapore.

Central banks traditionally influence economic activity by changing borrowing costs, liquidity conditions and expectations.

Agentic finance could potentially alter the speed at which these signals propagate.

Changes in interest-rate differentials might trigger faster deposit reallocations.

Market stress could generate more synchronised portfolio adjustments.

Collateral optimisation could increase efficiency under normal conditions while intensifying liquidity demands during periods of volatility.

The central distinction is between greater financial efficiency and greater financial resilience.

They are not necessarily the same thing.

This adds a new dimension to the Great Split:

AI can increase the productivity of financial intermediation while simultaneously increasing the speed of financial contagion.

5. THE EUROPEAN CONSEQUENCE

For the ECB, this development reinforces the need to integrate monetary innovation with financial-stability supervision.

Europe is already confronting AI-related financial concentration, cyber risk and dependence on external technology providers.

Agentic finance introduces a further question: whether existing liquidity supervision, collateral frameworks and settlement infrastructure are sufficiently prepared for autonomous transaction activity.

European policymakers should therefore connect three agendas more closely: AI governance in banking, the development of trusted digital settlement infrastructure, and liquidity-risk supervision.

The Digital Euro and wholesale settlement initiatives should be assessed not merely as payment innovations, but as components of an increasingly automated financial architecture.

FIVE KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. A new transmission channel: MAS has explicitly identified autonomous capital movement as an emerging concern for liquidity and collateral management.
2. A new supervisory perimeter: Runtime authorisation and accountability are becoming essential controls for agentic financial transactions.
3. A new financial-stability risk: Faster allocation decisions may improve efficiency while potentially compressing the response time available during market stress.
4. A new monetary infrastructure: Tokenised deposits, central-bank digital currency and interoperable settlement networks are becoming increasingly relevant to the development of agentic finance.
5. A new European priority: The ECB and European policymakers need to anticipate the interaction between autonomous financial agents, liquidity management and trusted settlement systems.

THE GREAT SPLIT NOW HAS A FOURTH DIMENSION

The AI Supercycle is changing four fundamental economic variables:

THE PRODUCTIVITY OF LABOUR.

THE DEMAND FOR CAPITAL.

THE COST OF ENERGY.

THE VELOCITY OF MONEY.

The first three are already influencing central-bank assessments of inflation, growth and monetary conditions.

The fourth is beginning to enter the supervisory agenda.

No comparable new AI-driven policy-rate decision or formal neutral-rate reassessment has been established in the latest developments. The consequential change is in the understanding of future financial transmission.

The next monetary regime will be shaped not only by how much money central banks create or how much interest they charge, but increasingly by how quickly autonomous intelligence can move capital across the financial system.

The ultimate test of financial stability may become whether institutional safeguards can operate as quickly as the intelligent systems they are designed to govern.

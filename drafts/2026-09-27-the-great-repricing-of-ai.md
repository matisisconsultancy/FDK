NEW RULES, NEW INVESTORS’ PLAYBOOK
The AI race is no longer about who leads technologically. It is about who captures the economics—and what investors are paying for that capture.
With the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield at 5.158% at the September 25 close, the market is imposing a harder discipline.
Velocity identifies the leaders. Economic capture explains the profits. Valuation determines the return.


⸻


THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
Who captures the economics of AI after compute, power, security, infrastructure and capital costs are paid—and how much of that future is already priced in?
Capability without monetization is optionality.
Backlog without utilization is promise.
Capex without excess ROIC is capital accumulation.
And exceptional economics can still disappoint when valuation already discounts too much success.

THE THREE-LAYER FRAMEWORK
1. VELOCITY — WHO IS ACCELERATING?
GVI measures structural Corporate Velocity across:
innovation · capital formation · productivity · adoption · infrastructure · ecosystem · resilience · talent · optionality · execution
High GVI means high strategic Velocity—not automatically high expected equity return.
2. ECONOMIC CAPTURE — WHO KEEPS THE PROFITS?
The critical equation is:
Pricing Power × Utilization × Cash Conversion × ROIC
This explains why TSMC, Schneider Electric and Palo Alto belong alongside model and cloud leaders.
The AI profit pool is migrating toward companies controlling scarce, monetizable bottlenecks.
3. VALUATION — HOW MUCH IS ALREADY PRICED?
Expected shareholder returns depend on:
Corporate Velocity + Economic Capture − Expectations Embedded in Price
A great company can remain a poor security at the wrong price.


⸻


TWO VALUATION RULES THAT MATTER
RULE 1 — HIGH GVI + HIGH EXPECTATIONS = VALUATION SENSITIVITY
Where valuation already discounts sustained growth, margin expansion and near-perfect execution, the burden of proof rises.
A modest operating miss can create a disproportionate equity reaction while structural Velocity remains high.
Excellent businesses can still be priced for perfection.
This is the critical reading for Nvidia and Palantir.
RULE 2 — HIGH GVI + STRONG CASH CONVERSION = QUALITY
The strongest combination is high structural Velocity plus:
pricing power · utilization · free-cash-flow conversion · ROIC above WACC
Velocity becomes investable quality when it converts into cash without requiring proportionally more capital.
This is the key reading for Microsoft, TSMC and Schneider Electric.


⸻


THREE SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS
1. AI CAPEX CREATES A SCARCITY PREMIUM
Every additional AI system requires:
packaging · memory · networking · power · cooling
The scarcer the enabling layer, the stronger its pricing power.
AI capex is repricing the infrastructure surrounding the processor.
2. CAPITAL COST BECOMES A COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE
With the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield at 5.158%, internal cash generation and balance-sheet strength matter more.
The strongest platforms can keep scaling while weaker balance sheets face higher hurdle rates.
The AI race is becoming partly a financing race.
3. AGENTIC AI CREATES A SECURITY SUPERCYCLE
More agents create more machine identities, permissions and transactions.More autonomy → greater security intensity → more recurring cybersecurity revenue.
That structurally strengthens Palo Alto, Microsoft and Palantir.


⸻


THE BACKLOG REPRICING
Not all backlog is equal.
Announced → Contracted → Financed → Powered → Utilized → Cash-Generating
Each transition removes risk and increases economic quality.
With the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield at 5.158%, delay between contract and cash carries an explicit duration cost.
Powered, prepaid, high-utilization demand deserves a premium. Unfunded or unpowered backlog does not.
Backlog is becoming a quality-of-cash-flow question, not merely a growth statistic.

THE NEW INVESTORS’ PLAYBOOK
1. CONTROL THE RISK
Ask whether governance, resilience and execution protect capital.
Positive signal: Microsoft · Palantir · Palo Alto Networks
2. CONVERT THE CASH
Ask whether adoption, scarcity and capacity translate into utilization and free cash flow.
Positive signal: TSMC · Schneider Electric · Microsoft
3. EARN EXCESS ROIC
Demand incremental returns sustainably above company-specific WACC.
Growth without excess ROIC is not value creation.
4. PRICE THE EXPECTATIONS
Only then ask how much future success the security already discounts.
Investment Verdict
Highest Conviction: strong control + strong cash conversion + excess ROIC + valuation with upside.
Positive / Valuation-Sensitive: exceptional economics, demanding expectations.
Watch: high Velocity, but cash conversion or ROIC not yet proven.
Discount: capital intensity, backlog or autonomy rising faster than cash, control or returns.
Investor rule: protect capital first, demand cash second, require excess returns third, then decide what price is worth paying.
RISK → CASH → ROIC → VALUATION

Are you paying the bottleneck—or becoming the bottleneck others must pay?

OVERALL METHODOLOGY
The FDK Global Velocity Index — Corporate Benchmark measures how rapidly globally significant corporations convert technological, financial and organizational capabilities into future economic value.
Innovation 14% · Capital Formation 12% · Productivity 10% · AI Adoption 10% · Infrastructure 10% · Ecosystem 10% · Resilience 9% · Talent 8% · Optionality 9% · Execution 8%.
~70% measured evidence — revenue, margins, free cash flow, capex, backlog, capacity, utilization and execution milestones.
~30% FDK proprietary data analytics — capital productivity, cash-conversion velocity, ecosystem control, bottleneck scarcity, resilience, optionality, concentration risk and probability of conversion into cash-generating assets.
Valuation remains outside the GVI score.
The 5.158% U.S. 10-year Treasury yield is used consistently as the market-rate reference in this edition. It is not a substitute for company-specific WACC.
GVI = Structural Velocity
Economic Capture = Quality of Monetization
Valuation = Price Paid for Future Velocity

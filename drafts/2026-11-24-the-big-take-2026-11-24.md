1 | THE RISE OF COMPASS COMPANIES AND COMPASS CEOs
Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Huang on November 23 following consultations that included senior US government officials. Its independent investigation remains ongoing. 
NVIDIA Newsroom

What changed: Leadership uncertainty declined dramatically. The underlying technological capabilities and reported earnings did not change overnight.
The November 23 market repricing
The NER-C interpretation is that Compass Companies and Compass CEOs perform an economic function extending beyond their corporate boundaries: they coordinate expectations across investors, suppliers, customers and governments.
Strategic credibility → Lower perceived uncertainty → Valuation recovery → Improved financing conditions → Potential investment acceleration.
The second-order risk is systemic dependence on individual leaders. Monday's rally restored confidence but did not resolve concerns about infrastructure leverage, governance or capital productivity.

3 | EUROPE'S CAPITAL-MARKET COUNTEROFFENSIVE
On November 23, SIX launched its European Growth Program, connecting three exchanges:
SIX Swiss Exchange — Switzerland.
BME — Spain.
Aquis Stock Exchange — United Kingdom.
The initiative includes a new growth index comprising 31 companies and coordinated international investor engagement. 
SIX+1

The strategic opportunity: Europe could begin addressing one of its greatest structural disadvantages in the AI race—fragmented capital formation.
Greater investor visibility could support liquidity, improve financing conditions and help European technology companies scale without relocating their financial centre of gravity.
No new capital raised or reduction in financing costs has yet been demonstrated.
4 | ENERGY AND THE NEW ECONOMIC GEOGRAPHY
Armenia has announced plans to connect its electricity network with Turkey and Azerbaijan as expanding AI infrastructure increases prospective power demand.
Firebird is targeting 300 MW of infrastructure by the end of 2027. Neither the proposed interconnections nor the full computing expansion should be treated as operational.

The second-order implication is particularly consequential: AI infrastructure could become a catalyst for new cross-border economic relationships and regional energy integration.
For Europe, electricity interconnectivity is increasingly an instrument of technological competitiveness.

THE NEW RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS
THE SYSTEMIC COMPASS PREMIUM
Can the strategic credibility of a Compass CEO generate a measurable valuation premium across an entire industrial ecosystem?
NER-C should develop an empirical framework distinguishing corporate earnings revisions from leadership-driven changes in risk premia, supplier valuations, credit spreads and investment expectations.
The hypothesis should be tested through comparative event studies, controlling for interest rates, market momentum and company fundamentals.
November 23 provides a compelling observation—not yet proof of a persistent systemic premium.
THE FINAL VELOCITY INSIGHT
FROM TECHNOLOGICAL LEADERSHIP TO FINANCIAL SUPREMACY
The AI Supercycle is revealing three increasingly consequential dynamics.
For investors: The next capital rotation may favour companies combining strategic leadership, financial resilience and superior capital productivity.
For boards: Leadership credibility is becoming a financial asset, but excessive dependence on individuals represents a systemic vulnerability.
For Europe: The SIX initiative could become the foundation of a broader continental capital-market architecture supporting AI-native industrial champions.
The next phase of global competition will extend beyond technological superiority. It will concern the capacity to coordinate intelligence, capital, infrastructure and industrial deployment at unprecedented speed and scale.
The nations that lead the Next Economy will be those capable of translating technological leadership into financial supremacy and financial supremacy into productive power.

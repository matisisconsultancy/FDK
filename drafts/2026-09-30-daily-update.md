THE PRICE OF VELOCITY

THE BIG TAKE

GVI: 108.0 | China: 91.2 ↑0.1 | United States: 94.3 ↓0.1 | No ranking changes | Confidence: 96%

The Evidence

Three numbers define today’s regime.

China’s official manufacturing PMI returned to expansion at 50.1, with production at 51.7 and new orders at 50.5. High-tech manufacturing profits had already risen 54.7%, strengthening the evidence that China’s recovery is being led disproportionately by the industries supplying the Next Economy.

In the United States, the opposite pressure is emerging through finance. The 10-year Treasury reached 5.293% and the 30-year 5.621%, while job openings declined to 7.08 million and consumer confidence fell to 81.9.

Brent fell 2.5% to $102.59, providing some physical-cost relief—but not enough to offset the repricing of long-duration capital.

The Thesis

The AI Supercycle is not slowing. Its cost of capital is rising faster than its cost of intelligence is falling.

That changes everything.

The next phase will not be defined by AI versus non-AI.

It will be defined by who sits on the right side of three divides:

CASH GENERATORS vs CAPITAL CONSUMERS
BOTTLENECK OWNERS vs CAPACITY BUYERS
PRODUCTIVITY LEADERS vs AI SPENDERS

The GVI remains 108.0 because the productive engine and the financial brake are strengthening simultaneously.

The Second-Order Effects

I — The bond market becomes the new AI gatekeeper

For most of the Supercycle, equity markets determined the price of AI ambition.

That is changing.

At 5.29% on the US 10-year, the bond market increasingly determines which projects survive.

This creates a recursive mechanism:

AI CAPEX ↑ → financing demand ↑ → yields/spreads ↑ → hurdle rates ↑ → marginal projects fail → capital concentrates in stronger balance sheets.

The first-order effect is higher financing costs.

The second-order effect is concentration.

The largest cash-generative platforms gain an advantage not simply because their technology is better, but because their WACC is structurally lower than that of challengers.

The AI Supercycle may therefore become more concentrated precisely as the technology itself becomes more widely available.

II — The market rotates from Compute to Scarcity

Compute created the first extraordinary rents.

But every incremental accelerator now creates derivative demand:

COMPUTE → MEMORY → OPTICS → NETWORKING → POWER → TRANSFORMERS → COOLING → GRID

Those layers cannot expand at software speed.

Europe’s new EIB/BNP Paribas guarantee programme—potentially catalysing €2.8 billion of grid-equipment investment—illustrates where capital is beginning to migrate.

The second-order implication is crucial:

The more abundant intelligence becomes, the more valuable its physical bottlenecks become.

That suggests a market rotation within AI, not away from it.

III — Higher rates accelerate the Great Split inside corporate AI

When money was cheap, experimentation carried little penalty.

At 5%+ long rates, unused compute, underutilised agents and speculative data-centre capacity become expensive balance-sheet assets.

The corporate hierarchy therefore changes:

AI ADOPTION is no longer enough.

The relevant sequence becomes:

AI DEPLOYMENT → UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → MARGIN → CASH FLOW

Companies that cannot complete that chain will discover that AI can increase revenue while simultaneously destroying economic value.

This is where ROIC becomes more important than AI revenue growth.

IV — China could trigger the next industrial rotation

China’s manufacturing return to expansion matters because it coincides with extraordinary strength in high-tech manufacturing.

If confirmed over the next several months, the transmission could become:

CHINA INDUSTRIAL ACCELERATION
→ COMPONENT DEMAND
→ COMMODITIES
→ INDUSTRIAL AUTOMATION
→ POWER EQUIPMENT
→ GLOBAL CAPEX CYCLE

That would broaden the AI trade away from US software and hyperscalers toward the global physical-industrial stack.It could also begin narrowing the extraordinary valuation dispersion between technology and industrial assets.

This is the signal to watch.

V — The Supercycle becomes self-selecting

This is perhaps the deepest second-order effect.

High rates do not necessarily kill the AI Supercycle.

They may improve its quality.

Weak projects lose financing.

Speculative capacity is cancelled.

Capital migrates toward proven demand.

Scarcity assets acquire pricing power.

Cash-generative leaders gain market share.

M&A accelerates as strong balance sheets acquire capabilities from weaker ones.

The result is:

HIGHER HURDLE RATE → CAPITAL DISCIPLINE → CONSOLIDATION → HIGHER PRODUCTIVITY OF CAPITAL

Paradoxically, tighter money could make the surviving AI ecosystem stronger.

The Evolutionary Market Rotation

The market is therefore likely to move through four phases:

PHASE I — AI NARRATIVE

Model capability. GPU scarcity. Multiple expansion.

Largely behind us.

PHASE II — AI CASH FLOW

Revenue conversion. Margins. Free cash flow. Balance-sheet strength.

Happening now.

PHASE III — AI BOTTLENECKS

Memory. Networking. Power. Transformers. Cooling. Grid infrastructure.

Beginning.

PHASE IV — AI PRODUCTIVITY

Industrials, banks, healthcare, transportation, logistics and consumer businesses that use AI to increase ROIC, margins and asset productivity.

The potentially largest phase still lies ahead.

That final rotation is the one that can transform the AI Supercycle from a technology boom into a whole-economy productivity regime.

What Changes for Capital Allocation

For investors, the relevant hierarchy is becoming:

CASH FLOW → SCARCITY → PRODUCTIVITY → OPTIONALITY

Cash flow protects against higher rates.

Scarcity creates pricing power.

Productivity expands margins.

Optionality creates the next growth curve.

For boards, the hierarchy is equally clear:

CAPACITY → UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW → ROIC > WACC

Anything that stops before cash flow remains an AI investment thesis.

Anything that reaches ROIC above WACC becomes competitive advantage.

Five Takeaways

1. The AI trade is not ending—it is rotating.
From models and compute toward cash flow, bottlenecks and productivity.

2. 5%+ rates accelerate concentration.
Cash-rich leaders gain structural advantage over externally financed challengers.

3. Physical scarcity becomes the next source of excess returns.
The more intelligence scales, the more valuable power, memory, networking, cooling and grids become.

4. China may be the trigger for the next industrial leg.
If its manufacturing recovery persists, AI capital formation could broaden into a global industrial and infrastructure cycle.

5. ROIC becomes the ultimate AI benchmark.
The next winners will not be those deploying the most AI—but those producing the greatest incremental cash return per dollar of intelligence deployed.

Financial Verdict

GVI: 108.0. China accelerates. America absorbs the higher hurdle rate. The market begins to rotate.

The first phase rewarded intelligence.

The second rewarded scale.

The next will reward capital productivity.

Intelligence is getting cheaper. Capital is getting more expensive. The winners will own the spread.

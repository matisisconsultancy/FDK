The second-order effects are bigger than the Gigafactories

Today’s developments point to something more consequential than another AI-capacity announcement.

The EIB–BNP Paribas agreement will create a €700 million guarantee portfolio for European grid-equipment manufacturers, expected to enable as much as €2.8 billion of real-economy investment. Together with a €600 million EIB–Crédit Agricole CIB programme announced last week, Europe is beginning to use public risk absorption to expand private financing precisely where the AI build-out is encountering a physical constraint: the electricity grid. 

Meanwhile, Legrand has raised its 2027–2030 organic-growth ambition to 6–8%, expects more than €3 billion of data-center sales in 2026, and plans to deploy around 60% of free cash flow into acquisitions. ESA has separately selected OVHcloud and CGI to create sovereign European compute infrastructure for an Earth-observation estate expected to exceed 500 petabytes by 2035. 

Taken together, the signal is clear:

AI capital formation is beginning to escape the data center and reorganize the industries around it.

1 | THE FIRST SECOND-ORDER EFFECT: THE BOTTLENECK CREATES THE PROFIT POOL

The first AI winners were the companies producing scarce accelerators.

The next scarcity is increasingly physical:

AI CAPEX ↑
→ electricity demand ↑
→ grid congestion ↑
→ transformers / switchgear / power-management demand ↑
→ pricing power ↑
→ margins and cash flow ↑
→ industrial CAPEX + M&A ↑

Legrand is an early example of this propagation: data centers already account for roughly 32% of sales, and the company is now explicitly using the AI-driven infrastructure cycle to raise growth targets and accelerate acquisitions. 

The investment implication is important:

The AI trade is migrating from who makes the GPU toward who removes the next bottleneck.

That expands the opportunity toward electrical equipment, grids, storage, cooling, photonics and fiber.

2 | THE SECOND EFFECT: PUBLIC CAPITAL CAN CREATE A CAPITAL MULTIPLIER

The EIB–BNP structure is more interesting than its €700 million headline.

The EIB absorbs part of the risk.

BNP Paribas can issue more guarantees.

Grid manufacturers can accept larger orders.

Manufacturing capacity expands.

Grid projects accelerate.

More sites become power-ready.

More private AI infrastructure becomes financeable.

The recursive chain is:

PUBLIC RISK ABSORPTION → BANK CAPACITY → INDUSTRIAL CAPEX → GRID CAPACITY → AI CAPACITY → PRIVATE CASH FLOW

The €700 million guarantee programme is expected to support up to €2.8 billion of investment—a 4× gross mobilization effect relative to the guarantee portfolio. 

This is exactly how Europe should finance the AI Supercycle: not by having the state replace private capital, but by making private capital move faster.

3 | THE THIRD EFFECT: THE GRID BOTTLENECK COULD TRIGGER INDUSTRIAL CONSOLIDATION

Scarcity creates strategic value.

As electrical infrastructure becomes critical to AI deployment, companies controlling scarce capabilities can command higher valuations and stronger cash flows.

Those cash flows then finance acquisitions.

That produces:

AI DEMAND
→ BOTTLENECK PROFITS
→ M&A
→ INDUSTRY CONSOLIDATION
→ SCALE
→ HIGHER BARRIERS TO ENTRY

Legrand’s intention to deploy roughly 60% of free cash flow into acquisitions is therefore not incidental. 

The AI Supercycle may trigger a consolidation wave across the physical infrastructure stack.

For investors, the next winners may include not only bottleneck owners but the consolidators of bottleneck industries.

4 | THE FOURTH EFFECT: SOVEREIGN PROCUREMENT CREATES DEMAND BEFORE THE MARKET DOES

ESA’s Digital EO contract with OVHcloud and CGI reveals the demand-side counterpart.

Europe is placing strategic workloads onto European-operated infrastructure across France, Germany and Italy.The platform will combine storage, compute and AI tools and serve an Earth-observation estate expected to exceed 500 PB by 2035. 

The causality is powerful:

PUBLIC WORKLOAD
→ ANCHOR DEMAND
→ UTILIZATION VISIBILITY
→ LOWER COMMERCIAL RISK
→ MORE FINANCEABLE COMPUTE
→ MORE PRIVATE CAPACITY

This is how procurement becomes industrial policy.

The state does not simply subsidize supply.

It creates the first customer.

For the Gigafactory programme, that may prove more powerful than another grant.

5 | THE FIFTH EFFECT: AI INFRASTRUCTURE COULD DEEPEN EUROPEAN CAPITAL MARKETS

Put the supply and demand sides together.

Europe is beginning to create:

EIB guarantees
→ bank financing
→ grid investment
→ power-ready infrastructure
→ sovereign compute procurement
→ predictable cash flows
→ institutional refinancing

Once those cash flows become sufficiently predictable, AI infrastructure can migrate toward:

bank debt → infrastructure funds → insurers → pension funds → bonds.

That matters enormously.

The Commission says the Gigafactory programme is explicitly designed to de-risk and crowd in private investment; the earlier expression-of-interest process produced 77 proposals across 60 sites in 16 Member States. 

AI may therefore become one of the forces that finally pushes Europe toward deeper capital markets.

6 | THE SIXTH EFFECT: EUROPE’S INDUSTRIAL GEOGRAPHY WILL FOLLOW POWER

Grid expansion does more than enable data centers.

It changes where economic activity can locate.

The longer-term chain is:

GRID CAPACITY
→ AI INFRASTRUCTURE
→ COMPUTE AVAILABILITY
→ INDUSTRIAL AI
→ CUSTOMER DENSITY
→ TALENT
→ SUPPLIERS
→ CAPITAL
→ MORE GRID INVESTMENT

That is path dependence.

Regions that solve time-to-power first can attract not only Gigafactories but the industrial ecosystems surrounding them.

The Gigafactory race is therefore quietly becoming a race over the future geography of European industrial power.

THE BIG TAKE

Our framework should evolve again.

We moved from:

Announced MW → Bankable MW → Financeable MW → Strategic MW → Compounding MW

But today the more important unit becomes:

COMPOUNDING INFRASTRUCTURE

Infrastructure where each investment makes the next investment more economic:

GRID → COMPUTE → DEMAND → CASH FLOW → CAPITAL → MORE GRID → MORE COMPUTE

That is the flywheel Europe needs.

And it changes the investor question from:

Who owns the AI?

to:

Who owns the bottleneck that the next trillion dollars of AI CAPEX cannot avoid?

INVESTORS — KEY TAKEAWAYS

• Move outward from GPUs toward grid equipment, power management, cooling, storage, photonics and fiber.
• Watch pricing power at bottlenecks, not simply revenue growth.
• Look for companies able to convert AI-driven cash flow into M&A and industry consolidation.
• Distinguish ordinary MW from power-ready, contracted and financeable MW.
• Sovereign procurement can materially lower demand risk for European cloud and compute assets.
• The emerging allocation thesis is increasingly AI infrastructure picks-and-shovels + bottleneck consolidators.

EU POLICYMAKERS — KEY TAKEAWAYS

• Use public money to multiply private capital, not replace it.
• Treat grid equipment manufacturing as part of AI industrial policy.
• Connect Gigafactory support directly to sovereign and industrial compute procurement.
• Measure time-to-power, not announced GW.
• Build cross-border grids capable of connecting Europe’s complementary energy and industrial geographies.
• The objective is no longer merely more compute. It is a self-reinforcing European capital-formation system.

The first phase of the AI Supercycle rewarded whoever owned the intelligence.

The next phase may reward whoever owns the infrastructure intelligence cannot scale without.

And that is why the grid is no longer adjacent to the AI trade.

The grid is becoming the AI trade.

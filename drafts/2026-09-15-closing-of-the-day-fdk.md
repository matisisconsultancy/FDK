August 13, 2026
THE REPRICING OF RELEVANCE
AI is no longer merely changing earnings expectations. It is beginning to change the price of capital itself.
⸻
PART I — THE DAY: THE COST OF MONEY FELL. THE VALUE OF RELEVANCE ROSE.
Markets received what they wanted today.
U.S. producer prices were unchanged in July, strengthening the argument that the inflation impulse is moderating. Treasury yields declined, the dollar weakened, technology rallied and the S&P 500 moved to another intraday record. By late European trading, the S&P 500 was approximately +0.7%, Nasdaq +0.9%, while the STOXX Europe 600 finished around +0.1%. (Reuters)
But the important signal was not the index move.
It was the change underneath it.
The probability assigned by markets to the Federal Reserve holding rates unchanged in September rose to roughly 65%, from about 50% before today’s producer-price release. (Reuters)
That created the conventional chain:
Lower inflation pressure ↓ Lower expected policy path ↓ Lower Treasury yields ↓ Higher duration valuations ↓ Higher equities
But another chain is becoming more consequential:
AI investment ↓ Infrastructure requirements ↓ Capital requirements ↓ Credit issuance ↓ Financing differentiation ↓ Cost-of-capital divergence ↓ Investment divergence ↓ Productivity divergence ↓ Cash-flow divergence ↓ Valuation divergence
That is today’s hidden pattern.
THE GREAT SPLIT IS MIGRATING INTO CREDIT
Equity markets continue to celebrate AI.
Credit markets are beginning to ask who must finance it.
That distinction matters enormously.
Barclays analysis highlighted today an unusual divergence: demand for downside protection has been falling in equities while increasing in investment-grade credit instruments. Equity investors are becoming more comfortable with risk precisely as credit investors become more sensitive to it. (MarketWatch)
This is not necessarily a contradiction.
It may be the beginning of the next stage of the AI trade.
The first stage rewarded companies producing intelligence.
The second rewarded companies building the infrastructure required to deliver it.
The third will differentiate companies according to their ability to finance, deploy and monetize intelligence.
And that means AI exposure is becoming a balance-sheet variable.
⸻
PART II — CAPITAL: FROM AI CAPEX TO THE GREAT CAPITAL FORMATION CYCLE
The scale of what is happening is extraordinary.
The AI investment boom is increasingly being connected directly to the world’s largest pools of institutional capital.
Nvidia’s emerging infrastructure-financing ecosystem reportedly involves Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR around a potential $500 billion AI data-center financing initiative. Nvidia could itself guarantee as much as 25% of certain projects. (Financial Times)
This changes the nature of the AI cycle.
AI is no longer financed principally through technology-company cash flows.
It is beginning to absorb:
**private credit
infrastructure capital
insurance capital
pension capital
investment-grade debt
sovereign capital.**
Approximately $200 billion of private credit exposure to AI borrowers and around $350 billion of technology bond issuance in 2026 illustrate how rapidly the financing architecture is expanding. (Financial Times)
The market therefore needs a different analytical framework.
AI CAPEX → CAPITAL MARKETS
The conventional question is:
How much will hyperscalers spend?
The more important question is becoming:
What happens when AI investment becomes large enough to change the allocation and pricing of global capital itself?
Consider the propagation mechanism:
AI DEMAND ↓ ACCELERATED COMPUTING ↓ DATA CENTERS ↓ POWER + GRID + COOLING + MEMORY + OPTICS ↓ LOGISTICS + CONSTRUCTION + INDUSTRIAL EQUIPMENT ↓ DEBT + INFRASTRUCTURE FINANCING ↓ HIGHER CAPITAL FORMATION ↓ PRODUCTIVITY DIFFERENTIALS ↓ CASH-FLOW DIFFERENTIALS ↓ CREDIT DIFFERENTIALS ↓ COST-OF-CAPITAL DIFFERENTIALS
And then the loop begins again.
Companies with greater access to capital can invest more.Companies investing more can deploy AI faster.
Companies deploying AI faster can improve productivity.
Higher productivity supports margins and cash flows.
Better cash flows improve financing capacity.
Greater financing capacity permits still more investment.
THIS IS THE PROPAGATION LOOP
AI adoption → cash-flow resilience → lower relative financing friction → greater investment capacity → higher productivity → stronger cash flow → greater AI adoption.
The opposite loop is equally important:
AI irrelevance → weaker expected productivity → financing disadvantage → lower investment → widening technology gap → weaker competitive position → still higher relevance risk.
This is why the Great Split could become self-reinforcing.
⸻
PART III — TOMORROW: WATCH THE PRICE OF CAPITAL, NOT JUST THE PRICE OF NVIDIA
Tomorrow morning, the temptation will again be to ask whether technology stocks can continue rising.
That is too narrow.
The market is moving toward a much larger repricing.
FIVE SIGNALS TO WATCH
Treasury yields
Today’s decline matters because AI is extraordinarily capital intensive. Lower long-duration yields simultaneously support technology multiples and reduce the financing burden of the physical infrastructure behind AI.
Credit versus equity
Watch whether the divergence between equity optimism and credit caution persists.
If credit spreads begin differentiating aggressively between AI-relevant and AI-exposed-but-overleveraged borrowers, the next Great Split has begun.
Semiconductors
The semiconductor index had fallen roughly 29% between late June and late July, yet stronger infrastructure earnings have revived confidence that AI spending remains constrained more by supply than by demand. Nvidia’s August 26 earnings are becoming the next major system-wide test. (Business Insider)
Oil
Brent fell toward roughly $87 today despite continuing geopolitical uncertainty. Lower energy prices simultaneously reduce inflation pressure and improve the economics of energy-intensive infrastructure investment. (Reuters)
Physical AI infrastructure
Watch engineering, construction, electrical equipment, cooling, power, memory, optical networking and logistics.
The AI trade is propagating outward.
One striking example today: Larsen & Toubro announced a major order to construct an Nvidia B300 AI Factory infrastructure project in Chennai. (Upstox - Online Stock and Share Trading)
That is exactly what propagation looks like.
⸻

These numbers do not describe separate phenomena.
They describe a single emerging capital cycle.
⸻
THE HIDDEN PATTERN
For three years financial markets have treated AI primarily as a question of technology leadership.
That framework is becoming obsolete.
AI is becoming a mechanism for reallocating capital across the entire economy.
And once that happens, the relevant unit of analysis changes:
STOCKS ↓ CASH FLOWS ↓ CREDIT ↓ INFRASTRUCTURE ↓ INDUSTRIES ↓ CITIES ↓ COUNTRIES
The AI trade is becoming the AI capital-formation cycle.
That is much bigger.
Because the ultimate winner will not necessarily be the company producing the most intelligence.
It may be the company, infrastructure network, city or country capable of turning intelligence into productive capital faster than everybody else.
⸻
VELOCITY INSIGHT
The first AI trade repriced intelligence. The second repriced infrastructure. The third will reprice the cost of capital.
And that third transition may prove the most consequential.
Because once AI changes expected cash flows, it changes credit risk.
Once it changes credit risk, it changes the cost of capital.Once it changes the cost of capital, it changes investment.
And once it changes investment, today’s technological divergence becomes tomorrow’s economic divergence.
AI → Productivity → Cash Flow → Credit → Cost of Capital → Investment → More AI
That is the propagation mechanism markets are still underestimating.
Tomorrow’s question is therefore no longer simply whether the AI rally continues.
It is:
Who gets financed next—and at what price?
That is where The Great Repricing begins.

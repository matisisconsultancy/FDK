Markets received what they wanted today. U.S. producer prices were unchanged in July, strengthening the argument that the inflation impulse is moderating. Treasury yields declined, the dollar weakened, technology rallied and the S&P 500 moved to another intraday record.

But the important signal was not the index move. It was the change underneath it — the probability assigned by markets to the Federal Reserve holding rates unchanged in September rose to roughly **65%**, from about 50% before today's producer-price release.

Equity markets continue to celebrate AI. Credit markets are beginning to ask ==who must finance it==. That distinction matters enormously.

::pull THE GREAT SPLIT
Equity investors are becoming more comfortable with risk precisely as credit investors become more sensitive to it — and AI exposure is becoming a balance-sheet variable.

::section The Great Split Is Migrating Into Credit | equity optimism, credit caution
Barclays analysis highlighted today an unusual divergence: demand for downside protection has been falling in equities while increasing in investment-grade credit instruments. This is not necessarily a contradiction. It may be the beginning of the next stage of the AI trade.

The first stage rewarded companies producing intelligence. The second rewarded companies building the infrastructure required to deliver it. The third will differentiate companies according to their ability to finance, deploy and monetize intelligence.

::section From AI Capex To The Great Capital Formation Cycle | who finances intelligence
The AI investment boom is increasingly being connected directly to the world's largest pools of institutional capital. Nvidia's emerging infrastructure-financing ecosystem reportedly involves Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR around a potential **$500 billion** AI data-center financing initiative. Nvidia could itself guarantee as much as 25% of certain projects.

This changes the nature of the AI cycle. AI is no longer financed principally through technology-company cash flows. It is beginning to absorb private credit, infrastructure capital, insurance capital, pension capital, investment-grade debt and sovereign capital. Approximately **$200 billion** of private credit exposure to AI borrowers and around **$350 billion** of technology bond issuance in 2026 illustrate how rapidly the financing architecture is expanding.

The conventional question — how much will hyperscalers spend? — is giving way to a more important one: what happens when AI investment becomes large enough to change the allocation and pricing of global capital itself? Companies with greater access to capital can invest more, deploy AI faster, improve productivity, and improve financing capacity — a self-reinforcing loop. The opposite loop, from AI irrelevance to financing disadvantage to still higher relevance risk, is equally important.

::signals Five Signals To Watch | the price of capital, not just the price of Nvidia
# Treasury yields
Today's decline matters because AI is extraordinarily capital intensive. Lower long-duration yields simultaneously support technology multiples and reduce the financing burden of the physical infrastructure behind AI.
signal: Lower yields ease both valuations and the cost of building AI.

# Credit versus equity
Watch whether the divergence between equity optimism and credit caution persists. If credit spreads begin differentiating aggressively between AI-relevant and AI-exposed-but-overleveraged borrowers, the next Great Split has begun.
signal: Credit differentiation marks the start of the next split.

# Semiconductors
The semiconductor index had fallen roughly 29% between late June and late July, yet stronger infrastructure earnings have revived confidence that AI spending remains constrained more by supply than by demand. Nvidia's August 26 earnings are becoming the next major system-wide test.
signal: Nvidia's August 26 print is the system-wide test.

# Oil
Brent fell toward roughly $87 today despite continuing geopolitical uncertainty. Lower energy prices simultaneously reduce inflation pressure and improve the economics of energy-intensive infrastructure investment.
signal: Cheaper energy aids both inflation and infrastructure economics.

# Physical AI infrastructure
Watch engineering, construction, electrical equipment, cooling, power, memory, optical networking and logistics. One striking example today: Larsen & Toubro announced a major order to construct an Nvidia B300 AI Factory infrastructure project in Chennai.
signal: The AI trade is propagating outward — that is what it looks like.

::takeaways Key takeaways | the AI trade becomes a capital-formation cycle
- For three years markets treated AI primarily as a question of technology leadership; that framework is becoming obsolete.
- AI is becoming a mechanism for reallocating capital across the entire economy — the unit of analysis moves from stocks to cash flows to credit to infrastructure to industries, cities and countries.
- Once AI changes expected cash flows, it changes credit risk; once it changes credit risk, it changes the cost of capital; once it changes the cost of capital, it changes investment.
- The ultimate winner may not be the company producing the most intelligence, but the company, network, city or country able to turn intelligence into productive capital fastest.

::close Tomorrow's question is no longer simply whether the AI rally continues. It is: ==who gets financed next — and at what price?== That is where The Great Repricing begins.

The Geography of Intelligence Is Being Rewritten
19 September 2026 | FDK
The Fundamental Question
Which European corridors can convert Compute + Energy + Capital + Industrial AI + Talent into self-reinforcing economic Velocity—and which have the greatest upside from here?
The next European AI map will not be organized primarily around countries.
It will be organized around corridors.
That distinction is fundamental. Frontier AI requires a combination of compute, electricity, fiber, capital, research, industrial customers, engineering talent and increasingly large physical sites. These assets rarely coincide neatly inside national boundaries. They cluster around metropolitan systems and then propagate along energy, transport, industrial and digital networks.
Europe is already moving in this direction. The Commission is establishing 19 AI Factories, alongside 13 associated antennas, and its July 2026 call envisages up to seven AI Gigafactories, supported by as much as €10 billion of public funding and expected to unlock at least €20 billion of private investment. Individual Gigafactories are intended to combine more than 100,000 advanced AI processors with power, networking and energy-efficient data-center infrastructure. 
Meanwhile, Europe’s traditional FLAP-D data-center markets—Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Paris and Dublin—have reached approximately 3.8 GW of live capacity, with another 1.4 GW under construction. But capital is beginning to spread toward Milan, Iberia and the Nordics as electricity and land become the binding constraints. 
This produces a new law:
AI FOLLOWS COMPUTE.
COMPUTE FOLLOWS POWER.
CAPITAL FOLLOWS PRODUCTIVITY.
PRODUCTIVITY CREATES THE CORRIDOR.

1. LONDON–CAMBRIDGE–OXFORD
THE CAPITAL & INTELLIGENCE CORRIDOR
GVI 94 | Upside 82
London remains Europe’s deepest intersection of capital, AI companies, global financial institutions and cloud infrastructure, while Cambridge and Oxford supply extraordinary research and scientific density.
London is also still EMEA’s largest individual data-center market, with approximately 1.38 GW operational and a pipeline around 1.77 GW. 
Its weakness is becoming physical rather than intellectual.
Power.
Land.
Grid connections.
Cost.
That distinction matters enormously.
The corridor remains Europe’s benchmark in Speed Up, but its marginal unit of AI infrastructure is becoming increasingly expensive.
Velocity thesis: leadership remains formidable, but future Velocity increasingly requires geographic expansion beyond London itself.


⸻


2. PARIS–SACLAY–NORTHERN FRANCE
THE SOVEREIGN AI CORRIDOR
GVI 91 | Upside 91
France may possess Europe’s most balanced AI equation.
Paris combines models, talent, research and capital. Saclay supplies scientific density.France’s nuclear system provides something increasingly valuable: relatively abundant low-carbon baseload electricity.
Paris delivered 72.5 MW of new data-center capacity in H1 2026, the largest addition among the core FLAP-D markets. JLL explicitly links France’s nuclear capacity and EDF’s grid-ready pipeline to the acceleration in demand. 
This makes France potentially the strongest European challenger to London’s current leadership.
Its equation is unusually complete:
**Models
Nuclear Energy
Capital
Compute
Research
State Coordination
= Sovereign AI Velocity.**
Paris has the clearest path from European challenger to European leader.

3. FRANKFURT–RHINE-MAIN–RHINE-RUHR
THE INDUSTRIAL COMPUTE CORRIDOR
GVI 90 | Upside 84
Frankfurt remains one of Europe’s great compute nodes.
Supply grew 23% year-on-year in Q1 2026, while net absorption reached 218 MW. Vacancy is only around 5%. 
But Frankfurt’s strategic advantage is not simply data centers.
Connect Frankfurt’s compute to Germany’s extraordinary manufacturing system and the result becomes:
Compute → Mittelstand → Automotive → Chemicals → Engineering → Robotics → Industrial AI.
Germany therefore possesses perhaps Europe’s largest latent Industrial AI propagation opportunity.
The problem is execution.
JLL reports connection lead times of 24 months or more in Frankfurt, despite 311 MW under development and another 687 MW planned. 
Germany’s challenge is therefore not demand.
It is:
PHYSICAL CAPACITY EXPANSION VELOCITY.

4. HELSINKI–ESPOO–KAJAANI–STOCKHOLM
THE ENERGY-TO-INTELLIGENCE CORRIDOR
GVI 88 | Upside 96
This may be Europe’s most underestimated AI corridor.
The Nordics combine:
abundant lower-carbon electricity;
cool climate;
land;
strong grids;
digital sophistication;
high institutional quality;
and increasingly important sovereign-compute infrastructure.
Finland hosts the LUMI AI Factory and Sweden the MIMER AI Factory within the EuroHPC network. 
More importantly, the economics of AI infrastructure are increasingly moving in their direction.
Recent European data-center analysis shows capital expanding toward Nordic markets as power constraints intensify in the traditional core hubs. 
This is the corridor where our central GVI thesis becomes physical:
ENERGY ABUNDANCE BECOMES INTELLIGENCE ABUNDANCE.
The Nordics may not replace London or Paris as financial or model-development centers.
They may become something equally important:
Europe’s AI production system.


⸻


5. AMSTERDAM–EINDHOVEN–LEUVEN
THE SEMICONDUCTOR INTELLIGENCE CORRIDOR
GVI 87 | Upside 86
This corridor has a different strategic logic.
Amsterdam contributes digital infrastructure and capital.
Eindhoven contributes Europe’s extraordinary semiconductor-equipment ecosystem.
Leuven adds research and advanced semiconductor capabilities.
This makes the corridor less dependent on becoming Europe’s largest data-center cluster.
Its strategic importance comes from controlling part of the technology stack underneath AI.
The Netherlands is also establishing an NLAIF AI Factory, while Belgium hosts an AI Factory antenna. 
The corridor’s constraint is familiar:
power + permitting + physical expansion.
But its scarcity value is enormous.
Compute capacity can move. Semiconductor know-how is much harder to relocate.


⸻


6. DUBLIN–LONDON DIGITAL ARC
THE HYPERSCALER CORRIDOR
GVI 85 | Upside 76
Dublin remains strategically important because of the extraordinary concentration of U.S. technology platforms and cloud infrastructure connecting Ireland with Britain and transatlantic networks.
But it illustrates Europe’s emerging constraint particularly clearly.
Power availability is increasingly limiting data-center growth across Dublin and the other established hubs. 
This means its GVI remains high while its marginal upside is lower.
It is already highly developed.
The challenge is no longer attracting AI demand.
It is physically accommodating it.


⸻


7. MADRID–BARCELONA–ARAGÓN
THE SOUTHERN POWER CORRIDOR
GVI 82 | Upside 95Spain could be one of the largest relative winners of Europe’s next AI infrastructure cycle.
Its proposition is fundamentally physical:
renewable electricity + land + improving connectivity + major metropolitan demand + industrial customers.
As AI-scale infrastructure expands beyond traditional hubs, Iberia becomes increasingly attractive.
JLL already identifies Iberia among the regions attracting incremental capital as AI-scale power requirements push investment away from constrained core locations. 
Spain also has two AI Factories, BSC AIF and 1HealthAI. 
Its opportunity is to connect Madrid’s capital and enterprise ecosystem with Barcelona’s science and computing capabilities and Aragón’s energy-and-land proposition.
That could produce:
Madrid → Capital & Enterprise
Barcelona → Compute & Science
Aragón → Energy & Hyperscale
Spain could become Europe’s principal Southern AI infrastructure gateway.

8. MILAN–BOLOGNA–TURIN
THE INDUSTRIAL AI CORRIDOR
GVI 81 | Upside 97
This is our highest-upside corridor.
Not because Italy currently possesses Europe’s largest AI infrastructure.
It does not.
But because Northern Italy possesses an unusually dense combination of assets waiting to be connected:
Milan → Capital + corporate headquarters + finance
Bologna → Leonardo / EuroHPC + research + data
Turin → automotive + robotics + aerospace + manufacturing
Add logistics, rail, energy systems, universities and Northern Italy’s industrial districts, and the corridor becomes a natural laboratory for AI applied to the physical world.
Italy’s IT4LIA AI Factory is already part of the EuroHPC architecture. 
Meanwhile, JLL identifies Milan specifically as one of the markets attracting new capital as data-center expansion broadens beyond Europe’s traditional core. 
The opportunity is therefore not to replicate Silicon Valley.
It is to build something Europe needs much more:
THE INDUSTRIAL AI VALLEY.
Manufacturing.
Mobility.
Robotics.
Infrastructure.
Energy.
Defence.
Aerospace.
Pharmaceuticals.
Financial services.
Italy’s weakness is execution velocity.
Its upside is enormous precisely because the installed industrial base is already there.
If Milan–Bologna–Turin closes the Compute–Energy–Capital loop, its GVI rerating could be the largest in Western Europe.

9. ZURICH–LAUSANNE–GENEVA
THE HIGH-VALUE INTELLIGENCE CORRIDOR
GVI 80 | Upside 79
Switzerland combines extraordinary universities, capital, pharmaceuticals, precision engineering and high-value services.
Its advantage is not scale.
It is intelligence density.
This corridor should excel in AI domains where the value per unit of compute is exceptionally high:
life sciences;
finance;
robotics;
precision engineering;
scientific AI.
Switzerland also participates through the HEARTS AI Factory antenna. 
The limitation is obvious: physical and demographic scale.
Switzerland can become Europe’s highest-value AI cluster without becoming its largest.

10. WARSAW–POZNAŃ–PRAGUE
THE CONVERGENCE CORRIDOR
GVI 74 | Upside 93
Central Europe deserves much greater attention.
Poland now has two AI Factories, PIAST and Gaia, while Czechia hosts CZAI. 
The corridor combines lower costs, increasingly strong technical talent, industrial supply chains, manufacturing and proximity to Germany.
Its opportunity is classic GVI convergence:
**lower starting base
capital inflows
infrastructure
AI diffusion
industrial upgrading
= faster relative Velocity.**
This is the corridor most likely to surprise on the upside if capital formation accelerates.

THE MOST IMPORTANT FINDING
Europe’s existing AI geography and its future AI geography are not the same.
Today the center of gravity remains:
LONDON + FRANKFURT + PARIS + AMSTERDAM + DUBLIN
Those five markets still account for around 43% of EMEA operational data-center supply. 
But the next marginal euro of AI infrastructure increasingly searches for:
**POWER
LAND
GRID ACCESS
PERMITTING
INDUSTRIAL DEMAND.**
That favors:
NORDICS + IBERIA + NORTHERN ITALY + CENTRAL EUROPEThe geography of European intelligence is therefore beginning to move from a metropolitan model toward a corridor model.

THE GLOBAL VIEW
Europe does not need one Silicon Valley.
That is the wrong mental model.
Europe needs a network of specialized AI corridors.
London for capital.
Paris for sovereign AI.
Frankfurt for industrial compute.
The Nordics for energy-to-intelligence.
Amsterdam–Eindhoven for semiconductors.
Spain for renewable hyperscale.
Northern Italy for Industrial AI.
Switzerland for high-value intelligence.
Central Europe for convergence.
Then connect them.
Energy grids.
Fiber.
Capital markets.
High-speed rail.
Research networks.
Industrial supply chains.
AI Factories.
That creates a completely different European architecture:
SPECIALIZATION × CONNECTIVITY × SCALE
Europe’s weakness has historically been fragmentation.
Its next competitive advantage could be networked specialization.

THE VELOCITY INSIGHT
The decisive unit of competition in the AI economy may no longer be the company.
And it may not even be the country.
It may be the corridor.
Because the corridor is where:
capital meets compute;
compute meets energy;
energy meets industry;
industry meets talent;
and
talent turns intelligence into productivity.
That gives us the new law of European Velocity:
AI DOES NOT SCALE WHERE THE MODEL IS.
AI SCALES WHERE THE ENTIRE SYSTEM CLOSES.
The corridors capable of closing that system will compound.
Those unable to do so will increasingly pay for somebody else’s intelligence.

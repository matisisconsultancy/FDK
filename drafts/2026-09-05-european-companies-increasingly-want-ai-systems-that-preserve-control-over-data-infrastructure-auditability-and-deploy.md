Oil Below $80 Is Not the Same as Energy Normalization
Verified facts
Brent traded near $79.12 as Iran and Oman reported progress on the coordinates of a possible shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz. A proposed framework could give Iran control over inbound traffic entering the Gulf—an arrangement US officials have previously opposed.
Gulf crude and condensate exports remained approximately 40% below pre-conflict levels in July. The Houthis also claimed missile attacks against Saudi tankers in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden, sustaining concern over wider regional shipping security.
FDK interpretation
The decline in oil is already easing inflation expectations and bond-market pressure.
But it is pricing diplomatic probability—not restored physical capacity.
A durable energy normalization requires:
regular vessel movements;
lower insurance premiums;
predictable access rules;
restored loading schedules;
and confidence that routes will not close again.
The proposed settlement could also produce an important geopolitical transfer of value.
Control over inbound passage through Hormuz would give Iran influence not merely over oil supply, but over the commercial access of Gulf economies to the global system.
The oil price is trading diplomacy. The physical supply chain is still trading strategic control.
For Europe, lower crude is an immediate positive.
But the strategic lesson remains unchanged: energy sovereignty cannot be measured only through production. It must include routes, insurance, storage, ports and alternative delivery capacity.

The Bond Market Is Refusing to Restore the Old Cost of Capital
Verified facts
The ten-year Treasury yield declined modestly to approximately 4.607%, while markets reduced the implied probability of a September Fed increase to around 54%.
The US Treasury expects to borrow $739 billion in the third quarter and $628 billion in the fourth. It has kept note and bond auction sizes unchanged for the moment, avoiding an immediate long-duration issuance shock.
Nevertheless, trading in SOFR swap futures surged above 167,000 contracts, representing approximately $16 billion in notional value, as investors hedged the possibility that rates remain higher for longer. Mortgage extension risk is pushing hedging requirements further along the yield curve.
FDK interpretation
Lower oil can reduce inflation expectations.
Slower employment can reduce the probability of additional monetary tightening.
Neither eliminates the structural demand for capital.
Governments are financing deficits, defense, energy security and industrial policy.
Hyperscalers are financing AI factories, power, data centres and networks.
Utilities are financing grids.
Companies are financing supply-chain resilience.
All are competing for the same duration.
The cost of capital is no longer determined by the central bank alone. It is being determined by the aggregate financing requirements of the Next Economy.
The Treasury’s decision not to increase long-term auction sizes provides tactical relief.
It does not resolve the underlying funding equation.
A structurally higher real rate may ultimately be one of the consequences of AI success—not evidence of AI failure.

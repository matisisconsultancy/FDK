A consequential inflection point has emerged in the U.S.–China AI competition. On October 15, Meta released Muse Spark Open, its most capable U.S.-developed open-weight model to date, directly challenging China’s growing influence over the global distribution of accessible AI. A day later, Taiwan disclosed plans to tighten controls on advanced AI hardware exports to China.
Together, these developments strengthen our central thesis while introducing a critical refinement: the United States is beginning to contest China’s diffusion advantage, even as access to advanced computing becomes more geopolitically restricted. 
1. The American Counteroffensive
Meta’s October 15 release introduces an open-weight model designed for advanced reasoning, software engineering and autonomous agents.
Its specifications are significant:
816 billion parameters, with 42 billion active during inference.
One-million-token context window, supporting complex, extended workflows.
Local deployment using two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios.
Broad distribution through major cloud and inference platforms.
Meta’s performance claims still require independent comparison with leading Chinese alternatives, and its license contains commercial restrictions. Nevertheless, the release marks a significant expansion of the American open-weight offering. 
The second-order implication: The United States is no longer relying exclusively on proprietary frontier-model leadership. American technology companies are also competing over the distribution architecture through which intelligence reaches developers and enterprises.
2. The Semiconductor Divide Deepens
On October 16, Taiwan announced plans to align restrictions on advanced AI chips and servers more closely with U.S. export controls.
Once implemented, unauthorized shipments of covered technologies to China would become criminal offenses under Taiwanese law.
This is a proposed policy change, not an already implemented prohibition. 
The development acquires additional significance alongside IDC data reported by Reuters on October 13:
34% :: Nvidia’s share of China’s AI-accelerator market, H1 2026.
28% :: Huawei’s share of the same market.
Nvidia’s shipments declined 44% year over year, while Chinese alternatives expanded. These are shipment-market shares, not measures of relative computing performance. 
The emerging distinction is between American leadership in advanced computing and China’s accelerating localization of AI infrastructure.
3. The AI Supercycle: A New Economic Bifurcation
Three second-order effects follow.
First, inference economics become a central competitive variable. Open-weight models increase opportunities for enterprises to customize and deploy intelligence, although hardware requirements, licensing and operating costs still determine the actual savings.
Second, the geography of computing becomes increasingly strategic. Export restrictions can accelerate domestic semiconductor substitution while increasing the cost of maintaining globally interoperable AI infrastructure.
Third, capital formation becomes more selective. Investors must distinguish frontier-model capability from the cost of delivering intelligence at scale.
For The Velocity Edge, the emerging investment framework is:
Frontier Capability × Inference Efficiency × Industrial Diffusion × Economic Capture
The AI Supercycle is evolving from a race to produce superior models into a competition over the entire economics of accessible intelligence.
The Big Take
The American frontier advantage remains important, but the competitive landscape is becoming more complex.
China’s challenge is pushing American firms to compete more aggressively in open-weight AI, while semiconductor restrictions are reinforcing China’s incentives to develop an alternative computing ecosystem.
The investment implications extend beyond Nvidia, Huawei, Meta and Apple.They reach enterprise software, industrial automation, agentic AI, accelerated computing and the financing of increasingly distributed AI infrastructure.
The decisive question is no longer simply who builds the most intelligent model. It is who can deliver economically useful intelligence at the lowest sustainable cost—and capture the resulting value.
Velocity Insight: America is defending the frontier while entering the diffusion race; China is building an alternative computing ecosystem to compete on the economics of intelligence.

BENEATH THE PATTERNS
IN FOCUS
The New Investors’ Playbook for the AI Supercycle
The AI Supercycle has entered its capital-allocation phase.
The financial test is no longer whether companies can deploy more AI capital. It is whether that capital can generate cash before financing costs, depreciation and technological obsolescence consume the return.
With the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield at 5.24% at the September 28 close, the hurdle has moved higher. Every dollar committed to compute, data centers, power infrastructure or frontier models now has to convert into utilization and free cash flow fast enough to preserve economic spread over the cost of capital.
At the same time, the return pool is fragmenting.
Model capability is proliferating and inference costs are falling, while advanced packaging, power, cooling and grid access remain constrained. Scarcity is therefore migrating toward the physical layers where pricing power is stronger and capacity takes longer to reproduce.
The next AI winners will be the companies that capture the widest spread between ROIC and cost of capital—and sustain it as the stack commoditizes.
That reframes the entire cycle:
Capex is not the signal. Productive cash conversion is.
The financial hierarchy is now:
Scarcity → Utilization → Pricing Power → Free Cash Flow → ROIC − Cost of Capital
The market is moving from AI spending to AI economic spread.

TWO DECISIVE STRUCTURAL DRIVERS
1. PHYSICAL SCARCITY IS BECOMING THE FIRST GREAT REDISTRIBUTOR OF AI ECONOMICS
The next constraints increasingly sit in:
Advanced Packaging → Memory → Networking → Power → Cooling → Grid Access
These layers cannot scale at software speed.
That creates a powerful economic transmission mechanism:
Scarcity → Pricing Power → Utilization → Cash Conversion → ROIC
TSMC captures manufacturing scarcity.
Schneider Electric captures power architecture.
Siemens Energy sits against generation and grid constraints.
Vertiv monetizes thermal density.
The implication is straightforward:
The second-order AI trade is everything compute cannot operate without.
As model capability proliferates, the scarce physical layers underneath AI become more strategically valuable.


⸻


2. CAPITAL PRODUCTIVITY IS BECOMING THE DECISIVE FINANCIAL FILTERThe next divide will not be between companies that invest in AI and those that do not.
It will be between companies whose capital compounds and those whose capital merely accumulates.
CAPITAL THAT COMPOUNDS
Capex → Utilization → Cash Flow → ROIC → Reinvestment
CAPITAL THAT ACCUMULATES
Capex → Capacity → Depreciation → Financing Cost → Margin Pressure
The critical question is no longer:
How much is being spent?
It is:
How rapidly does each incremental dollar of AI capital become productive free cash flow?
That is the emerging hurdle rate of the AI Supercycle.

THE BACKLOG REPRICING
Not all backlog has equal economic value.
Announced → Contracted → Financed → Powered → Utilized → Cash-Generating
At the 5.24% September 28 U.S. 10-year Treasury close, every delay between commitment and monetization has a real duration cost.
This creates a new hierarchy.
Powered backlog is worth more than announced backlog.
Utilized capacity is worth more than installed capacity.
Cash-generating infrastructure is worth more than financed infrastructure.
The next divide is nominal backlog versus economic backlog.

THREE FORWARD SIGNALS
TIME-TO-POWER
Grid access increasingly determines when capital begins earning.
Time-to-power therefore becomes a financial variable—not simply an engineering constraint.
DISTRIBUTION
As intelligence becomes cheaper, control of workflow, customer and transaction becomes more valuable.
SECURITY
Agentic AI creates more identities, permissions, transactions and liabilities.
More autonomy → greater security intensity.
Distribution and security matter because they determine how scarce infrastructure and AI capital are ultimately monetized.

EXPLICIT DOWNSIDE SCENARIOS
Capex glut: excess capacity → pricing pressure → weaker utilization → lower FCF conversion → lower ROIC.
Rate shock: higher long yields → higher hurdle rates → weaker project economics → valuation compression.
Model commoditization: lower model margins → value migrates toward scarce infrastructure and distribution.
Power delays: idle capital → delayed revenue → higher financing cost → accelerated obsolescence.
Agentic-liability event: slower deployment → higher governance and cybersecurity intensity.
The common denominator is capital productivity.
Every downside scenario ultimately tests how much capital is trapped before it becomes cash-generating capacity.

ADDENDUM — GVI METHODOLOGY & READING GUIDE
A. WHAT THE BENCHMARK ACTUALLY MEASURES
The FDK GVI Corporate Benchmark measures Structural Corporate Velocity:
the rate at which a corporation improves its capacity to convert technology, capital, talent, infrastructure and ecosystem position into future economic value.
Operating Purpose
Identify structural corporate leadership early, distinguish real economic acceleration from narrative momentum, and convert that signal into disciplined capital-allocation analysis.
It is not a stock-return ranking, valuation model, market-cap ranking or measure of current profitability alone.

E. EVIDENCE HIERARCHY
Level 1 — Verified measured data: revenue, margins, FCF, capex, backlog, capacity, utilization, official rates and executed transactions.
Level 2 — Verified corporate actions: signed contracts, deployments, acquisitions, financing, capacity additions and restructuring.
Level 3 — Forward claims: guidance, targets and announced capacity, discounted for funding, timing and execution probability.
Level 4 — FDK proprietary data analytics: capital productivity, cash-conversion velocity, ecosystem control, bottleneck scarcity, resilience, optionality and second-order effects.
Approximately 70% measured and verified evidence / 30% FDK proprietary data analytics.
Data establish what happened. FDK analytics determine what changes next.

F. INVESTOR INTERPRETATION FRAMEWORK
GVI LEVEL → GVI DIRECTION → ECONOMIC CAPTURE → INVESTMENT BURDEN → VALUATION

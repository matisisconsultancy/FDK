THE INVESTMENT VERDICT
For investors, physical electricity infrastructure is becoming an increasingly important determinant of project quality.
For European banks, construction risk, grid-delivery risk and customer risk should be evaluated separately.
For CEOs and boards, future computing expansion should be matched to enforceable electricity arrangements and commercially credible deployment schedules.
For policymakers, the lesson is that Europe’s AI ambitions require faster conversion of electricity infrastructure investment into operational and economically productive capacity.

THE BIG TAKE
Two material developments since November 16 demonstrate that Europe’s computing-infrastructure competition is increasingly determined by the conversion of electricity access into deliverable physical capacity.
Germany’s Digital Realty has commissioned a dedicated high-voltage connection representing a triple-digit-million-euro infrastructure investment. Sweden’s Stendörren Fastigheter has signed an agreement for 20 MW of electricity capacity at a permitted data-center site.
Neither development constitutes a new EuroHPC Gigafactory award. Both materially improve the infrastructure foundations for future European digital and AI investment.

1 | GERMANY — DIGITAL REALTY COMMISSIONS A MAJOR POWER CONNECTION
November 17 — Confirmed construction and commissioning milestone.
Digital Realty announced that the dedicated 110 kV electricity connection serving its Digital Park Fechenheim campus in Frankfurt is now operational.
The underground connection extends approximately 16 kilometers from the Karben substation. It took around 20 months to construct and represents a triple-digit-million-euro investment.
The Frankfurt campus currently operates three data centers. At full development, it is planned to accommodate 11 facilities with up to 200 MW of IT capacity, with a fourth facility scheduled to open in 2027. globenewswire.com
Why it matters
This is a physical delivery milestone rather than another prospective grid application.
It strengthens the credibility of future campus expansion and reduces a major category of infrastructure execution risk. The ultimate IT capacity nevertheless remains a development target, dependent on further construction and commercialization.
For investors and banks, completed private power infrastructure can support asset valuations and potentially improve the financeability of subsequent phases.
For CEOs, it reinforces the value of securing electrical infrastructure before committing to large-scale computing deployment.
For policymakers, Frankfurt offers an example of private capital financing grid infrastructure to support digital expansion.
2 | SWEDEN — STENDÖRREN CONTRACTS 20 MW FOR A PERMITTED DATA-CENTER SITE
November 17, 07:30 CET — Confirmed electricity-capacity agreement.
Stendörren Fastigheter announced an agreement for 20 MW of electricity capacity for its planned data center at Almnäs 5:23 in Södertälje, south of Stockholm.
The development covers approximately 20,000 square meters and already possesses a building permit.
The power connection is expected during the first half of 2028. Expansion to 40 MW remains a possibility, not a contractual commitment. Stendörren
Why it matters
This moves the project beyond the purely permitted development stage, increasing its potential attractiveness to future operators and investors.
However, Stendörren has not identified a tenant, disclosed construction financing or confirmed the precise nature and counterparty of the power agreement.
The Swedish development is not part of the AI Gigafactory Northern consortium announced on November 16.
Its importance is broader: Nordic industrial real estate with credible power arrangements is becoming a potential platform for digital-infrastructure capital formation.
For investors, the next value-creation milestone is a binding lease, sale agreement or customer commitment. For boards, the distinction between contracted power and operational compute remains essential.

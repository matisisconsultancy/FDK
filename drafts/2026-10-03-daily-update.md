THE GREAT DECOUPLING
THE BIG TAKE
GVI: 108.0 | Underlying bias: marginally lower | United States: 94.1 ↓0.1 | France: 81.2 ↓0.1 | No ranking changes | Confidence: 95%
Friday produced one of the most revealing market signals of the current cycle.
US payrolls rose by only 29,000 in September, versus 90,000 expected; unemployment increased to 4.2%, July and August payrolls were revised down by a combined 60,000, and wage growth slowed to 3.0% y/y. Yet after initially rallying, Treasuries reversed: the US 10-year yield ended around 5.28%, up almost 5 bps on the day. Meanwhile the Nasdaq rose 1.2% and the S&P 500 0.7%. 
That divergence matters enormously.
The long end of the bond market is beginning to trade independently of the Fed.
A weaker economy reduced the probability of an October rate increase, but it did not bring long-term borrowing costs down. Fiscal risk, energy inflation, geopolitical uncertainty and term premium are increasingly dominating the traditional monetary-policy transmission mechanism. 
Europe revealed the second half of the same problem. Euro-area inflation accelerated to an estimated 3.8% in September from 3.2%, with energy inflation at 18.8%. At the same time, France’s 10-year risk premium over Germany reached roughly 150 basis points, its highest since the euro-area debt crisis, while French five-year CDS climbed to around 87 bps, nearly three times their level one month ago. 
And beneath those macro signals, capital is already moving.
Global equity funds still attracted $34.76 billion in the latest week, but technology funds lost $2.63 billion, while financials gained $1.13 billion and utilities $468 million. 
This is the market beginning to price the next phase of the AI Supercycle.

Five-Pillar Readout
Intelligence Infrastructure — Strong, but capital is becoming selective
AI capital formation remains extraordinary. US hyperscaler capital expenditure is estimated at roughly $800 billion in 2026, with consensus around $1.1 trillion in 2027. Yet the more revealing development is where capital is beginning to migrate: technology funds suffered outflows while utilities and financials attracted money. 
The market is starting to price the infrastructure around intelligence.
COMPUTE → MEMORY → NETWORKING → POWER → UTILITIES → FINANCING
The infrastructure cycle remains strong. Its ownership structure is broadening.
Innovation Velocity — Stable
No new national research, model-performance or patent variable meets today’s scoring threshold.
Innovation momentum remains structurally strong, but innovation itself is becoming less scarce than the capital and infrastructure required to commercialise it.
Enterprise Transformation — The US economy is splittingThe US employment report contains a remarkable internal divergence.
Headline hiring weakened sharply, yet construction added 11,000 jobs and manufacturing added 9,000. Reuters noted that AI-related infrastructure investment may be contributing to both; US factory employment has increased 72,000 since its December trough. 
This is not simply labour-market weakness.
It increasingly looks like labour reallocation:
INFORMATION / SERVICES ↓
versus
AI INFRASTRUCTURE / CONSTRUCTION / MANUFACTURING ↑
The AI cycle is beginning to reorganise employment as well as capital.
Smart Capital — The principal decelerator
This is today’s defining pillar.
The US 10-year yield rising to roughly 5.28% despite a weak payroll report suggests that long-duration borrowing costs are becoming less sensitive to expectations for the next Fed meeting. 
In Europe, the problem is more complex.
Higher energy inflation is pushing inflation upward just as sovereign fragmentation is tightening financing conditions independently of the ECB. France’s 10-year spread versus Germany is around 150 bps, while Italy’s is around 120 bps. 
That inversion is historically striking.
France—not Italy—is currently the euro area’s most visible large-economy sovereign stress point.
Discovery Capacity — Stable
No new high-confidence scientific-output, talent or research-commercialisation variable crosses today’s materiality threshold.

The Second-Order Effects
I — 
The Fed is losing control of the long end
The immediate response to weak payrolls was textbook: Treasury yields fell and expectations for an October Fed hike declined.
Then the long end reversed.
That reversal may be more important than the payroll number itself.
The transmission mechanism is evolving from:
FED → POLICY RATE → BOND YIELDS
toward:
FISCAL RISK + ENERGY + GEOPOLITICS + TERM PREMIUM → LONG-END YIELDS
If that regime persists, a Fed pause—or eventually even easing—may not automatically lower corporate WACC.
That would fundamentally change the valuation regime for long-duration assets.


⸻


II — 
The AI rotation is moving beyond technology
The fund-flow evidence is particularly revealing.
Investors are still buying equities.
They are simply beginning to sell technology while buying utilities and financials. 
That is precisely what the next AI phase should look like.
The first trade was:
MODELS → GPUs → HYPERSCALERS
The second becomes:
POWER → GRID → UTILITIES → CREDIT → INDUSTRIAL INFRASTRUCTURE
The market is not abandoning AI.
It is moving downstream into the assets required to finance and power it.


⸻


III — 
France changes the European risk map
A French-German spread near 150 bps is not merely a French problem.
France is central to Europe’s sovereign-credit architecture, banking system and political core.
The second-order transmission could run:
FRENCH SOVEREIGN RISK
→ BANK FUNDING
→ CORPORATE CREDIT
→ EURO WEAKNESS
→ IMPORTED ENERGY COSTS
→ ECB CONSTRAINT
Italy provides the surprising counterpoint.
Italian spreads remain elevated, but France has moved above Italy against Germany in the risk hierarchy Reuters describes, while Italian debt has relatively outperformed France. 
The euro-area fragmentation story is being rewritten.


⸻


IV — 
Weak macro growth may actually accelerate AI concentration
A slower labour market reduces aggregate growth.
But it simultaneously increases corporate pressure to automate.
That creates a paradox:LOWER EMPLOYMENT GROWTH
→ GREATER PRODUCTIVITY PRESSURE
→ MORE AI INVESTMENT
→ FURTHER LABOUR REALLOCATION
Weak macro data could therefore slow the economy while accelerating enterprise AI adoption.
The result would be a sharper divide between companies able to finance automation and companies unable to do so.


⸻


V — 
Energy intervention reveals the new scarcity
The G7 reserve release may temporarily lower fuel prices.
But its composition is telling.
The system is increasingly constrained not simply by crude oil availability but by refining capacity, diesel supply, logistics and energy resilience. 
The same structural pattern appears across AI:
Abundance at the headline level conceals scarcity in the conversion layer.
Crude can be available while diesel is scarce.
Compute can be available while power is scarce.
Capital can be abundant while long-duration financing is expensive.
This is the economic architecture of the next phase.


⸻


The Evolutionary Market Rotation
The rotation is now becoming visible in actual capital flows:
AI TECHNOLOGY → AI CASH FLOW → POWER → FINANCE → INDUSTRIAL PRODUCTIVITY
The crucial development is that stocks and bonds are beginning to tell different stories.
Equities are pricing future productivity.
Bonds are pricing the cost of getting there.
The winners will be those businesses that can bridge the two.
That suggests a new capital-allocation hierarchy:
1. Free-cash-flow AI leaders
2. Scarcity infrastructure owners
3. Utilities and energy systems with contracted demand
4. Financial institutions financing productive AI capacity
5. Industrial companies demonstrating measurable AI productivity
At the bottom of the hierarchy sit long-duration businesses requiring continuous external financing but unable yet to demonstrate economic conversion.

Five Takeaways
1. Do not interpret weaker payrolls as an automatic duration signal.
The US 10-year rose despite the jobs miss. The long end increasingly reflects fiscal, geopolitical and inflation risk rather than merely Fed expectations.
2. The AI trade is already rotating.
Technology funds are losing capital while utilities and financials gain it. The next excess returns may increasingly accrue to those who power and finance intelligence, not only those who produce it.
3. Europe has entered a three-variable stress regime.
ENERGY INFLATION + SOVEREIGN FRAGMENTATION + WEAK GROWTH will increasingly constrain the ECB and European capital formation.
4. Watch France before Italy.
France’s roughly 150-bp spread and rapidly rising CDS are now a central European systemic variable. Italy remains vulnerable, but the old sovereign-risk hierarchy is changing.
5. For boards, the AI capital test becomes harder again.
CAPACITY → CONTRACTED UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW → ROIC > WACC
At a 5%+ long-end hurdle rate, AI deployment without economic conversion is no longer strategic ambition.
It is expensive optionality.
Financial Verdict
GVI: 108.0. Structural velocity remains high. Financial velocity is becoming increasingly selective.
The most consequential signal of the week is not that American employment weakened.
It is that bond yields refused to believe weaker growth would make capital cheap again.
When weak jobs lift equities but fail to lift bonds, the market is telling us where the next scarcity lies: not in intelligence—but in credible capital.

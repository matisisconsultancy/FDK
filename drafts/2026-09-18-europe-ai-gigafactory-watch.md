18 September 2026 | FDK
SPAIN MOVES FROM PIPELINE TO PHYSICAL EXECUTION
Today brings one development consequential enough to trigger the Watch: Nabiax has formally started construction of an €800 million, 80 MW AI/cloud data center in Alcalá de Henares, Madrid. The first stone was laid on September 18. The facility is designed for AI and cloud workloads, will use electricity supplied from 100% renewable sources, and is expected to support about 1,000 jobs during construction. It will become Nabiax’s fifth data center in the Madrid region.
This is not an EU AI Gigafactory award. At 80 MW, it is materially smaller than the eventual Gigafactory class.
But strategically, it may be more informative than another multi-gigawatt announcement.
Because it has crossed the line from:
PLAN → PERMIT → CAPITAL COMMITMENT → CONSTRUCTION.
That is precisely where much of Europe’s announced AI capacity still fails.
⸻
1 | THE IMPORTANT NUMBER IS NOT 80 MW. IT IS €800 MILLION THAT HAS BECOME PHYSICAL CAPEX
Our framework has increasingly distinguished announced MW from bankable MW.
Today Spain adds something more valuable:
MW UNDER CONSTRUCTION
Madrid now has 41 operating data centers and 15 under construction, with another 40 projects envisaged that the regional government says could represent around €15 billion of investment.
The causality matters:
LAND → GRID → CAPITAL → CONTRACTORS → CONSTRUCTION → OPERATING COMPUTE
Every step eliminates another layer of execution risk.
And this creates an important distinction for investors:
A theoretical 500 MW project five years from operation may be economically less valuable than an 80 MW project already pouring concrete.
This should now become another metric in the Watch:
EXECUTABLE MW
Not just announced capacity.
Not just financed capacity.
But capacity that has crossed the physical point of no return.
⸻
2 | THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECT: SPAIN’S REGULATORY CONFLICT IS PRODUCING A NATURAL EXPERIMENT
This development comes at a fascinating moment.
Spain is simultaneously tightening the discussion around data-center grid access, renewable additionality and economic value creation while private infrastructure capital continues to enter the Madrid market.
That means the relevant question is no longer simply:
Will regulation stop investment?
It becomes:
Which type of investment survives the new regulatory environment?
A plausible selection mechanism is beginning to emerge:
SPECULATIVE GRID RESERVATIONS → greater difficulty
while
CAPITALIZED + PERMITTED + EXECUTABLE PROJECTS → continue progressing.
If that pattern persists, Spain could ultimately end up with fewer headline GW but a higher percentage reaching operation.
That would validate our Capacity Conversion Velocity thesis.
The empirical test over the next year will therefore be:
Operating MW / Announced MW
If the denominator shrinks but the numerator rises faster, the system has become more productive rather than less.
⸻
3 | MADRID IS BECOMING AN AI INFRASTRUCTURE CLUSTER, NOT A COLLECTION OF DATA CENTERS
The deeper consequence is geographic.
One project creates capacity.
Many projects create an ecosystem.
Madrid’s accumulation of operating and constructing facilities begins generating circular effects:
DATA CENTERS ↑ → FIBER DENSITY ↑ → ENERGY INFRASTRUCTURE ↑ → SPECIALIZED CONSTRUCTION ↑ → TECHNICAL LABOR POOL ↑ → SUPPLIER DENSITY ↑ → TIME-TO-BUILD ↓ → MORE CAPITAL ATTRACTED
This is industrial clustering applied to accelerated computing.
And once the flywheel begins, it becomes increasingly difficult for a competing geography to replicate quickly.
That is why today’s €800 million matters beyond Nabiax itself.
Madrid is accumulating execution capability.
⸻
4 | THE NEW SCARCE ASSET: TIME-TO-POWER
Europe increasingly has capital.
It increasingly has demand.
It even has announced projects.
The scarce variable is becoming:
TIME-TO-POWER
An AI infrastructure investor increasingly asks four questions in sequence:When can I obtain grid capacity? When can construction begin? When can accelerators arrive? When can customer revenue start?
The gap between those dates determines capital efficiency.
Every additional year between committed equity and operating compute:
**raises financing cost
delays cash generation
increases technology-obsolescence risk
increases the probability that competing capacity enters first.**
The Gigafactory race is therefore partly becoming a competition in calendar time.
⸻
5 | THIS CHANGES THE MEANING OF EUROPE’S €30+ BILLION GIGAFACTORY PROGRAMME
The EuroHPC tender can support up to seven AI Gigafactories, with as much as €10 billion of EU and national support intended to mobilize at least €20 billion of private investment. Selected projects are expected to begin operating within roughly 18 months of selection.
That timetable is extraordinarily demanding.
And it creates a significant advantage for consortia that already control:
LAND + GRID CONNECTION + PERMITS + ENGINEERING + CAPITAL + DATA-CENTER OPERATING EXPERIENCE.
The tender may therefore favor not merely the consortium with the strongest political or technological narrative, but the consortium with the largest amount of pre-solved infrastructure.
Nabiax’s construction milestone illustrates why existing data-center ecosystems matter so much.
⸻
6 | ITALY — A DIFFERENT FORM OF AI VALUE CAPTURE IS APPEARING
There is also a useful Italian industrial signal today.
Italian cable group Prysmian has partnered with Rio Tinto to supply low-carbon aluminum electrical cabling for an Amazon data-center project in Ohio, using aluminum produced through ELYSIS inert-anode technology.
The project is outside Europe, so it does not change today’s European Gigafactory capacity map.
But it illustrates an important second-order opportunity for Italy.
AI infrastructure requires enormous volumes of:
cables transformers switchgear cooling power electronics construction engineering storage grid equipment.
Europe does not need to own every GPU to capture substantial value from the AI Supercycle.
European industrial companies can become suppliers to the global physical AI stack.
That broadens the European value-capture equation:
**COMPUTE OWNERSHIP
ENERGY INFRASTRUCTURE
ELECTRICAL EQUIPMENT
INDUSTRIAL ENGINEERING
SOFTWARE
APPLICATIONS**
The infrastructure supply chain itself may become one of Europe’s largest AI profit pools.

↓
BANKABLE INTELLIGENCE

BANKABLE INTELLIGENCE ECOSYSTEM

Today we should add another layer:
EXECUTION VELOCITY
Because none of the previous variables matter economically until capital becomes operational compute.
The complete equation increasingly looks like:
AI INFRASTRUCTURE VALUE = BANKABLE MW × CAPACITY CONVERSION VELOCITY × UTILIZATION × VALUE CAPTURE
This is becoming the core analytical framework for the European race.
⸻
THE VELOCITY INSIGHT
The €800 million Nabiax project matters because Europe’s AI infrastructure race is gradually moving from PowerPoint to concrete.
The decisive competition will not be won by the country that announces the most gigawatts.
Nor necessarily by the country offering the largest subsidy.
It will be won by the ecosystems that can repeatedly execute this chain:
CAPITAL → POWER → PERMISSION → CONSTRUCTION → COMPUTE → CUSTOMERS → CASH FLOW
Madrid today supplied another piece of evidence that execution itself is becoming a competitive advantage.
And this gives us the next metric for the Watch:
AI INFRASTRUCTURE EXECUTION VELOCITY
Operating MW added per year ÷ Bankable MW in the pipeline
That number may ultimately tell us more about Europe’s AI competitiveness than almost any headline investment announcement.

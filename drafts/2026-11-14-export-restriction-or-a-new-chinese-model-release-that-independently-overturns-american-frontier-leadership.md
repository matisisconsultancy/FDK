THE FINAL TAKE
The next contest in the AI Supercycle is not simply who builds the most capable model. It is who can produce and distribute intelligence at the lowest sustainable economic cost while preserving control over its computing infrastructure.
The American advantage remains particularly significant where frontier training depends on restricted advanced accelerators.
China’s opportunity lies in turning increasingly capable models into widely accessible economic infrastructure.
But the new evidence raises a fundamental question: can China sustain its cost advantage if access to American computing becomes materially more expensive or constrained?
America controls critical computing capabilities. China is challenging the price of intelligence. The next great market rotation will depend on which advantage proves more durable.

THE BIG TAKE
New technical evidence suggests that China’s progress in frontier AI remains more dependent on American accelerated computing than its success in lower-cost models might imply.
In a November 12 investigation, reported more widely on November 13 and examined further on November 14, former OpenAI researcher Will DePue identified hardware fingerprints compatible with Nvidia’s restricted Blackwell processors in six Chinese open-weight AI models. Unemployment Labs +2
The findings cover four developers:
Chinese AI developer Models identified
DeepSeek V4-Flash, V4-Pro
Moonshot AI Kimi K3, K3.1-Pro
MiniMax M3
Ant Group Ling-3.1-flash
The researcher reproduced key initialization patterns on physical Blackwell hardware. However, this establishes evidence about how the models’ initial weights were generated—not conclusive proof of where or on which hardware their full training occurred. The findings do not establish illegal exports, and full independent replication remains outstanding.
That distinction is essential. The research nevertheless provides a substantial new technical basis for investigating the computing infrastructure behind China’s leading models.
WHY IT CHANGES THE ANALYSIS
The emerging competition has three layers:
American computing leadership
Nvidia’s advanced accelerators remain strategically important to some leading Chinese model developers.
Chinese model efficiency and diffusion
Open-weight releases, competitive pricing and adaptable deployments allow Chinese AI to compete internationally.
The emerging financial contradiction
China can expand the consumption of inexpensive intelligence while remaining exposed to scarce, expensive American computing capacity.
This directly strengthens our governing thesis: the United States leads in critical frontier-computing infrastructure, while China challenges the economics and diffusion of intelligence.
It also exposes a potential vulnerability. If Washington restricts offshore computing access, Chinese model developers could experience higher training costs and deployment constraints.
Reuters reported on November 13 that Southeast Asian data-center operators anticipate greater regulatory scrutiny. Malaysia has approved approximately $94.7 billion in data-center-related investments since 2021, making the potential effects on regional infrastructure financing consequential. Reuters
A SECOND DEVELOPMENT — ALIBABA’S QWEN4
Alibaba’s November 11 release of Qwen4-Max-Preview adds another relevant signal.
The model supports multimodal reasoning, agentic tasks, tool calling and a one-million-token context window. It remains a limited preview through November 25, without sufficient independently validated evidence to establish a change in global frontier rankings. help.aliyun.com
This is evidence of continuing Chinese product development, but not yet a separate decisive inflection.

The decisive tests are whether independent researchers reproduce the Blackwell findings, whether the U.S. enacts new controls on remotely accessed computing, and whether Huawei-based systems demonstrate competitive cost per successfully completed task at industrial scale.

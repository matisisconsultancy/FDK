The AI supercycle is lowering the cost of intelligence — while raising the price of capital needed to build it.
The strongest overnight signal did not come from Silicon Valley. It came from the Bank of Japan.
Deputy Governor Shinichi Uchida said this morning that AI has become a major positive demand shock, boosting investment and asset prices, potentially lifting the natural rate of interest. He also warned that massive AI-related bond issuance is already putting upward pressure on long-term yields — and that markets could correct if expected profits fail to arrive. 
Markets are starting the week inside that contradiction. The probability of an October Fed hike has fallen below 20%, yet long yields remain elevated. The euro has fallen to a 17-month low near $1.116, France’s 10-year spread over Germany has moved above 150 bps, and Japan’s 30-year yield has reached a record 4.235%. 
The monetary cycle may be pausing. The capital cycle is not.
THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
What if AI lowers the cost of intelligence — but raises the global cost of capital first?
That is the emerging divide.
The AI boom increases productivity potential, but before those gains arrive it requires enormous amounts of:
capital → electricity → chips → construction → debt → industrial capacity
That creates an inflationary and capital-intensive first phase before the deflationary productivity phase can dominate.
The Great Split is therefore evolving again:
not simply between AI winners and losers — but between economies and companies able to finance acceleration and those crowded out by its cost.

FIVE SIGNALS SHAPING THE NEXT ECONOMY
1 | AI IS BECOMING A MACROECONOMIC RATE SHOCK
VERIFIED. The BOJ now explicitly identifies AI as a significant positive demand shock. Uchida said stronger investment, asset prices and capital accumulation could affect the natural interest rate, while heavy AI corporate bond issuance is already exerting upward pressure on long-term yields. 
FDK READ.
This is a critical confirmation.
The first-order effect of AI is not yet deflation.
It is capital formation.
AI requires extraordinary spending before productivity gains diffuse through the economy. That means more demand for money, power, labour, grids and physical assets.
The sequence is becoming clearer:
AI INVESTMENT → HIGHER CAPITAL DEMAND → HIGHER LONG RATES → STRONGER PRODUCTIVITY LATER
The consequence is uncomfortable for markets:
AI can be bullish for growth and simultaneously bearish for duration.
That distinction will increasingly decide valuation.

2 | FRANCE IS TURNING SOVEREIGN RISK INTO EURO RISK
VERIFIED. The euro fell roughly 0.7% this morning to a 17-month low as French fiscal concerns intensified. France’s risk premium over Germany is above 150 basis points, around its highest since the euro-area debt crisis, while French equities and banks have already materially underperformed Europe. 
FDK READ.
France has moved beyond a domestic bond story.
The transmission chain is now:
FISCAL CREDIBILITY → SOVEREIGN SPREAD → BANK FUNDING → EURO → CORPORATE COST OF CAPITAL
That makes France today’s most important European market test.
If the spread widens while the euro continues falling, markets will be signalling that sovereign fragmentation is becoming a continental financing problem.
Europe’s strategic weakness is therefore becoming brutally visible.It wants to finance AI, defence, grids and industrial renewal precisely when sovereign capital is getting more expensive.


⸻


3 | INDUSTRIAL AI IS MOVING FROM CAPEX TO CONTROL
VERIFIED. Schneider Electric confirmed this morning that it will acquire U.S. industrial-software group PTC for $22.6 billion in equity value, or $23.7 billion enterprise value, paying a 42.3% premium. The transaction will use equity and new debt. Schneider is already one of the central suppliers of power distribution, cooling and racks for data centres. 
Separately, OpenAI and Synopsys have agreed to build a specialized model for semiconductor design, with revenue sharing tied to improved chip-design outcomes. 
FDK READ.
This is the next stage of economic capture.
The strongest industrial companies are not merely adopting AI.
They are buying the software layer around their physical assets.
Schneider’s logic is particularly important:
POWER + DATA CENTRES + INDUSTRIAL SOFTWARE + AI
That is vertical integration for the Industrial-AI era.
Capital should increasingly reward companies able to control both the physical bottleneck and the intelligence layer operating on top of it.

4 | $100 OIL IS ACCELERATING THE MOBILITY TRANSITION
VERIFIED. Brent was around $101.59 early Monday despite higher Middle Eastern crude exports and the G7 decision to release 100 million barrels from emergency stocks. 
At the same time, EU electric-vehicle sales reached 1.64 million units in January–August, up 45% year on year, lifting EV market share to 22%. Diesel prices have risen about 38% since the Iran conflict began. 
FDK READ.
The second-order effect matters more than the oil headline.
The energy shock raises inflation today while accelerating substitution tomorrow.
The chain is now:
HIGH OIL → HIGHER TRANSPORT COST → BETTER EV ECONOMICS → MORE ELECTRIFICATION → MORE GRID DEMAND
That shifts capital toward batteries, charging, storage, power electronics and urban electric mobility.
The mobility transition is increasingly being driven not simply by regulation — but by relative economics.

5 | BANKING IS SPLITTING BETWEEN BALANCE-SHEET SCALE AND PLATFORM SCALE
VERIFIED. Intesa Sanpaolo has added another €800 million of cash to its offer for Monte dei Paschi, whose shareholders vote on October 29 over alternative bids for Banco BPM and Banca Generali. Intesa’s MPS transaction is valued around €34 billion. 
At the other end of the model, Revolut is privately valued at $115 billion, has about 80 million customers and generated £1.7 billion of pretax profit in 2025 — despite lending far less than traditional banks. 
FDK READ.
European banking is developing two competing scale models.
One is balance-sheet concentration.
The other is software-driven distribution scale.
Both attack the same weakness: fragmentation.
That means the banks most exposed are those caught in the middle — neither large enough to dominate capital-intensive businesses nor digital enough to scale distribution at very low marginal cost.
The European Banking Union is increasingly being built through transactions and technology rather than regulation alone.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN
Three apparently separate developments are converging.
Japan is warning that AI may lift the natural rate of interest.
France is discovering that sovereign credibility determines corporate financing capacity.
Schneider is borrowing and issuing equity to control more of the Industrial-AI stack.
The common mechanism is simple:
THE AI SUPERCYCLE IS BECOMING A COMPETITION FOR CAPITAL ITSELF.
This is the first phase of the new regime.
Technology demand pushes investment higher.
Investment raises demand for capital and power.
Higher funding costs then force markets to discriminate more aggressively between projects capable of generating economic returns and those financed primarily by expectations.
That is why the next rotation should not be read as AI versus non-AI.
It will be:
productive AI capital versus speculative AI capital.

One additional market is likely to command attention today: Brazil. Flavio Bolsonaro unexpectedly led Lula 47% to 45% in Sunday’s first round, setting up an October 25 runoff. Markets had already associated Bolsonaro’s improving prospects with a more business-friendly policy outlook. 
The broader investment rule:
Follow the capital to where scarcity becomes cash flow.

BEFORE THE CLOSE
Investors: watch the France–Germany 10-year spread first. Above 150 bps, every additional widening increasingly matters for the euro and European banks. Also watch whether technology equities rally while long yields remain high. That combination would confirm that AI is simultaneously easing equity financial conditions and tightening debt financial conditions.
Executives: watch Schneider. The PTC acquisition is an early template for the Industrial-AI consolidation cycle: physical infrastructure businesses buying software and intelligence rather than building everything internally.
Boards: move the hurdle-rate discussion to the centre of strategy. Projects approved under structurally cheaper capital assumptions need to be stress-tested against a world in which AI investment itself keeps long-term rates higher.
European policymakers: today’s French and German services/composite PMIs and euro-area producer-price data matter because weak growth plus persistent prices would deepen Europe’s central dilemma: investment needs rising faster than its capacity to finance them.

FINAL TAKE — THE GREAT SPLIT ENTERS THE COST OF CAPITAL
For three years, markets treated AI principally as a technology revolution.
It is becoming something larger.
AI is now influencing electricity markets, sovereign yields, corporate debt issuance, industrial M&A, banking scale and monetary-policy thinking.
That means the decisive variable is changing.
The first advantage was intelligence.
The second was compute.
The next is the ability to finance both without destroying returns.
The Great Split will increasingly separate companies and countries that can convert expensive capital into productivity from those that merely accumulate expensive assets.
AI may eventually make the economy cheaper to run.
Before it does, it is making the future more expensive to build.
That is the rate AI built.

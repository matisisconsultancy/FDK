3. United Kingdom — AI-related activity contributes to measurable economic growth
The UK’s Office for National Statistics reported third-quarter real GDP growth of 0.7%, compared with 0.5% in the second quarter.
Services expanded 0.9%, while information and communication increased 2.1%.
Computer programming, consultancy and related activities grew 4.4%.
Manufacturing expanded 0.9%, supported in part by computer, electronic and optical products.
However, construction contracted 0.7%, and business investment increased only 0.4%.
The ONS identified AI- and cloud-related activity among the contributors to growth but cautioned that its data could not isolate the precise AI contribution. Office for National Statistics
This is one of today’s strongest new national Enterprise Transformation signals.
It provides evidence that technological investment is beginning to affect measured economic output beyond the technology suppliers themselves.
But the relatively modest increase in business investment raises a further question: can this expansion develop into sustained, broad-based productive capital formation?
GVI decision: Positive high-confidence UK economic momentum, with moderate confidence in persistence. Update underlying GDP and sector indicators; withhold a numerical country-score increase until normalisation and persistence tests can be applied.

VII. SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS — THE NEXT CAPITAL ROTATION
The November 13 evidence reveals three potential developments.
First, technological diversification may become increasingly valuable. Nvidia remains central to accelerated computing, but the market response to its leadership change suggests renewed investor attention to alternative architectures and equipment suppliers.
Second, productive investment could remain resilient despite equity-market turbulence. Applied Materials and Cerebras provide evidence of sustained demand. Whether that demand supports broader productivity depends on deployment, capital costs and operational returns.
Third, the geography of AI value creation may broaden. Britain’s latest GDP data demonstrate how technology-related services can contribute to national output. The next test is whether such growth spreads into manufacturing, business investment and more sustainable productivity gains.

II. THE FOUR DEVELOPMENTS THAT MATTER
1. United States — Nvidia’s leadership rupture tests institutional resilience
On November 12, Nvidia formally announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as chief executive, president and board member with immediate effect.
Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress became interim CEO. Harvey C. Jones was appointed the company’s first board chair, and a search for a permanent successor began.
Nvidia stated that it expected operations, customer commitments and its technology roadmap to continue without interruption. That is management’s stated expectation, not a verified future outcome. NVIDIA Announces CEO Transition
The succession follows reports and investigations involving Huang’s personal relationships and potential national-security concerns. Allegations concerning foreign-intelligence involvement remain allegations; no compromise of Nvidia’s technology or confidential information should be presumed.
The financial reaction was significant but differentiated.
Nvidia shares declined 4.3% on November 12 after falling as much as 14% intraday. Meanwhile, AMD advanced 5.9% and Broadcom increased 5.5%.

DeepSeek and Huawei are turning technological constraint into a sovereign AI stack.
30 September 2026 | FDK
The Big Take
China’s pivotal AI advance is no longer a better model or a better chip. It is the beginning of an alternative intelligence-production system.
DeepSeek has released programming infrastructure optimized for Huawei’s Ascend processors, including an Ascend-compatible version of TileLang and compute and communications libraries. DeepSeek and Huawei have also optimized a 128-chip Ascend 950 supernode.
The significance goes far beyond DeepSeek.
Nvidia’s moat has never been the GPU alone. It is:
GPU × CUDA × LIBRARIES × DEVELOPERS × INSTALLED BASE
China has spent years trying to reduce dependence on the first variable.
It is now attacking the other four.
The race moves from:
Chip vs. Chip
to:
STACK vs. STACK.
⸻
From Substitution to System Formation
Until now, China’s AI response remained fragmented.
Huawei had increasingly capable accelerators—but not CUDA.
DeepSeek had efficient models—but Chinese frontier labs still depended significantly on American compute.
Domestic hardware existed—but migration costs remained high.
Those pieces are beginning to connect:
DeepSeek models
→ Huawei Ascend
→ SuperPoD / UnifiedBus
→ CANN + open programming tools
→ lower migration friction
→ more developers
→ more workloads
→ better optimization
→ more Ascend adoption.
Huawei says Ascend now supports more than 90 major open-source projects, over 40 models have been natively pretrained on Ascend/CANN, and CANN has more than 5,200 monthly active developers. These are Huawei-reported ecosystem metrics, but they reveal the direction of travel.
China is moving from component substitution to ecosystem formation.
That is the pivotal advance.
⸻
The Second-Order Effects
1. China does not need Nvidia parity. It needs Nvidia sufficiency.
This is the most important distinction.
Huawei does not need Ascend to outperform Nvidia’s best accelerator on every benchmark.
CANN does not need to replace CUDA globally.
China needs its combined system to become sufficiently capable that dependence on the American stack stops being economically prohibitive.
The relevant metric therefore changes from:
GPU performance
to:
USEFUL INTELLIGENCE / TOTAL SYSTEM COST
A weaker accelerator combined with better interconnect, more efficient software and a DeepSeek-style sparse model can still deliver competitive economics across large categories of inference.
That lowers the technological hurdle dramatically.
⸻
2. Export controls may be accelerating the architecture they were designed to constrain
U.S. restrictions still impose real costs. China remains behind in leading-edge fabrication, frontier accelerators and software maturity.
But constraints change incentives.
The recursive chain is increasingly:
Export controls
→ scarcity
→ higher returns to substitution
→ domestic software investment
→ lower switching costs
→ greater domestic adoption
→ larger ecosystem
→ lower dependency.
The strategic question for Washington therefore becomes harder:
At what point does restricting access preserve the frontier gap while accelerating the creation of an alternative system beneath it?
The two effects can coexist.
⸻
3. DeepSeek is becoming infrastructure
This may be today’s most underestimated development.
DeepSeek began as the symbol of Chinese model efficiency.
Its strategic function is becoming broader.
By helping adapt programming tools and workloads to Huawei hardware, DeepSeek can become:
THE SOFTWARE BRIDGE BETWEEN CHINESE INTELLIGENCE AND CHINESE COMPUTE.
That creates a powerful recursive relationship.
DeepSeek improves Ascend economics.
Ascend reduces DeepSeek’s dependence on U.S. infrastructure.
More deployment creates more optimization.
More optimization makes the Chinese stack more competitive.
DeepSeek therefore matters increasingly not simply as a model company, but as part of China’s technology architecture.
⸻
4. The Great Split becomes self-reinforcing
The U.S.ecosystem compounds through its installed base:
More Nvidia
→ more CUDA developers
→ better software
→ more applications
→ greater Nvidia demand.
China is attempting to create the same mechanism:
More Ascend
→ more CANN developers
→ better optimization
→ lower migration friction
→ more workloads
→ greater Ascend demand.
Once the second loop reaches sufficient scale, technological decoupling stops being driven primarily by policy.
It becomes economically endogenous.
That is much harder to reverse.
⸻
5. Model efficiency becomes a geopolitical asset
DeepSeek’s contribution is particularly important because efficient models reduce the amount of frontier hardware required to produce useful intelligence.
The chain becomes:
Model efficiency ↑
→ compute required per task ↓
→ domestic hardware viability ↑
→ inference cost ↓
→ deployment ↑.
China is therefore attacking the U.S. compute advantage from both sides:
Huawei increases the effective supply of domestic compute.
DeepSeek reduces the amount of compute required.
This is the deeper strategic logic.
China is not simply trying to produce more chips.
It is trying to reduce the strategic value of each missing American chip.
⸻
The Global Implication
A credible Chinese stack would not remain purely Chinese.
Many countries and enterprises do not require the absolute frontier.
They require intelligence that is:
capable, inexpensive, deployable, customizable and sovereign.
That creates the possibility of two increasingly distinct global offers:
U.S. STACK
**Maximum capability
Nvidia/CUDA
hyperscale cloud
frontier agents
premium economics**
CHINESE STACK
**Sufficient capability
open models
domestic compute
lower system cost
sovereign deployment
industrial integration**
The Chinese proposition could become particularly attractive across emerging markets and countries seeking greater technological optionality.
The Great Split would then move from U.S.–China decoupling toward global stack competition.
⸻
Why This Matters for the AI Supercycle
This is strongly expansionary.
The United States continues building:
Nvidia + HBM + networking + hyperscalers + AI factories + power.
China increasingly builds:
Huawei + domestic memory + networking + CANN + DeepSeek + sovereign AI factories + power.
The result is not infrastructure substitution.
It is infrastructure duplication.
TWO STACKS → TWO CAPITAL CYCLES
More accelerators.
More memory.
More networking.
More optics.
More data centers.
More electricity.
More software investment.
Meanwhile, competition between the two systems drives down the cost of useful intelligence.
That gives us the defining Supercycle paradox:
INTELLIGENCE COST ↓
DEPLOYMENT ↑
INFRASTRUCTURE DUPLICATION ↑
TOTAL AI CAPEX ↑
Technological fragmentation can therefore be capital-formation inflationary even while intelligence itself becomes profoundly deflationary.
⸻
KEY TAKEAWAYS
1. China’s pivotal advance is system-level.
The breakthrough is the connection of model + compute + interconnect + software + developers.
2. America still owns the stronger frontier.
China has not achieved Nvidia/CUDA parity. Advanced fabrication and frontier compute remain substantial U.S.-aligned advantages.
3. But China no longer needs parity everywhere.
It needs a sufficiently capable stack at a competitive total system cost.
4. DeepSeek is becoming strategic infrastructure.
Its role increasingly extends from model efficiency to making Chinese compute economically usable.
5. Export controls have a second-order consequence.
They constrain Chinese capability today while increasing the return on investment from substitution tomorrow.
6. Watch adoption, not announcements.
The critical metrics now become CANN developers, native Ascend workloads, utilization, migration rates and cost per completed task.
7. The AI Supercycle gets larger.
Two increasingly independent intelligence systems require two enormous physical infrastructure stacks.
⸻
THE VELOCITY INSIGHT
The old Chinese objective was:
Replace Nvidia.
The new strategy is far more sophisticated:
MAKE NVIDIA LESS NECESSARY.That is why today’s DeepSeek–Huawei development matters.
America still leads the frontier.
But China is beginning to assemble a system capable of compounding beneath that frontier without depending on it.
And once models, chips, interconnect, software and developers begin reinforcing one another, the strategic contest changes permanently.
It is no longer:
WHO HAS THE BEST CHIP?
It becomes:
WHO CAN PRODUCE THE MOST USEFUL INTELLIGENCE PER DOLLAR, WATT AND UNIT OF COMPUTE?
That is a much larger battlefield.
And it extends directly into agents, robotics, autonomous vehicles, manufacturing, defense and Physical AI.
AMERICA STILL LEADS THE FRONTIER.
CHINA IS BUILDING THE SECOND AI STACK.
The Great Split has moved from technology access to technology architecture.
And that makes the AI Supercycle larger, more physical—and increasingly irreversible.

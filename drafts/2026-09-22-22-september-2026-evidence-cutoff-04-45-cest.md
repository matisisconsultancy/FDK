United States — AI moves upstream into power
Google and Georgia Power have agreed to finance upgrades at the Vogtle and Hatch nuclear plants that could add approximately 96 MW of generation. The architecture matters more than its national scale:
AI demand → hyperscaler contract → nuclear uprate → incremental electricity capacity.
This is strong Intelligence Infrastructure evidence, but the 96 MW scale is insufficient for another US country-score increase. Regulatory approval is also still required.
US momentum nevertheless improves at the margin because lower oil and Treasury yields are reducing the immediate financial pressure on AI infrastructure. The Nasdaq’s record close and 4.3% semiconductor rally provide market confirmation of that repricing, but equity prices themselves do not alter the structural GVI. 
Europe — efficiency becomes part of the production function
The European Commission has proposed a common efficiency-label system for data centres of at least 500 kW. Operators would report energy and water efficiency, local water stress and their ability to support energy systems through measures such as waste-heat reuse. 
The underlying numbers are significant:
EU data-centre capacity: ~12 GW in 2025 → ~28 GW expected by 2030.
Data centres already consume approximately 2.5% of EU electricity. 
No national score receives points because this remains an EU-wide proposal.
But it introduces an important GVI concept:
The relevant metric is shifting from installed compute toward productive intelligence per unit of electricity, water and grid capacity.

Institutional interpretation — 
The Efficiency Constraint
The fundamental question is becoming:
How much economic intelligence can a system produce from each marginal unit of energy, capital and physical infrastructure?
The AI race is migrating through a sequence of bottlenecks:
Accelerated Computing → Memory → Optical Bandwidth → Power → Grid → Capital → Efficiency.
The technological frontier continues to advance.
But the economic frontier increasingly depends on system efficiency.
The US power agreement illustrates hyperscalers moving upstream into generation. Europe is beginning to measure energy and water efficiency explicitly. Spain is trying to combine compute, sovereign models and environmental constraints. Markets immediately reward AI again when oil and bond yields retreat. 
For investors, the premium should migrate toward permitted, powered, contracted and highly utilised capacity—and toward bottlenecks such as memory, optics, transformers, grids, cooling and energy.
For executives, the crucial KPI is becoming:
economic value created per megawatt of intelligence deployed.
For policymakers, AI competitiveness can no longer be separated from energy policy. The objective is not simply more data centres. It is more productive intelligence without destabilising grids, water systems or household energy costs.

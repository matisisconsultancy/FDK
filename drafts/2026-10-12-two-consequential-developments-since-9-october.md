EUROPE’S AI RACE: THE €7 BILLION OFFTAKE BREAKTHROUGH

Rotterdam secures its first major AI customer. Europe’s telecoms seek to unlock €30 billion in investment capacity.

THE BIG TAKE

Europe’s AI capital-formation race has reached another turning point: customer commitments are becoming the critical bridge between infrastructure ambition and bankable investment.

Today’s developments reveal two complementary shifts.

In Rotterdam, a European AI cloud company has agreed in principle to become the first major customer of a proposed €7 billion AI Gigafactory.

Meanwhile, Europe’s telecommunications industry is pressing Brussels to reform spectrum licensing, potentially releasing €30 billion over ten years for digital-network investment.

Neither announcement represents €7 billion or €30 billion of newly committed capital.

But both are consequential for how Europe finances its next generation of digital infrastructure.

⸻

1 | NETHERLANDS — NEBIUS BECOMES ROTTERDAM’S FIRST MAJOR AI CUSTOMER

12 October 2026

Amsterdam-headquartered Nebius has signed a letter of intent with Dutch developer VOLT to acquire a substantial portion of the computing capacity planned for Rotterdam’s AI Gigafactory.

VOLT’s proposed campus now targets 600 MW, with an initial development phase comprising two 100-MW facilities.

The first phase carries an estimated investment requirement of €7 billion, divided into €2.5 billion for buildings and infrastructure and €4.5 billion for computing equipment.

Construction is not yet financed, and the definitive commercial arrangements remain outstanding. VOLT is targeting construction from 2028. 

Why this matters

Until now, European Gigafactory proposals have largely emphasized sites, technology partners, public financing and energy infrastructure.

Rotterdam introduces a more commercially meaningful dimension:

Identifiable demand before financial close.

A credible long-term customer can improve projected utilization, provide greater revenue visibility and potentially support project-finance underwriting.

However, the Nebius letter of intent is not yet a binding, fully priced offtake contract. Its ultimate value depends on final contractual terms.

The proposed power architecture is also significant.

VOLT intends to use offshore wind electricity near its landing point in Rotterdam’s port, potentially reducing additional pressure on congested inland transmission infrastructure. The full project’s power arrangements and permits remain important execution risks. 

THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECT

AI infrastructure is beginning to resemble project finance with technology risk attached.

The financing sequence is becoming:

ANCHOR DEMAND → CONTRACTED REVENUE → FINANCING → COMPUTE DEPLOYMENT → ADDITIONAL CUSTOMERS

For investors, the decisive questions shift toward counterparty creditworthiness, contract duration, accelerator utilization and equipment depreciation.

For CEOs and boards, the competitive advantage increasingly lies in securing customers before committing billions to infrastructure.

For policymakers, Rotterdam demonstrates why sovereign-AI programmes need commercially credible demand, not simply public funding.

The qualification: Rotterdam has crossed an important commercial threshold, but it has not yet secured financial close.

2 | EUROPEAN TELECOMS — A €30 BILLION CAPITAL-ALLOCATION OPPORTUNITY

12 October 2026

The GSMA formally renewed its call for European spectrum-licensing reform as EU telecommunications ministers gathered in Limerick.

Its proposal advocates longer-term or indefinite spectrum rights and more predictable renewals.

The industry estimates that reform could release up to €30 billion over the next decade, currently associated with spectrum-related costs.

This is an estimate of potential savings, not an approved funding programme.

The proposal feeds into discussions surrounding the EU Digital Networks Act.No binding legislative decision has been taken. 

Why this matters for Industrial AI

European telecommunications operators face competing investment requirements: fiber, 5G, edge computing, cybersecurity, sovereign cloud and enterprise AI.

Spectrum reform could potentially release capital for these investments.

The second-order effect would be particularly important for operators such as Telefónica, Orange, Deutsche Telekom and TIM.

A stronger investment capacity could improve their ability to participate in Industrial AI infrastructure while maintaining financial discipline.

However, there is no assurance that prospective spectrum savings would be allocated to AI rather than network modernization, debt reduction or shareholder distributions.

For investors: the opportunity lies in improved free cash flow and capital allocation, not automatic AI investment.

For boards: the challenge is to translate regulatory savings into productivity and recurring revenue.

For policymakers: spectrum policy can influence Europe’s AI competitiveness, but the allocation of released capital will ultimately depend on company economics.

THE COMPETITIVE READ-THROUGH

Today’s developments strengthen the case for adding a sixth model to Europe’s emerging AI infrastructure architecture.

France — Stack integration. The challenge is to convert sovereign technology capabilities into sufficient commercial demand.

Spain — Capital formation. Rotterdam reinforces the importance of securing long-term customers alongside Spain’s existing public-private financing architecture.

Germany — Industrial demand. The opportunity remains substantial, but industrial customers must increasingly translate AI ambitions into commercially committed workloads.

Italy — Industrial conversion. Italy’s potential advantage in Industrial AI will matter only if its Gigafactory proposal can demonstrate actual customer demand, secured power and financeable project economics.

Nordics — Physical infrastructure. Finland’s recent announcements demonstrate its attractiveness to developers. Rotterdam shows that access to power must ultimately be matched with contracted demand.

Netherlands — Commercial validation. The Nebius–VOLT agreement is a meaningful step toward connecting AI infrastructure development with a prospective revenue base.

No comparably material new Gigafactory-specific financing, site, consortium or construction commitment was verified today for Spain, France, Germany, Italy or the Nordics.

⸻

FIVE KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. Contracted demand is becoming a competitive differentiator. A credible customer commitment can materially improve a Gigafactory’s financing prospects.

2. Europe’s AI financing architecture is evolving. The central question is shifting from how much equity can be raised to how reliably future computing revenues can support investment.

3. Power remains essential, but it is insufficient. Secured electricity must translate into utilized computing capacity and recurring revenue.

4. European telecoms face a major capital-allocation decision. Potential spectrum savings could strengthen investment capacity, but their economic benefits will depend on deployment discipline.

5. Investors should distinguish commercial progress from financial completion. Rotterdam has an anchor customer in principle. It still needs definitive contracts, financing, permits and execution.

⸻

THE CLOSING VERDICT

Today’s Rotterdam development offers an important lesson for the European AI Gigafactory race.

The critical missing asset is not always capital. It is demand sufficiently credible to attract capital.

The next European winners will need to demonstrate that computing capacity can be sold at prices and utilization levels capable of supporting infrastructure investment and rapid technological replacement.

For governments, the challenge is to create the conditions for bankable projects.

For corporate boards, it is to establish a credible relationship between investment and revenue.For investors, it is to distinguish productive infrastructure from speculative capacity.

Europe’s AI race is moving beyond the announcement economy.

THE WINNERS WILL NOT BE THOSE WHO RAISE THE MOST CAPITAL. THEY WILL BE THOSE WHO MAKE CAPITAL PRODUCTIVE FASTEST.

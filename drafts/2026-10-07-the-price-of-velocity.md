Wall Street is at record highs. But the two inputs that determine whether the AI supercycle can keep compounding — capital and power — are becoming structurally more expensive.
The Big Take — The most important overnight development is not another record for the S&P 500 or Nasdaq. It is the increasingly visible transformation of AI from a technology cycle into a capital-and-energy cycle. U.S. equities closed at new highs on Tuesday, yet the 10-year Treasury is again around 5.3%, Brent is above $101, Google has contracted 3.59 GW of power, SpaceX is reportedly seeking $40 billion largely to buy Nvidia processors, and Blackstone-backed AirTrunk is putting another $1 billion into a Japanese hyperscale campus. The physical economy is beginning to set the price of the digital economy. 
The market can sustain high valuations and high interest rates simultaneously only if AI converts extraordinary capital spending into extraordinary productivity and cash flow. That conversion — not model capability — is becoming the decisive financial test of the supercycle.


⸻


THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
What happens when the fastest technological acceleration in history runs into a world where neither capital nor energy is cheap anymore?
For the first phase of generative AI, access to chips created advantage.
The second phase is harder.
The winners now need access simultaneously to accelerated computing, electricity, grid capacity, financing, sovereign stability and deployment speed.
That is The Great Split entering its industrial phase.

THE FIVE OVERNIGHT SIGNALS
1. THE EQUITY MARKET IS PRICING ABUNDANCE. THE BOND MARKET IS PRICING SCARCITY.
Verified fact. The S&P 500 gained about 0.6% Tuesday and the Nasdaq 0.45%, both closing at records. Yet the U.S. 10-year Treasury moved back toward 5.3% in Asian trading. Markets now assign only about a 19–20% probability to an October Fed hike, while a $39 billion U.S. 10-year Treasury auction today will provide another direct test of global demand for duration. 
Velocity interpretation.
This is the contradiction at the center of the market.
The front end is saying:
the Fed may pause.
The long end is saying:
capital is still scarce.
The S&P is effectively assuming that AI-driven earnings growth can exceed a sovereign hurdle rate above 5%.
For a handful of firms, that may be true.
For the broader market, it is a much higher bar.
The next rotation therefore should not be framed simply as technology versus value.
It will increasingly be cash-flow conversion versus capital consumption.


⸻


2. AI HAS CROSSED THE LINE FROM TECHNOLOGY INFRASTRUCTURE INTO THE POWER SYSTEM
Verified fact. Google has signed agreements with Constellation Energy covering 3,590 MW of electricity. The structure includes a 20-year contract for 890 MW of nuclear power, while Constellation plans more than $4.3 billion of upgrades to existing reactors. A separate agreement covers another 2,700 MW in the PJM grid.Constellation shares jumped more than 13% following the announcement. 
Blackstone-backed AirTrunk separately announced another $1 billion of investment into its 300+ MW Japanese data-center campus, including large-scale liquid cooling for AI workloads. Its CEO explicitly argued that AI infrastructure deployment is getting harder in the United States and already difficult in Europe, potentially making Asia a major beneficiary. 
Velocity interpretation.
This may be the most important structural signal of the morning.
The strategic unit of AI is no longer the GPU.
It is becoming:
CHIP + POWER + GRID + COOLING + LAND + CAPITAL
Google is effectively procuring electricity on an industrial scale once associated with heavy manufacturing.
Data-center economics are therefore beginning to reshape utility economics.
And utility economics will increasingly reshape technology valuations.
That is a profound inversion.
For the past twenty years, digital companies sat on top of physical infrastructure.
For the next twenty, the largest digital companies may have to help build the physical infrastructure themselves.


⸻


3. THE AI BOOM IS BECOMING A CREDIT BOOM
Verified fact. SpaceX is reportedly seeking $40 billion of financing led by Apollo to purchase Nvidia processors: roughly $10 billion of bank loans and $30 billion of investment-grade debt. Morgan Stanley estimates AI infrastructure may require roughly $1.5 trillion of financing by 2028. 
At the same time, Marvell lifted its fiscal-2028 revenue target to around $20 billion and projects $70–90 billion of revenue by fiscal 2031. Its custom-AI-chip revenue could reach $12 billion by fiscal 2029, reflecting hyperscalers’ push to develop alternatives to Nvidia’s general-purpose accelerators. 
Velocity interpretation.
AI has entered its financing phase.
That matters enormously.
The first wave was financed mainly through the enormous cash flows of Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta.
The second wave increasingly involves:
corporate bonds
bank loans
private credit
vendor financing
infrastructure funds
asset-backed structures
long-duration power contracts.
AI is therefore migrating from corporate capex into the global credit system.
This creates scale.
But it also creates leverage.
And once leverage enters the architecture, the market must start asking a different question:
Is demand financing the infrastructure — or is financing creating the demand?
That distinction will become increasingly important in 2027.


⸻


4. ENERGY IS NO LONGER JUST AN INPUT COST. IT IS BECOMING THE SYSTEMIC CONSTRAINT
Verified fact. Brent rose to about $101.63 this morning and WTI to $90.24 as a developing Gulf of Mexico storm threatened facilities responsible for about 15% of U.S. oil production and roughly 5% of gas production. Gulf Coast states contain around half of U.S. refining capacity. At the same time, Houthi attacks on Saudi targets are preserving geopolitical risk even as Middle Eastern export volumes recover. 
Chevron CEO Mike Wirth said the underlying energy system has become more fragile and estimated that the landed physical price of oil in parts of Asia is currently closer to $150 than $100 per barrel, with fuel-market tightness exceeding what headline crude futures alone suggest. 
Velocity interpretation.
This is why $100 Brent should not be treated simply as another commodity cycle.
Energy is transmitting simultaneously into:
inflation,
central-bank policy,
transportation,
industrial margins,
data-center economics,
sovereign fiscal balances,
and ultimately the cost of capital.
The hidden variable is therefore not just oil.
It is the shrinking energy buffer of the global economy.
AI is entering its most energy-intensive phase precisely when geopolitical fragmentation is reducing the flexibility of global energy supply.
That collision is structurally inflationary.


⸻


5. THE GREAT SPLIT IS BECOMING A GEOGRAPHY OF CAPITAL
Verified fact. France’s 10-year spread over Germany narrowed to around 132 basis points after approaching 160 bps last week, helped by Marine Le Pen’s pledge to cut spending.French 10-year yields fell more than 11 basis points Tuesday. But France’s funding costs remain dramatically elevated relative to their previous regime. 
Meanwhile, the Reserve Bank of India raised its repo rate 25 basis points to 5.50%, its first increase in nearly four years, citing broadening inflation pressures amid strong growth. The RBI moved to a stance of “calibrated tightening.” 
Velocity interpretation.
These are apparently unrelated stories.
They are not.
They show the return of national capital constraints.
Countries that control energy, savings, fiscal credibility and deep capital markets will be able to sustain strategic investment.
Those that do not will increasingly pay an economic penalty through higher sovereign spreads, weaker currencies or higher rates.
The Great Split is therefore moving beyond corporations.
It is separating countries.
AI competitiveness is becoming inseparable from sovereign balance-sheet competitiveness.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN
Look at the overnight developments together:
Google commits to 3.6 GW of power.
SpaceX seeks $40 billion of financing for chips.
Marvell projects an explosion in custom silicon.
Blackstone commits another $1 billion to Japanese AI infrastructure.
Oil remains above $100.
Treasury yields remain near multi-decade highs.
France pays a growing fiscal premium.
India restarts monetary tightening.
These are not eight stories.
They are one.
THE GLOBAL ECONOMY IS REPRICING THE PHYSICAL COST OF INTELLIGENCE.
For years, software lowered marginal costs.
Industrial AI reverses part of that logic.
Every additional unit of intelligence increasingly requires:
power,
transformers,
GPUs,
memory,
networking,
cooling,
real estate,
debt,
and sovereign permission.
Artificial intelligence may ultimately be deflationary through productivity.
But the infrastructure required to create that productivity is initially capital-intensive and inflationary.
That is the two-stage regime the market is only beginning to understand.


⸻


THE GREAT SPLIT — PHASE III
The dividing line is becoming clearer.
THE FIRST GROUP — CAPITAL SOVEREIGNS
Hyperscalers, cash-rich platforms, sovereign wealth funds, infrastructure managers and companies with investment-grade access can keep expanding despite expensive capital.
THE SECOND GROUP — INFRASTRUCTURE CONTROLLERS
Utilities, grid companies, nuclear operators, semiconductor manufacturers, memory producers, networking firms and cooling specialists are acquiring pricing power because they control physical bottlenecks.
THE THIRD GROUP — CAPITAL DEPENDENTS
Corporates that need external financing before AI productivity appears face a sharply more difficult equation.
THE FOURTH GROUP — SOVEREIGN CONSTRAINED
Governments already carrying heavy debt loads face the most dangerous trade-off of all: finance the new industrial economy or defend fiscal credibility.
This is why the next AI cycle will increasingly reward balance sheets before narratives.


⸻


IMPLICATIONS
FOR INVESTORS
The most important equity distinction is shifting from AI exposure to AI economic capture.
Prefer companies that control scarce bottlenecks, convert AI investment into cash flow, possess pricing power and can finance capex internally.
Watch the 5.30–5.35% U.S. 10-year zone closely today. A weak Treasury auction would increase the discount rate applied to precisely the long-duration AI assets currently driving equity records. 
And increasingly treat utilities, grids, nuclear, storage and data-center infrastructure as part of the technology stack rather than as separate sectors.


⸻


FOR CEOs AND BOARDS
The governing AI question must change from:
“How quickly can we deploy?”
to:
“How quickly can we create measurable cash flow from every dollar deployed?”
Every major AI investment should now carry four explicit board metrics:
time to revenue
time to productivity
energy requirement
return above the sovereign hurdle rate.
At 5%+ risk-free yields, experimentation without an economic path becomes expensive very quickly.


⸻


FOR EUROPEAN POLICYMAKERSEurope’s central problem is no longer a lack of AI ambition.
It is the absence of an integrated capital-energy-compute architecture capable of competing at scale.
The Iberian Peninsula illustrates the opportunity: data-center capacity in Spain and Portugal is projected to increase rapidly toward 2030, supported by renewables, storage and power-market advantages. But grid congestion, storage requirements and permitting remain binding constraints. 
Europe cannot build an AI industrial strategy through subsidies alone.
It needs:
deeper capital markets,
continental energy infrastructure,
faster permitting,
sovereign-scale compute,
and cross-border investment vehicles.
Otherwise European savings will continue financing someone else’s acceleration.

Record equities no longer mean easy financial conditions. The cost of capital remains historically restrictive.
AI’s decisive bottleneck is moving from chips toward power and financing.
The AI supercycle is becoming a credit supercycle. Balance-sheet quality therefore becomes increasingly important.
$100 oil is not just an energy signal. It raises the hurdle rate across the entire industrial economy.
The Great Split is moving from companies to countries. Sovereign balance sheets will increasingly determine technological competitiveness.

THE VELOCITY INSIGHT
The first chapter of AI rewarded those who controlled the algorithms.
The second rewarded those who controlled the chips.
The third is beginning now.
It will reward those who control the capital, energy and physical infrastructure required to turn intelligence into economic power.
That is the strategic agenda for today.
And it gives us the clearest rule yet for this cycle:
Do not follow the technology alone. Follow who can still afford to accelerate.

The AI Supercycle is entering the financial system. The next Great Split will be between those who can fund velocity—and those forced to absorb someone else’s cost of capital.

The U.S. 10-year Treasury is around 5.25% after touching 5.344%. The dollar is near a 17-month high. European sovereign stress is widening. Brent remains around $100.

Yet AI investment keeps accelerating.

Broadcom has agreed to provide Anthropic with up to $42 billion of financing tied to compute infrastructure. Amazon is reportedly considering moving about $8 billion of Nvidia chips into an SPV and leasing them back.

These are not isolated transactions.

AI IS MOVING FROM A TECHNOLOGY CYCLE INTO A FINANCING CYCLE.

That changes who can scale, who carries the risk, and where returns ultimately accrue.

⸻

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

What if the next constraint on AI is not intelligence or compute—but the economics of financing scale?

The winners will be those able to:

secure capacity
lock in demand
protect free cash flow
refinance through the cycle

The competitive divide is moving from technology capability to financial resilience.

That reinforces demand for batteries, charging, grids and storage.

Geopolitical instability is therefore accelerating part of the energy transition.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN

The system is changing in sequence.

Scarce compute creates long-term contracts.

Contracts make infrastructure financeable.

Financing accelerates capacity.

More capacity creates more exposure to utilization, depreciation and refinancing.

AI IS EXPORTING RISK FROM TECHNOLOGY INTO FINANCE.

The likely result is not necessarily slower AI.

It is a more concentrated market, where the strongest operators can keep scaling while weaker ones lose strategic freedom.

FINAL TAKE — THE CAPITAL DIVIDE

Broadcom is committing up to $42 billion to finance Anthropic’s compute buildout. Amazon is reportedly considering an $8 billion Nvidia-chip financing vehicle. The U.S. 10-year has traded above 5.3%.

Put together, they signal a structural shift:

AI is scaling faster just as financing that scale is becoming more expensive.

Long-term compute and power commitments are turning infrastructure into financeable assets. That accelerates deployment, but also raises exposure to utilization, depreciation and refinancing.

The strongest players can convert cheaper capital into more capacity, more market share and greater strategic control.

The weakest lose optionality first.

Investors: watch who funds the buildout, who ultimately holds the risk and when refinancing arrives.

Policymakers: AI sovereignty now requires capital-market depth as well as chips and power.

The first AI race was about intelligence.

The second was about compute.

The third will be about capital discipline.

Scale alone is no longer enough.

The winners will be those who can finance it, monetize it and preserve returns through the cycle.

That is where The Great Split widens next.

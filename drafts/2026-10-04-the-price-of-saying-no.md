Intesa has turned October 29 into a decision over what MPS becomes next — and who controls the next phase of Italian banking consolidation.
4 October 2026


⸻


THE BIG TAKE
The Italian banking Risiko has crossed another threshold.
On October 3, Intesa Sanpaolo increased the cash component of its MPS offer from €1.00 to €1.25 per share — roughly €800 million of additional consideration — while keeping the 1.6 Intesa-share exchange ratio.
But the extra cash is not the decisive development.
The condition is.
Intesa has made clear that if MPS shareholders approve either defensive transaction — Banco BPM or Banca Generali — it does not intend to waive the resulting failure of the conditions attached to its offer.
October 29 therefore becomes:
**TWO VOTES.
FOUR COMBINATIONS.
THREE FUTURES FOR MPS.**
The battle has moved beyond price.
It is now about execution, irreversibility and control.


⸻


1 | THE DECISION TREE
THE VOTE IS THE STRATEGY
MPS shareholders face two separate substantive decisions:
Vote A — Banco BPM
Approve or reject the capital increase supporting the Banco BPM offer.
Vote B — Banca Generali
Approve or reject the capital increase supporting the Banca Generali offer.
Banco BPM Banca Generali Strategic outcome
NO NO Intesa path remains open
YES YES Full MPS architecture
YES NO MPS prioritises banking scale
NO YES MPS prioritises wealth and distribution
These four combinations produce three strategic scenarios:
neither passes, both pass, or exactly one passes.
The relevant qualified majority applies to shareholders voting on each resolution — not to MPS’s entire capital base.
Turnout therefore matters.
If 75% of capital were represented and voting, two-thirds would correspond to roughly 50% of total capital. At 60% participation, it would correspond to roughly 40%.
The Treasury’s roughly 4.9% stake will not participate, increasing the relative weight of private shareholders who do vote.
OCTOBER 29 IS ABOUT TURNOUT, COALITIONS AND THE ARITHMETIC OF CONTROL.


⸻


2 | THE DECISIVE ISSUE
CERTAINTY VERSUS COMPLEXITY
Intesa offers:
certainty,
simpler execution,
liquidity,
and a defined industrial path.
MPS offers:
independence,
greater scale,
wealth economics,
and materially greater execution complexity.
Banco BPM and Banca Generali are not interchangeable.
Banco BPM is a scale decision.
Banca Generali is a business-model decision.
Both together represent the most ambitious version of MPS — and the hardest to execute.
THE €800 MILLION IS THE PRICE INTESA IS WILLING TO PAY TO FORCE SHAREHOLDERS TO CHOOSE NOW.


⸻


3 | THE STRATEGIC MAP
INTESA | DISCIPLINE
Higher price. Harder condition. Credible walk-away.
Role: the consolidator with the strongest capacity to refuse bad economics.


⸻


MPS / MEDIOBANCA | EXECUTION
Mediobanca strengthened the industrial case.
BPM and Banca Generali now test whether MPS can convert ambition into returns.
THE BURDEN OF PROOF HAS SHIFTED FROM STRATEGY TO EXECUTION.


⸻


BANCO BPM | THE CLEARING ASSET
If the BPM resolution fails — or if Intesa ultimately prevails — Banco BPM immediately becomes the pivotal remaining domestic platform.
Crédit Agricole already owns 29.3%.
Role: the likely centre of the next consolidation battle.


⸻


CRÉDIT AGRICOLE | INFLUENCE
Its BPM stake provides leverage without requiring full ownership.
THE MORE EXPENSIVE CONTROL BECOMES, THE MORE VALUABLE INFLUENCE BECOMES.


⸻


UNICREDIT / COMMERZBANK | CONTINENTAL SCALE
UniCredit is playing beyond the Italian perimeter.
Commerzbank remains politically constrained, but strategically coherent.
Role: the clearest test of whether Banking Union can create genuinely European scale.


⸻


GENERALI | STRATEGIC SCARCITY
The next phase increasingly concerns savings, wealth, insurance and distribution, not just deposits and loans.
Role: the scarce financial infrastructure around which the second wave may form.


⸻


BANCA GENERALI | THE WEALTH CHOICE
Banca Generali offers a fundamentally different growth model from BPM.Role: wealth, fee income and lighter balance-sheet intensity.

Watch:

If they diverge, investors will already be pricing the split vote.
**THE MARKET IS NOT PRICING ONE DEFENCE.
IT IS PRICING THREE FUTURES.**

III | THE SPLIT VOTE
25% | Rising

BPM: NO / Banca Generali: YES

7 | BANKING UNION BY NEGOTIATION
Europe wants larger banks.
National governments still want influence over credit, headquarters, employment, savings and strategic assets.
Rome is letting shareholder arithmetic play a greater role in MPS.
Berlin is negotiating safeguards around UniCredit–Commerzbank.
Brussels and the ECB continue to favour deeper integration without weakening prudential standards.
CONTINENTAL SCALE — UNDER NATIONAL CONDITIONS.
That is the emerging operating model of Banking Union.


⸻


8 | THE HIDDEN PATTERN
WHO STILL HAS ROOM TO MOVE?
Each protagonist derives leverage from a different source:
Intesa: discipline.
UniCredit: continental scale.
Crédit Agricole: influence.
Generali: distribution scarcity.
MPS: ambition.
The advantage after this consolidation round will belong to the institutions that have not exhausted their strategic flexibility.

INVESTOR VERDICT
Three things matter now.
First, October 29 is a decision tree, not a referendum.
Two votes create four combinations and three economically distinct outcomes.
Second, the split vote is the key underpriced scenario.It would leave MPS with one strategic leg rather than the full defence — and immediately clarify whether shareholders prefer BPM’s banking scale or Banca Generali’s wealth economics.
Third, execution now dominates vision.
Intesa has converted price into pressure. MPS must demonstrate that whichever structure survives can actually deliver returns.
Banco BPM remains the likely next pivot if the defence weakens. UniCredit remains the continental counterpoint, testing whether Banking Union can produce genuinely European scale.
**THE PRICE OF SAYING NO IS KNOWN.
OCTOBER 29 WILL REVEAL WHICH STRUCTURE CAN SURVIVE.**

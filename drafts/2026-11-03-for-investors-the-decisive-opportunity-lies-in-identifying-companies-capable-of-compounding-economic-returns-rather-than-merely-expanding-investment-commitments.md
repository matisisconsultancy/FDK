For boards, the imperative is to align technological ambitions with financing durability and disciplined capital allocation.
For policymakers, the priority is preserving the capacity of the financial system to support productive investment without undermining monetary and fiscal credibility.
The coming months will determine whether the enormous financing requirements of artificial intelligence generate broader prosperity or greater financial concentration.
One principle is becoming increasingly difficult to ignore.
The future of competition will be decided not only by who controls intelligence, but by who can finance its production, capture its economic returns and reinvest those returns into the next source of advantage.
And the final consequence is even more important.
The next trillion dollars may not be won by the companies that build the most artificial intelligence. It may be won by those that can finance it most efficiently, monetize it most profitably and compound its returns most consistently.
In the Age of Velocity, intelligence creates opportunity. Capital determines capacity. But the productivity of capital determines enduring economic power.
The next great redistribution of global wealth will be decided by who can make intelligence profitable without making capital prohibitively expensive for everyone else.

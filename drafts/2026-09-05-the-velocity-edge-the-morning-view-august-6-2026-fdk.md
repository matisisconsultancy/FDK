AI Is Growing. Jobs Are Not.
The Great Split is no longer between equities and bonds. It is now between productivity and employment, profits and wages, capital and labor.
Data cut: 07:45 CEST
The overnight evidence points to a more consequential phase of the AI Supercycle.
The US services economy remains strong. New orders are accelerating. European companies are reporting record industrial profits, expanding cloud backlogs and stronger demand for enterprise AI deployment.
But employment is no longer moving at the same velocity.
The US services employment index has fallen back into contraction. Private payroll creation slowed sharply in July. Some businesses are already attributing reductions in headcount to AI implementation.
At the same time, Asian semiconductor shares are selling off despite extraordinary underlying demand, while investors continue to question whether even strong AI revenues can justify the capital intensity, duration and valuations required to produce them.
This is the next expression of The Great Split:
Output is accelerating faster than employment. Profits are accelerating faster than payrolls. Intelligence is becoming more abundant while financial and social participation in its gains becomes more selective.
⸻
The Fundamental Question
What if the AI Supercycle is already increasing output, profits and productivity faster than employment, purchasing power and the financial system’s capacity to distribute its benefits?
The market is no longer debating whether AI will produce economic value.
It is beginning to price who captures that value—and who is displaced while it is being created.

Five Signals Shaping the Day Ahead
The AI Productivity Shock Is Appearing in the Labor Market
Verified facts
The US services PMI held at 54.1 in July, while new orders rose to 57.2. Yet the employment component fell from 51.2 to 47.4, indicating contraction. ADP reported that private employers added only 44,000 jobs, down from 95,000 in June and below the 70,000 consensus forecast.
The ISM reported that some companies were reducing employment alongside AI implementation. Businesses also described strong demand for electrical equipment, longer delivery times and increasing financial stress among smaller suppliers.
FDK interpretation
This may be the first clear macroeconomic expression of the AI productivity cycle:
stronger output;
higher orders;
fewer incremental workers.
AI is beginning to separate business growth from employment growth.
That is positive for operating margins and productivity. It is more ambiguous for wage formation, household income and the breadth of consumer demand.The completed AI system may lower the cost of producing services.
But during the transition, it can also reduce the labor required to satisfy each additional unit of demand.
AI is becoming deflationary for headcount while remaining inflationary for capital, electricity and scarce industrial inputs.
This is not a traditional recession signal. Demand is still expanding.
It is a signal that the relationship between economic activity and labor absorption is changing.
The Great Split is moving from financial markets into factor incomes:
capital receives the productivity upside immediately;
labor must renegotiate its role inside the new productive system.
⸻
AI Demand Is Accelerating. Valuation Support Is Not.
Industrial momentum remains powerful, but the market is withdrawing unconditional capital from companies whose growth no longer compensates for duration, capital intensity and execution risk.
Verified facts
South Korea’s KOSPI fell 3.64%, Japan’s Nikkei declined 1.57%, SK Hynix lost 6.95%, Kioxia dropped 9.61% and Tokyo Electron fell 4.61%.
Yet the broader earnings picture remains exceptionally strong. Capital expenditure by Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Oracle is expected to approach $800 billion in 2026. S&P 500 second-quarter earnings are on course to increase 31.1%, while technology-sector earnings are expected to rise 72%.
FDK interpretation
The semiconductor selloff does not invalidate the AI infrastructure cycle.
It reveals the difference between:
industrial acceleration;
financial expectations;
and the price already paid for future leadership.
The market can believe simultaneously that accelerated-computing demand will remain extraordinary and that individual securities have discounted too much of that future too early.
A company can be strategically right and financially overowned.
The AI trade is therefore broadening economically while narrowing financially.
More companies are benefiting from data-centre construction, semiconductors, memory, power, networking and enterprise deployment.
But fewer companies are receiving unconditional permission to spend, finance and scale without demonstrating incremental margins and cash-flow conversion.
The next winners will not merely report AI exposure.
They will prove that AI revenue is increasing faster than the capital required to generate it.

Europe Is Discovering Its Real AI Advantage
Verified facts
Siemens reported its highest-ever quarterly industrial profit and orders. Industrial profit increased 25% to €3.52 billion, revenue rose 7% to €20.79 billion and orders advanced 13% to €27.90 billion. Profit at Digital Industries increased 44%, with Siemens identifying industrial AI and automation as important growth drivers.
SAP’s cloud backlog increased 26% to €22.9 billion. Capgemini bookings rose 9.2%, Sopra Steria’s organic growth accelerated to 5.3% and OVHcloud’s public-cloud revenue increased 20.2%. Airbus expects about 70 critical applications to run on the European-controlled Scaleway platform by the end of 2028.
Euro-area business activity also reached an eight-month high. The Composite PMI rose to 52.0, services reached 51.7 and Spain recorded its strongest performance in more than eighteen months. France remained in contraction.
FDK interpretation
Europe may not need to defeat the United States in frontier-model scale to capture a decisive share of AI value.
Its opportunity lies in making intelligence operational inside:
factories;
transportation systems;
energy networks;
defense platforms;
healthcare;
corporate software;
and critical infrastructure.
The bottleneck is moving from access to models toward the integration of models with proprietary data, legacy systems, regulation, security and physical operations.
This is Europe’s natural competitive terrain.
The next phase of AI will be won not only by those who produce intelligence—but by those who make intelligence work inside the real economy.
Sovereignty strengthens that opportunity.

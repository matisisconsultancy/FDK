The Nasdaq is at a record. Nvidia is worth $5.76 trillion. The U.S. 10-year just touched 5.3493%. The market is not ignoring the cost of capital — it is deciding who can afford it.
Monday produced the clearest expression yet of The Great Split.
The Nasdaq rose 1.05% to a record close, Nvidia gained 2.1%, and the S&P 500 advanced 0.66%. Yet the U.S. 10-year Treasury touched 5.3493%, its highest since 2002, while the 30-year briefly reached 5.7029%. The probability of another Fed hike this month has collapsed to roughly 23%, but long yields are still climbing. 
That is not a contradiction.
It is a repricing of who gets charged the new hurdle rate.
Cash-rich AI leaders with visible earnings are trading through it. Sovereigns, leveraged infrastructure, weaker corporates and capital-intensive laggards increasingly cannot.
THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
What if 5%+ long-term rates do not kill the AI boom — but instead concentrate it?
That is the more consequential possibility.
The market may be moving from a single discount rate to two economic realities:
one for companies able to self-finance acceleration;
another for everyone dependent on external capital.
That would make higher rates not merely a valuation problem.
They become a competitive-selection mechanism.

FIVE SIGNALS SHAPING THE NEXT ECONOMY
1 | THE EQUITY–BOND DIVERGENCE IS BECOMING STRUCTURAL
VERIFIED. Treasury yields reached fresh 24-year highs even after weak U.S. employment data pushed the implied probability of an October Fed hike down from about 71% a week ago to roughly 23%. At the same time, the Nasdaq closed at a record and consensus forecasts point to roughly 27% S&P 500 earnings growth in the latest quarter, with more than half of that growth expected from companies benefiting from AI infrastructure spending. 
FDK READ.
The market is separating the Fed rate from the structural price of capital.
That is the key shift.
Weak jobs can restrain the central bank without lowering the long-end hurdle rate if fiscal deficits, inflation, energy risk and extraordinary AI investment keep demand for capital high.
The consequence:
duration is becoming expensive, but earnings certainty is becoming more valuable.
That explains why the Nasdaq can rise while Treasuries sell off.
The real risk is therefore not a broad technology collapse.
It is a widening valuation gap between self-financing growth and financed growth.

2 | EUROPE’S RECOVERY IS RAISING THE PRICE OF EUROPEAN CAPITAL
VERIFIED. Euro-area business activity accelerated sharply in September. The composite PMI rose to 53.1, its strongest reading since April 2023, and new orders increased at the fastest pace in 41 months. Yet inflation simultaneously jumped to 3.8%, while markets now price more than two additional ECB hikes by mid-2027. France’s sovereign stress remains elevated and Spain has added political uncertainty with a snap election. 
FDK READ.
This is Europe’s emerging paradox.
Better growth should improve the investment case.
Instead, it may delay monetary relief precisely when governments need enormous capital for:
AI
defence
grids
rail
energy security
industrial renewal
The transmission chain is becoming dangerous:better activity → persistent inflation → higher ECB path → higher sovereign yields → higher corporate hurdle rates
France exposed the fiscal side first.
Spain now adds political uncertainty.
If the euro remains weak while activity strengthens, markets are telling Europe something uncomfortable:
growth alone cannot offset fragmented capital markets.

3 | THE MODEL LAYER IS COMMODITIZING FASTER THAN THE INFRASTRUCTURE LAYER
VERIFIED. DeepSeek is reportedly close to raising at least 80 billion yuan — about $11.9 billion — in a funding round backed by Tencent and CATL, according to Bloomberg reporting carried by Reuters; Reuters said it could not independently verify the report. Separately, Nvidia-backed Reflection AI has launched its Beam open-weight model to compete directly with low-cost Chinese models. 
At the same time, Morgan Stanley estimates U.S. data-centre developers face a 34% net power shortfall through 2028, equivalent to roughly 32 GW. 
FDK READ.
This is the next Great Split inside AI.
Competition is pushing the price of intelligence downward.
But the physical inputs required to deploy that intelligence remain scarce:
power
accelerators
memory
networking
land
grid access
capital
That means model economics and infrastructure economics are moving in opposite directions.
The strategic implication is profound:
the more intelligence commoditizes, the more valuable the bottlenecks beneath it become.
Capital should therefore become more selective even within semiconductors.
Nvidia and Broadcom may retain stronger order visibility because they sit closest to deployment decisions. Secondary suppliers face greater risk if power delays push installations outward. 


⸻


4 | OIL SUPPLY IS RECOVERING — ENERGY FRICTION IS NOT
VERIFIED. Brent traded near $100.59 this morning. Gulf oil flows excluding Iran recovered to more than 81% of pre-war levels in September, supported by Saudi export resilience. Yet tanker attacks, insurance costs, rerouting and record Gulf-to-Asia freight rates remain elevated, while G7 countries are releasing 100 million barrels of emergency crude and diesel stocks. 
FDK READ.
The headline oil price increasingly understates the economic shock.
Barrels may move.
But moving them is becoming more expensive.
That creates a second-order inflation channel:
shipping risk → insurance → refined fuels → logistics → industrial margins
This matters for AI infrastructure, construction and mobility because all three remain physically intensive.
And it reinforces electrification.
High hydrocarbon costs improve the relative economics of EVs, electric buses, storage, rail and urban mass mobility, even while they pressure near-term inflation.
The energy transition is therefore being accelerated by the very instability that makes capital more expensive.

5 | BANKING SCALE IS BECOMING A RESILIENCE ASSET
VERIFIED. Intesa Sanpaolo’s improved €35 billion bid for Monte dei Paschi has secured the support of MPS’s largest shareholder, Delfin, which owns 17.6%. MPS shareholders vote on their defence strategy on October 29. 
Meanwhile, South Korean authorities are investigating attacks on several major banks after President Lee Jae Myung said there were signs that AI had been used in some hacking incidents. Shinhan, KB Kookmin and other banks reported cyberattacks and customer-data breaches. 
FDK READ.
These stories belong together.
The cost structure of banking is changing.
Banks increasingly need scale to absorb:
technology investment
AI deployment
cybersecurity
regulatory capital
data infrastructure
cross-border competition
That raises the minimum efficient size of a financial institution.
Italian consolidation is therefore not simply M&A.
It is part of a broader structural response to rising fixed costs in the financial system.
The Great Split in banking will increasingly separate institutions that can spread technology and resilience costs across scale from those that cannot.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN
The market is beginning to price two different costs of capital.One applies to scarce assets with cash flow, strategic importance and financing access.
The other applies to everything whose economics depend on cheap money.
That explains the seemingly contradictory tape:
Nasdaq at a record.
Treasuries at 24-year yield highs.
Nvidia at $5.76 trillion.
Europe under sovereign pressure.
AI funding accelerating.
Infrastructure bottlenecks intensifying.
The common mechanism is not exuberance.
It is capital concentration.
Money is moving toward assets capable of turning structural scarcity into cash flow faster than the discount rate can destroy value.
That is The Great Split now.

WHERE CAPITAL IS LIKELY TO MOVE
ACCELERATING: AI leaders with visible cash flow; advanced accelerators; contracted compute; grid infrastructure; firm power and nuclear; data-centre electrical systems; selected networking; asset-backed infrastructure finance; large-scale banks; cybersecurity; electrified mobility; logistics automation.
BECOMING MORE SELECTIVE: memory, optics, power-management semiconductors and other components exposed to deployment delays; AI developers dependent on repeated external financing; highly leveraged data-centre projects.
DECELERATING: sovereign duration with weakening fiscal credibility; long-duration growth without free cash flow; weak European financial institutions; energy-intensive manufacturers without pricing power; businesses whose investment thesis depends on falling long-term rates.
The investment rule is changing:
DO NOT ASK ONLY WHO BENEFITS FROM AI.
ASK WHO CAN STILL COMPOUND AT A 5%+ RISK-FREE RATE.

BEFORE THE CLOSE
Investors: the most important number remains the U.S. 10-year. If yields stay around or above 5.3% and technology leadership remains intact, the market is confirming that the equity–bond divergence is structural rather than temporary. If secondary semiconductor names begin underperforming Nvidia and Broadcom, the power bottleneck is starting to enter earnings expectations.
Executives: stress-test capital allocation against a world in which the hurdle rate does not return quickly to pre-AI norms. Access to power, financing and contracted demand now belongs in the same strategic conversation as technology.
European boards: watch the euro, French spreads and today’s euro-area activity data together. Stronger growth accompanied by weaker FX and elevated sovereign spreads would confirm that Europe’s problem is shifting from demand weakness to the cost of financing expansion.
Policymakers: today’s central question is whether sovereign markets are beginning to differentiate fiscal credibility more aggressively than central banks can offset. Europe’s political uncertainty and Japan’s gradual normalization mean the global era of uniformly cheap sovereign capital is increasingly over. The BOJ is expected to signal later this month that underlying inflation has reached its 2% objective, reinforcing expectations of further normalization.

FINAL TAKE — THE MARKET HAS CHANGED THE HURDLE
A 5.35% U.S. Treasury yield would once have been enough to break a technology rally.
It is not breaking this one.
That does not mean the price of capital no longer matters.
It means the market has decided who is allowed to ignore it.
The strongest AI platforms can fund investment, secure power, capture scarcity and compound earnings despite high rates.
The weakest players cannot.
That is why The Great Split is becoming more consequential.
It is no longer simply:
AI versus non-AI.
It is becoming:
cash-flow velocity versus cost of capital.
And that is a much harsher test.
At 5.35%, the market is not saying growth is cheap. It is saying certainty is priceless.

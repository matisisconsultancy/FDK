IN FOCUS
AI Has Entered the Capital-Productivity Test
The AI Supercycle has crossed a financial threshold.
With long-term rates above 5%, capital committed today must overcome a materially higher hurdle while AI assets face rapid depreciation and technological obsolescence.
At the same time, the stack is diverging.
Model capability is proliferating. Packaging, power, cooling and grid access remain constrained. The result is a redistribution of pricing power toward the physical layers and a higher financial burden on companies funding capacity ahead of cash generation.
The emerging divide is:
Productive AI Capital vs. Trapped AI Capital
The market is therefore moving beyond the question of how much AI capacity can be built.
It is beginning to ask:
What return does that capital ultimately produce?


⸻


THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
Which corporations can translate structural AI leadership into durable cash returns before financing cost, depreciation and obsolescence absorb the advantage?
That is now the question beneath the GVI.

FIRST NEW LAW
CAPACITY HAS TO EARN
The AI cycle has created two financial paths.
COMPOUNDING CAPITAL
Capex → Revenue → FCF → ROIC → Reinvestment
STRANDED CAPITAL
Capex → Capacity → Depreciation → Financing Cost → Margin Pressure
The difference is not technological ambition.
It is the distance between capital committed and cash generated.
That distance is becoming one of the most important corporate variables in the AI economy.


⸻


SECOND NEW LAW
PHYSICAL SCARCITY DETERMINES WHERE PRICING POWER FORMS
The physical stack cannot expand at software speed:
Packaging → Memory → Networking → Power → Cooling → Grid Access
As intelligence becomes easier to reproduce, these harder-to-replicate layers gain relative strategic value.
Scarcity → Pricing Power → Cash Generation
TSMC, Schneider Electric, Siemens Energy and Vertiv increasingly sit at this intersection.
The first-order AI trade was intelligence. The second-order trade is scarcity.


⸻


TIME-TO-POWER IS A FINANCIAL VARIABLE
Power determines when infrastructure can begin producing revenue.
Every delay adds:
Financing Cost + Depreciation + Obsolescence + Deferred Revenue
Time-to-power therefore affects project economics directly.
It should increasingly be treated alongside capex, backlog and capacity as a core financial KPI.


⸻


BACKLOG WILL BE REPRICED
The old assumption was:
More backlog = more visibility
The emerging hierarchy is:
Announced → Contracted → Financed → Powered → Cash-Generating
The farther backlog moves through that chain, the higher its economic quality.
Nominal backlog measures demand. Economic backlog measures monetizability.


⸻


THE EVOLUTIONARY FINANCIAL DYNAMICS
The AI Supercycle is moving through five stages:
I — BUILD
Models. Chips. Data centers.
II — BOTTLENECK
Power. Packaging. Cooling. Grid.
III — DEPLOY
Capacity reaches customers.
IV — CONVERT
Revenue becomes cash.
V — REPRICE
Markets separate structural Velocity from economic capture.The next rotation therefore occurs inside AI:
Models → Bottlenecks → Distribution → Security → Cash

BOARD-LEVEL IMPLICATIONS
Boards should ask five questions:
Is AI capital earning above its cost?
How quickly is investment becoming free cash flow?
Where is the binding physical bottleneck?
Is time-to-cash shortening?
Is incremental ROIC improving?
The governing question is:
Does the next dollar of AI investment increase future cash generation faster than it increases the capital base?

FIVE TAKEAWAYS
1. The AI cycle has moved from capital formation to return formation.
2. Physical scarcity is concentrating pricing power in the layers hardest to reproduce.
3. Higher-for-longer increases the cost of every delay between investment and cash generation.
4. Backlog will increasingly be valued by economic quality, not nominal size.
5. The next AI rotation occurs inside the stack: from technological creation toward bottlenecks, distribution, security and cash.


⸻


THE VELOCITY EDGE
The first phase established who could build.
The next will establish who can earn.
Technology creates strategic position. Capital productivity determines whether that position compounds.
THE NEXT AI DIVIDE IS NOT BUILDERS VERSUS LAGGARDS. IT IS COMPOUNDING CAPITAL VERSUS STRANDED CAPITAL.

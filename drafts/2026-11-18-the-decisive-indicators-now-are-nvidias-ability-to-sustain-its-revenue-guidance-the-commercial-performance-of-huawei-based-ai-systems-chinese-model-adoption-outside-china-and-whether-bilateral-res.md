THE FINAL TAKE
The U.S.–China AI competition is entering a more financially consequential phase.
The United States is seeking to preserve the scarcity value of advanced computing. China is being pushed toward technological self-sufficiency and more economical deployment.
For investors, the emerging distinction is between control over the infrastructure of intelligence and the ability to generate sustainable returns from its widespread use.
The AI Supercycle may remain global in its economic consequences while becoming increasingly divided in its technological foundations. The next great capital rotation will depend on which side converts that structural change into enduring profitability.

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
What happens when the world’s leading AI computing company can sustain extraordinary growth while planning for no data-center computing revenue from the world’s second-largest economy?
This is today’s consequential development.
On November 17, Nvidia reported record quarterly revenue and issued fourth-quarter guidance excluding Chinese data-center computing sales. On November 18, Reuters confirmed that Nvidia has stopped shipping U.S.-licensed Hopper processors to China, although the company has not explained whether the suspension is permanent. THE BIG TAKE — DECOUPLING WITHOUT DEMAND DESTRUCTION
Nvidia sold $2.4 billion of licensed Hopper data-center products to China in the quarter ended October 25. That represented approximately 2.1% of total quarterly revenue.
Despite excluding that business from its next-quarter outlook, Nvidia expects continued substantial growth. Its Vera Rubin NVL72 accelerated-computing platform has also begun shipping. Reuters +1
Three implications follow.
First, U.S. frontier computing leadership is becoming less dependent on Chinese demand. Nvidia’s earnings trajectory demonstrates the scale of investment being mobilized elsewhere, although it does not establish that every AI infrastructure investment will deliver attractive returns.
Second, China’s incentive to develop domestic computing capacity is strengthening. Beijing has itself restricted imports of Nvidia’s H200 chips, with Reuters previously reporting approximately 600,000 processors awaiting approval in Hong Kong. Supply constraints and industrial policy are now reinforcing each other. Reuters
Third, the AI Supercycle may increasingly divide into distinct computing ecosystems without producing an equivalent division in AI applications. China can continue distributing comparatively inexpensive models even as the infrastructure supporting them becomes more domestically oriented.
That is the hidden pattern: the geography of computing production is fragmenting faster than the global market for intelligence.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE VELOCITY EDGE
Today’s results strengthen the first half of our governing thesis: America continues to dominate key elements of frontier computing and is demonstrating the capacity to finance its expansion without relying on Chinese data-center sales.
They do not independently prove the second half—that Chinese developers have achieved superior inference economics. That proposition still requires independently validated cost-per-task comparisons and evidence of profitable industrial deployment.

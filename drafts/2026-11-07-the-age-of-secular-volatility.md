Why the World Survived 150 Years of Crises — and Why the Next Regime May Be Different
The central risk is not bigger crises. It is a permanently higher cost of resilience.
THE MARKET THESIS
Secular Volatility is not simply more volatility.
It is a different pricing regime.
For most of the Great Moderation, investors could rely on four assumptions:
inflation would mean-revert,
central banks would ease into shocks,
sovereign bonds would anchor portfolios,
and globalisation would keep capital and production cheap.
Those assumptions supported duration, leverage, compressed sovereign spreads and high valuations for distant cash flows.
The emerging regime challenges all four.
Geopolitical competition raises the cost of security.
Energy constraints raise the cost of production.
Sovereign borrowing raises the cost of duration.
AI, defence and infrastructure raise the demand for capital.
The result is:
THE DISCOUNT RATE STAYS HIGHER, DISPERSION STAYS WIDER, AND BALANCE-SHEET QUALITY MATTERS MORE.
Returns should depend less on multiple expansion and more on the ability to generate cash flows above a higher cost of capital.
The central market question is:
WHO CAN EARN A RETURN ON CAPITAL ABOVE A HIGHER AND MORE VOLATILE COST OF CAPITAL?


⸻


THE BIG TAKE
The world has survived depressions, wars, energy shocks, financial collapses, pandemics and technological bubbles because adaptation eventually caught up with disruption.
Institutions changed.
Capital moved.
Technology raised productivity.
Balance sheets healed.
And the system usually had time.
That may now be changing.
Public debt is already high before the next emergency arrives.
Energy systems face simultaneous pressure from security, electrification and AI.
Technology moves faster than institutions.
Financial shocks transmit almost instantly.
And new disruption increasingly arrives before the previous shock has cleared.
THE DEFINING RISK IS THE LOSS OF RECOVERY TIME.
The old sequence was:
CRISIS → RESPONSE → RECOVERY → NORMALISATION
The emerging sequence is:
CRISIS → PARTIAL RESPONSE → NEW CRISIS → FURTHER ADAPTATION.
That is Secular Volatility.
It is not permanent recession.
It is a regime in which adaptation must occur faster and with less policy room.


⸻


I. WHY THE WORLD KEPT SURVIVING
History shows a consistent pattern.
Railway capital collapsed, but railways transformed economies.
The Great Depression broke banks, but produced new financial institutions.
World War II destroyed physical capital, but reconstruction produced Bretton Woods and decades of growth.
The 1970s produced stagflation, but also energy efficiency and a new monetary regime.
The dot-com crash destroyed valuations, not the Internet.
The Global Financial Crisis destroyed leverage, but strengthened banks and liquidity frameworks.
The pandemic shut the economy, but fiscal support, central banks, vaccines and digital infrastructure prevented depression.
Five forces repeatedly drove recovery:
technology, institutions, capital mobility, entrepreneurial adaptation and time.
The first four remain powerful.
The fifth is becoming scarcer.
THE WORLD SURVIVED BECAUSE SYSTEMS ADAPTED BEFORE DAMAGE BECAME PERMANENT.


⸻


II. WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT FROM 2008
The Global Financial Crisis had an identifiable centre:
REAL ESTATE
→ MORTGAGE CREDIT
→ BANK LEVERAGE
→ LIQUIDITY CRISIS
→ CREDIT CONTRACTION.
Once policymakers identified the banking system as the epicentre, the response was clear:
recapitalise,
guarantee,
provide liquidity,
cut rates,
support demand.
Today there may be no single epicentre.
Instability can originate simultaneously in:
geopolitics, energy, sovereign debt, trade, technology, cybersecurity and infrastructure.
Central banks can create liquidity.
They cannot create electricity grids, semiconductors, engineers, natural gas or geopolitical stability.
The macro trade-off is harder too.
A shock can now produce:
WEAKER GROWTH + HIGHER INFLATION + FINANCIAL INSTABILITY
at the same time.And unlike 2008, markets may question not only private balance sheets but also the governments doing the rescuing.
The question changes from:
Who will the government rescue?
to:
WHO WILL FINANCE THE GOVERNMENT DOING THE RESCUING?
That is the deeper regime change.


⸻


III. GEOECONOMICS — THE RETURN OF THE RISK PREMIUM
The post-Cold War world reduced the economic price of geopolitics.
That era is ending.
Conflict increasingly extends into:
energy,
shipping,
payments,
semiconductors,
critical minerals,
cloud infrastructure,
trade,
sanctions,
and technology standards.
The economic consequences are structural.
Defence spending rises.
Supply chains gain redundancy.
Strategic inventories grow.
Production becomes less globally optimised.
Security acquires a price.
The world does not necessarily deglobalise.
It reorganises around geoeconomics.
Markets therefore charge more for:
duration, political uncertainty, energy dependence, sovereign weakness and strategic vulnerability.
That is the resilience premium.


⸻


IV. AI — THE PERFECT EXAMPLE OF THE NEW REGIME
AI is often described as deflationary.
Eventually, it may be.
But the path to that productivity dividend is initially capital- and energy-intensive.
THE BUILD PHASE
AI requires:
data centres,
semiconductors,
power generation,
grids,
cooling,
construction,
specialist labour,
and capital.
The transmission is:
AI CAPEX
→ HIGHER DEMAND FOR ENERGY + EQUIPMENT + LABOUR + CAPITAL
→ BOTTLENECKS
→ HIGHER PRICES + HIGHER HURDLE RATES.
AI CAN BE DEFLATIONARY IN WHAT IT PRODUCES WHILE BEING INFLATIONARY IN WHAT MUST BE BUILT TO PRODUCE IT.
The energy channel is critical:
MORE COMPUTE
→ MORE ELECTRICITY
→ MORE GENERATION + GRIDS
→ MORE CAPITAL.
AI also competes for financing with defence, infrastructure and sovereigns:
AI + DEFENCE + ENERGY + SOVEREIGN BORROWING
→ HIGHER CAPITAL DEMAND
→ HIGHER REAL RATES / TERM PREMIUMS
→ HIGHER HURDLE RATES.
THE PRODUCTIVITY PHASE
Only later does the second sequence emerge:
AI ADOPTION
→ AUTOMATION
→ HIGHER OUTPUT PER WORKER
→ LOWER UNIT COSTS
→ PRODUCTIVITY-LED DISINFLATION.
The uncertainty is the lag between the two phases.
If productivity arrives quickly, the investment validates itself.
If it arrives slowly:
AI INVESTMENT
→ INFLATION
→ HIGHER RATES
→ HIGHER FINANCING COSTS
→ LOWER CAPEX
→ DELAYED PRODUCTIVITY.
That is the AI paradox.
THE INVESTMENT REQUIRED TO CREATE FUTURE DEFLATION CAN GENERATE CURRENT INFLATION — AND THE MONETARY RESPONSE CAN DELAY THE FUTURE DEFLATION.


⸻


V. THE CENTRAL MECHANISM
The governing tension is simple:
DOES DISRUPTION RAISE THE COST OF CAPITAL FASTER THAN ADAPTATION RAISES PRODUCTIVE RETURNS?
The destructive sequence is:
SHOCK
→ HIGHER INPUT COSTS + INFLATION
→ HIGHER RATES + RISK PREMIUMS
→ HIGHER FUNDING COSTS
→ LOWER PRODUCTIVE INVESTMENT
→ LOWER PRODUCTIVITY
→ WEAKER FISCAL CAPACITY
→ GREATER VULNERABILITY TO THE NEXT SHOCK.
But shocks can also accelerate adjustment.
Higher prices encourage supply.
Scarcity encourages substitution.
Strategic vulnerability encourages infrastructure investment.
Technological pressure accelerates innovation.
The question is therefore whether the economy can convert disruption into productive investment before financing conditions shut that investment down.


⸻


VI. THE VELOCITY PROBLEM
Disruption and adaptation operate on different clocks.
MARKET TIME — HOURS
MONETARY TIME — MONTHS
FISCAL TIME — YEARS
INFRASTRUCTURE TIME — YEARS
PRODUCTIVITY TIME — YEARS TO DECADES
Markets can reprice immediately.
Grids cannot be built immediately.
Factories cannot be reshored immediately.
Workers cannot be retrained immediately.
Productivity cannot scale immediately.
That is why recovery time matters.
Adaptation increasingly has to occur while the next shock is already arriving.


⸻


VII. THE PROBABILITY FRAMEWORK
The scenario probabilities are current strategic weights for the dominant macro-financial regime most likely to shape global asset pricing over the next three to five years.
They are driven by two questions:
1. WILL PRODUCTIVITY BROADEN MEANINGFULLY?
2.WILL CAPITAL REMAIN STRUCTURALLY EXPENSIVE?
 PRODUCTIVITY FAST PRODUCTIVITY SLOW
CAPITAL EXPENSIVE High-Rate Productivity — 40% Stagflationary Capital Squeeze — 30%
CAPITAL EASIER Productivity Boom — 20% Liquidity-Dependent Boom — 10%
These four probabilities sum to 100%.
Two useful marginal probabilities follow.
70% — CAPITAL REMAINS STRUCTURALLY EXPENSIVE
**40% High-Rate Productivity
30% Stagflationary Capital Squeeze
= 70%.**
This means a 70% probability that real rates, term premiums and required returns remain above the Great Moderation norm.
60% — PRODUCTIVITY BROADENS MEANINGFULLY
**40% High-Rate Productivity
20% Productivity Boom
= 60%.**
This means a 60% probability that AI, automation and related investment generate measurable productivity gains beyond a narrow technology cohort.
The two probabilities overlap.
The key intersection is:
40% — HIGH PRODUCTIVITY + EXPENSIVE CAPITAL.
That is the base case.
The four outcomes are therefore:
40% — expensive capital + strong productivity.
30% — expensive capital + weak productivity.
20% — easier capital + strong productivity.
10% — easier capital + weak productivity.
The portfolio message is simple:
CAPITAL PROBABLY STAYS EXPENSIVE.
PRODUCTIVITY PROBABLY IMPROVES.
THE PRINCIPAL RISK IS THAT IT IMPROVES TOO SLOWLY.

VIII. BASE CASE — 40%: HIGH-RATE PRODUCTIVITY
AI begins generating measurable productivity.
Automation spreads.
Software and knowledge costs fall.
Energy and infrastructure capacity expand.
Corporate margins improve among productive adopters.
But the world does not return to zero-rate economics.
Defence remains expensive.
Sovereign issuance remains high.
Energy infrastructure remains capital-intensive.
AI itself requires sustained capital formation.
The result is:
HIGHER PRODUCTIVITY + HIGHER REAL RATES + HIGHER DISPERSION.
The decisive condition is:
RETURN ON PRODUCTIVE CAPITAL > COST OF CAPITAL.
Confirmation signals
productivity broadens beyond technology;
unit labour costs moderate;
AI capex generates visible cash flow;
electricity and grid capacity expand;
real yields remain high while credit spreads stay contained;
defaults remain controlled;
equity leadership broadens;
sovereign differentiation remains orderly.
Likely winners
cash-rich compounders,
industrial automation,
power and grids,
selected financials,
defence and strategic infrastructure,
productive AI adopters,
fiscally credible sovereigns.


⸻


IX. DOWNSIDE — 30%: STAGFLATIONARY CAPITAL SQUEEZE
Productivity arrives too slowly.
Energy and infrastructure remain expensive.
Sovereign borrowing increases.
Rates stay restrictive.
Corporate refinancing tightens.
Inflation limits the ability of central banks to rescue growth.
Investment is cut before productivity can scale.
Warning signals
rising sovereign term premiums;
widening credit spreads;
punitive refinancing;
private-credit stress;
grid bottlenecks;
falling industrial capex;
weak productivity despite high technology spending;
rising unemployment with inflation still elevated.
Likely winners
cash,
strong sovereigns,
short-duration quality credit,
gold and selected real assets,
defensive cash-generative equities,
energy-secure infrastructure.


⸻


X. UPSIDE — 20%: PRODUCTIVITY BOOM
AI moves rapidly from infrastructure into broad adoption.
Automation raises output.
Energy capacity expands.
Unit costs fall.
Real wages rise.
Inflation moderates.
Central banks can ease because productive capacity grows rather than because demand collapses.
The decisive market signal is:
EQUITY LEADERSHIP BROADENS FROM AI BUILDERS TO AI USERS.
That is when technological promise becomes macroeconomic productivity.


⸻


XI. TAIL RISK — 10%: LIQUIDITY-DEPENDENT BOOM
Rates fall before productivity is proven.
Valuations rise.
Credit expands.
Debt-funded investment accelerates.
But productive returns remain uncertain.
The key warning sign is:
COST OF CAPITAL FALLS FASTER THAN RETURN ON PRODUCTIVE CAPITAL RISES.
That extends the cycle but increases misallocation.
The technology can remain revolutionary while the capital financing it is destroyed.


⸻


XII.THE INDICATORS THAT MATTER
The scenario weights should move with ten variables:
1. Productivity — output per hour, TFP, unit labour costs.
2. Return on AI capital — revenue and cash flow relative to capex.
3. Energy — power prices, grid queues, transformer availability.
4. Real rates — sustainable if productivity rises, dangerous if it does not.
5. Term premiums — the market price of duration and sovereign supply.
6. Sovereign spreads — the price of fiscal differentiation.
7. Credit spreads — the bridge from repricing to economic damage.
8. AI capex breadth — diffusion versus concentration.
9. Labour dispersion — specialist wages versus broad employment.
10. Fiscal capacity — deficits, interest burdens and debt maturity.
Together they answer one question:
IS PRODUCTIVITY RISING FAST ENOUGH TO CLEAR THE NEW HURDLE RATE?


⸻


XIII. FROM BETA TO DISPERSION
The investment consequence is not uniformly weak returns.
It is wider dispersion.
Between countries.
Between companies.
Between sovereigns.
Between energy producers and importers.
Between AI builders and AI users.
Between cash-rich and leveraged businesses.
Between fiscally credible and fiscally constrained governments.
The Great Moderation compressed these differences.
The new regime should widen them.
The investment approach therefore shifts from:
BUY THE INDEX
toward:
PRICE THE DIFFERENCE.
The new alpha is selection.


⸻


XIV. FIVE INVESTMENT CONCLUSIONS
1. THE DISCOUNT RATE MATTERS AGAIN
The test is:
ROIC > WACC THROUGH MULTIPLE REGIMES.
Businesses dependent on falling rates become fragile.
Businesses able to compound at higher discount rates become more valuable.
2. RESILIENCE BECOMES OFFENSIVE
Cash.
Liquidity.
Debt maturity.
Energy access.
Pricing power.
Supply-chain flexibility.
Technological adaptability.
These attributes allow strong companies to keep investing while weaker competitors retreat.
3. SOVEREIGN RISK RETURNS
The sovereign equation becomes:
DEBT SUSTAINABILITY = FISCAL CREDIBILITY × NOMINAL GROWTH × PRODUCTIVITY × MARKET TRUST.
Country selection matters again.
4. ENERGY BECOMES PART OF THE COST OF INTELLIGENCE
Compute requires electricity.
Electricity requires grids.
Grids require capital.
Energy-secure economies shorten the path from investment to productivity.
5. THE NEW ALPHA IS ADAPTATION SPEED
The key distinction is:
who can convert investment into productivity before financing conditions turn against them.


⸻


XV. THE EUROPEAN QUESTION
Europe faces a particularly difficult version of this regime.
Its previous model relied on:
cheap Russian energy,
American security,
Chinese demand,
and globally integrated trade and capital.
Each assumption is being reconsidered.
Europe must therefore move from an efficiency architecture toward a resilience and productivity architecture.
That requires:
energy security,
deeper capital markets,
Industrial-AI deployment,
faster infrastructure,
scalable companies,
credible sovereign finances.
Europe’s challenge is not simply to spend more.
It is to generate more productivity from every unit of capital deployed.
If it does not, higher energy and financing costs risk suppressing the investment required to restore competitiveness.


⸻


THE FINAL TAKE — THE HURDLE RATE HAS CHANGED
The entire argument reduces to one proposition:
THE WORLD SURVIVED PAST CRISES BECAUSE ADAPTATION HAD TIME TO CATCH UP.
That time is becoming scarcer.
War raises the cost of security.
Energy raises the cost of production.
Sovereign debt raises the cost of intervention.
AI raises both the demand for capital today and the possibility of much higher productivity tomorrow.
So the decisive question is whether productive returns can clear a structurally higher hurdle rate.
The probability framework says:
70% — capital remains expensive.
60% — productivity broadens.
40% — both happen together.
That 40% intersection is the core thesis.
THE MOST LIKELY WORLD IS NOT A RETURN TO CHEAP MONEY.
NOR IS IT PERMANENT STAGNATION.
IT IS HIGHER PRODUCTIVITY INSIDE A HIGHER-COST-OF-CAPITAL REGIME.
That shifts the source of returns.
From leverage to resilience.From multiple expansion to cash-flow delivery.
From broad beta to selection.
From waiting for normalisation to identifying who can compound without it.
VELOCITY INSIGHT
THE NEXT REGIME WILL REWARD THOSE WHO CAN COMPOUND WITHOUT THE OLD WORLD RETURNING.

Regulatory Sequencing, Equity Currency and Continental Scale: the battle is moving from valuation to execution

17 September 2026 | FDK
Data cut: 09:10 CEST

The Italian banking contest has moved into another phase.

Until a few days ago, the decisive variable was equity currency: could MPS keep its shares strong enough to make the Banco BPM and Banca Generali counteroffers economically credible?

That remains essential. But a second variable has now moved to the centre:

TIME.

Yesterday Intesa Sanpaolo secured an important insurance-sector clearance from IVASS for the indirect qualified shareholdings in Generali and the AXA-MPS insurance companies that would result from acquiring MPS. At almost the same moment, however, Italy’s competition authority formally opened an in-depth investigation into the transaction. 

The transaction therefore received one regulatory green light while another regulator opened a new front.

That changes the equation.

MPS has its extraordinary shareholder meeting on 29 October. Its offers for Banco BPM and Banca Generali remain alive even in the event of a subsequent change of control. Intesa is trying to complete its own approval path quickly enough to prevent MPS from converting its defensive architecture into contractual path dependency. 

The emerging contest is therefore:

EQUITY CURRENCY × REGULATORY SEQUENCING × SHAREHOLDER COALITION × TIME

The market is still part of the Risiko.

But now the calendar has become part of the capital structure.

⸻

1. WHAT CHANGED IN THE LAST 24 HOURS

INTESA CLEARS IVASS — BUT ANTITRUST MOVES TO THE FRONT

Confirmed. On 16 September, Intesa announced that IVASS had granted prior authorisation for the indirect qualified holdings in Assicurazioni Generali, AXA MPS Assicurazioni Vita and AXA MPS Assicurazioni Danni arising from the MPS transaction. This removes one meaningful insurance-supervision uncertainty from the deal path. 

But the competition issue is becoming materially more complex.

Confirmed. Italy’s AGCM opened an investigation on 14 September to examine the effects of Intesa–MPS across numerous banking and insurance markets at both local and national level. 

Reuters reports that the authority is examining potential competition issues in 20 provinces for deposits and 17 for SME lending, while Intesa has proposed selling around half of MPS’s branches. The regulator is also examining the implications of Intesa indirectly obtaining roughly 13% of Generali, including governance links and the potential exchange of competitively sensitive information. 

This is not a rejection of the transaction.

But it means the central regulatory issue has shifted from:

Can Intesa buy MPS?

to:

WHAT MUST INTESA GIVE UP TO BUY MPS?

Branches.

Distribution density.

Potential Generali governance influence.

Possibly some of the very network economics that underpin the industrial rationale.

That distinction matters enormously for valuation.

⸻

UNICREDIT–COMMERZBANK: THE FIGHT MOVES FROM OWNERSHIP TO GOVERNANCE

The Berlin negotiation has also entered a harder phase.

Reported, not formally confirmed by UniCredit. Reuters, citing sources, says Andrea Orcel does not want Bettina Orlopp to remain CEO of Commerzbank or Jens Weidmann to remain chairman, and opposes continued German-government supervisory-board representation after a takeover. 

That follows Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil’s demands that Commerzbank remain headquartered and listed in Frankfurt and continue serving the German Mittelstand. 

This means the negotiation is no longer principally about whether UniCredit can obtain control.

It is becoming a negotiation over what control actually means.

Germany wants:

national identity + headquarters + listing + institutional influence.

UniCredit appears to want:

ownership + board control + management control + freedom to execute the integration.

The unresolved variable is therefore becoming governance sovereignty.That is more consequential than the ownership percentage itself.

⸻

CRÉDIT AGRICOLE HARDENS THE BANCO BPM PROBLEM

There is still no public Banco BPM endorsement of MPS’s proposal in the bank’s latest regulated communications. Press reports say the board has continued evaluating the offer without taking a formal decision. 

Meanwhile, Crédit Agricole’s position remains the most important obstacle.

Reported. Reuters, citing La Stampa, said Crédit Agricole regards MPS’s Banco BPM offer as unattractive and insufficiently value-creating and continues to prefer, over time, a combination between Banco BPM and Crédit Agricole Italia. Crédit Agricole owns 29.3% of Banco BPM. 

The issue is therefore no longer whether MPS can design a third pole.

It can.

The issue is whether MPS can design a transaction in which Crédit Agricole is better off inside the architecture than outside it.

That threshold has not yet been crossed.

⸻

2. THE DECISIVE ISSUE

REGULATORY SEQUENCING HAS BECOME STRATEGY

The market has been focused on MPS’s equity currency.

It should now focus equally on which approval comes first.

The two competing strategic clocks are:

INTESA CLOCK
IVASS → AGCM → other regulatory clearances → Consob → tender → control of MPS.

MPS CLOCK
Shareholder coalition → 29 October vote → regulatory approvals → Banco BPM/Banca Generali offers → contractual path dependency.

Whoever advances farther down that sequence first may constrain the strategic freedom of the other.

This creates a new equation:

TIME × APPROVALS × EQUITY CURRENCY = EXECUTION POWER

A delay is no longer simply a delay.

For MPS, additional time can create coalition-building capacity.

For Intesa, additional time creates the risk that the target enters new commitments before control changes hands.

For Crédit Agricole and Generali, additional time increases bargaining power.

TIME IS NOW AN ASSET.

⸻

3. UPDATED STRATEGIC MAP

INTESA SANPAOLO — FROM ACQUISITION RISK TO REMEDY RISK

The IVASS approval is clearly positive.

The AGCM investigation is equally clearly a reminder that the transaction’s economics may eventually be determined not only by the purchase price but by the remedies required to secure approval. 

Intesa’s original strategic logic remains powerful: more than 27 million clients, roughly €2 trillion of customer financial assets by 2029, and projected accretion of around 8% in EPS, DPS and capital distribution per share. 

But investors now need a second calculation:

headline synergies – regulatory remedies = realizable synergies.

That is the number that ultimately matters.

FDK VIEW: STRONG OVERWEIGHT

Continental Scale Premium — but the Remedy Discount is rising.

⸻

MPS — MAXIMUM OPTIONALITY, MAXIMUM SEQUENCING RISK

MPS has not changed the formal architecture since its 14 September Consob disclosure. Its Banco BPM and Banca Generali offers remain active and the extraordinary meeting remains scheduled for 29 October. 

But the strategic campaign is intensifying.

ANSA reported that CEO Luigi Lovaglio was meeting investors in New York earlier this week, while Italian press reports indicate an effort to broaden institutional support ahead of the shareholder vote. This should be treated as a reported investor-engagement campaign, not evidence that voting commitments have already been secured. 

The shareholder map explains why every vote matters: Delfin holds 17.53%, the Caltagirone group 10.26%, the Italian Treasury 4.86%, BlackRock 4.66% and Banco BPM 3.74%. Barclays has disclosed a 5.03% aggregate economic position, but only 0.08% consists of direct voting rights. 

The key distinction therefore remains:

ECONOMIC EXPOSURE IS NOT THE SAME THING AS VOTING POWER.

FDK VIEW: EVENT-DRIVEN / HIGH CONVEXITY

The upside is enormous if MPS wins the coalition. The execution tree remains the most complex in the sector.

⸻

BANCO BPM — THE ASSET WHOSE VALUE COMES FROM OTHER PEOPLE’S OPTIONS

Banco BPM increasingly looks less like a conventional takeover target and more like an optionality junction.

MPS wants it.

Crédit Agricole already owns 29.3%.

A successful Intesa–MPS combination could make Banco BPM even more strategically scarce.

UniCredit is no longer a natural domestic consolidator because its primary strategic attention has shifted toward Germany.

This raises Banco BPM’s long-term bargaining value even if the current MPS premium remains insufficient for Crédit Agricole.

FDK VIEW: EVENT-DRIVEN POSITIVE

Optionality Premium remains intact.

⸻

CRÉDIT AGRICOLE — THE HOLDER OF TIME

Crédit Agricole does not need to rush.

Its 29.3% stake allows it to observe:

MPS’s share price.

The October vote.

Intesa’s regulatory progression.

The Italian Government’s eventual position.

And the economics of any revised offer.

Reuters Breakingviews has separately highlighted the French bank’s potential kingmaker role, including possible alternative structures around Crédit Agricole Italia and Banco BPM. 

The strategic resource Crédit Agricole controls is therefore not merely 29.3%.

It is:

29.3% + TIME.

⸻

GENERALI — STRATEGIC SCARCITY GETS STRONGER

Generali has formally said it is willing to evaluate MPS’s proposal and the broader industrial collaboration attached to the Banca Generali offer, while stressing that the transaction was unsolicited and not agreed in advance. 

Nothing in the last 24 hours changes that position.

But the AGCM investigation introduces another reason why Generali matters.

Intesa’s potential indirect 13% position in Generali is itself now part of the competition review. 

So Generali is simultaneously:

a strategic asset inside MPS/Mediobanca;

the controlling shareholder of Banca Generali;

an object of the MPS counteroffer architecture;

and now a regulatory variable inside the Intesa–MPS antitrust process.

That is extraordinary strategic scarcity.

FDK VIEW: OVERWEIGHT

Strategic Scarcity Premium

⸻

BANCA GENERALI — THE CLEANEST ASSET, THE MOST DEPENDENT TRANSACTION

The business case remains attractive.

But the transaction remains dependent on decisions made elsewhere: by Generali, by MPS shareholders, by regulators and indirectly by Intesa’s progress.

That creates a paradox.

Banca Generali is arguably the cleanest operating asset inside the contested architecture.

But its transaction outcome is among the least autonomous.

FDK VIEW: EVENT-DRIVEN POSITIVE

Asset Quality High — Transaction Independence Low.

⸻

UNICREDIT — CONTROL IS NO LONGER THE SAME AS INTEGRATION

UniCredit has already accumulated close to half of Commerzbank. Germany has moved from blanket opposition toward conditional negotiation. 

That was the de-risking phase.

The emerging governance dispute represents the next phase.

If Reuters’ reporting on the desired removal of Orlopp and Weidmann proves accurate, UniCredit is signaling that obtaining economic control without management control would be insufficient. 

This is a crucial distinction.

OWNERSHIP CREATES SCALE. GOVERNANCE DETERMINES WHETHER SCALE CAN BE MONETIZED.

FDK VIEW: OVERWEIGHT

European Transformation Premium — Governance Execution Risk rising.

⸻

COMMERZBANK — THE CONTROL PREMIUM MEETS THE GOVERNANCE DISCOUNT

Commerzbank remains the core asset in Europe’s most important cross-border banking experiment.

But the sharper the governance confrontation becomes, the more investors must separate:

takeover probability

from

integration value.

That distinction was visible in yesterday’s market action.

⸻

4. CAPITAL-MARKETS REACTION

The 16 September close produced a revealing divergence.

Intesa +0.60% to €6.74 after the IVASS clearance. MPS -0.14% to €11.79. Banco BPM +0.41% to €16.10. Banca Generali +0.93% to €65.00. Generali +0.20% to €45.72. UniCredit +0.41% to €82.92. 

The outlier was Commerzbank, which fell about 2.6% to €41.35 on 16 September. 

The divergence matters.

UniCredit rose while Commerzbank fell.

That is consistent with a market beginning to distinguish value to the acquirer from value to the target under a harder governance negotiation. It does not prove causality, but it is a signal worth watching.

There is also a second signal.MPS underperformed both Banco BPM and Banca Generali yesterday. That means the two relative ratios we identified earlier — MPS/BPM and MPS/Banca Generali — moved modestly against MPS.

And because those offers are share-based, relative underperformance is not cosmetic.

IT WEAKENS THE CURRENCY OF THE DEFENCE.

The macro background is simultaneously becoming less forgiving. Euro-area yields remain elevated; Germany’s 10-year Bund closed around 3.50% on 16 September, while markets continue to price further ECB tightening. The Federal Reserve raised rates overnight and signalled a still-hawkish stance. 

Higher rates support banking revenues up to a point.

But they also increase:

funding costs,

credit risk,

cost of equity,

and the hurdle rate applied to acquisitions.

That makes capital allocation discipline more valuable.

Risk-adjusted strategic optionality — not personalized investment advice

Best combination of earnings power, capital generation and scale. IVASS is positive; antitrust remedy risk is now the variable to watch.

2. GENERALI — OVERWEIGHT

3. UNICREDIT — OVERWEIGHT

4. BANCO BPM — EVENT-DRIVEN POSITIVE
Its scarcity rises as competing architectures form around it.

5. BANCA GENERALI — EVENT-DRIVEN POSITIVE
Excellent asset economics, but highly dependent on Generali and the wider MPS architecture.

6. MPS — HIGH-CONVEXITY EVENT-DRIVEN
Potentially the largest upside if its coalition forms, but the widest distribution of outcomes.

7. COMMERZBANK — EVENT-DRIVEN / GOVERNANCE SENSITIVE
Takeover optionality remains high; the value of that optionality increasingly depends on the negotiated governance settlement.

6. THREE SCENARIOS

FDK analytical probabilities — not market-implied probabilities

SCENARIO I — CONTINENTAL BIFURCATION

48% | Slightly lower

Intesa ultimately acquires MPS after a negotiated package of competition remedies.

UniCredit obtains control of Commerzbank under a German institutional settlement.

Generali remains independent.

Banco BPM remains anchored around Crédit Agricole.

This is still the central scenario because it produces two plausible European-scale architectures:

INTESA → Continental Capital Allocator

UNICREDIT → Pan-European Banking Platform

Why down from 50%? Because the AGCM investigation adds execution friction to Intesa while the UniCredit–Commerzbank governance fight is becoming sharper. IVASS approval partially offsets that deterioration. 

⸻

SCENARIO II — THE MPS THIRD POLE

22% | Lower

MPS secures the 29 October shareholder mandate, preserves a strong enough equity currency and reaches economically acceptable settlements with Crédit Agricole and Generali.

The architecture becomes:

MPS + MEDIOBANCA + BANCO BPM + BANCA GENERALI

The industrial case remains conceivable.

The coalition remains the problem.

Crédit Agricole’s reported opposition lowers the probability today. 

⸻

SCENARIO III — CONTROL COLLISION

30% | Higher

Intesa advances toward control of MPS, but MPS first obtains approval for one or both counteroffers.

The result is an institution whose ownership changes after its strategic commitments have already changed.

This scenario rises from 25% because regulatory sequencing is becoming more complicated while MPS’s offers remain irrevocable absent their contractual conditions. 

It is the scenario in which lawyers, regulators and sequencing matter more than strategic presentations.

7. POLITICAL AND REGULATORY IMPLICATIONS

The Italian Government’s formally stated position remains neutrality on Intesa–MPS, while Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti has previously said Golden Power conditions could theoretically be imposed, particularly around territorial competition and SME access to banking services.No new Golden Power action on Intesa–MPS has been publicly identified in the last 24 hours. 

At European level, the broader dispute over Italy’s Golden Power framework remains unresolved. Brussels has previously pressed Rome for further changes to ensure national-security powers do not improperly overlap with EU banking and single-market competences. 

This matters because the European banking consolidation debate is now being tested simultaneously in two jurisdictions.

Italy is testing:

how much domestic competition can be sacrificed for Continental Scale.

Germany is testing:

how much national institutional identity can be preserved inside Continental Scale.

These are two sides of the same Banking Union problem.

EUROPE WANTS BIGGER BANKS — BUT MEMBER STATES STILL WANT CONTROL OVER WHAT “BIGGER” MEANS.

⸻

8. THE HIDDEN PATTERN

THE LAW OF SEQUENCING POWER

The previous hidden pattern was Defensive Reflexivity:

Bid → Scarcity → Equity Currency → Counter-bid → Greater Defence

Today we add a second law.

WHEN MULTIPLE TRANSACTIONS COMPETE FOR THE SAME ASSET, SEQUENCE CAN BECOME MORE IMPORTANT THAN PRICE.

The chain is:

REGULATORY CLEARANCE

↓

ABILITY TO LAUNCH

↓

ABILITY TO CREATE CONTRACTUAL COMMITMENTS

↓

REDUCED OPTIONALITY FOR THE OTHER SIDE

↓

GREATER CONTROL

That means value is no longer produced only by paying more.

It can be produced by moving first through the institutional bottlenecks.

The Italian banking Risiko is therefore evolving from:

M&A

to:

M&A + Game Theory + Regulatory Sequencing.

⸻

9. WHAT TO WATCH IN THE NEXT 24–72 HOURS

The first variable is AGCM. Any early indication of the likely scope of remedies — especially branch disposals or restrictions connected with Generali — will directly affect the realizable economics of Intesa–MPS. 

The second is Commerzbank governance. Watch for responses from Bettina Orlopp, Jens Weidmann, Berlin or employee representatives to the Reuters report regarding UniCredit’s desired leadership changes. 

The third is Crédit Agricole language. The first move from “insufficient value” toward discussion of price, governance or industrial conditions would be much more important than another generic MPS statement.

The fourth is the MPS investor campaign. Watch Delfin, Caltagirone, BlackRock, the Treasury and institutional investors carefully. MPS needs a voting coalition, not merely an attractive strategic presentation. 

The fifth remains the two market ratios:

MPS / BANCO BPM

and

MPS / BANCA GENERALI

If both continue to fall, MPS’s defensive currency weakens.

If they turn decisively upward, the economic credibility of the counteroffers strengthens.

And finally, watch European yields. The higher the hurdle rate moves, the less tolerance investors will have for transactions whose synergy capture is compromised by political or regulatory remedies. 

⸻

10. VELOCITY INSIGHT

Yesterday’s development changes the hierarchy of variables.

The first stage of the Italian banking consolidation was about ownership.

The second was about equity currency.

The third is becoming about sequence.

Intesa has scale.

MPS has optionality.

Crédit Agricole has time.

Generali has scarcity.

UniCredit has Continental ambition.

Germany has governance leverage.

Italian regulators control the timetable.

And the market continuously reprices the currency with which every actor is trying to move.

The new equation is therefore:

CAPITAL × EQUITY CURRENCY × REGULATORY SPEED × COALITION SUPPORT = STRATEGIC CONTROL

The critical insight is simple:

THE WINNER MAY NOT BE THE BANK WITH THE HIGHEST BID.

It may be the institution that reaches the point of irreversibility first.

And that is why today the most valuable asset in the Italian banking Risiko is no longer simply capital.

IT IS TIME.

How Agentic AI Is Rewriting Computing, Mobility and the Architecture of Economic Power

THE BIG TAKE — THE END OF THE APPLICATION-CENTRIC AGE

For sixty years, computing has evolved around one enduring principle: humans operate machines through applications.

From IBM mainframes to personal computers, from the internet to smartphones, each technological revolution expanded computing power, connectivity and accessibility without fundamentally overturning that relationship.

The human initiated. The application mediated. The machine executed.

Pervasive Agentic AI is beginning to reverse that relationship.

The emerging paradigm is organized not around applications that users open, but around intelligent agents capable of interpreting intentions, understanding context, coordinating devices and executing tasks across digital and physical environments.

The Apple–Meta collaboration reported this week is an early signal of this transition.

Meta’s Muse Spark Open model has been demonstrated on Apple’s Mac Studio computing architecture using the MLX framework. Meanwhile, Meta is extending multimodal intelligence into the physical world through AI-native smart glasses, while Apple continues expanding its integrated personal computing ecosystem.

Their technologies are converging. Their competitive ambitions are colliding.

But the implications extend far beyond either company.

The smartphone, personal computer, smart glasses, connected home, automobile, robot and industrial machine could progressively become interconnected nodes in a distributed intelligence system.

The application becomes an execution tool. The device becomes an intelligent node. The agent becomes the interface.

This is not simply another generation of personal computing.

It is the emergence of a new computational architecture—and potentially a new economic order.

The next Great Split will increasingly separate companies that manufacture intelligent products from those capable of controlling the intelligence connecting them.

⸻

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

What happens to economic power when intelligence is no longer confined to individual machines, applications or cloud platforms—but becomes pervasive across the physical world?

The answer could redefine competitive advantage across technology, automotive manufacturing, mobility, financial services and industrial infrastructure.

The decisive shift is from computing as a product toward intelligence as a continuously available, distributed capability.

That is why this time is different.

⸻

I. SIXTY YEARS OF COMPUTING — AND THE END OF ITS ORGANIZING PRINCIPLE

The IBM 3090, introduced in 1985, represented a mature expression of the mainframe era, whose origins extend to the 1960s.

Computing was centralized. Applications performed defined functions. Users submitted instructions.

The personal computer decentralized access.

The internet connected information.

The smartphone made applications mobile.

Cloud computing centralized computational resources again, this time across globally distributed data centres.

Generative AI introduced increasingly capable conversational interfaces.

But the next transition is more fundamental.

Agentic AI changes the unit of computing from the instruction to the objective.

Instead of opening several applications to organize a journey, the user delegates the desired outcome.

Instead of manually coordinating a calendar, transport provider, hotel, expense system and communications, an authorized agent could increasingly orchestrate the workflow.

The applications remain necessary.

But their economic position changes.

They become resources invoked by agents rather than destinations selected by humans.

This introduces a new competitive hierarchy.

The value of owning an application may decline relative to the value of controlling the agent that decides when and how the application is used.

The next great platform may not be the operating system that launches applications.It may be the intelligence system that orchestrates them.

⸻

II. WHY THIS TIME IS DIFFERENT

Three structural developments distinguish Pervasive Agentic AI from previous computing revolutions.

1 | Intelligence becomes goal-directed

Conventional computing executes specified operations.

Agentic computing can interpret an objective, break it into tasks, select tools, monitor results and adjust subsequent actions.

The human role moves progressively from direct operation toward delegation and supervision.

This does not imply unrestricted autonomy. High-value deployment will require permissions, verification, accountability and reliable human intervention.

But it changes the economics of interaction.

The relevant measure becomes the cost, reliability and value of a successfully completed task—not simply the number of application sessions or model tokens consumed.

2 | Intelligence becomes contextual

Smart glasses, vehicles, connected homes and industrial systems introduce access to physical context.

A wearable can provide visual and audio information.

A vehicle can provide location, navigation and operating conditions.

A home system can coordinate authorized environmental functions.

An enterprise system can provide schedules, documents and workflows.

With appropriate permissions, these sources can support agents that respond to changing circumstances rather than isolated user commands.

Computing begins to move from episodic interaction toward continuity of context.

3 | Intelligence becomes distributed

No single device must perform every computational task.

Smart glasses can provide immediate perception.

A smartphone can manage identity, communications and personal context.

A personal computer can supply more powerful local inference.

Cloud infrastructure can execute demanding reasoning.

A vehicle can provide mobility and physical execution.

The strategic advantage increasingly lies in coordinating these capabilities.

The next computing platform may be the system connecting devices—not any individual device.

⸻

III. APPLE–META — CONVERGENCE BELOW, COLLISION ABOVE

The Apple–Meta collaboration is important because it reveals how the emerging architecture could evolve.

Meta’s Muse Spark Open model has been demonstrated on Apple’s Mac Studio infrastructure using the MLX framework.

The immediate achievement is technical: sophisticated models can be deployed on specialized local computing systems rather than exclusively through remote cloud infrastructure.

It does not establish that the same model can run economically on ordinary smartphones or smart glasses, nor does it constitute an announced cross-device Apple–Meta consumer integration.

Nevertheless, the direction matters.

Apple brings control of silicon, operating systems, personal devices, identity and software integration.

Meta brings advanced AI research, open-weight model distribution, social platforms and an expanding smart-glasses ecosystem.

At the model and infrastructure layers, their capabilities can be complementary.

At the interface layer, their ambitions increasingly compete.

Apple seeks to preserve control of the personal computing environment. Meta seeks to establish a new interface between human perception and digital intelligence.

The strategic collision will emerge around personal context, agent permissions, identity, payments and the authority to execute actions.

The most plausible competitive architecture is therefore not exclusive partnership or complete separation.

It is selective interoperability combined with intense platform rivalry.

⸻

IV. THE NEW ECOSYSTEM — FROM CONNECTED DEVICES TO COORDINATED INTELLIGENCE

The Internet of Things connected physical objects to networks.

Pervasive Agentic AI could go further by enabling authorized devices and software agents to coordinate objectives and actions.

The distinction is fundamental.

Connected devices exchange data.

Coordinated agents can potentially exchange context, tasks, permissions and verified outcomes.

Consider a future business journey.An enterprise agent identifies a meeting commitment.

A personal agent prepares the itinerary.

Smart glasses provide contextual navigation.

A vehicle receives the destination and adjusts the route.

A hotel system manages authorized check-in.

A home agent coordinates arrival and departure settings.

The user need not manually manage every application or device.

The system maintains continuity across environments.

This requires secure identity, interoperability, reliable communication and explicit control over sensitive information.

It is not yet a universal commercial capability.

But it defines the architecture toward which the industry may be moving.

The value of the ecosystem increasingly resides in continuity of intelligence rather than ownership of individual devices.

⸻

V. THE AUTOMOBILE BECOMES AN AGENTIC PLATFORM

The next automotive battle is over software architecture, customer access and recurring economic value.

The transformation is already visible in automotive investment decisions.

Toyota has announced plans to deploy its Arene software platform across 50 vehicle models and approximately 10 million new vehicles annually by 2030.

Volkswagen and Rivian are developing a shared zonal electronics and software architecture.

Mercedes-Benz and Nvidia have extended their collaboration in automated driving.

Rivian is developing a proprietary vehicle-agent framework capable of supporting selected third-party AI agents.

Apple’s CarPlay Ultra demonstrates how an external computing ecosystem can extend across vehicle displays and selected vehicle functions.

These initiatives differ in scope, maturity and commercial status. They do not establish that all participating vehicles possess comparable autonomous or agentic capabilities.

But they reveal a common direction.

Automobile manufacturers are increasingly competing over who controls the vehicle’s software architecture—and therefore the foundation for future intelligent services.

The automotive industry is moving beyond the addition of digital features to conventional vehicles.

The strategic objective is increasingly to create vehicles whose capabilities can evolve through software, secure connectivity and AI-enabled services.

This changes the economics of automotive competition.

The traditional industry emphasized manufacturing scale, engineering, brand, distribution and physical performance.

The emerging industry increasingly adds software control, computing architecture, customer identity, agentic orchestration, recurring digital services and fleet intelligence.

The next competitive frontier is not simply the software-defined vehicle.

It is the agentically coordinated mobility ecosystem.

⸻

VI. THE GREAT AUTOMOTIVE VALUE MIGRATION

The automotive value chain is developing four distinct economic layers.

Layer I — Physical mobility

Vehicle engineering, batteries, powertrains, manufacturing, safety systems and distribution.

Layer II — Embedded vehicle intelligence

Vehicle operating systems, sensors, diagnostics, over-the-air updates, driver assistance and automated-driving capabilities.

Layer III — Agentic orchestration

Personal mobility agents coordinating destinations, preferences, navigation, energy requirements and authorized interactions with external services.

Layer IV — Mobility distribution and monetization

Charging, parking, insurance, subscriptions, fleet management, payments and multimodal journey services.

The potential migration of economic value occurs primarily between layers three and four.

A platform that controls mobility intent can influence which vehicle is selected, which service is purchased, which charging network is used and how the journey is organized.

That creates a new source of bargaining power.

But the outcome is not predetermined.

Vehicle manufacturers retain substantial advantages in physical engineering, safety certification, embedded systems, manufacturing and customer relationships.The critical question is whether they can retain control of the agentic layer or become increasingly dependent on external platforms.

The manufacturer that builds the vehicle may not be the institution capturing the highest-margin relationship with the traveler.

This is the automotive equivalent of the earlier shift in smartphones, where operating systems and digital distribution became central sources of economic value.

The parallel is instructive, but not exact.

Automobiles are regulated, safety-critical machines with longer product lives, higher capital intensity and more demanding operational requirements.

That makes control of the vehicle’s embedded architecture particularly important.

⸻

VII. RIVIAN — THE EARLY TEST OF AN OPEN AGENTIC VEHICLE ARCHITECTURE

Rivian’s recent disclosures offer an instructive example.

The company has described an in-house orchestration system capable of routing requests between local vehicle models and cloud-based AI, depending on the complexity of the task.

It also plans to support selected external AI agents.

But Rivian has emphasized an essential boundary.

External agents will not receive authority over safety-critical steering or braking functions.

That distinction defines the practical difference between consumer agentic computing and autonomous vehicle control.

An agent may coordinate navigation, cabin preferences, charging information or authorized services.

Safety-critical driving functions remain governed by separate systems requiring rigorous validation and regulatory compliance.

A sophisticated conversational interface does not establish autonomous-driving capability.

Nor does autonomous-driving technology automatically create a profitable mobility-services platform.

These are distinct technologies and business models.

Rivian’s approach illustrates a potentially important strategic compromise:

Open the vehicle to external intelligence without surrendering control of its operating architecture.

That may become a defining competitive model.

VIII. APPLE–META — THE COLLISION MOVES INTO MOBILITY

Apple and Meta approach automotive computing from different strategic positions.

Apple — The trusted personal ecosystem

Apple could extend its device, identity, payment and agent infrastructure into mobility.

The vehicle becomes another environment in which personal intelligence operates.

The advantage is continuity across devices, applications and personal information.

Meta — The perceptual interface

Meta’s smart glasses could become a natural interface for navigation, contextual information, communication and mobility-related tasks.

The advantage is immediate interaction with the physical environment.

Automakers — Control of the physical system

Vehicle manufacturers can defend their position through embedded software, safety-critical systems, differentiated vehicle intelligence and direct customer relationships.

A future interaction could follow this sequence:

Smart glasses identify a destination → personal agent prepares the journey → vehicle agent receives an authorized request → vehicle software executes supported functions → personal agent maintains continuity after arrival.

This is a prospective architecture, not an announced Apple–Meta automotive product.

The commercial conflict emerges over three questions.

Who owns the user relationship?

Who authorizes cross-device actions?

Who captures the economic value of mobility transactions?

The automobile becomes the meeting point of personal intelligence, wearable intelligence and vehicle intelligence.

IX. THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS — THE GREAT SPLIT ENTERS THE PHYSICAL ECONOMY

Five consequences deserve particular attention.

First — the economic unit shifts from the device to the completed outcome. Value increasingly depends on what an agent accomplishes rather than which application or device performs the task.

Second — software distribution becomes more powerful. The platform coordinating agents may influence which services, vehicles and providers are selected.Third — physical assets become programmable. Vehicles, buildings, robots and infrastructure can increasingly participate in coordinated workflows.

Fourth — computing demand becomes more pervasive. Distributed intelligence increases demand for efficient processors, memory, networking, secure identity and edge infrastructure.

Fifth — capital concentration may accelerate. Companies controlling agent orchestration, trusted interfaces and large ecosystems could capture a disproportionate share of economic value.

This creates a reinforcing competitive mechanism:

More devices → More authorized context → Better agent execution → Greater user reliance → Stronger platform position.

The loop is not inevitable. Trust, interoperability, regulation and competition can constrain concentration.

But the strategic direction is consequential.

The Great Split is moving from technological capability toward control of the intelligent systems coordinating economic activity.

⸻

X. WHAT’S NEXT — THREE COMPETITIVE SCENARIOS

Scenario I — Hybrid convergence

Apple, Meta, Google, Microsoft and automotive manufacturers support selective interoperability.

Personal agents operate across devices while cloud services supply additional computational capacity.

This could expand the overall market while preserving competition across hardware and software layers.

Scenario II — Platform concentration

A small number of companies control dominant agentic interfaces.

Applications, devices and mobility providers become increasingly dependent on these platforms for customer access.

Economic value migrates toward identity, orchestration, distribution and recurring services.

Scenario III — Fragmented intelligence ecosystems

Privacy, cybersecurity, regulatory restrictions and proprietary technologies prevent seamless coordination.

Consumers and enterprises operate multiple agents across incompatible environments.

Adoption continues, but the full productivity potential of pervasive computing develops more slowly.

The most consequential variables will be interoperability, cost per completed task, user trust and the reliability of autonomous execution.

⸻

XI. THE INVESTOR HIERARCHY

The new computing paradigm could create a different hierarchy of economic value.

Tier I — Agentic operating environments

Companies controlling identity, permissions, orchestration and cross-device distribution.

Tier II — AI-native interfaces

Smart glasses, wearables, voice systems and contextual computing platforms.

Tier III — Distributed computing infrastructure

Efficient silicon, memory, networking, local inference and secure edge computing.

Tier IV — Intelligent physical systems

Automotive platforms, robotics, industrial automation and connected infrastructure.

Tier V — Productivity capture

Companies applying agentic intelligence to generate measurable improvements in operating performance.

For investors, the key metrics will increasingly include cost per successfully completed agentic task, reliability, human-intervention requirements, cross-device interoperability, user retention, trusted access to personal context, recurring revenues and free-cash-flow conversion.

The most vulnerable business models may be those relying on application-level distribution without differentiated data, workflow ownership or integration into agentic environments.

⸻

XII. FIVE KEY TAKEAWAYS FOR INVESTORS

1. THE AGENTIC PLATFORM BECOMES THE NEW CONTROL POINT.

The highest strategic premium may migrate from individual devices and applications toward platforms controlling identity, permissions, contextual intelligence and autonomous execution.

Prioritize companies capable of orchestrating intelligence across multiple devices and services, while demonstrating customer retention and recurring economic returns.

2. THE APPLICATION ECONOMY FACES A STRUCTURAL REPRICING.

As agents increasingly select and operate applications on behalf of users, some traditional software distribution advantages may weaken.Differentiate application companies that own critical business processes from those dependent primarily on user-interface engagement.

3. AUTOMOTIVE VALUE WILL INCREASINGLY FOLLOW CONTROL OF INTELLIGENCE.

The automobile is becoming a connected computational environment. Vehicle manufacturers that retain control of embedded software, agent orchestration and customer relationships may capture new recurring revenues.

Those surrendering these capabilities risk becoming increasingly dependent on external technology platforms.

Revalue automotive companies according to software architecture, customer ownership, recurring-service economics and capital productivity—not vehicle deliveries alone.

4. DISTRIBUTED AI CREATES A NEW INFRASTRUCTURE SUPERCYCLE.

Pervasive Agentic AI will require increasingly efficient processors, memory, connectivity, cybersecurity and local inference capabilities.

The opportunity extends beyond hyperscale data centres toward a distributed network of personal, enterprise, automotive and industrial computing.

Follow the companies reducing the cost of reliable inference across devices and environments.

5. THE NEXT GREAT SPLIT WILL BE BETWEEN ECOSYSTEM CONTROLLERS AND ECOSYSTEM DEPENDENTS.

Companies controlling agent distribution, trusted identity, interoperability and customer access may capture a growing share of economic value.

Companies supplying undifferentiated hardware or services risk losing bargaining power.

The decisive valuation test is the ability to convert ecosystem influence into sustainable free cash flow and returns on invested capital.

FINAL TAKE — THE END OF THE APPLICATION EMPIRE

The Apple–Meta collaboration is not simply another alliance between technology companies.

It is an early indication that the organizing principle of computing may be changing.

For sixty years, the computer was the center of the system.

Applications determined how humans interacted with it.

Operating systems controlled access.

Hardware manufacturers competed to make devices faster, smaller and more powerful.

Pervasive Agentic AI challenges that entire hierarchy.

The next computing environment will increasingly be organized around human intentions rather than individual applications.

Intelligence will move across devices. Agents will coordinate workflows. Vehicles, homes, wearables and industrial machines will become interconnected participants in a distributed computational ecosystem.

And the most valuable competitive position may shift toward whoever controls the trusted intelligence coordinating them.

This is where Apple and Meta become strategically consequential.

Apple approaches the future through control of the personal computing ecosystem.

Meta approaches it through wearable perception, multimodal intelligence and new forms of human interaction.

Their technologies can converge. Their competition for the human interface will intensify.

The automobile illustrates the magnitude of the transformation.

The traditional automotive industry created value by designing, manufacturing and distributing vehicles.

The emerging mobility economy will increasingly create value through software, intelligence, autonomous capabilities and coordinated services.

The manufacturer may continue to build the machine. The agentic platform may increasingly control the relationship with the customer.

That is the potential migration of economic power.

And it extends far beyond automotive manufacturing.

The same transformation could reshape banking, healthcare, retail, logistics, robotics and industrial infrastructure.

The first computing age centralized processing.

The second democratized access.

The third connected people, applications and devices.

The fourth will distribute intelligence throughout the physical world.

The next competitive battle will therefore not be decided by which company sells the most devices, builds the largest AI model or controls the most applications.It will be decided by which companies can coordinate intelligence across environments, earn the trust required to act and convert autonomous execution into measurable economic value.

THE APPLICATION-CENTRIC AGE IS GIVING WAY TO THE AGENT-CENTRIC ECONOMY.

THE NEXT GREAT MIGRATION OF VALUE WILL BE FROM THE MACHINES WE OPERATE TO THE INTELLIGENCE THAT OPERATES THEM.

That is the emerging computational paradigm.

That is the next frontier of capital formation.

And that is where The Great Split enters its most consequential phase.

THE PRODUCTIVITY SPREAD
THE BIG TAKE
GVI: 108.0 | US: 94.0 ↓0.1 | Germany: 83.3 ↑0.1 | No ranking changes | Confidence: 96%
The Evidence
The message from today’s data is decisive:
Growth is holding. The price of growth is not.
US services remain strong: ISM 54.9, new orders 59.8. But prices paid surged to 74.0, the highest since 2022, while export orders collapsed to 46.9. Simultaneously, the US 10-year Treasury touched 5.35%.
Germany moves the other way. Its composite PMI rose to 53.8, the strongest in almost a year; services returned decisively to expansion, employment increased and investment demand strengthened.
The result:
US −0.1 to 94.0. Germany +0.1 to 83.3. Global GVI unchanged at 108.0.
The AI Supercycle is not running out of growth. It is running into the price of capital.

Five Signals
1. 
America: demand without disinflation
The US economy is still expanding strongly. That is no longer unequivocally bullish.
Strong orders + rising prices + 5.35% Treasuries mean the economy is too resilient for capital to become cheap again quickly.
That is today’s principal decelerator.
2. 
Germany: the industrial recovery broadens
Germany is no longer merely a manufacturing rebound.
Services, employment and investment are joining the recovery. The composite PMI at 53.8 crosses the GVI threshold for a +0.1 upgrade.
Europe’s industrial core is beginning to regain Velocity.
3. 
Europe: growth returns—but so does inflation
Euro-area composite activity reached 53.1, its strongest since April 2023, while export orders expanded for the first time in 39 months.
But pricing pressure is rising simultaneously.
Europe is therefore escaping stagnation without escaping the higher-for-longer regime.
4. 
Industrial AI: the conversion race begins
Schneider Electric’s agreed $22.6 billion PTC acquisition captures the next phase perfectly: electrical infrastructure is converging with engineering software, industrial data and AI.
The market’s reaction was equally important: Schneider shares fell sharply.
Strategic logic is no longer enough.
The market wants returns.
5. 
The 5% hurdle becomes structural
The 10-year Treasury above 5% is no longer a temporary valuation disturbance.
It is becoming an industrial selection mechanism.
At these rates, weak AI projects do not merely receive lower multiples.
They become uneconomic.
The Second-Order Effects
I — Higher rates accelerate AI concentration
Cash-rich leaders can self-finance.
Weaker competitors must borrow.
At 5%+ sovereign yields, that difference compounds rapidly.
The result:
HIGHER RATES → HIGHER WACC → FEWER VIABLE PROJECTS → MORE M&A → GREATER CONCENTRATION
The AI ecosystem may become more concentrated precisely as intelligence becomes more commoditised.
II — Capital rotates from intelligence to 
conversion
The first AI trade rewarded models.
Then GPUs.
Then memory, networking and power.
The next rotation is toward businesses that turn intelligence into operating economics:
INDUSTRIAL SOFTWARE → AUTOMATION → ROBOTICS → DIGITAL TWINS → PRODUCTIVITY
That is why Schneider–PTC matters.
The next scarcity is not AI.
It is implementation at scale.
III — Germany could become Europe’s conversion trade
Europe is unlikely to outspend America on frontier models.
It does not need to.
Germany’s opportunity sits where Europe already possesses deep comparative advantage:ENGINEERING + AUTOMATION + INDUSTRIAL SOFTWARE + POWER SYSTEMS + MANUFACTURING
If AI moves from model creation toward industrial deployment, Europe’s apparent weakness can become an advantage.
IV — Strong growth can become bearish for duration
This is the market paradox.
Better growth normally supports equities.
But when inflation remains elevated:
STRONGER GROWTH → HIGHER YIELDS → HIGHER DISCOUNT RATE → LOWER LONG-DURATION MULTIPLES
The winners therefore migrate toward businesses producing current cash flow, not distant optionality.
V — The market rotation becomes broader
The likely sequence is now:
AI NARRATIVE → AI INFRASTRUCTURE → AI BOTTLENECKS → INDUSTRIAL AI → PRODUCTIVITY
This is not an exit from AI.
It is AI spreading into the rest of the economy.
And that may ultimately be the largest phase of the Supercycle.
Five Takeaways
1. Stop waiting for cheap capital.
The economy is too resilient and inflation too persistent. Build portfolios and investment cases around 5%+ long rates.
2. Move from AI exposure to AI conversion.
The next premium belongs to companies turning intelligence into margins, productivity and cash flow.
3. Germany is becoming Europe’s most important conversion signal.
Its recovery now spans manufacturing, services, employment and investment.
4. Industrial software becomes strategic infrastructure.
The layer connecting intelligence to physical assets may become more valuable than another incremental model.
5. ROIC is now the final AI benchmark.
For investors and boards:
INTELLIGENCE → UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW → ROIC > WACC
Anything that fails this chain is not transformation.
It is expensive experimentation.
Financial Verdict
GVI: 108.0. Growth survives. Cheap capital does not.
The AI Supercycle is entering its most financially demanding phase.
The market will increasingly stop rewarding how much intelligence companies deploy—and start rewarding the spread between what that intelligence earns and what the capital behind it costs.
The next AI winners will not own the most intelligence. They will own the widest productivity spread.

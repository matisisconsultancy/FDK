The Great Split is moving from earnings to the cost of capital. AI still has extraordinary growth. What it no longer has is a free discount rate.
The Fundamental Question
What if the next bottleneck of the AI Supercycle is neither chips nor electricity—but the risk-free rate itself?
That is the question markets are confronting this morning.
The U.S. 30-year Treasury yield reached 5.3371% on Tuesday, its highest in nearly two decades, before easing toward 5.28% in Asian trading. German 10- and 30-year yields have reached their highest levels since 2011, France’s 30-year yield has risen almost 50 basis points since the end of June, and Japan’s 10-year yield is approaching 3% for the first time since the mid-1990s.
At the same time, Brent is above $91, the Strait of Hormuz remains deeply impaired, and hyperscalers are issuing record amounts of debt to finance AI infrastructure. Reuters explicitly identifies heavy borrowing for data centers and AI investment as another source of competition for global capital alongside swelling sovereign issuance.
Tuesday’s market reaction was revealing. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 5%; Micron lost 7%, Nvidia 2.3%, Sandisk 9% and Western Digital 7.4%. Energy rose 1.8%, healthcare 1.6% and consumer staples 1.1%. The Nasdaq fell 1.33%.
Yet overnight in Shanghai, Unitree—the Chinese humanoid-robotics leader—rose almost sixfold on its stock-market debut, briefly valuing the company near $50 billion.
The message is not that the AI Supercycle is ending.
The price investors are willing to pay for future intelligence is becoming increasingly dependent on the price of present capital.
That is a much more consequential regime.

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy
The risk-free rate has become an AI variable
Verified fact. Long-term borrowing costs are rising across the United States, Europe and Japan even as weaker U.S. employment and consumption have reduced expectations of another near-term Fed move. Investors cite sovereign borrowing, inflation risk from Middle East energy disruption, weaker foreign appetite for Treasuries and heavy corporate financing for AI infrastructure among the forces lifting yields.
FDK interpretation.
The transmission mechanism is changing.
For the first phase of the Supercycle:
AI growth → higher earnings expectations → higher valuations.
The next phase adds another chain:
AI CAPEX → MORE DEBT → COMPETITION FOR CAPITAL → HIGHER TERM PREMIUM → HIGHER WACC → LOWER PRESENT VALUE OF FUTURE PROFITS.
This is why Tuesday’s semiconductor correction matters.
It was not evidence that demand for accelerated computing disappeared overnight.
It was evidence that the discount rate can overpower even exceptional structural growth.
The most important AI multiple may therefore become:
return on invested AI capital ÷ cost of capital.
Not revenue growth alone.
⸻AI is building a credit system around itself
Anthropic provides this morning’s clearest signal.
Verified fact. Its annualized revenue exceeded $65 billion at the end of July. Yet the company is simultaneously assembling a revolving bank facility potentially exceeding $10 billion, with banks reportedly seeking large commitments partly to position themselves for a future IPO. Anthropic has confidentially filed for that IPO and is forecasting dramatically higher revenues through 2028.
Alphabet is following the same broader migration toward external capital. It recently raised $25 billion in dollar bonds, roughly $85 billion of equity earlier this year and is preparing its first Australian-dollar bond as AI spending pushes hyperscalers beyond the purely self-financed model that defined Big Tech for much of the previous decade.
FDK interpretation.
AI has moved through three financing eras:
venture capital → hyperscaler cash flow → global credit markets.
We are now entering the fourth:
AI-specific financial architecture.
Supplier guarantees, project finance, infrastructure funds, private credit, bank revolvers, multi-currency bonds and eventually securitized infrastructure can all lower financing friction inside the AI ecosystem.
That creates a new competitive advantage:
financial engineering capable of turning future AI demand into present productive capacity.
Banks are no longer peripheral to the AI Supercycle.
They are becoming part of the technology stack.

The semiconductor selloff marks a transition from AI beta to AI economics
Tuesday’s 5% fall in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index was violent but analytically useful. European technology also fell 2.5%, with Infineon down 7.6% and Aixtron 8.8%.
Verified fact. There was no comparable collapse in underlying AI demand overnight. Cerebras this morning launched its new CS-4 inference system and says it plans to deploy 600 megawatts of capacity by end-2027, targeting major improvements in inference throughput.
FDK interpretation.
The market is beginning to separate two concepts it treated as synonymous:
AI demand and AI equity returns.
Demand can continue rising while equity multiples fall.
That happens whenever:
earnings velocity < increase in discount rate + increase in expectations.
The next phase of the Supercycle will therefore reward less generic AI exposure and more demonstrable:
**pricing power
utilization
free cash flow
balance-sheet strength
capital efficiency.**
This is not the end of the AI trade.
It is the end of the easiest version of it.

China is taking Physical AI into the capital markets
Unitree may be the most strategically interesting market event of the morning.
Verified fact. The humanoid-robot company finished its Shanghai morning session at 883.87 yuan versus a 150.8-yuan IPO price, valuing it around $50 billion. Unitree is already profitable, but Reuters notes that few of its robots are yet operating in commercial industrial environments and many sales still go to universities and research institutions.
At least six other Chinese humanoid-robotics companies are preparing listings. Unitree’s debut also coincides with the World Robot Conference in Beijing and with China explicitly promoting robotics as a response to its shrinking workforce.
FDK interpretation.
This is a crucial evolution.
The AI Supercycle is leaving the cloud and meeting the physical world.
But Unitree also reveals the next investment challenge:
embodied intelligence valuation is moving faster than embodied intelligence deployment.
The eventual winners will not be determined by which robot can dance, jump or run fastest.
They will be determined by measurable industrial economics:
cost per productive hour × autonomy × uptime × safety × energy efficiency × labor substitution × asset utilization.
China’s advantage is increasingly its ability to connect innovation to manufacturing scale and then to capital markets.
Europe must recognize that as a competitive system, not simply a robotics story.

Europe’s Great Split is becoming a contest between earnings strength and capital scarcityVerified fact. The STOXX 600 fell 0.69% Tuesday to a two-week low as Germany’s 10-year Bund yield reached 3.261%, its highest since 2011. Technology fell 2.5%, while energy gained 0.4%.
Yet the underlying corporate picture remains considerably stronger than the market narrative suggests. Current estimates put STOXX 600 Q2 earnings growth around 23.4%, and about 12.3% even excluding energy.
Europe simultaneously remains exposed to the Hormuz energy shock. Earlier this month EU gas inventories were only 57% full, a record low for that point in the year, creating added winter sensitivity to LNG availability and pricing.
FDK interpretation.
Europe now contains both sides of the Next Economy.
The accelerating side:
banks energy grids defense industrial automation rail and infrastructure Industrial AI.
And the decelerating side:
energy-intensive industry long-duration technology without cash flow leveraged companies consumption exposed to energy inflation.
Higher Bund yields will force investors to distinguish between them.
This could ultimately be constructive.
Capital scarcity imposes strategic discipline.
Europe’s problem is not simply that it needs more investment.
It needs investment with returns above a rapidly rising European hurdle rate.

Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines
The first hidden pattern: AI is beginning to crowd into the sovereign capital market
Governments and hyperscalers increasingly want the same thing at the same time:
long-duration capital.
Governments need it for deficits, defense, energy resilience and ageing populations.
Technology companies need it for accelerated computing, power, data centers and networks.
Reuters now explicitly identifies heavy AI-related corporate borrowing as one of the factors increasing competition for investor capital.
That produces a powerful recursive chain:
AI investment → debt issuance → capital competition → higher yields → higher WACC → weaker non-AI investment → greater productivity divergence.
The AI Supercycle can therefore accelerate itself while simultaneously making capital more expensive for the rest of the economy.
That is a radically different interpretation from the conventional bubble debate.
⸻
The second hidden pattern: The Great Split is migrating from equity markets into balance sheets
The first Great Split rewarded AI equities.
The second rewarded companies generating AI-driven profits.
The third may reward companies possessing superior financing capacity.
The distinction increasingly becomes:
cash-rich / strategically financed / guaranteed / investment-grade
versus
refinancing-dependent / low-return / highly leveraged.
Higher Treasury and Bund yields turn that difference into a competitive weapon.
Capital access determines investment.
Investment determines productivity.
Productivity determines cash flow.
Cash flow determines future capital access.
The Great Split is becoming recursive.
⸻
The third hidden pattern: Physical AI could be the next Great Value Migration
Unitree’s extraordinary debut reveals enormous market appetite for the next application layer of intelligence.
The progression is becoming visible:
LLMs → inference → agents → Industrial AI → robotics → autonomous physical systems.
But the scarce assets change as AI enters the physical world.
The bottlenecks become motors, sensors, batteries, precision components, machine vision, manufacturing scale, electricity and field data.
This could ultimately broaden the AI Supercycle far beyond today’s semiconductor leadership.
⸻
Likely Market and Competitive Implications Today
The immediate regime remains higher-yield defensive rotation.
Tuesday’s market already showed the template: semiconductors and long-duration technology sold off while energy, healthcare and consumer staples gained. Unless long-end yields retreat decisively, that relative pattern can persist.
Within AI, however, the structural opportunity remains intact.The distinction should increasingly favor profitable semiconductor leaders, networking, inference infrastructure, electrical equipment, power generation and companies with contracted demand over businesses whose valuations require perpetual cheap capital.
For Europe, the opportunity becomes more selective rather than weaker. Banks with strong deposit franchises, energy infrastructure, defense, grids, rail and industrial automation can benefit from the emerging capital regime, while highly indebted and energy-intensive businesses face a double penalty from higher financing and operating costs. This is an analytical inference from the current yield, energy and earnings configuration.
For China, Unitree’s debut reinforces the strategic premium being attached to robotics, advanced manufacturing and Physical AI, but its enormous first-day valuation also raises the hurdle for future commercial execution.
And for the global market, duration itself is becoming a sector.
Companies able to deliver cash today gain relative value against companies promising it ten years from now.

The Velocity Insight
The biggest mistake today would be to interpret Tuesday’s semiconductor selloff as evidence that the AI Supercycle has exhausted itself.
Something more important is happening.
The financial architecture around AI is changing.
Anthropic is generating revenue at a scale almost unprecedented for a company of its age—and simultaneously assembling a multibillion-dollar bank facility.
Alphabet is moving across global bond markets.
Nvidia is using its balance sheet to support infrastructure development.
Governments are issuing enormous quantities of sovereign debt.
Oil above $90 is rebuilding an inflation premium.
And investors are demanding more compensation to lock up capital for decades.
The next recursive chain of the Next Economy is therefore becoming:
AI SUPER-CYCLE → CAPITAL FORMATION → DEBT ISSUANCE → COMPETITION FOR SAVINGS → HIGHER REAL YIELDS → HIGHER WACC → CAPITAL SELECTIVITY → PRODUCTIVITY DIVERGENCE → THE GREAT SPLIT.
But there is another chain running simultaneously:CHEAPER INTELLIGENCE → MORE INFERENCE → MORE INDUSTRIAL AI → MORE ROBOTICS → HIGHER PRODUCTIVITY → STRONGER CASH FLOWS → GREATER CAPITAL ACCESS.
These two loops will increasingly determine winners and losers.
The question is no longer whether AI creates value.
It does.
The question is who can capture that value after paying the full physical and financial cost of industrializing intelligence.
The cost of intelligence continues to fall.
The hurdle rate for turning intelligence into competitive advantage is rising.
That is why the bond market—not the chatbot—may now be telling us the most important story about the next phase of the AI Supercycle.

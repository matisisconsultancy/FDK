SECTOR ALLOCATION MAP
The next allocation opportunity is not simply technology versus non-technology.
It is the migration of economics across the AI stack.
Allocation Zone Structural Thesis Financial Test Representative Exposure
POWER & GRID AI demand collides with constrained electrical capacity Pricing power + backlog conversion + time-to-power Schneider Electric · Siemens Energy
ADVANCED SEMICONDUCTOR INFRASTRUCTURE Compute growth increases dependence on manufacturing and packaging bottlenecks Utilization + pricing + capital discipline TSMC
COOLING & DATA-CENTER INFRASTRUCTURE Higher compute density raises thermal and physical requirements Orders → deployment → cash conversion Vertiv / infrastructure ecosystem
HYPERSCALE CLOUD Capacity becomes valuable only when enterprise demand fills it Capex → utilization → FCF Amazon · Microsoft
ENTERPRISE DISTRIBUTION Cheaper intelligence increases the value of customer and workflow ownership Monetization per installed user / workflow Microsoft · Meta
AGENTIC SECURITY Greater AI autonomy creates permanent governance and security demand Recurring revenue + operating leverage Palo Alto Networks
GOVERNED AI DEPLOYMENT Enterprises pay for operationalization, not model access alone Deployment → recurring revenue → FCF Palantir
FRONTIER MODELS Strategic importance remains high, but capital requirements escalate Revenue growth must outrun compute obligations Anthropic
CAPITAL-INTENSIVE LEGACY INDUSTRIES Transformation capital competes with repair capital ROIC recovery + balance-sheet flexibility Autos · chemicals · other repair-mode sectors
The allocation hierarchy is therefore shifting:
MODEL EXPOSURE → BOTTLENECK OWNERSHIP → UTILIZATION → DISTRIBUTION → SECURITY → CASH
WHERE CONVICTION SHOULD RISE
Scarcity owners with pricing power.
Power, packaging, cooling and grid infrastructure sit closest to unavoidable constraints.
Infrastructure with visible utilization.
Hyperscale capex becomes attractive only when demand converts rapidly into recurring cash.
Distribution with monetization.
As models converge, customer ownership and workflow integration gain bargaining power.
Security with recurring economics.
Agentic adoption structurally expands identities, permissions and attack surfaces.
WHERE THE BURDEN OF PROOF IS RISING
Frontier-model economics.
Technological leadership increasingly has to outrun fixed compute commitments.
Capacity built ahead of demand.
Unutilized infrastructure converts strategic ambition into depreciation and duration risk.
Legacy transformation stories.
Capital used to repair existing businesses competes directly with capital available for new profit pools.
The allocation shift is not from technology to infrastructure. It is from technological promise to economic necessity.


⸻


BOARD-LEVEL IMPLICATIONS
Boards should govern AI as a capital-allocation discipline, not a technology program.
Five questions matter:
Is AI capital earning above its cost?
How much installed capacity is actually utilized?
Where is the binding bottleneck?
Is time-to-cash shortening?
Is capital funding future profit pools—or repairing the past?
The board test is simple:
Does the next dollar of AI investment generate cash faster than it expands the asset base?
If not, scale may be increasing while value creation deteriorates.


⸻


FIVE TAKEAWAYS
1. AI is becoming a financial selection mechanism.
Technology leadership without superior returns will be repriced.
2.Scarcity is moving toward the physical stack.
Power, packaging, cooling and grids increasingly control the economics of deployment.
3. Capital productivity is replacing capex as the key test.
The decisive variable is the speed from investment to utilization to cash.
4. Higher-for-longer raises the cost of being early.
Idle capacity, delayed power and distant monetization become increasingly expensive.
5. The next rotation happens inside AI.
Models → Bottlenecks → Utilization → Distribution → Security → Cash

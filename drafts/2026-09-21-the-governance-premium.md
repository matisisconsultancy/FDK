The control test moves to Milan. Ownership is becoming easier. Governability is becoming scarce.

21 September 2026 | FDK
Data cut: 09:20 CEST

The Italian Banking Risiko opens the week with no new binding move from MPS or Intesa, but with one important hard catalyst: UniCredit’s extraordinary shareholders’ meeting begins today at 10:00 CEST in Milan. The agenda is not a new vote on Commerzbank itself; it concerns governance changes, authority for up to €5 billion equivalent of convertible AT1 instruments, and a separate capital authorization linked to Total Return Swap settlements. Yet the timing matters. UniCredit is entering the final phase of its Commerzbank campaign while simultaneously expanding the capital and governance machinery available to the group. 

The wider Italian contest remains where it ended on Friday: Intesa has the cleanest execution path; MPS has the most ambitious alternative architecture; Crédit Agricole has the strongest blocking optionality; Generali is becoming the strategic centre of gravity; UniCredit is testing whether Banking Union can actually support Continental Scale.

The fundamental question is no longer simply who buys whom?

IT IS WHO CAN FINANCE, CONTROL AND GOVERN THE PLATFORM THAT EMERGES.

⸻

1. WHAT CHANGED IN THE LAST 24 HOURS

CONFIRMED — UniCredit moves to the foreground today. Its extraordinary meeting will vote on changes to the articles of association, authorization for convertible AT1 issuance of up to €5 billion equivalent and authority to issue up to 10.603 million shares connected with specified TRS contracts. This is not formally a Commerzbank resolution, but it strengthens UniCredit’s capital and governance flexibility at precisely the moment when ownership of Commerzbank is moving toward effective control. 

CONFIRMED — no new MPS strategic filing over the weekend. MPS’s public offer pages still show the latest substantive offer-related disclosures as the 18 September notice concerning Banca Generali and the 14 September information released at Consob’s request. The October 29 extraordinary meeting therefore remains the next decisive formal MPS shareholder event. 

CONFIRMED — the European policy backdrop hardened on Friday and remains highly relevant this morning. EU finance ministers and central-bank officials explicitly argued that Europe needs larger banks, deeper capital markets and fewer political obstacles to cross-border bank combinations. ECB Vice President Boris Vujcic simultaneously rejected the idea that competitiveness requires weaker capital standards. 

INFERENCE. The contest is therefore splitting into two layers: Italy is testing coalition governance; UniCredit is testing cross-border governance. Both are manifestations of the same European problem.

⸻

2. THE DECISIVE ISSUE

CONTROL WITHOUT GOVERNABILITY IS NOT CONTROL.

MPS has shown that it can construct an industrially credible alternative around MPS + Mediobanca + Banco BPM + Banca Generali. But the resulting shareholder structure could be fragmented, with Crédit Agricole potentially emerging as the largest shareholder of the enlarged group. Reuters calculations previously indicated roughly 11% for Crédit Agricole in the combined structure, alongside Delfin, Generali, Caltagirone and institutional investors. 

UniCredit faces the mirror image in Germany. It has accumulated 47.6% of Commerzbank, but Berlin wants headquarters, listing, Mittelstand support and institutional safeguards preserved. UniCredit, meanwhile, reportedly wants leadership changes and opposes continuing German state representation on the supervisory board. 

The equation is now clear:

OWNERSHIP ≠ GOVERNANCE

And therefore:

SCALE × CAPITAL × GOVERNANCE = EXECUTABLE CONTROL

That is the real variable investors should price.

⸻

3. UPDATED STRATEGIC MAP

INTESA SANPAOLO — EXECUTION STILL WINS

Intesa remains the most executable transaction in the Italian system.Shareholders have authorized the capital increase supporting the MPS offer, and the original transaction already included an agreement to dispose of roughly 635 MPS branches to Unipol to pre-empt competition issues. 

The problem is now narrower but more consequential: Generali and competition law. AGCM has identified possible issues in deposits in 20 provinces, SME lending in 17 provinces and in the competitive relationship created by the 13% Generali stake embedded in MPS/Mediobanca. 

FDK VIEW: STRONG OVERWEIGHT.

The question is not whether Intesa understands execution. It is how much strategic value must be surrendered to obtain clearance.

⸻

MPS — THE COALITION BANK

MPS has confirmed that its Banco BPM and Banca Generali offers are not automatically extinguished if Intesa acquires control of MPS first. They remain subject to their own conditions and legal mechanisms. This creates genuine path dependency. 

MPS’s industrial case is meaningful: for Banco BPM it estimates €1.4 billion of annual run-rate synergies, a 2029 ROTE above 18% and pro-forma CET1 above 13%; the Banca Generali combination adds wealth-management scale and approximately €0.4 billion of additional annual synergies. 

But MPS is evolving from a banking consolidation thesis into a coalition-governance thesis.

FDK VIEW: HIGH CONVEXITY / HIGH GOVERNANCE RISK.

⸻

BANCO BPM — THE INDUSTRIAL ASSET EVERYONE NEEDS

Banco BPM remains strategically irreplaceable: Northern Italy, SME lending, deposits, distribution and Anima.

Crédit Agricole owns 29.3% and has publicly confirmed that stake. Market reporting says the French bank views the current MPS offer as insufficiently attractive and prefers a potential combination between Banco BPM and Crédit Agricole Italia. 

Banco BPM is therefore not simply a target.

IT IS THE BRIDGE BETWEEN THE ITALIAN AND FRENCH BANKING SYSTEMS.

⸻

CRÉDIT AGRICOLE — THE SYSTEM’S MOST VALUABLE OPTION

Crédit Agricole can resist the MPS proposal, negotiate improved economics, promote a BPM–Crédit Agricole Italia combination or ultimately become a leading shareholder of an MPS-led platform. Reuters Breakingviews estimates its position could translate into roughly 11% of the enlarged group. 

Its real asset is therefore not simply the 29.3% holding.

It is optionality over the architecture of Italian banking.

FDK VIEW: STRATEGIC OVERWEIGHT.

⸻

GENERALI — STRATEGIC SCARCITY

Generali’s formal position remains disciplined: it is evaluating MPS’s unsolicited Banca Generali proposal and the broader industrial collaboration it envisages, without having pre-agreed the transaction. 

At the same time, Generali sits inside virtually every scenario: MPS/Mediobanca owns 13.3%, Banca Generali is its controlled wealth platform, UniCredit is itself a significant Generali shareholder, and AGCM views the Intesa–Generali relationship as a competitive issue. 

Generali’s value is increasingly derived from strategic scarcity, not merely insurance fundamentals.

FDK VIEW: OVERWEIGHT.

⸻

BANCA GENERALI — THE PUREST EXECUTION-DISCOUNT ASSET

MPS has positioned Banca Generali as the centre of the enlarged group’s wealth-management franchise, with approximately 2,500 advisers and €117 billion of total financial assets in the MPS scenario. 

The asset quality is not the central debate.

The probability of execution is.

FDK VIEW: EVENT-DRIVEN POSITIVE.

⸻

UNICREDIT–COMMERZBANK — FROM OWNERSHIP TO CORPORATE CONTROL

UniCredit has already authorized the share-capital increase serving its Commerzbank exchange offer, and today’s separate extraordinary meeting expands additional capital and governance flexibility. 

Berlin has moved from attempting to stop the transaction to negotiating conditions. That is a structural shift. Germany now wants the Frankfurt headquarters, German listing and Mittelstand role protected. 

The next battle is therefore not acquisition.

It is post-acquisition governance.

FDK VIEW: OVERWEIGHT.

⸻

ITALIAN GOVERNMENT — FROM SHAREHOLDER TO RULE-SETTER

Rome still owns roughly 4.9% of MPS.Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti has maintained a formally neutral stance between competing combinations while explicitly reserving the possibility of imposing Golden Power conditions, particularly to protect territorial competition. 

The government’s leverage may therefore matter less through its residual equity stake than through the conditions attached to the winning structure.

⸻

INTERNATIONAL INVESTORS — THE QUIET SWING VOTE

Institutional investors remain critical. Fitch-related disclosures cited BlackRock at roughly 4.66% of MPS and 4.61% of Banco BPM; MPS’s fragmented shareholder base means the October 29 outcome will depend heavily on institutional voting behaviour rather than only on Italian strategic shareholders. 

This is where politics meets markets.

A transaction can have national sponsorship.

It still needs international capital to vote for it.

⸻

4. CAPITAL-MARKETS REACTION AND VALUATION

Friday’s close remains the cleanest market reference at this morning’s cut.

Intesa closed at €6.668, -2.14%; MPS at €11.764, -1.56%; Banco BPM at €15.70, -2.03%; Banca Generali at €65.00, -1.52%; Generali at €45.28, -1.97%; and UniCredit at €81.13, -3.05%. Commerzbank closed at €40.64, -2.59%. 

MPS’s latest formal recalibration, based on 11 September prices, showed an implied +3.4% premium for Banco BPM and +12.8% for Banca Generali; when the Intesa-induced uplift in MPS is stripped out and pre-Intesa reference prices are used, the offers instead imply discounts. That is exactly why the market continues to price execution risk rather than simply headline exchange ratios. 

For Commerzbank, the current exchange ratio remains 0.485 UniCredit shares per Commerzbank share. At Friday’s UniCredit close of €81.13, the mechanical implied value is approximately €39.35, versus Commerzbank at €40.64. The market therefore values Commerzbank about 3.3% above the current mechanical offer value, signalling expectations of improved economics, strategic optionality or a control premium. 

The market message remains consistent:

VALUE IS NOT THE PROBLEM. EXECUTION CERTAINTY IS.

6. THREE SCENARIOS

SCENARIO I — CONTINENTAL BIFURCATION

42% | UNCHANGED

Intesa completes MPS after acceptable competition remedies.

UniCredit converts ownership of Commerzbank into negotiated control.

Banco BPM eventually gravitates toward Crédit Agricole.

Generali remains strategically independent.

This still produces the cleanest European architecture: Intesa as Italian capital allocator; UniCredit as pan-European commercial bank.

⸻

SCENARIO II — THE COALITION THIRD POLE

26% | UNCHANGED

MPS wins the October 29 shareholder vote and proceeds toward Banco BPM and Banca Generali.

The outcome is not a classic national champion.

It is a coalition bank governed through Crédit Agricole, Delfin, Generali, Caltagirone, institutional capital and a residual state presence.

The upside is scale.

The risk is permanent negotiation.

⸻

SCENARIO III — COLLISION OF CONTROL

32% | UNCHANGED

Intesa secures control of MPS while MPS’s defensive offers remain alive.

Ownership and strategic commitments diverge.AGCM, Consob, ECB/SSM, IVASS and political authorities effectively determine which industrial architecture survives.

There is not enough new hard evidence this morning to justify moving the probabilities.

That discipline matters.

⸻

7. POLITICAL AND REGULATORY IMPLICATIONS

The regulatory map is becoming unusually coherent.

Rome wants competition, territorial presence and strategic safeguards. 

Berlin wants Commerzbank’s identity, headquarters, listing and Mittelstand role protected. 

Brussels wants fewer national barriers to cross-border mergers. 

Frankfurt wants larger European banks without relaxing capital discipline. 

This is not a contradiction.

It is the emerging European model:

CONTINENTAL SCALE + NATIONAL SAFEGUARDS + EUROPEAN SUPERVISION

Banking Union is being built less through treaty design than through actual transactions.

⸻

8. THE HIDDEN PATTERN

GOVERNABILITY IS BECOMING THE SCARCE ASSET

Europe has enough capital.

Europe has enough banks.

Europe has enough potential transactions.

What Europe lacks is a proven mechanism for transforming cross-border ownership into stable corporate control.

MPS demonstrates the problem inside Italy.

UniCredit demonstrates the same problem across borders.

The hidden pattern is therefore larger than the Italian Risiko:

CAPITAL IS EUROPEANISING FASTER THAN GOVERNANCE.

The next phase of Banking Union will not be decided by balance-sheet size alone.

It will be decided by whether institutions can create governance structures capable of surviving politics, regulation and fragmented ownership simultaneously.

⸻

9. WHAT TO WATCH IN THE NEXT 24–72 HOURS

The first catalyst is UniCredit’s extraordinary meeting today at 10:00 CEST. Approval itself is likely less important than what management communicates about capital flexibility and strategic optionality after the vote. 

The second is Berlin: any concrete move from general political conditions toward an agreed governance framework for Commerzbank would be more important than another headline about ownership percentages.

The third is MPS/Consob: any new clarification on the Banco BPM or Banca Generali documents, especially on governance, Generali, remedies or transaction sequencing.

The fourth is Crédit Agricole: the market needs to know whether opposition to MPS is merely price-based or reflects a fundamentally different strategic destination for Banco BPM. 

The fifth is Generali: its formal evaluation of the Banca Generali proposal remains one of the most underpriced catalysts in the system. 

The sixth is AGCM: any shift from identifying competition issues to defining actual remedies would materially change the Intesa valuation equation. 

⸻

10. VELOCITY INSIGHT

The Italian Banking Risiko began as a contest for assets.

Then it became a contest for capital.

Then time.

Then execution.

Now it is a contest for governability.

THE NEXT EUROPEAN BANKING CHAMPION WILL NOT BE THE BANK THAT OWNS THE MOST.

IT WILL BE THE BANK THAT CAN TURN OWNERSHIP INTO CONTROL — AND CONTROL INTO CAPITAL ALLOCATION.

That is the Governance Premium.

And today, from Siena to Milan to Frankfurt, that is the variable that matters most.

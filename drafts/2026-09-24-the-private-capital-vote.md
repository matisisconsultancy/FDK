Rome steps out, Crédit Agricole steps forward, and Lovaglio turns October 29 from a binary takeover choice into a referendum on option value.

24 September 2026 | FDK
Data cut: 08:50 CEST — before the Milan market open

The Italian Banking Risiko has moved again.

The most important development is not another bid. It is the redistribution of decision-making power.

Confirmed: Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti said yesterday that the Italian Treasury, which owns 4.86% of MPS, will not participate in the October 29 shareholder vote on the defensive Banco BPM and Banca Generali acquisitions. His message was explicit: the government will follow the market’s decision and expects the market to reward the better offer. 

Confirmed: Luigi Lovaglio simultaneously reframed the vote. MPS shareholders, he argued, can authorize the defensive strategy on October 29 and still later tender their shares to Intesa. He also stated that the two MPS offers can work independently: Banco BPM creates scale and most of the synergies; Banca Generali transforms the group’s wealth-management profile and would bring Generali into the shareholder base. 

And this morning comes the third signal.

Confirmed public statement: Crédit Agricole CFO Clotilde L’Angevin described a combination between Banco BPM and Crédit Agricole Italia as a “very interesting” scenario, while reiterating that the French group will remain patient and evaluate its options. The earlier market speculation about a joint UniCredit–Crédit Agricole solution around BPM therefore remains unconfirmed; Crédit Agricole’s clearest stated preference is its own Italian combination with Banco BPM. 

The consequence is decisive:

THE OCTOBER 29 VOTE IS NO LONGER GOVERNMENT VERSUS MARKET.

IT IS PRIVATE CAPITAL VERSUS PRIVATE CAPITAL.

⸻

1. WHAT CHANGED IN THE LAST 24 HOURS

THE STATE WITHDRAWS FROM THE VOTE

The Treasury’s abstention materially changes the shareholder arithmetic. MPS’s current disclosed ownership includes Delfin at 17.53%, Caltagirone Group at 10.26%, MEF at 4.86%, BlackRock at 4.66% and Banco BPM at 3.74%. The defensive resolutions require approval by at least two-thirds of votes cast. 

At the April MPS meeting, 64.11% of capital was represented. If participation were similar on October 29 and the Treasury’s 4.86% stayed outside the vote, the approval hurdle would mechanically correspond to roughly 39½% of total capital. That is only an illustration—not a forecast—but it shows why Delfin, Caltagirone, BlackRock, Banco BPM and international free-float investors now become the real constitutional assembly of MPS. 

MPS MAKES THE DEFENCE MODULAR

Lovaglio’s statement that the Banco BPM and Banca Generali offers can work individually is more consequential than it appears. It lowers the strategic dependence of the defence on achieving the entire €34 billion architecture at once. Banco BPM is the scale leg; Banca Generali is the wealth-management and Generali-alignment leg. 

Inference: MPS is quietly moving from a single grand bargain toward a portfolio of defensive options.

CRÉDIT AGRICOLE HARDENS THE BPM ALTERNATIVE

Crédit Agricole’s public preference for a BPM–Crédit Agricole Italia combination is now considerably harder to dismiss as background optionality. With 29.3% of Banco BPM, the French group remains the institution with the greatest ability to determine whether MPS can obtain control of the asset it needs most. 

⸻

2. THE DECISIVE ISSUE

WHO CONTROLS THE MPS SHAREHOLDER VOTE — AND WHO CONTROLS BPM AFTER IT?

The Risiko now has two sequential veto points.

The first is October 29. MPS must persuade its own shareholders to authorize defensive capital allocation.

The second is Banco BPM itself. Even if MPS receives authorization, Crédit Agricole’s 29.3% stake means the French group remains central to any realistic control outcome.

That creates a new asymmetry:

MPS can authorize an offer without Crédit Agricole.

MPS cannot easily complete the strategic architecture against Crédit Agricole.And that is why Lovaglio’s modular formulation matters.

THE DEFENCE NO LONGER NEEDS BOTH ASSETS TO CREATE OPTION VALUE.

But the full Third Pole still needs both to create the original strategic vision.

⸻

3. UPDATED STRATEGIC MAP

INTESA SANPAOLO — THE SIMPLICITY PREMIUM

The government’s abstention is incrementally positive for Intesa because MPS’s defence must now win exclusively through shareholder economics rather than through direct state support.

Intesa’s offer remains 1.6 newly issued Intesa shares plus €1 cash for each MPS share, subject to a 66.67% minimum acceptance threshold that Intesa can waive. Intesa has repeatedly presented its architecture as a European-scale transaction backed by substantial synergies and pre-arranged competition remedies. 

FDK VIEW: STRONG OVERWEIGHT.

The comparative advantage is no longer merely scale.

It is execution simplicity versus coalition complexity.

⸻

MPS — FROM THIRD POLE TO OPTION PLATFORM

Lovaglio is no longer asking shareholders to decide definitively between MPS and Intesa on October 29.

He is asking them to preserve MPS’s strategic optionality.

That is sophisticated. It also reveals the problem.

If the vote is not final ownership selection, shareholders must decide whether the value of keeping alternatives alive exceeds the risks of dilution, complexity and regulatory friction. 

FDK VIEW: HIGH CONVEXITY / HIGH VOTE RISK.

The industrial thesis survives.

The shareholder burden of proof is rising.

⸻

BANCO BPM — THE SCARCE ASSET

Banco BPM closed Wednesday at €16.285, +1.21%, while MPS gained only 0.37%. Over the last week, Banco BPM has outperformed MPS materially. 

The reason is increasingly structural.

Banco BPM has several plausible destinations:

MPS

Crédit Agricole Italia

a negotiated European solution

and potentially renewed UniCredit involvement if circumstances change.

Scarcity rises with the number of credible buyers.

FDK VIEW: EVENT-DRIVEN OVERWEIGHT.

⸻

CRÉDIT AGRICOLE — THE VETO BECOMES A STRATEGY

Crédit Agricole is moving from passive kingmaker toward explicit strategic architect.

Its preferred scenario is now publicly identifiable: combine Banco BPM with Crédit Agricole Italia. Yet it remains patient and does not need to force timing. 

That gives it something unusually valuable:

CONTROL OVER THE CLOCK.

FDK VIEW: STRATEGIC OVERWEIGHT.

⸻

UNICREDIT — CONTINENTAL SCALE WITHOUT ITALIAN DEPENDENCE

No material new Commerzbank development emerged overnight.

That itself is strategically useful.

UniCredit does not need Banco BPM to justify its scale strategy. Its primary transformation remains Germany, where its Commerzbank position approaches control and Berlin has shifted toward negotiating safeguards around headquarters, listing and Mittelstand lending rather than simply blocking the transaction. 

FDK VIEW: OVERWEIGHT.

UniCredit retains Italian optionality without making Italy existential.

⸻

COMMERZBANK — THE CONTROL-PREMIUM TEST

Commerzbank closed at €41.39 on September 23. UniCredit closed at €83.47. At the current 0.485 exchange ratio, the mechanical offer value is approximately €40.48 per Commerzbank share. Commerzbank therefore trades roughly 2.2% above that mechanical value. 

The market is still pricing something beyond the existing consideration:

an improved exchange ratio,

a governance settlement,

or a residual control premium.

⸻

GENERALI — STRATEGIC SCARCITY REMAINS

Generali remains embedded in every major Italian outcome: MPS/Mediobanca owns a significant stake, Generali controls Banca Generali, and the insurer sits at the intersection of competition, insurance distribution and national financial sovereignty.

There is no new formal Generali decision this morning on the MPS Banca Generali proposal. Generali has previously said the proposal could offer growth opportunities but remains unsolicited and subject to evaluation. 

FDK VIEW: OVERWEIGHT.

⸻

BANCA GENERALI — THE WIDER SPREAD

Banca Generali closed Wednesday at €65.85, down just 0.15%. 

Unlike Banco BPM, it has not yet developed a multi-bidder premium.That makes the transaction spread significantly wider—and the payoff more binary.

FDK VIEW: EVENT-DRIVEN POSITIVE.

⸻

ITALIAN GOVERNMENT — NEUTRAL SHAREHOLDER, NOT NEUTRAL STATE

Giorgetti’s abstention removes the Treasury from the October 29 vote.

It does not eliminate government influence over the eventual architecture.

Golden Power, competition policy and broader national-interest considerations remain available, and Intesa’s own offer documentation explicitly lists Golden Power and other FDI approvals among its regulatory conditions. 

The state is stepping out of ownership voting.

It is not stepping out of rule-setting.

⸻

INTERNATIONAL INVESTORS — NOW THE REAL SWING BLOCK

BlackRock alone has 4.66% of MPS voting capital, while most of the remaining shares sit in institutional and dispersed ownership. 

With the Treasury abstaining, October 29 becomes much more explicitly an institutional-investor judgment about capital allocation.

That is precisely what makes the vote European rather than merely Italian.

⸻

4. CAPITAL-MARKETS REACTION AND VALUATION

The market continues to differentiate sharply between the two MPS target assets.

MPS closed at €11.894 and Banco BPM at €16.285. Before settling its offers, MPS proposes an extraordinary distribution of €1.208 per share. Adjusting MPS for that distribution gives an analytical ex-distribution value of roughly €10.686. Applying the 1.567 exchange ratio produces an implied Banco BPM value of approximately €16.74—only about 2.8% above the market price. 

The BPM spread is therefore becoming extremely tight.

That is the market saying:

MPS DOES NOT OWN THE UPSIDE IN BPM.

The same calculation for Banca Generali is very different. Applying the 6.958 ratio to the adjusted MPS currency implies approximately €74.35 per Banca Generali share versus a €65.85 close—a spread of roughly 12.9%. 

That gap is pure execution risk.

For MPS itself, Intesa’s current terms mechanically imply roughly €11.74 per MPS share using Intesa’s €6.71 September 23 close. MPS closed at €11.894—around 1.3% above Intesa’s mechanical value. 

The market is therefore preserving a small but real price for MPS optionality.

7. POLITICAL AND REGULATORY IMPLICATIONS

The Italian state’s decision is subtle but important.

Rome is effectively saying:

we will not choose the winning capital allocator through our MPS shareholding.

That is consistent with Giorgetti’s formulation that the market should reward the superior offer. 

But regulators still define the perimeter.

MPS has already received ECB authorization for the Mediobanca merger; the Banco BPM and Banca Generali offers remain subject to supervisory, competition, insurance and Golden Power processes. 

At the European level, the direction remains clear. EU finance ministers and ECB officials have argued that European banks need more scale and deeper capital markets, while simultaneously resisting any weakening of capital standards. 

The model emerging is therefore not laissez-faire consolidation.

It is:

PRIVATE CAPITAL SELECTION + NATIONAL SAFEGUARDS + EUROPEAN SUPERVISION

That is Banking Union in practice.

⸻

8. THE HIDDEN PATTERN

THE GOVERNMENT IS PRIVATISING THE DECISION — NOT THE STRATEGY

This is the real shift.

Italy still cares deeply about MPS, Generali, Banco BPM and domestic savings.

But the government is increasingly allowing market prices and private shareholders to determine who gets the capital, while retaining the ability to determine the rules under which that capital can move.

That is a profound institutional change.

MPS’s October vote is therefore becoming a test of whether European banking consolidation can finally move from:

political allocation of ownership

to

market allocation of control

without surrendering national strategic safeguards.

The deeper equation is:

MARKETS CHOOSE THE ARCHITECTURE. POLITICS DEFINES THE PERIMETER.

That may be the most important development in the Italian Risiko so far.

⸻

9. WHAT TO WATCH — NEXT 24–72 HOURS

First: Crédit Agricole. The decisive question is whether “very interesting” becomes a mandate, a formal study or eventually a concrete BPM–Crédit Agricole Italia proposal.

Second: Delfin, Caltagirone and BlackRock. With MEF abstaining, every signal from these blocks becomes disproportionately important for October 29.

Third: MPS. Watch whether management increasingly markets the two offers separately rather than as one indivisible Third Pole.

Fourth: Banco BPM. A sustained price above €16.30–16.50 would compress the adjusted MPS premium close to irrelevance and increase pressure for better economics.

Fifth: Generali. A formal strategic position on Banca Generali could immediately reduce the uncertainty behind the current ~13% implied spread.

Sixth: Consob and ECB/SSM. Any clarification on timing, passivity-rule interpretation or transaction documentation will directly affect the option value Lovaglio is asking shareholders to preserve.

Seventh: Berlin. The UniCredit–Commerzbank spread remains a live indicator of whether investors expect additional consideration or a negotiated governance premium.

10. VELOCITY INSIGHT

The Italian Banking Risiko started as a contest over assets.

Then it became a contest over scale.

Then capital allocation.

Then governance.

Now the state has stepped aside from the decisive shareholder vote.

The contest has reached its most consequential phase:

WHO DOES PRIVATE CAPITAL TRUST TO ALLOCATE THE NEXT EURO?

Intesa offers execution.

MPS offers optionality.

Crédit Agricole offers patience and veto power.

UniCredit offers Continental Scale.

Generali offers strategic scarcity.And international investors now supply the deciding votes.

THE WINNER WILL BE THE INSTITUTION THAT CONVERTS CAPITAL ALLOCATION INTO LEGITIMATE CONTROL.

That is The Italian Banking Paradox 2.0.

And today, for the first time, the market—not the state—is being asked to write the next chapter.

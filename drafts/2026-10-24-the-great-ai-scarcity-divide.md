America is selling computing capacity years ahead. China is advancing frontier models. Washington is turning capital into a strategic weapon.

MATERIAL INFLECTION ALERT — HIGH SIGNIFICANCE

THE BIG TAKE

Three developments on October 22–24 materially strengthen The Velocity Edge thesis—but introduce an important qualification: America’s capital-intensive AI infrastructure strategy is demonstrating substantial forward demand, even as China advances its alternative model ecosystem.

The emerging divergence is no longer simply between American technological superiority and Chinese inference efficiency.

It increasingly concerns who controls the scarce inputs—computing capacity, investment capital and deployable intelligence—that determine the economics of the AI Supercycle.

1. AMAZON: AMERICAN COMPUTING CAPACITY IS ALREADY CONTRACTED INTO 2028

On October 23, Amazon CEO Andy Jassy disclosed that AWS computing capacity is largely committed through 2027, with substantial commitments extending into 2028.

Amazon expects approximately $220 billion in capital expenditure during 2026.

Jassy indicated that additional investment could be economically attractive given current demand. 

Why this matters

This is an important counterweight to the market’s recent concerns about excessive AI infrastructure spending.

The investment cycle is not simply being driven by speculative capacity construction. At least part of America’s hyperscale expansion is supported by substantial customer commitments.

However, contracted capacity does not guarantee satisfactory returns.

The decisive financial tests remain contract quality, actual utilization, operating margins, depreciation and free-cash-flow conversion.

Velocity interpretation: America’s infrastructure advantage increasingly resembles a strategic capacity franchise. Its financial value will depend on whether committed demand becomes durable cash flow.

2. MOONSHOT: CHINA’S FRONTIER CHALLENGE ENTERS A NEW PHASE

On October 24, Chinese AI developer Moonshot introduced Kimi K3.1 Pro, which the company describes as a three-trillion-parameter-class model.

The model features a one-million-token context window, while a faster variant reportedly delivers two to three times the standard model’s speed.

Moonshot plans to release its open weights by November 4, 2026.

Significantly, the company deliberately reduced the model’s cyber-offensive capabilities while seeking to preserve broader coding performance.

Its internal cyber benchmark declined from 90.5% to 81.0% after risk-reduction measures. These results have not been independently validated. 

Why this matters

China’s challenge is extending beyond DeepSeek.

Multiple Chinese developers are advancing increasingly sophisticated models while pursuing open-weight distribution.

Yet the Moonshot announcement also reveals a new constraint: unrestricted diffusion becomes more difficult as models acquire capabilities with national-security implications.

China is therefore confronting a strategic trade-off between technological openness and control over advanced capabilities.

Velocity interpretation: China’s competitive advantage may increasingly depend on its ability to distribute powerful, commercially useful intelligence without triggering restrictions that undermine international adoption.

The November 4 release will be an important test. It is not yet evidence of a change in the global frontier ranking.

3. WASHINGTON: CAPITAL BECOMES A STRATEGIC BOTTLENECK

On October 22, the U.S. Treasury proposed regulations expanding restrictions on American investment in sensitive technologies associated with China and other designated countries.

The proposed framework encompasses artificial intelligence, advanced semiconductors, quantum technologies, supercomputing and additional strategic sectors.

Depending on the technology and transaction, covered investments could be prohibited or subject to notification requirements.These are proposed regulations, not newly effective prohibitions. The existing outbound-investment regime remains in force, and the consultation period closes on December 7, 2026. 

The financial implications are potentially substantial.

Washington is moving beyond restricting technology exports toward tighter control over the capital, expertise and commercial relationships that help finance advanced technology development.

Ordinary public-market securities investments generally remain excepted; the implications are more direct for covered venture investments, acquisitions, joint ventures and other transactions. 

Velocity interpretation: Capital formation is becoming another contested component of the global AI production system.

This could increase financing fragmentation while strengthening incentives for China to mobilize domestic capital.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN: SCARCITY IS MOVING UP THE AI VALUE CHAIN

The three developments reveal a more complex competitive architecture.

America controls valuable computing capacity and is seeking to protect its technological and financial advantages.

China is expanding model capabilities and diffusion strategies while seeking greater independence from American technology and capital.

The resulting transmission mechanism is:

Computing scarcity → Forward infrastructure commitments → Capital concentration → Strategic restrictions → Domestic substitution → Competing AI ecosystems

The fundamental investment insight is that restrictions intended to preserve technological leadership can simultaneously encourage the emergence of alternative industrial ecosystems.

This does not imply that the two systems possess equivalent capabilities.

It means that the global AI economy may become increasingly fragmented even as demand for intelligence continues expanding.

THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECT: REPRICING THE AI SUPERCYCLE

For investors, the latest developments create three distinct financial tests.

American infrastructure: Does forward-contracted capacity generate adequate returns on invested capital?

Chinese frontier developers: Can increasingly capable models achieve sustainable inference economics and international diffusion under growing regulatory constraints?

Global capital markets: Will restrictions on technology investment accelerate the formation of separate American and Chinese AI financing ecosystems?

These questions determine whether the current AI Supercycle produces broad-based productivity growth or an increasingly concentrated distribution of economic returns.

The next critical dates are October 29, when Amazon is scheduled to report third-quarter results; November 4, when Moonshot plans to release Kimi K3.1 Pro weights; and December 7, the deadline for comments on the proposed U.S. outbound-investment rules.

THE VELOCITY EDGE — INVESTMENT VERDICT

The October 24 evidence strengthens our central thesis, with one important refinement.

America’s capital intensity is not merely a financial vulnerability. When accompanied by substantial contracted demand, it can reinforce its structural competitive advantage.

China’s increasingly sophisticated models strengthen its potential challenge to deployment economics, but do not yet establish superior total computing costs or frontier leadership.

The market should therefore distinguish three sources of value: ownership of scarce computing capacity, control over capital formation and the ability to commercialize intelligence at scale.

The next decisive inflection will occur when independent evidence demonstrates whether Chinese models can deliver comparable commercial workloads at materially lower total costs—and whether American hyperscalers can convert their infrastructure commitments into superior free cash flow.

VELOCITY INSIGHT

AMERICA LEADS THE FRONTIER AND CONTROLS MUCH OF ITS SCARCE CAPACITY. CHINA IS CHALLENGING THE ECONOMICS OF INTELLIGENCE. THE NEXT BATTLE WILL BE WON BY THOSE WHO TURN TECHNOLOGICAL ADVANTAGE INTO ECONOMIC POWER—WITHOUT LOSING CONTROL OF THE CAPITAL REQUIRED TO SUSTAIN IT.

The evidence points to a decisive shift in the AI Supercycle: the world is announcing AI capacity faster than it is converting AI capacity into measurable economic output.

This is today's central discipline. Capital can be raised, models can be trained, data centres can be announced — but economic Velocity appears only when infrastructure becomes powered, utilised and productive. The GVI rewards execution, not announcements.

::signals Five Signals | The evidence beneath the index
# Japan: Wages finally convert into purchasing power
Real wages rose 1.5% y/y in August — the eighth consecutive increase — while nominal wages advanced 3.8%, base pay 3.8% and overtime pay 5.2%. Eight consecutive months of positive real wages materially improve Japan's domestic economic architecture.
But the second-order effect matters: stronger wages also increase the Bank of Japan's freedom to tighten. Enterprise Transformation improves; Smart Capital offsets it.
signal: Growth quality improves. Cheap capital does not.

# Germany: The recovery survives the headline shock
August factory orders fell 10.6% m/m — a figure that looks alarming. But the headline largely reflects the reversal of unusually large aircraft, ship and rail orders. Excluding large-scale orders, bookings declined only 0.1%, while the three-month measure remained +1.3%.
Germany's problem is therefore not collapse. It is breadth. The next test is whether stronger PMIs translate into ordinary industrial orders, investment and sustained production.
signal: Less clean — but not broken.

# United States: AI investment continues to pull capital inward
The trade deficit widened 13.7% to $105.6 billion as imports surged 4.3% to a record $420.8 billion. Yet part of that increase came from capital goods and semiconductors, consistent with continuing AI and infrastructure investment.
The widening deficit is a GDP headwind, but not evidence of declining structural Velocity. The US is absorbing a near-term external deficit partly to build tomorrow's intelligence infrastructure.
signal: Current GDP arithmetic ≠ future productive capacity.

# France: Europe's model layer strengthens
Mistral unveiled Large 4, potentially narrowing Europe's frontier-model gap and strengthening the case for sovereign, open-weight AI deployed inside regulated and industrial environments.
But capability claims are not economic output. Mistral still requires independent validation and public deployment.
signal: Momentum improves. Score unchanged.

# Spain: Infrastructure ambition meets the execution test
More than €60 billion of expected investment is turning Aragón into a potential European AI-infrastructure cluster. But most of that capital remains future rather than operating capacity.
The crucial variable is no longer capital announced. It is TIME TO POWER → TIME TO OPERATE → TIME TO REVENUE.
signal: That is the execution gap.

::section The Hidden Pattern | The AI economy's four stages
The AI economy is moving through four stages: MODELS → COMPUTE → INFRASTRUCTURE → EXECUTION. The first three are increasingly abundant. The fourth is not.

That changes the market's unit of measurement. Announced CapEx matters less; operating output matters more. The valuation hierarchy increasingly becomes: CAPITAL → CAPACITY → UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW.

::signals Second-Order Effects | What comes next
# Capital moves from promises to execution
As infrastructure announcements multiply, scarcity shifts toward permitted, powered and operating capacity. Announced gigawatts will progressively lose valuation relevance; time-to-revenue will gain it.
signal: Time-to-revenue becomes the scarce metric.

# The AI premium migrates downstream
The first winners were models and accelerators. The next rotation should increasingly favour MEMORY → NETWORKING → POWER → GRID → COOLING → INDUSTRIAL SOFTWARE → AUTOMATION → ROBOTICS — the layers required to transform compute into productive output.
signal: The rotation stays inside the Supercycle but moves closer to the physical economy.

# Europe gets a second chance
Europe does not need to reproduce the US hyperscaler model. A different architecture is emerging: SOVEREIGN AI + INFRASTRUCTURE + INDUSTRIAL SOFTWARE + MANUFACTURING. France provides part of the model layer, Spain provides infrastructure, Germany provides the industrial conversion base.
signal: The window is measured in execution speed — not policy ambition.

# Higher rates accelerate concentration
At 5%+ long rates, strong balance sheets can continue building, acquiring and consolidating; weak balance sheets cannot. AI technology becomes more democratised while AI economics become more concentrated.
signal: Higher rates favour cash-rich incumbents and contracted-revenue assets.

# Productivity becomes the final scarcity
Models will proliferate, compute will expand, infrastructure will catch up. What remains difficult is the organisational capability to translate all three into MARGINS → ASSET TURNS → LABOUR PRODUCTIVITY → FREE CASH FLOW → ROIC.
signal: That is where the next Great Split will emerge.

::takeaways Five Takeaways | The discipline of the index
- **The index refuses to reward announcements.** €60 billion of infrastructure ambition and a promising frontier model matter — but only operating capacity and measurable economic conversion move structural Velocity.
- **Time-to-deployment becomes competitive advantage.** Permitting, grid connection and organisational execution increasingly determine where AI capital earns its return.
- **Europe has a second route into the AI race.** Its strongest position may be industrial AI — combining sovereign models, infrastructure, engineering, mobility, automation and manufacturing.
- **Higher rates make execution more concentrated.** The longer capital remains expensive, the larger the advantage enjoyed by companies capable of self-financing deployment.
- **Enforce one chain relentlessly:** CAPITAL → CAPACITY → UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW → ROIC > WACC. Anything that stops before productivity remains a thesis; anything that completes the chain becomes advantage.

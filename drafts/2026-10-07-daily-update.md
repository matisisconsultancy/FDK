THE EXECUTION GAP
THE BIG TAKE
GVI: 108.0 | Previous: 108.0 | No score changes | No ranking changes | Confidence: 96%
The Evidence
The evidence points to a decisive shift in the AI Supercycle:
The world is announcing AI capacity faster than it is converting AI capacity into measurable economic output.
Japan provides the strongest measured positive signal. Real wages rose 1.5% y/y in August—the eighth consecutive increase—while nominal wages advanced 3.8%, base pay 3.8% and overtime pay 5.2%. Household purchasing power is becoming a more durable component of Japan’s recovery. But stronger wages also give the Bank of Japan greater room to tighten. Enterprise Transformation improves; Smart Capital offsets it.
Germany provides the counter-signal. August factory orders fell 10.6% m/m, but the headline largely reflects the reversal of unusually large aircraft, ship and rail orders. Excluding large-scale orders, bookings declined only 0.1%, while the three-month measure remained +1.3%. The recovery is less clean—but not broken.
In the United States, the trade deficit widened 13.7% to $105.6 billion, as imports surged 4.3% to a record $420.8 billion. Yet part of that increase came from capital goods and semiconductors, consistent with continuing AI and infrastructure investment. The deficit is a GDP drag, but not evidence of declining structural Velocity.
Europe, meanwhile, is assembling the pieces of an alternative AI architecture. France’s Mistral unveiled Large 4, while Spain’s Aragón has attracted more than €60 billion of expected data-centre and digital-infrastructure investment. Neither changes today’s score: Mistral still requires independent validation and public deployment; most of Aragón’s capital remains future rather than operating capacity.
That is today’s central discipline: the GVI rewards execution—not announcements.

Five Signals
1 — Japan: 
Wages finally convert into purchasing power
Eight consecutive months of positive real wages materially improve Japan’s domestic economic architecture.
But the second-order effect matters: stronger wages also increase the BOJ’s freedom to tighten.
Growth quality improves. Cheap capital does not.
2 — Germany: 
The recovery survives the headline shock
A 10.6% collapse in orders looks alarming.
Strip out large orders and the decline is only 0.1%.
Germany’s problem is therefore not collapse. It is breadth.
The next test is whether stronger PMIs translate into ordinary industrial orders, investment and sustained production.
3 — United States: 
AI investment continues to pull capital inward
The widening trade deficit is a GDP headwind, but record imports include capital goods and semiconductors.
That reinforces an important distinction:
current GDP arithmetic ≠ future productive capacity.
The US is absorbing a near-term external deficit partly to build tomorrow’s intelligence infrastructure.
4 — France: 
Europe’s model layer strengthens
Mistral’s Large 4 potentially narrows Europe’s frontier-model gap and strengthens the case for sovereign, open-weight AI deployed inside regulated and industrial environments.
But capability claims are not economic output.
Momentum improves. Score unchanged.
5 — Spain: 
Infrastructure ambition meets the execution testMore than €60 billion of expected investment is turning Aragón into a potential European AI-infrastructure cluster.
The crucial variable is now not capital announced.
It is:
TIME TO POWER → TIME TO OPERATE → TIME TO REVENUE
That is the execution gap.
The Hidden Pattern
The AI economy is moving through four stages:
MODELS → COMPUTE → INFRASTRUCTURE → EXECUTION
The first three are increasingly abundant.
The fourth is not.
Capital can be raised.
Models can be trained.
Data centres can be announced.
But economic Velocity appears only when infrastructure becomes powered, utilised and productive.
That changes the market’s unit of measurement.
Announced CapEx matters less.
Operating output matters more.
And the valuation hierarchy increasingly becomes:
CAPITAL → CAPACITY → UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW
Second-Order Effects — What Comes Next
I — Capital moves from promises to execution
As infrastructure announcements multiply, scarcity shifts toward permitted, powered and operating capacity.
Announced gigawatts will progressively lose valuation relevance.
Time-to-revenue will gain it.
II — The AI premium migrates downstream
The first winners were models and accelerators.
The next rotation should increasingly favour:
MEMORY → NETWORKING → POWER → GRID → COOLING → INDUSTRIAL SOFTWARE → AUTOMATION → ROBOTICS
These are the layers required to transform compute into productive output.
The next market rotation therefore remains inside the AI Supercycle—but moves progressively closer to the physical economy.
III — Europe gets a second chance
Europe does not need to reproduce the US hyperscaler model.
A different architecture is emerging:
SOVEREIGN AI + INFRASTRUCTURE + INDUSTRIAL SOFTWARE + MANUFACTURING
France provides part of the model layer.
Spain provides infrastructure.
Germany provides the industrial conversion base.
Europe’s competitive opportunity increasingly lies where intelligence meets the physical economy.
But the window is measured in execution speed—not policy ambition.
IV — Higher rates accelerate concentration
At 5%+ long rates, strong balance sheets can continue building, acquiring and consolidating.
Weak balance sheets cannot.
The paradox is striking:
AI technology becomes more democratised while AI economics become more concentrated.
Higher rates therefore favour cash-rich incumbents and assets already generating contracted revenue.
V — Productivity becomes the final scarcity
Models will proliferate.
Compute will expand.
Infrastructure will eventually catch up.
What remains difficult is the organisational capability to translate all three into:
MARGINS → ASSET TURNS → LABOUR PRODUCTIVITY → FREE CASH FLOW → ROIC
That is where the next Great Split will emerge.
The Evolutionary Market Rotation
The investable sequence is increasingly clear:
AI NARRATIVE → COMPUTE → INFRASTRUCTURE → CONVERSION → PRODUCTIVITY
The first two phases rewarded technological scarcity.
Infrastructure rewarded physical scarcity.
The next phase will reward execution scarcity.
That means capital should progressively differentiate between companies selling AI capacity and companies using AI to transform existing revenue pools and operating economics.
The latter may ultimately represent the largest phase of the entire Supercycle.
Five Takeaways
1. The index refuses to reward announcements.
€60 billion of infrastructure ambition and a promising frontier model matter—but only operating capacity and measurable economic conversion move structural Velocity.
2. Time-to-deployment becomes competitive advantage.
Permitting, grid connection and organisational execution increasingly determine where AI capital earns its return.
3. Europe has a second route into the AI race.
Its strongest position may be industrial AI, combining sovereign models, infrastructure, engineering, mobility, automation and manufacturing.
4. Higher rates make execution more concentrated.
The longer capital remains expensive, the larger the advantage enjoyed by companies capable of self-financing deployment.
5. Investors and boards should enforce one chain relentlessly:CAPITAL → CAPACITY → UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW → ROIC > WACC
Anything that stops before productivity remains an investment thesis.
Anything that completes the chain becomes competitive advantage.
Financial Verdict
GVI: 108.0. The infrastructure race continues. The execution race has begun.
The next Great Split will not be between companies that have AI and companies that do not.
It will be between those that convert intelligence into returns before everybody else catches up—and those left carrying the cost of the infrastructure.
Intelligence will become abundant. Execution will remain scarce. Own the scarcity.

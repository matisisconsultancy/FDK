The AI Supercycle has proved that earnings can still accelerate. Today the market asks whether the financial system will allow those earnings to retain their value. Nvidia surged 9%. Ten of eleven S&P sectors fell. The Great Split is no longer merely AI versus non-AI: it is becoming Earnings Velocity versus Discount-Rate Velocity.
Data cut: 07:45 CEST
The Fundamental Question
Can the extraordinary growth of AI cash flows continue to outrun a 4.67% U.S. 10-year yield, a 5.20% 30-year yield and a Federal Reserve increasingly uncomfortable with inflation—or does the WACC now become the binding constraint on the AI Supercycle?
Yesterday delivered the cleanest expression yet of the new market regime.
Nvidia rose almost 9% to a record close after projecting roughly 70% revenue growth next fiscal year. The Nasdaq gained 1.6%, the S&P 500 0.7% and technology 3.4%. Yet every other S&P sector fell. The semiconductor rally spread to Micron, Broadcom, Intel and SK Hynix, but not to the broader economy.
Overnight, markets stopped celebrating and started waiting. Taiwan rose 1.2% and Japan 0.5%, but Korea fell 1% and Hong Kong 0.3%. S&P and Nasdaq futures were marginally negative, while EURO STOXX 50 futures gained about 0.3%.
At 16:00 CEST, Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote. The U.S. 10-year Treasury sits around 4.67%, the 30-year around 5.20%, and markets assign roughly a 35% probability to a September rate increase while pricing at least one hike by year-end.
The equation for today is therefore unusually simple:
NVIDIA = THE NUMERATOR
WARSH = THE DENOMINATOR

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy
Nvidia passed the demand test. The market failed the breadth test.
Verified fact. Nvidia’s exceptional outlook produced a 9% stock gain, a 3.4% technology rally and record share-price levels. Yet healthcare, consumer sectors and every other major S&P sector declined.
FDK interpretation.
This is not simply an AI bull market.
It is an increasingly concentrated capital-allocation regime.
Capital is moving toward companies capable of demonstrating three characteristics simultaneously:
structural growth + scarcity + free cash flow.
Yesterday’s market says something important about The Great Split:
AI is not pulling the whole economy upward. It is increasingly separating from it.
The stronger Nvidia becomes, the more visible that divergence becomes.
⸻
Warsh now controls the denominator of the valuation equation
Verified fact. The 10-year Treasury stands near 4.67%; the 30-year near 5.20%. Several Fed officials at Jackson Hole have argued that inflation remains sticky and that current monetary policy may not be sufficiently restrictive. The Fed’s target rate remains 3.50%–3.75%.
Warsh speaks at 10:00 a.m. New York time / 16:00 CEST.
FDK interpretation.
Nvidia has demonstrated extraordinary Earnings Velocity.
Warsh determines how those earnings are discounted.
The market’s governing equation is moving toward:
Earnings Growth ÷ Cost of Capital
rather than earnings growth alone.
This is why a superb corporate result can coexist with vulnerable equity valuations.
If Warsh restores inflation credibility without forcing long yields materially higher, AI assets receive another extension of duration.
If he signals that 3.7% inflation requires materially tighter policy, the WACC rises—and the weakest AI narratives will be repriced immediately.
⸻
AI infrastructure is beginning to create its own inflation
Two apparently unrelated developments reveal the next bottleneck.
Germany’s Schwarz Group—owner of Lidl—is investing up to €5.6 billion in a northern German data center designed initially for 240 MW, eventually expandable to 1 GW. The facility alone would initially consume energy comparable to roughly 600,000 households.
In Australia, NextDC reported rising profitability but also deteriorating resource efficiency: water use increased to 2.40 litres per kWh from 2.25, while power usage effectiveness deteriorated to 1.49 from 1.44.
FDK interpretation.
This is a crucial economic paradox.
AI may eventually become profoundly deflationary through productivity.
But its industrialization can initially be inflationary.
The chain is:
AI demand → accelerated computing → data centers → electricity + grids + cooling + water + land → scarcity → higher infrastructure prices → higher capital requirements.
The next pool of excess returns therefore migrates toward the owners of the constraints.
Power. Grids. Memory. Optics. Cooling. Land. Capital.
⸻
Europe is moving from strategic autonomy rhetoric to strategic capital
Europe is beginning to change behavior.
Verified fact. German industrial associations are pressing Chancellor Friedrich Merz for a tougher China policy. Germany’s trade deficit with China reached €89.3 billion last year; an OECD study cited by Reuters estimates Chinese manufacturers receive three to eight times the state support of OECD peers relative to revenue. Volkswagen and other industrial groups are increasingly discussing local-content requirements and stronger European trade-defense instruments.
Simultaneously, Europe is building domestic AI infrastructure: Schwarz Group’s €5.6 billion project explicitly aims to reduce reliance on overseas cloud and AI providers.
And Europe’s banking architecture is consolidating. UniCredit now controls roughly 48% of Commerzbank, with Berlin increasingly moving from trying to prevent a transaction toward discussing its terms.
FDK interpretation.
These are not separate stories.
They describe Europe’s emerging response to the Next Economy:
**industrial policy
digital sovereignty
banking scale
capital formation.**
The European Pivot is moving from regulation toward balance-sheet capacity.
That is where Europe must go.
You cannot finance the Next Economy with fragmented banks, imported AI infrastructure and industrial strategies built entirely around defensive regulation.
⸻
Oil is falling. Energy risk is not.
Verified fact. Brent trades around $89.45, down roughly 5.3% this week, as Iran and Oman work toward arrangements that could improve shipping through Hormuz. Yet actual shipping remains only about 5%–15% of normal activity, according to tracking estimates cited by Reuters. Iran is preparing conditions for fuller reopening, while the United States has stepped back from direct talks and intensified economic sanctions.
Next week’s U.S.-hosted G20 finance meeting will explicitly focus on resilient energy supply chains, sovereign debt, global imbalances and enforcement of sanctions against Iran.
FDK interpretation.
The oil price is signaling diplomatic hope.
The physical infrastructure is signaling fragility.
That distinction matters.
A $5–10 fall in Brent can improve inflation expectations quickly.
But the geopolitical shock is already changing long-term capital allocation toward:
LNG grids storage electrification renewables nuclear alternative transport corridors.
Energy security has become a permanent investment category.

Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines
The Great Split is becoming vertical
Yesterday only technology rose inside the S&P 500.
The divergence is no longer simply:
AI companies vs. traditional companies.
It is increasingly visible through the entire production chain:
accelerated computing → memory → optics → data centers → power → grids → financing.Capital migrates toward each successive bottleneck.
⸻
AI is simultaneously a productivity shock and a capital-intensity shock
That distinction will become increasingly important for central banks.
AI can lower the marginal cost of intelligence.
But creating that intelligence requires extraordinary investment in scarce physical resources.
Therefore:
AI can be inflationary before it becomes deflationary.
That is one reason the AI Supercycle and higher long-term interest rates can coexist.
⸻
The state is returning to price formation
Treasury buybacks are influencing long-duration bond markets.
U.S. sanctions are influencing global trade flows.
European industrial policy is beginning to influence sourcing decisions.
Governments are regulating grid access for data centers.
And sovereign infrastructure policy increasingly determines where AI factories can be built.
The Next Economy is therefore not being shaped by markets alone.
Capital allocation is becoming geopolitically conditioned.
⸻
Europe’s real competitive problem is scale
Germany’s China debate, the Schwarz data center and UniCredit–Commerzbank point toward the same structural issue.
Europe possesses technology, industrial capability, savings and engineering expertise.
Its constraint remains fragmentation.
The next European competitive advantage will come from creating enough scale in:
capital energy AI infrastructure banking mobility industrial platforms.

Likely Market and Competitive Implications Today
The favored side of the Great Split remains clear:
accelerated computing, HBM/memory, optical networking, power equipment, grids, cooling, data-center infrastructure, cybersecurity and well-capitalized banks.
Europe adds a second layer:
sovereign cloud, industrial automation, electrification and strategic infrastructure.
Pressure remains on:
high-duration businesses without free cash flow, heavily indebted companies, energy-intensive manufacturing without pricing power, commercial real estate and legacy automotive models excessively exposed to China without a credible technology transition.
The German auto debate deserves particular attention. Chinese groups such as BYD, Chery and Leapmotor are expanding quickly in Europe while German manufacturers lose share in China. Europe is approaching the point where industrial policy and competitive strategy become inseparable.

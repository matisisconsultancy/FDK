THE NEW COMMERCE OF INTELLIGENCE
China Moves to Define the Unit of AI Value. Europe Confronts the Price of Sovereignty. Financial Markets Demand Proof of Returns.
3-minute read
THE BIG TAKE
The AI Supercycle is moving beyond the competition to build computing capacity toward a more consequential contest: who controls the commercialization, distribution and financial value of intelligence.
Three developments define November 23.
China has announced plans to strengthen standards for AI-token measurement, billing and quality assessment, positioning tokens as an emerging commercial unit of the intelligent economy.
Europe is beginning to translate digital sovereignty into procurement decisions. Yet American hyperscalers still control more than 70% of Europe’s cloud market, according to a European Commission estimate cited by Reuters today.
Financial markets are reassessing capital-intensive AI businesses. Meanwhile, the ECB’s latest financial-stability analysis reveals the extent of contractual commitments underlying the infrastructure boom. National Data Bureau +2
The central thesis: The next phase of the AI-native economy may reward companies that control commercially useful intelligence, customer relationships and cash-flow conversion—not simply computing infrastructure.
1 | CHINA: THE TOKEN SOVEREIGNTY DOCTRINE
On November 20, China’s National Data Administration outlined plans to improve standards governing AI-token metering, billing and quality evaluation. The official account was published November 21.
The agency described emerging token factories that combine computing capacity, models and datasets into metered intelligence services.
No final technical standard or mandatory pricing mechanism has been published. National Data Bureau
The hidden pattern: China is exploring the institutional foundations of intelligence as a measurable commercial service.
The causal chain is:
Computing → Tokens → Intelligent Services → Commercial Transactions → Revenue.
But tokens measure processing activity, not necessarily economic value.
The critical distinction will be between the cost of generating tokens and the value of completing useful tasks.
For Europe, the challenge is to develop interoperable, competitive AI-service markets before technological dependence becomes commercial dependence.
2 | EUROPE: SOVEREIGNTY ENTERS THE PROCUREMENT MARKET
Reuters’ November 23 investigation identifies a shift in European cloud purchasing.
Public institutions and regulated businesses increasingly demand operational autonomy, encryption-key control and the ability to switch providers.

3 | THE GREAT SPLIT: THE FINANCIAL SUPERVISORS ENTER THE AI CYCLE
The ECB’s November Financial Stability Review estimates more than $4 trillion in off-balance-sheet commitments among major technology companies as of the second quarter of 2026.
These commitments are not equivalent to outstanding debt.
Euro-area banks had €24.3 billion in commitments to 165 important AI-supply-chain businesses and subsidiaries as of June, including €11 billion undrawn. The ECB also identifies risks from interconnected financing and circular commercial relationships. European Central Bank
The financial transmission:
Infrastructure Commitments → Financing Exposure → Utilization Risk → Cash-Flow Coverage → Credit Spreads.
The financial concern is not necessarily a collapse in computing demand. It is whether demand becomes profitable quickly enough to support the accumulated obligations.
Today’s Asian equity weakness reinforces the market’s continuing sensitivity. But early Monday trading does not establish a new structural deterioration in AI operating revenues. Reuters
4 | ENERGY AND INDUSTRIAL PRODUCTIVITY
The IEA’s November 19 World Energy Outlook 2026 projects global data-centre electricity consumption doubling to approximately 1,000 TWh by 2030 in its Stated Policies Scenario.
The same report projects data-centre-related emissions rising approximately 70% by 2030. These are modeled scenarios, not realized outcomes. IEA +2
For Europe, electricity costs and grid access will remain critical determinants of industrial AI competitiveness.
Yet the Commission’s November economic forecast also identifies stronger exports of European power, cooling and semiconductor equipment benefiting from global AI investment.
Europe can capture industrial value from the AI Supercycle without dominating frontier-model development.
The European Commission’s current forecast, however, makes no adjustment for a future AI contribution to total factor productivity. There is no new verified economy-wide AI productivity inflection today. Economy and Finance
5 | EUROPE 2030: THE CAPITAL-ALLOCATION CONFLICT
Today’s new political signal is significant.
More than 75 European companies and industrial associations warned EU leaders against reducing the proposed European Competitiveness Fund.
The latest negotiating proposal would reduce the overall 2028–2034 budget by €40 billion relative to October’s draft.
The earlier proposal had already reduced the Competitiveness Fund to €356 billion in 2025 prices, according to the companies’ assessment. These remain negotiating figures, not adopted appropriations. Reuters
EU leaders will discuss the budget on November 26–27.
The policy contradiction: Europe wants to accelerate industrial and technological competitiveness while facing pressure to constrain the very investment instruments intended to support it.
For policymakers, the priority should be additional private-capital mobilization, commercially accountable investment and measurable industrial returns.

THE NEW RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS
THE SOVEREIGN INTELLIGENCE PREMIUM
Could control over commercially deployable intelligence become more valuable than ownership of the computing infrastructure producing it?
NER-C should investigate whether providers offering operational autonomy, verified task quality and low switching costs can command more durable revenue and superior risk-adjusted returns.
The proposed financial indicators are quality-adjusted cost per completed task, customer retention, migration costs, operating margins and incremental ROIC relative to WACC.
The hypothesis remains unproven. Today’s evidence establishes changing procurement preferences and prospective measurement standards, not a verified valuation premium.
THE FINAL VELOCITY INSIGHT
THE NEXT GREAT CAPITAL ROTATION WILL FOLLOW ECONOMIC CONTROL
The AI Supercycle is creating a new architecture of economic power.
China is seeking to organize and measure the commercial exchange of intelligence.
Europe is attempting to turn sovereignty into procurement demand while confronting financial and industrial constraints.
And capital markets are demanding greater evidence that infrastructure commitments will generate sustainable cash flows.For investors, the critical opportunity lies in identifying who captures the financial value of intelligence.
For corporate boards, it is reducing dependence while improving productivity.
For European policymakers, it is connecting capital formation, energy competitiveness and industrial deployment.
The winners of the Next Economy will not necessarily be those who produce the most intelligence—but those who can turn intelligence into the greatest sustainable economic value.

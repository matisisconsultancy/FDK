America is building maximum capability. China is optimizing maximum diffusion.
THE EVIDENCE
In the United States, the AI buildout is becoming an integrated capital-and-infrastructure system.
SpaceX is reportedly seeking roughly $40 billion of financing to acquire Nvidia AI chips.
Google has secured 3.59 GW of U.S. power capacity through long-duration agreements with Constellation Energy.
Major private-capital firms are assembling increasingly large pools of financing around data centers, accelerated computing and power infrastructure.
At the same time, frontier U.S. models continue to define the top end of capability.
China is moving differently.
DeepSeek is reportedly preparing a major financing round while expanding around Huawei Ascend infrastructure and a more independent domestic software stack.
Chinese models continue to improve rapidly while competing aggressively on price.
Industrial deployment is accelerating across manufacturing, logistics, commerce, mobility and infrastructure.
The evidence points to a clear structural divergence:
THE UNITED STATES IS SCALING THE FRONTIER.
CHINA IS SCALING THE ECONOMICS BENEATH IT.
⸻
FROM EVIDENCE TO THESIS
The significance of these developments is not simply that both countries are investing more in AI.
It is that they are investing against different economic constraints.
The U.S. constraint is increasingly physical: frontier intelligence requires more advanced chips, larger clusters, more power and unprecedented amounts of capital.
The Chinese constraint is technological: restricted access to the most advanced U.S. compute requires China to extract more capability from domestic hardware while driving down the cost of deployment.
Their responses are creating two different production systems.
The U.S. flywheel
better models
→ more compute
→ larger capital commitments
→ frontier capability
→ premium economic value
The Chinese flywheel
lower-cost models
→ cheaper inference
→ more deployment
→ higher utilization
→ faster learning and industrial adoption
This is why the race can no longer be judged by benchmark leadership alone.
THE U.S. ADVANTAGE DEPENDS ON THE ECONOMIC VALUE OF THE CAPABILITY GAP.
If frontier models generate materially greater productivity, U.S. leadership can support extraordinary investment and attractive returns.
But if cheaper Chinese models become sufficiently capable for most commercial workloads, the gap can remain technologically real while becoming economically less valuable.
That is the inflection.
China does not need to build the world’s best model.
It needs to make paying a large premium for the world’s best model unnecessary across an expanding share of the economy.
And once that happens, the competitive question shifts:
**FROM WHO OWNS THE FRONTIER
TO WHO OWNS THE ECONOMICS OF INTELLIGENCE.**
The U.S. still leads the technology frontier.
China is increasingly challenging the economic rent attached to that lead.
⸻
THE THESIS
This is becoming two AI economies.
The U.S. production model
**frontier models
frontier chips
hyperscale compute
dedicated power
deep private capital**
The Chinese production model
**near-frontier models
domestic accelerators
lower inference costs
open software
rapid industrial deployment**
The United States is building the most powerful intelligence system ever assembled.
China is trying to make intelligence sufficiently cheap and pervasive that deployment scale matters almost as much as absolute capability.
The central question is therefore:
WHO CREATES THE MOST ECONOMIC VALUE FROM INTELLIGENCE — AND WHO CAPTURES IT?
⸻
AMERICA: THE ECONOMICS OF MAXIMUM CAPABILITY
The American advantage rests on four reinforcing layers.
Frontier models — leadership at the highest-capability end.
Accelerated computing — continued dominance in advanced AI infrastructure.
Capital formation — private credit, institutional capital and hyperscaler balance sheets financing the buildout.
Power — long-duration energy contracts becoming part of the competitive moat.Together, these create an unprecedented industrial machine for producing intelligence.
But that machine is extraordinarily capital intensive.
The financial question is therefore:
CAN AMERICA PRESERVE ENOUGH PRICING POWER TO EARN ATTRACTIVE RETURNS ON THE CAPITAL REQUIRED TO BUILD THE FRONTIER?
⸻
CHINA: THE ECONOMICS OF MAXIMUM DIFFUSION
China’s strategy is driven by three levers.
Cost Compression
Lower inference costs expand the number of economically viable AI workloads.
Domestic Substitution
Huawei Ascend and local software ecosystems reduce dependence on U.S. technology.
Industrial Deployment
AI is pushed rapidly into factories, logistics, commerce, mobility, energy and infrastructure.
The objective is not necessarily to maximize model-provider margins.
The larger prize is productivity.
If cheaper intelligence reduces defects, lowers inventories, optimizes grids or automates workflows, much of the value can accrue to the user of AI, rather than the model vendor.
CHINA’S BET IS THAT AI CAN CREATE MORE VALUE AS AN INPUT TO THE ECONOMY THAN AS A PREMIUM PRODUCT IN ITS OWN RIGHT.
⸻
THE PROFITABILITY EQUATION
Lower prices do not necessarily mean lower total economic value.
The sequence can be:
lower inference cost
→ more viable workloads
→ higher usage
→ deeper workflow integration
→ higher productivity
→ larger economic surplus
But that surplus can accrue to different layers:
model providers through scale;
cloud and semiconductor companies through utilization;
platforms through distribution and transactions;
industrial companies through productivity gains;
consumers through lower prices and better services.
DIFFUSION CREATES THE SURPLUS. IT DOES NOT DETERMINE WHO CAPTURES IT.
China can therefore succeed on diffusion even if model margins compress.
And the U.S. can dominate frontier capability while facing a return-on-capital challenge if the economic premium for frontier intelligence erodes too quickly.
That is the financial tension between the two AI economies.
⸻
THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECT
The U.S. AI system is absorbing extraordinary capital across:
chips
memory
networking
data centers
power
private credit
infrastructure funds
If intelligence becomes cheaper faster than capital intensity declines:
REVENUE CAN GROW WHILE RETURNS ON CAPITAL FALL.
China, by contrast, may generate less profit per unit of intelligence but far greater consumption of intelligence across its economy.
That creates an important distinction between:
profit per token
and
economic value per token.
The eventual winners will combine both.
⸻

TECHNOLOGICAL VELOCITY × CAPITAL FORMATION × DIFFUSION VELOCITY × VALUE CAPTURE
The United States currently dominates frontier technology and capital formation.
China is increasingly challenging on unit economics and diffusion.
Neither system wins simply by producing more intelligence.
It must convert that intelligence into sustainable economic returns.
United States
Maximum capability
Maximum capital formation
Higher required returns on capital
China
Near-frontier capability
Lower unit economics
Broader deployment
More dispersed value capture
The strategic contest is therefore about who creates the most economic surplus from AI — and who retains the highest-quality share of it.

That means separating:
AI adoption from AI monetization
AI volume from AI margins
AI beneficiaries from AI value capturers
The variables that increasingly matter are:

The most valuable businesses may not be those charging the highest price for intelligence.
They may be those using increasingly cheap intelligence to generate the highest returns elsewhere in the economy.
⸻
THE VELOCITY EDGE — WHAT’S NEXT
The U.S. still leads the frontier.
China is increasingly challenging the economics beneath it.
The distinction is precise:
AMERICA IS TRYING TO MAXIMIZE THE RETURNS ON FRONTIER INTELLIGENCE.
CHINA IS TRYING TO MAXIMIZE THE ECONOMIC IMPACT OF LOWER-COST INTELLIGENCE.
Both models can create enormous value.
They distribute that value differently.
The decisive measure of the next AI era will therefore be:
HOW MUCH ECONOMIC SURPLUS DOES AI CREATE — AND WHO CAPTURES IT?
The race will ultimately be decided by the system that combines capability, diffusion and capital efficiency most effectively.
THE AI RACE IS NO LONGER ABOUT WHO BUILDS THE SMARTEST MACHINE. IT IS ABOUT WHO BUILDS THE MOST PROFITABLE ECONOMY AROUND INTELLIGENCE.

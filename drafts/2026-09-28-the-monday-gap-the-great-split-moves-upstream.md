The AI boom is no longer just about who builds the best technology. It is about who controls the scarce inputs that make technology scale.

Oil and long-term yields are rising again this morning while equities soften. Brent is back around $107, and the market is reassessing whether higher rates are becoming structural rather than temporary.

That changes the investment regime.

Higher rates do not simply compress valuations.

Scarce power does not simply raise costs.

More compute does not automatically create value.

Together, they create a new hierarchy:

CONTROL FIRST. RETURNS SECOND. SCALE THIRD.

The next phase of the AI Supercycle will be won less by those who build the most—and more by those who can finance, power and monetize scale better than everyone else.

⸻

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

What happens when the most capital-intensive technology cycle in history collides with scarce power and expensive capital?

The answer is not simply slower growth.

It is concentration.

The winners will increasingly control:

capital
power
compute
customers
cash flow

Everyone else pays somebody else’s price.

⸻

1 | SCARCITY BECOMES THE NEW MOAT

The U.S. long end remains around 5%+, while investors increasingly question how high the neutral rate may settle.

That is more than a valuation problem.

It raises barriers to entry.

It strengthens balance-sheet leaders.

It weakens optionality for capital-dependent challengers.

And it accelerates consolidation.

SCARCITY DOES NOT JUST RAISE PRICES.

IT REDISTRIBUTES POWER.

The second-order effect is that higher rates may make AI more oligopolistic, not less important.

⸻

2 | POWER MOVES FROM COST TO STRATEGIC ASSET

The deeper bottleneck is no longer compute alone.

AI, electrification, defence and reshoring all compete for the same physical infrastructure:

generation
grids
storage
cooling
transmission

Oil above $100 reinforces the pressure, but the structural issue is broader.

COMPUTE WITHOUT POWER IS STRANDED CAPITAL.

As power becomes scarcer, bargaining power moves upstream.

Utilities, grids, storage, cooling and power-connected infrastructure capture more of the economics.

The next AI winners may increasingly sit outside traditional technology.

⸻

3 | THE AI TRADE MOVES FROM CAPACITY TO RETURNS

Markets have rewarded announced capacity.

That phase is becoming harder.

Corporate bond investors are already becoming more selective toward AI-linked issuance as hyperscaler borrowing rises and return visibility becomes more important.

The question therefore changes from:

How much can you build?

to:

What return can you earn?

That favors:

contracted demand
memory
networking
enterprise AI
power-secured infrastructure

THE AI RACE IS BECOMING A RETURN-ON-CAPITAL RACE.

The second-order effect is decisive:

capital structure becomes market share.

⸻

4 | CHINA’S AI ACCELERATION MAY INTENSIFY GLOBAL FRICTION

China’s industrial-profit growth slowed again in August as strong technology manufacturing failed to offset weak domestic demand.

That divergence matters.

AI raises productivity and industrial capacity.

But weak domestic absorption pushes more of that capacity outward.

The chain becomes:

AI PRODUCTIVITY → EXCESS CAPACITY → EXPORT PRESSURE → TRADE FRICTION → INDUSTRIAL POLICY

The result may be more geopolitical fragmentation before global productivity gains fully materialize.

The Great Split is increasingly visible inside economies as well as between them.

⸻

5 | EUROPE’S OPPORTUNITY MOVES TO THE PHYSICAL STACK

Europe’s strategic vulnerability is becoming clearer.

The value layer in mobility and industry is shifting toward:

AI software
compute architecture
data
autonomy

Europe risks owning the machine while renting the intelligence.

But Europe still controls major parts of the physical stack:

grids
rail
automation
semiconductor equipment
mobility infrastructure
energy systems

DO NOT BUY EUROPE AS ONE MACRO TRADE.BUY THE BOTTLENECKS EUROPE CONTROLS.

⸻

THE HIDDEN PATTERN

The Next Economy is being reorganized around three scarce resources:

CAPITAL. POWER. COMPUTE.

AI increases demand for all three.

Geopolitics restricts them.

Higher rates reprice them.

Scarcity then creates second-order effects:

higher barriers
greater concentration
stronger pricing power
more vertical integration
more industrial policy

The system is moving from efficiency to control.

That is the deeper evolution of The Great Split.

AI

FINAL TAKE — THE GREAT SPLIT MOVES UPSTREAM

Scarcity is now doing three things at once.

It is raising barriers.

It is concentrating returns.

It is moving value upstream toward those who own the bottlenecks.

That means the next winners increasingly sit where capital, power and compute meet:

grids
power generation
memory
networking
cooling
contracted infrastructure
platforms capable of financing scale

The strategic implication is simple.

Investors: own scarcity, contracted demand, pricing power and strong balance sheets.

Executives: secure power, financing and customers before committing to scale.

Policymakers: sovereignty now depends on the ability to finance, power and industrialize innovation.

THE VELOCITY INSIGHT

The first AI race rewarded intelligence.

The second rewarded scale.

The third will reward control of scarcity.

WHEN EVERYONE WANTS THE FUTURE, THE REAL RETURNS ACCRUE TO THOSE WHO OWN WHAT THE FUTURE CANNOT DO WITHOUT.

That is where capital moves next.

That is where The Great Split moves upstream.

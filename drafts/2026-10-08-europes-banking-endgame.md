CALTAGIRONE CHANGES THE ARITHMETIC. LOVAGLIO MUST CHANGE THE ECONOMICS.

8 October 2026 | Morning Edition

THE DECISIVE SHIFT

The MPS contest has entered its final strategic phase.

Francesco Gaetano Caltagirone has formally moved against the extraordinary resolutions at the October 29 MPS shareholders’ meeting.

Together with Delfin, the two largest strategic shareholders represent a base opposition bloc equal to:

27.79% OF MPS SHARE CAPITAL

That does not mathematically close the vote.

But it changes the nature of the contest.

Lovaglio can no longer prevail simply by defending the architecture he has already presented.

He must now produce a materially better economic reason for shareholders to remain independent.

**CALTAGIRONE HAS CHANGED THE ARITHMETIC.

LOVAGLIO MUST NOW CHANGE THE ECONOMICS.**

If he cannot, Intesa’s advantage moves from momentum toward control.

And once control shifts, the consequences move immediately beyond MPS.

1 | THE ARITHMETIC

WHY 27.79% AGAINST REQUIRES 83.37% TURNOUT

The critical point is simple:

MPS does not need two-thirds of its entire share capital to approve the extraordinary resolutions.

It needs:

TWO-THIRDS OF THE VOTES CAST AT THE MEETING

That means the result depends on two variables:

how much share capital actually votes;

and

how much of that voting capital votes NO.

The current base opposition positions are:

Delfin: 17.53% of MPS share capital

Caltagirone Group: 10.26% of MPS share capital

Together:

27.79% OF TOTAL SHARE CAPITAL

Assume all 27.79% attends and votes NO.

Now let T equal the percentage of total MPS share capital represented by votes cast at the meeting.

The YES vote can therefore be no greater than:

T − 27.79

For the resolutions to pass, that YES vote must equal at least two-thirds of all votes cast:

T − 27.79 ≥ ⅔T

Rearranging:

⅓T ≥ 27.79

Therefore:

T ≥ 83.37%

The intuition is even simpler.

At 83.37% turnout, the 27.79% NO bloc represents exactly one-third of all votes cast:

27.79 ÷ 83.37 = 33.33%

The remaining:

83.37 − 27.79 = 55.58%

represents exactly two-thirds of votes cast:

55.58 ÷ 83.37 = 66.67%

So:

83.37% IS THE BREAK-EVEN TURNOUT.

Below 83.37%, a fixed 27.79% NO bloc exceeds one-third of votes cast and the resolutions cannot reach two-thirds.

Above 83.37%, approval becomes mathematically possible — but only if virtually all additional votes are YES.

This distinction is essential:

**27.79% IS THE NO BLOC AS A PERCENTAGE OF TOTAL SHARE CAPITAL.

83.37% IS THE MINIMUM TOTAL CAPITAL THAT MUST VOTE FOR A TWO-THIRDS YES MAJORITY TO REMAIN POSSIBLE.**

The Treasury’s 4.86% stake is expected not to participate.

Maximum theoretical representation therefore falls from 100% to:

95.14%

At 95.14% turnout with 27.79% voting NO:

YES = 95.14 − 27.79 = 67.35

and:

67.35 ÷ 95.14 = 70.79%

So the resolutions remain mathematically passable.

If Banco BPM’s roughly 3.74% also stays away, maximum representation falls to:

91.40%

Then:

YES = 91.40 − 27.79 = 63.61

and:

63.61 ÷ 91.40 = 69.60%

Still above two-thirds.

Therefore, under the 27.79% base opposition case, even Treasury and Banco BPM non-participation do not mathematically block Lovaglio.

They simply make the coalition he needs exceptionally demanding.

2 | THE CALTAGIRONE VARIABLE

WHY 13.49% WOULD CHANGE THE EQUATION

MPS separately reports that the Caltagirone Group held 13.49% of share capital at the May dividend-payment date.

That figure should not yet be treated as 13.49% of confirmed voting power for October 29.

The conservative voting case therefore remains 10.26%.

But if the full 13.49% were confirmed as voting shares exercisable against the resolutions:

Delfin 17.53% + Caltagirone 13.49% = 31.02%

The same formula applies.

For a two-thirds majority to remain possible:

T ≥ 3 × 31.02

Therefore:

T ≥ 93.06%

At 93.06% turnout:

31.02% NO = exactly one-third of votes cast

and:

62.04% YES = exactly two-thirds.

That is why the 13.49% question matters so much.With the Treasury absent, theoretical maximum participation is only 95.14%.

So in the 31.02% opposition case, Lovaglio would have only:

2.08 PERCENTAGE POINTS OF TURNOUT HEADROOM

before the mathematics close.

And if Banco BPM’s 3.74% also stays away:

Maximum turnout = 91.40%

which is below the required 93.06%.

In that specific case:

THE RESOLUTIONS WOULD BE MATHEMATICALLY BLOCKED.

The distinction is therefore precise:

**10.26% CALTAGIRONE → EXTREMELY DIFFICULT.

13.49% CALTAGIRONE → POTENTIALLY DECISIVE.**

3 | DELFIN AND CALTAGIRONE: SAME VOTE, DIFFERENT ENDGAME

The two shareholders now reinforce one another against Lovaglio.

But they should not be treated as a permanent bloc.

Delfin has committed its MPS position to Intesa.

Caltagirone has opposed Lovaglio without formally binding itself to the Intesa transaction.

That difference is strategically important.

Delfin has chosen a transaction.

Caltagirone has preserved optionality.

They can therefore vote together on October 29 without wanting the same outcome on October 30.

That matters most for Mediobanca and Generali.

The old shareholder coalition is fragmenting at precisely the moment the ownership architecture around Italian savings is being rebuilt.

⸻

4 | LOVAGLIO’S ONLY REAL QUESTION

CAN HE CHANGE THE VALUE EQUATION?

Lovaglio’s problem is no longer strategic imagination.

It is credibility.

The current defence asks shareholders to underwrite:

Banco BPM, Banca Generali, Mediobanca integration, Generali exposure and a significantly more complex capital structure.

Intesa offers something easier to price:

CASH + INTESA EQUITY + EXECUTION CERTAINTY

Lovaglio therefore has only three meaningful levers.

MORE VALUE

Increase the immediate economic return to shareholders through distributions or other capital measures.

The problem is obvious.

If MPS uses Generali or excess capital to improve the defence, it may weaken the institution it is trying to preserve.

LESS COMPLEXITY

Reduce the number of interdependent transactions.

That could lower execution risk and make the standalone case easier to value.

But remove too much and the economic rationale for independence begins to disappear.

MORE TIME

Use the period around the October 15 board meeting to clarify the shareholder map, assess proxy-adviser positions and redesign the proposal.

But:

TIME WITHOUT A BETTER ECONOMIC OFFER IS ONLY DELAY.

Lovaglio must prove that remaining independent creates more risk-adjusted value than Intesa’s offer.

⸻

5 | THE INSTITUTIONAL FLOAT DECIDES — UNLESS THE ARITHMETIC CLOSES

Under the 27.79% base opposition case, institutional investors remain decisive.

Lovaglio requires exceptionally high turnout and overwhelming support from the remaining voting capital.

They will judge the independent strategy on:

ROTE

EPS accretion

CET1 consumption

synergies

integration costs

dividend capacity

execution probability

and terminal value

The argument cannot be:

MPS deserves to remain independent.

It must be:

MPS INDEPENDENCE IS WORTH MORE THAN INTESA’S OFFER.

But there is an important qualification.

If the opposition bloc grows sufficiently large that the required turnout exceeds the maximum capital capable of participating, institutional support can no longer solve the problem.

At that point, Lovaglio must change the opposition arithmetic itself.

That means changing a vote, changing participation, or changing the proposal sufficiently to fracture the NO coalition.

6 | WHY GENERALI MATTERS

Generali sits at the centre of Lovaglio’s dilemma.

It can potentially provide financial flexibility.

But it is also one of the principal strategic assets underpinning the wider MPS–Mediobanca architecture.

That creates the central contradiction:

THE MORE GENERALI IS USED TO DEFEND MPS TODAY, THE LESS GENERALI REMAINS TO JUSTIFY MPS INDEPENDENCE TOMORROW.

This is also where the Delfin–Caltagirone divergence becomes more important.

If Intesa acquires MPS, Mediobanca and its Generali exposure move into a much larger banking perimeter.Delfin has chosen that route.

Caltagirone has not.

That may become the next important fault line after October 29.

8 | THE EUROPEAN ENDGAME

Domestic consolidation is reducing the number of independent financial platforms large enough to matter.

As that happens, strategic scarcity shifts toward:

Banco BPM

Generali

Banca Generali

and the remaining cross-border opportunities.

For Crédit Agricole, the question becomes whether to convert influence in Banco BPM into control.

For UniCredit, the question becomes whether the next euro of capital should buy greater domestic depth or deepen the Commerzbank strategy.

For Brussels and the ECB, the question becomes institutional.

Europe has created a Banking Union capable of supervising banks across borders.

It has not yet demonstrated that it can accommodate cross-border ownership at true continental scale.

COMMON SUPERVISION WITHOUT COMMON OWNERSHIP IS AN INCOMPLETE BANKING UNION.

9 | WHAT’S NEXT

Three things now matter.

FIRST — THE TRUE CALTAGIRONE VOTING POSITION

At 10.26%, the base Delfin–Caltagirone opposition is 27.79% and the break-even turnout is 83.37%.

At 13.49%, the bloc becomes 31.02% and the break-even turnout rises to 93.06%.

That is the single most important arithmetic distinction.

SECOND — WHO ELSE DOES NOT VOTE

Every absent shareholder lowers the maximum available turnout.

When maximum possible turnout falls below three times the fixed NO bloc, the resolutions are mathematically blocked.

That is the general rule.

THIRD — OCTOBER 15

Lovaglio must decide whether to deliver:

more value, less complexity, more time — or all three.

Everything now flows from those three variables.

10 | KEY TAKEAWAYS

1 | The threshold is two-thirds of votes cast — not two-thirds of MPS share capital.

2 | A fixed NO bloc can therefore block the resolutions by becoming more than one-third of whatever capital actually votes.

3 | With 27.79% voting NO, turnout must reach at least 83.37%.

4 | At exactly 83.37% turnout, 27.79% is one-third and 55.58% is two-thirds.

5 | With 31.02% voting NO, turnout must reach at least 93.06%.

6 | Treasury non-participation caps theoretical turnout at 95.14%.

7 | If Banco BPM is also absent, theoretical turnout falls to 91.40%.

8 | Therefore 27.79% NO does not mathematically block the vote even with Treasury and BPM absent.

9 | But 31.02% NO would mathematically block it if Treasury and BPM are both absent.

10 | The general formula is simple:

MINIMUM REQUIRED TURNOUT = 3 × FIXED NO BLOC

If maximum possible turnout falls below that number, the two-thirds majority is impossible.

VELOCITY INSIGHT

The arithmetic is now transparent.

Lovaglio does not need two-thirds of MPS.

He needs two-thirds of whoever votes.

That makes turnout as important as ownership.

At 27.79% opposition, he still has a route.

At 31.02%, the route becomes extraordinarily narrow.

Add enough non-participation and it disappears entirely.

**THE QUESTION IS NO LONGER ONLY HOW MANY SHARES OPPOSE LOVAGLIO.

IT IS HOW LARGE THAT OPPOSITION BECOMES AS A SHARE OF THE VOTE THAT ACTUALLY SHOWS UP.**

Three developments on October 22–24 materially strengthen The Velocity Edge thesis—but introduce an important qualification: America's capital-intensive AI infrastructure strategy is demonstrating substantial forward demand, even as China advances its alternative model ecosystem.

The emerging divergence is no longer simply between American technological superiority and Chinese inference efficiency. It increasingly concerns who controls the scarce inputs—computing capacity, investment capital and deployable intelligence—that determine the economics of the AI Supercycle.

::signal
- **America is selling computing capacity years ahead**, with AWS largely committed through 2027 and substantial commitments into 2028.
- **Amazon expects roughly $220 billion** in capital expenditure during 2026, suggesting contracted rather than purely speculative demand.
- **China's Moonshot unveiled Kimi K3.1 Pro**, a three-trillion-parameter-class model with a one-million-token context window and open weights due November 4.
- **Moonshot deliberately cut cyber-offensive capability**, its internal benchmark falling from 90.5% to 81.0%, revealing a diffusion-versus-control trade-off.
- **Washington proposed expanded outbound-investment rules** on October 22, with the consultation period closing December 7, 2026.
- **Scarcity is moving up the value chain**, as computing capacity, capital and deployable intelligence now define the Supercycle's economics.

::signals The Three Developments | October 22–24
# Amazon: American capacity contracted into 2028
On October 23, CEO Andy Jassy disclosed that AWS computing capacity is largely committed through 2027, with substantial commitments extending into 2028, and indicated additional investment could be economically attractive given current demand.

This is an important counterweight to market concerns about excessive AI infrastructure spending: at least part of America's hyperscale expansion is supported by substantial customer commitments. But contracted capacity does not guarantee returns—contract quality, utilization, operating margins, depreciation and free-cash-flow conversion remain the decisive tests.
signal: America's infrastructure advantage resembles a strategic capacity franchise whose value depends on whether committed demand becomes durable cash flow.

# Moonshot: China's frontier challenge enters a new phase
On October 24, Chinese developer Moonshot introduced Kimi K3.1 Pro, a three-trillion-parameter-class model with a one-million-token context window and a faster variant reportedly two to three times quicker. Open weights are planned for November 4, 2026.

Significantly, the company deliberately reduced cyber-offensive capabilities while seeking to preserve coding performance—its internal benchmark falling from 90.5% to 81.0% (results not independently validated). China's challenge now extends beyond DeepSeek, but unrestricted diffusion grows harder as models acquire national-security-relevant capabilities.
signal: China faces a trade-off between openness and control; November 4 is a test, not yet evidence of a changed frontier ranking.

# Washington: capital becomes a strategic bottleneck
On October 22, the U.S. Treasury proposed regulations expanding restrictions on American investment in sensitive technologies tied to China and other designated countries—spanning AI, advanced semiconductors, quantum, supercomputing and more. These are proposals, not newly effective prohibitions; the existing regime remains in force and consultation closes December 7, 2026.

Ordinary public-market securities generally remain excepted; the implications are more direct for covered venture investments, acquisitions and joint ventures. Washington is moving beyond export controls toward tighter control over the capital, expertise and relationships that finance advanced technology.
signal: Capital formation is becoming a contested component of the AI system, raising financing fragmentation and incentives for Chinese domestic capital.

::section The Hidden Pattern: Scarcity Is Moving Up The Value Chain | transmission mechanism
America controls valuable computing capacity and is seeking to protect its technological and financial advantages. China is expanding model capabilities and diffusion strategies while seeking greater independence from American technology and capital.

The resulting transmission mechanism: computing scarcity → forward infrastructure commitments → capital concentration → strategic restrictions → domestic substitution → competing AI ecosystems.

The fundamental insight is that restrictions intended to preserve leadership can simultaneously encourage alternative industrial ecosystems. This does not imply equivalent capabilities—it means the global AI economy may become increasingly fragmented even as demand for intelligence keeps expanding.

::section The Second-Order Effect: Repricing The AI Supercycle | three financial tests
The developments create three distinct financial tests. **American infrastructure:** does forward-contracted capacity generate adequate returns on invested capital? **Chinese frontier developers:** can increasingly capable models achieve sustainable inference economics and international diffusion under growing regulatory constraints? **Global capital markets:** will investment restrictions accelerate separate American and Chinese AI financing ecosystems?

These questions determine whether the Supercycle produces broad-based productivity growth or an increasingly concentrated distribution of returns. The next critical dates: **October 29**, Amazon's third-quarter results; **November 4**, Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro weights; and **December 7**, the comment deadline for the proposed outbound-investment rules.

::section The Velocity Edge — Investment Verdict | one important refinement
The October 24 evidence strengthens our central thesis, with one refinement. America's capital intensity is not merely a financial vulnerability—when accompanied by substantial contracted demand, it can reinforce a structural competitive advantage. China's increasingly sophisticated models strengthen its challenge to deployment economics, but do not yet establish superior total computing costs or frontier leadership.

The market should distinguish three sources of value: ownership of scarce computing capacity, control over capital formation, and the ability to commercialize intelligence at scale. The next decisive inflection will arrive when independent evidence shows whether Chinese models can deliver comparable commercial workloads at materially lower total cost—and whether American hyperscalers can convert infrastructure commitments into superior free cash flow.

::close
America leads the frontier and controls much of its scarce capacity; China is challenging the economics of intelligence. ==The next battle will be won by those who turn technological advantage into economic power—without losing control of the capital required to sustain it.==

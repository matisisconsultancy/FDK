Bloomberg Intelligence now estimates that the best Chinese models trail leading U.S. models by only 3% on benchmark performance, down from roughly 9% in May and 15% earlier in 2026. The frontier has not changed hands—but the rate of convergence has accelerated sharply.

DeepSeek's September V4.1 Flash scored 81.1 on LiveBench versus 83.4 for Anthropic's best model, making it the strongest Chinese showing since DeepSeek R1. Only three of the top 15 models are Chinese, so the United States still leads the frontier. What is changing is the economics around it.

This materially strengthens our central thesis. The U.S. remains ahead in frontier capability, but China is compressing the capability gap while simultaneously attacking the economics of intelligence. The challenge is no longer only that Chinese models are cheaper—it is that increasingly comparable capability is being delivered with lower-cost models, domestic hardware optimization and a rapidly developing alternative software stack around Huawei Ascend.

Bloomberg Intelligence explicitly attributes part of the catch-up to Chinese researchers becoming better at optimizing models for domestic hardware despite U.S. chip restrictions.

::signals The Inflection | what the convergence means

# The second-order effect outweighs the benchmark
If a 15% performance gap can compress to 3% within months while Chinese models remain materially cheaper, the premium attached to frontier intelligence becomes harder to defend outside the most demanding workloads.

That shifts value away from model scarcity and toward distribution, inference cost, proprietary data, accelerated-computing efficiency and industrial deployment.
signal: Value moves from model scarcity to the production and diffusion of intelligence.

# Abundance, not dominance, reshapes the economics
China does not need to dominate the absolute frontier to reshape AI economics. It only needs to make near-frontier intelligence abundant enough to accelerate adoption across manufacturing, logistics, energy, robotics and mobility.
signal: Near-frontier intelligence made abundant is the strategic lever.

# The U.S. counter-signal
Nvidia-backed Reflection is preparing a powerful U.S. open-weight model explicitly aimed at matching leading Chinese open models and enabling enterprises to build localized, lower-cost "AI factories" on Nvidia infrastructure. Axios notes that the most powerful open-weight models are currently overwhelmingly Chinese.

The U.S. response appears to be moving beyond closed frontier leadership toward defending the open, lower-cost inference layer where China has been gaining strategic ground.
signal: America is moving to defend the open, lower-cost inference layer.

::section The Great Split, Sharpened | the production system for intelligence

For The Velocity Edge, this sharpens the Great Split: the competitive unit is no longer the model alone. It is the production system for intelligence. America still leads at the frontier through superior models, capital, chips and hyperscale infrastructure. China is increasingly competing through efficiency, open models, domestic hardware adaptation and faster diffusion.

For the AI Supercycle, the implication is bullish for infrastructure but more discriminating for margins. Near-frontier intelligence becoming cheaper does not reduce demand for compute; it expands the number of economically viable AI workloads. The next leg of the cycle moves from model scarcity to token abundance, then from token abundance to industrial AI volume.

That favors power, memory, networking, inference infrastructure, industrial software and physical-world deployment—but increases pressure on standalone model economics.

::takeaways Key takeaways | the strategic inflection

- The frontier has not changed hands; the economics around it are changing faster than expected.
- The U.S. still builds the strongest intelligence—superior models, capital, chips and hyperscale infrastructure.
- China is getting increasingly close and is trying to make that intelligence cheaper, more open and more pervasive.
- The AI Supercycle stays bullish for infrastructure but more discriminating for standalone model margins.

::close The race is moving from who creates the frontier to ==who industrializes it first==.

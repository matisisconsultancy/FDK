Weak U.S. payrolls have lowered the prospective cost of money. Nvidia is moving deeper into power infrastructure. European equity inflows are accelerating. Hormuz is reminding markets that physical access still commands the highest scarcity premium.

The pattern is becoming clear: AI is no longer constrained primarily by intelligence. It is constrained by **capital formation**.

The next phase of the Supercycle will be decided by who can finance, power, secure and scale intelligence faster than the cost of capital, infrastructure and geopolitical fragmentation rise around it. The Great Split is therefore shifting again — from technology versus legacy industry to ==capital-rich versus capital-constrained systems==.

::pull THE NEW CONSTRAINT
Intelligence is not the bottleneck. The bottleneck is the capital, power and access required to industrialize it.

::signals Five Key Signals | the shape of the shift
# Weak employment is becoming a financing event
The U.S. payroll contraction has reduced tightening expectations and lowered sovereign yields, improving the financing environment for capital-intensive AI infrastructure.
signal: A softer labor print reads through directly to cheaper AI buildout.

# Nvidia is evolving from supplier to capital allocator
Its proposed investment in Lancium signals a deeper move into the infrastructure ecosystem that creates demand for its own accelerated computing.
signal: The chipmaker is now financing the demand for its own chips.

# Europe is attracting marginal capital before capturing narrative leadership
Large equity inflows suggest investors are rotating toward banks, grids, defence, automation and industrial infrastructure.
signal: Capital is arriving in Europe ahead of the story.

# Security is becoming a mandatory AI input
Frontier-model deployment increasingly requires containment, cyber protection, auditability and regulatory approval — raising the cost of trusted intelligence.
signal: Trusted intelligence carries a rising security tax.

# Hormuz confirms that access commands more value than ownership
Oil can exist at the source and still be economically unavailable. The same logic applies to chips, electricity, grids, data centres and capital.
signal: Access, not ownership, sets the scarcity premium.

::takeaways Five Key Takeaways | what to hold onto
- The cost of money matters more as AI becomes more capital intensive.
- The value pool is migrating from models toward infrastructure and financing.
- The next winners will control access, not merely assets.
- Europe's opportunity lies in industrial conversion, not imitation of U.S. model platforms.
- The Great Split is becoming a capital-formation divide.

::close The defining competitive advantage of the next decade may not be who invents intelligence first — but ==who can finance its industrialization fastest==.

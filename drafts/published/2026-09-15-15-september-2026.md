The Next Economy is discovering a new scarcity. Intelligence may be becoming more abundant, but assured access to the systems that industrialize intelligence is becoming more expensive: access to energy routes, accelerated computing, frontier models, secure data, advanced lithography and capital.

Yesterday we identified *The Three Speed Limits* — Energy, Capital, Governance. This morning the deeper implication becomes visible: **when systems hit speed limits, control of access becomes valuable.** That is today's Velocity Spread.

::pull THE ACCESS SPLIT
Competitive advantage is moving from owning technology toward controlling the gateways through which technology becomes economically usable.

::section The Fundamental Question | scarcity migrates
What happens when intelligence becomes cheaper but reliable access to everything required to deploy it becomes more expensive?

The old causality chain — AI Cost ↓ → Usage ↑ → Accelerated Computing ↑ → Productivity ↑ — is being replaced. Intelligence abundance rises, but access to power, chips, models, secure data and capital carries a rising scarcity premium.

This creates a new version of The Great Split. On one side: companies and countries with guaranteed access to energy, accelerated computing, models, data, infrastructure and financing. On the other: those dependent on somebody else's infrastructure, somebody else's model, somebody else's power system and somebody else's balance sheet.

::signals The Five Most Important Overnight Signals | market data through the European morning of September 15
# Hormuz Has Turned Energy Scarcity Into an Access Problem
Only four commodity vessels crossed the Strait of Hormuz on Monday — two tankers entered, both in ballast — down from ten on Sunday and roughly 125 major vessels daily before the war, when the strait handled around 20% of global crude and LNG flows. Bab el-Mandeb traffic fell to 21 vessels from 28. Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, recently moving about 4 million barrels per day to Yanbu to bypass Hormuz, remains disrupted; traders warn as much as 4% of global supply could be removed from normal channels.

Nominal production capacity is no longer enough. The new chain reads: Energy Capacity − Logistics Risk − Security Risk = Accessible Energy Supply.
signal: The scarcity is increasingly the route, not the resource.

# The World's Hurdle Rate Is Now 5%
The Fed begins its two-day meeting today, with markets pricing ~93% odds of a hike tomorrow; 86 of 101 economists surveyed by Reuters expect a 25bp move to 3.75–4.00%, and a majority expect at least one further increase by end-March. The U.S. 10-year briefly crossed 5% for the first time since 2023, Germany's Bund topped 3.51% (highest since 2009), and Japan's 10-year is back near 3%.

The industry is attempting perhaps the largest capital-formation cycle since electricity and telecommunications — precisely when the risk-free rate is becoming more expensive. The new test: Return on Intelligence > 5% + risk premium.
signal: Anything below the new hurdle rate will struggle for capital.

# The AI Selloff Is Not Yet an AI Infrastructure Slowdown
Monday's response to calls for slower frontier development was violent: Nvidia −3.4%, Micron −5%+, Broadcom and AMD each −4%+, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index −5.9%. Yet the physical evidence differs. ASML's ~$200M EUV machines are essentially sold out through 2027; TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix have committed to its new $400M High-NA systems, printing features ~40% smaller. ASML held ~94% of the lithography market in 2025. JPMorgan says ASML is examining ways to produce more than 110 EUV systems in 2028, after targeting at least 80 in 2027.

Model velocity can slow while infrastructure velocity continues. Training may become more disciplined, but inference, deployment, sovereign AI and enterprise adoption can keep expanding.
signal: Frontier-model slowdown and infrastructure slowdown are no longer the same trade.

# Enterprise AI Has Discovered the Value of Trusted Access
Palantir, Nvidia and Booz Allen are reportedly reconsidering or restricting frontier-model use over proprietary-data and IP concerns. Palantir has sought irrevocable zero-data-retention guarantees; Nvidia reportedly limits Anthropic usage for sensitive work and relies on its own Nemotron models; Microsoft is commercializing the concern via isolated cloud environments. The question is shifting from "which model is smartest?" to "which model can I trust with my most valuable information?"
signal: Secure inference could become one of the highest-value layers in the enterprise AI stack.

# Europe Has Finally Identified AI Dependency as a Macroeconomic Risk
Christine Lagarde's Vienna speech made AI sovereignty macroeconomic. Europe risks dependence on external AI systems across border control, hospitals, banks, transport and public administration; its data-center capacity deficit could grow more than six-fold within a decade, even as AI adoption could lift European productivity by as much as 4% over ten years. She also flagged a financial exposure: U.S. tech investment needs are pushing into European debt markets, potentially raising borrowing costs, while European pension funds hold substantial U.S. tech exposure — so an American AI correction reaches European household savings.
signal: Europe is exposed twice — technology and finance — yet controls ASML, one of the industry's most important chokepoints.

::patterns Five Hidden Patterns Beyond the News | structural forces
# Scarcity Is Migrating From Intelligence to Access
Models proliferate and intelligence prices decline, but the entities capable of providing advanced lithography, secure power, trusted models, sovereign data environments and long-duration capital remain far smaller. Scarcity rents migrate from the model toward the gateway.

# The AI Supercycle Is Splitting Into Two Cycles
The frontier-model cycle may slow on governance and safety concerns. The industrialization cycle — data centers, inference, power, networking, memory, fiber, enterprise deployment — may continue regardless. The Great Split becomes Model Velocity vs. Infrastructure Velocity.

# Security Is Becoming Part of Product Quality
A model 5% smarter but unable to guarantee data protection may be economically inferior to a secure, less powerful one. The same applies to infrastructure: capacity without resilience, protection or sovereignty increasingly carries a discount.

# AI Leverage Is Becoming a Systemic Financial Variable
The BIS warned that the AI rally shows rising vulnerability as leverage climbs. Tech-company borrowing in private credit grew from ~$22 billion in 2010 to more than $1 trillion by 2025 — roughly 44% of private credit — with total tech-related loans near $2.5 trillion, and opaque, off-balance-sheet, circular structures flagged. AI is becoming a credit-market story.

# Sovereign Capital and AI Infrastructure Are Converging
SoftBank-backed SB Energy plans to sell up to $500 million of shares directly to Japanese investors in its U.S. listing. It develops both data centers and power generation. Nvidia has committed $1.5 billion to a private placement at the IPO price; OpenAI holds warrants valued at ~$5.5 billion. The boundaries between technology, infrastructure, energy and finance are disappearing.

::section Implications for Investors | three layers
The market may now separate the AI trade into three layers. **Scarcity Owners** — advanced lithography, accelerated computing, HBM, networking, optics, electricity, transformers, grid, secure cloud, fiber — possess physical bottleneck power. **Capital-Intensive Builders** — data centers, AI cloud, infrastructure platforms — face extraordinary demand but decisive financing pressure near a 5% risk-free rate. **Intelligence Applications** benefit from falling model costs but risk commoditization unless they hold proprietary data, distribution or domain expertise.

Do not merely ask who owns the best AI. Ask **who controls access to the scarce inputs AI cannot operate without.** That is where scarcity rents increasingly reside.

::section Implications for Executives | the Access Dashboard
Boards now need an Access Dashboard alongside their AI dashboard, measuring guaranteed accelerated-computing and electricity capacity, power-contract duration, memory availability, network and fiber redundancy, model portability, data-retention terms, sovereign-data compliance, capital availability, refinancing cost and alternative suppliers.

The key question is no longer what AI we use, but **what critical input we depend on that someone else can withdraw.** Any single point of dependency should be treated as a strategic balance-sheet liability.

::section Implications for Europe and Policymakers | access independence
Lagarde's warning should become industrial-policy doctrine. Europe does not need technological autarky; it needs strategic access independence — sufficient European-controlled capacity in accelerated computing, power, grid, data centers, fiber, secure cloud, industrial AI, semiconductor equipment and capital formation.

ASML demonstrates the power of controlling a critical gateway. Europe should build more such gateways. The objective is not to replicate every American model, but to guarantee Europe can keep operating economically if access conditions change — more achievable, and more important, than trying to recreate Silicon Valley.

The Great Split now emerging is not simply technology versus everything else, nor intelligence abundance versus physical scarcity, but increasingly **the Access Owners versus the Access Dependents.**

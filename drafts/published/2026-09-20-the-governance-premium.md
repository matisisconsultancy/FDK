The Italian banking battle has moved again. Yesterday, the key question was the Price of Scale. Today the deeper issue is becoming visible: control after consolidation.

There has been no new binding corporate transaction or regulatory decision announced during the Sunday morning window. MPS's public investor-relations archive still shows its latest formal deal-related release on 18 September. But the weekend has produced material new information about the architecture behind the MPS defence — and it changes the interpretation of the entire Risiko.

According to *la Repubblica*, citing MPS correspondence with Consob, a successful combination of MPS, Banco BPM and Banca Generali would create a shareholder structure in which Crédit Agricole could become the largest shareholder — and potentially, its largest shareholder would be French. This is no longer simply a battle about scale. It is a battle about the ==Governance Premium==.

::pull THE GOVERNANCE PREMIUM
MPS is defending its independence by designing a bank that would have no dominant controlling shareholder.

::section What Changed In The Last 24 Hours | industrial and governance mechanics
The first change is not a new offer. It is greater visibility into the industrial and governance mechanics of the existing offers.

The MPS–Banco BPM industrial plan reportedly assumes approximately 5,000 voluntary early-retirement departures to produce up to €400 million in personnel savings, partly offset by roughly 2,500 new hires. The reported antitrust modelling identifies between 57 and 115 potentially excess branches depending on whether the relevant local concentration threshold is assumed at 30% or 25%. That makes the MPS plan more credible — but also more politically visible.

MPS reportedly intends to purchase from Mediobanca the entire 13.3% Generali stake before the 29 October meeting, even though only around 4.5% of Generali would be required, at announcement values, for the €3 billion in-kind portion of the extraordinary shareholder distribution. The destination of the residual stake — roughly 8.8% under a simple arithmetic interpretation — therefore becomes strategically important.

MPS separately disclosed on 18 September that it acquired 100,000 Banca Generali shares at €56 through the exercise of a call option entered into in April, while simultaneously closing a 92,000-share securities-lending position. MPS explicitly described the underlying transaction as part of its ordinary Markets activity; the numbers are immaterial and should not be interpreted as evidence of strategic accumulation.

One development outside Italy may ultimately matter even more. Senior EU officials meeting in Dublin on 18 September explicitly argued that Europe needs larger banks, deeper capital markets and less political interference in bank mergers. ECB Vice President Boris Vujcic said European banks need greater scale to compete with U.S. institutions, while stopping short of advocating weaker capital requirements. The Italian Risiko is therefore beginning to converge with the European policy agenda.

::section The Decisive Issue | who governs the Third Pole?
Until now, the strategic question surrounding MPS has been: can Lovaglio build sufficient scale to block Intesa? The new question is harder: if he succeeds, who controls the institution that emerges?

The reported post-transaction cap table suggests no shareholder would exercise conventional control. Crédit Agricole could be first at 10.9%, Delfin second at 8.3%, Generali third at 6.4%, followed by Caltagirone at 4.9%, BlackRock at 3.9% and the Italian Treasury at 2.3%.

That transforms the equation. Strategic independence no longer equals single-shareholder control; strategic independence now equals ==stable coalition governance==. Capital is increasingly international. Regulation is increasingly European. But political legitimacy remains national. The institution capable of reconciling all three obtains the Governance Premium.

::signals Updated Strategic Map | the eight-actor board
# Intesa Sanpaolo — Execution Remains The Benchmark
Intesa still has the cleanest execution path. Its shareholders have already authorized the capital increase supporting the MPS offer, with 96.96% of represented capital voting in favour. The principal obstacle is now regulatory rather than financial: AGCM has identified possible competition issues in deposits in 20 provinces, SME lending in 17 provinces and, importantly, in the relationship between Intesa and Generali arising through MPS/Mediobanca's 13.3% stake.
signal: FDK View — STRONG OVERWEIGHT. How much strategic value must be given away to obtain clearance?

# MPS — The Control Paradox
MPS's public plan already envisages €1.4 billion of pre-tax benefits from Banco BPM and €400 million from Banca Generali, with the weekend reporting now providing greater operational granularity. But if both offers succeed, MPS would preserve independence from Intesa while creating an institution in which Crédit Agricole could become the largest shareholder. The 29 October meeting becomes a vote on the future coalition architecture of the bank.
signal: FDK View — HIGH CONVEXITY / HIGH GOVERNANCE RISK. MPS can win the battle for independence and lose the ability to govern alone.

# Banco BPM — The Industrial Engine
Banco BPM remains the indispensable operating asset in the MPS third-pole thesis, bringing Northern Italian density, SME lending, deposits, commercial distribution and the Anima franchise. But its largest shareholder could become the largest shareholder of the entire new group. Crédit Agricole therefore possesses a migration path from target shareholder to system shareholder.
signal: Banco BPM is the bridge through which French capital could enter the centre of Italian banking.

# Crédit Agricole — From Kingmaker To Potential Anchor
Crédit Agricole owns 29.3% of Banco BPM and reportedly still considers the MPS offer unattractive, preferring a combination with Crédit Agricole Italia. But if MPS acquires Banco BPM, it moves upstream — emerging with approximately 10.9% of the full combined institution, or around 12.5% if only the Banco BPM transaction succeeds.
signal: FDK View — STRATEGIC OVERWEIGHT. Oppose, renegotiate, or become the largest shareholder of the deal.

# Generali — The Centre Of Gravity Moves Closer
Generali could simultaneously be a major underlying strategic asset, the controlling shareholder of Banca Generali, a shareholder of the enlarged MPS group, and a key competition variable in the Intesa transaction. The reported plan to acquire the entire 13.3% stake before distributing only part dramatically increases the importance of the residual holding.
signal: FDK View — OVERWEIGHT. Scarcity + strategic independence + time.

# Banca Generali — The Optionality Asset
Its value lies less in ordinary banking scale than in high-quality wealth distribution. At Friday's close, the equity still trades materially below the adjusted implied value of MPS's exchange proposal. The market continues to price a substantial probability that execution will fail or terms will change.
signal: That is not a negative signal on the asset — it is an Execution Discount on the transaction.

# UniCredit–Commerzbank — Ownership Almost Solved; Governance Not
UniCredit has accumulated 47.6% of Commerzbank, close to effective shareholder control. Berlin has moved from outright resistance toward conditions: Commerzbank should remain headquartered and listed in Frankfurt and continue supporting the Mittelstand. Reuters reports UniCredit wants leadership changes and opposes continuing German government representation on the supervisory board.
signal: The debate has shifted from "can UniCredit acquire?" to "what governance model makes continental scale politically acceptable?"

::section Capital-Markets Reaction And Valuation | Friday 18 September, the last signal
There is no Sunday trading, so Friday 18 September remains the last available market signal. The session was broadly risk-off: the FTSE MIB fell 1.6%, UniCredit declined 3.0%, Intesa 2.1%, Banco BPM 2.0%, MPS 1.5% and Generali 1.9%. Italy's 10-year yield rose to approximately 4.43% and the BTP–Bund spread finished near 91 basis points.

The transaction spreads remain more interesting than the absolute moves. MPS closed at €11.76; netting the announced €1.208 extraordinary distribution gives an adjusted reference value of approximately €10.55. Using MPS's 1.567 exchange ratio, the implied value for Banco BPM is approximately €16.53, versus its €15.70 close — an execution spread of roughly 5.3%. Using the 6.958 Banca Generali exchange ratio, the same methodology implies approximately €73.42 per share, against a €65.00 close — a spread of roughly 13.0%.

In Germany, UniCredit closed at €81.13 and Commerzbank at €40.64. The existing 0.485-share exchange ratio mechanically implies approximately €39.35 per Commerzbank share — about 3.2% below market price. Investors are still pricing improved economics, strategic optionality or a control premium beyond the existing terms.

::section Political And Regulatory Implications | Rome, Brussels, Frankfurt
The Italian Government still holds roughly 4.9% of MPS. Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti has repeatedly described the government as neutral while reserving the possibility of Golden Power conditions relating to competition, SME lending and territorial presence. The Treasury has indicated it intends to avoid selling its residual stake in a way that interferes with the ongoing consolidation.

But the European context is moving against broad political discretion. Brussels is explicitly pushing for fewer political barriers to bank M&A, while ECB leadership argues Europe requires larger institutions and deeper capital markets. This creates a fundamental institutional tension: **Rome wants strategic safeguards. Brussels wants market integration. Frankfurt wants prudential soundness.** The winning transaction must satisfy all three. That is what Banking Union now means in practice.

::section The Hidden Pattern | from national champions to coalition champions
The Italian banking consolidation debate has traditionally been framed around ownership: who controls MPS, who protects Generali, who anchors Banco BPM, who keeps Italian savings Italian. But the latest architecture suggests that framing may be becoming obsolete.

The future European champion may not have one controlling shareholder. It may have French banking capital, Italian industrial families, international asset managers, an insurance company, a residual sovereign stake — and an executive team responsible for holding the equilibrium together. That is a different model of capitalism. ==Capital is becoming European faster than political legitimacy is becoming European.== This is why governance is becoming the scarce asset — and it produces a new law: continental scale without coalition stability destroys value; coalition stability without scale destroys competitiveness. The winners will produce both.

::close The next European banking champion will not simply be the bank that becomes biggest. It will be the bank that can turn fragmented ownership, national politics and European regulation into a ==stable system of control==.

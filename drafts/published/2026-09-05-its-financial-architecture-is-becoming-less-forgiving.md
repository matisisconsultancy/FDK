The Great Split is no longer between AI leaders and AI laggards. It is between companies whose intelligence earns the cost of its infrastructure—and those whose infrastructure is becoming more valuable than the intelligence it was built to produce.

::pull THE NEW DIVIDE
The question is no longer *can you build it*. It is whether what you build can ever pay for what it took to build.

::signals The Split | how to read the new divide
# Intelligence that earns its keep
On one side stand the companies whose models, products and workflows generate returns that justify the capital sunk into compute, data and infrastructure. Here, intelligence is the asset and the infrastructure is merely its cost of production.
signal: When output covers the build, infrastructure stays a means to an end.

# Infrastructure that outgrows its purpose
On the other side sit companies where the infrastructure—the data centers, the silicon, the committed spend—has quietly become more valuable than the intelligence it was meant to produce. The scaffolding has eclipsed the building.
signal: When the plumbing outvalues the intelligence, the thesis has inverted.

::takeaways Key takeaways | the board-level read
- The meaningful divide is now economic, not technical: leaders versus laggards has given way to earners versus over-builders.
- Intelligence must justify the cost of its infrastructure, or the infrastructure becomes the story.
- Watch for the inversion—where infrastructure value exceeds the intelligence it supports.

::close The next winners will be those whose intelligence ==earns== its infrastructure—not those who mistook the infrastructure for the win.

AI does not need to trigger another rate hike to matter. It is already reshaping the price of capital before it reshapes the price of everything else — and the sequence has a decisive asymmetry.

Three signals now point in the same direction, describing a single monetary path: AI capex drives input and financing pressure, tighter financial conditions follow, and central banks are left with less room to cut. Only later does AI diffusion lower unit costs, deliver disinflation, and restore room to ease.

::pull The tightening effect is here. The easing effect is still forming.

::signals Three signals, one direction | Central banks converge

# Real yields
Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack points to heavy technology investment intensifying financing demand.
signal: AI capex is lifting the real cost of capital now.

# Capacity
The ECB sees AI pressure in semiconductor supply, investment and corporate financing.
signal: Input and financing constraints are showing up across the chain.

# Financial stability
Kansas City Fed President Jeff Schmid is asking whether the AI ecosystem itself could become systemically important.
signal: The build-out is now a stability question, not just a growth one.

::section Central-bank implication | The lag that matters

If the build-out keeps capital demand, real yields and input costs elevated, central banks may have to cut later, cut less, or tighten again if second-round inflation broadens.

The key variable is the lag between capital formation and productivity diffusion. The tightening arrives first; the easing is still forming.

::patterns Two second-order effects | Where this leads

# Capital concentrates
Higher hurdle rates push weaker firms to retrench, capital flows to leaders, and capability gaps widen. This is how monetary tightening can accelerate the K-shaped economy.

# The build-out becomes a credit cycle
High real yields plus weaker AI returns create refinancing pressure, wider spreads, and eventually capex cuts and project failures. This is how an AI valuation correction can become a credit and investment event.

::section Investor takeaway | Not "more AI"

The next phase is not about owning "more AI." It is about identifying who can access capital, who can clear the hurdle rate, who can self-finance, and who sits on fragile leverage.

Favor scarce infrastructure selectively. Demand strong balance sheets and cash-flow conversion. Track refinancing risk underneath the build-out.

::takeaways Key takeaways | The monetary read on AI
- AI is already a tightening shock through capital formation; the disinflationary payoff is delayed.
- Real yields, capacity pressure and financial-stability risk all point the same way.
- Higher hurdle rates concentrate capital and can accelerate the K-shaped economy.
- The build-out carries a latent credit cycle if AI returns disappoint.
- Prioritize balance-sheet strength, cash-flow conversion and refinancing resilience.

::close ==The regime flips when AI stops tightening through capital formation — and starts easing through lower unit costs.==

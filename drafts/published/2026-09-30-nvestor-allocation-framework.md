The sequence is simple: GVI → Economic Capture → ROIC vs. Cost of Capital → Valuation → Allocation. Capital now flows to where velocity, utilization and cash conversion align — and away from spending that expands the asset base faster than it produces cash.

The first phase rewarded building. The next rewards earning. **Do not pay for spending. Pay for productive capital.**

::pull The positive signal
Velocity ↑ + Utilization ↑ + Cash Conversion ↑ — the warning signal reverses it: Capex ↑ + Utilization ↓ + Returns ↓.

::patterns The Four Regimes | Velocity · capture · returns · allocation logic
# Compounders
Rising velocity, strong economic capture, improving returns. Allocation logic: **increase conviction**.
# Priced Leaders
High velocity, strong capture, durable returns. Allocation logic: **hold; watch expectations**.
# Conversion Tests
High velocity, incomplete capture, unproven returns. Allocation logic: **wait for cash proof**.
# Capital Traps
Falling velocity, weak capture, deteriorating returns. Allocation logic: **reduce / reassess**.

::section Sector Allocation Map | The migration of economics across the AI stack
The allocation hierarchy is shifting: MODEL EXPOSURE → BOTTLENECK OWNERSHIP → UTILIZATION → DISTRIBUTION → SECURITY → CASH. The allocation shift is not from technology to infrastructure — it is from technological promise to economic necessity.

::patterns Allocation Zones | Structural thesis · financial test · representative exposure
# Power & Grid
AI demand collides with constrained electrical capacity. Test: pricing power + backlog conversion + time-to-power. Exposure: Schneider Electric · Siemens Energy.
# Advanced Semiconductor Infrastructure
Compute growth increases dependence on manufacturing and packaging bottlenecks. Test: utilization + pricing + capital discipline. Exposure: TSMC.
# Cooling & Data-Center Infrastructure
Higher compute density raises thermal and physical requirements. Test: orders → deployment → cash conversion. Exposure: Vertiv / infrastructure ecosystem.
# Hyperscale Cloud
Capacity becomes valuable only when enterprise demand fills it. Test: capex → utilization → FCF. Exposure: Amazon · Microsoft.
# Enterprise Distribution
Cheaper intelligence increases the value of customer and workflow ownership. Test: monetization per installed user / workflow. Exposure: Microsoft · Meta.
# Agentic Security
Greater AI autonomy creates permanent governance and security demand. Test: recurring revenue + operating leverage. Exposure: Palo Alto Networks.
# Governed AI Deployment
Enterprises pay for operationalization, not model access alone. Test: deployment → recurring revenue → FCF. Exposure: Palantir.
# Frontier Models
Strategic importance remains high, but capital requirements escalate. Test: revenue growth must outrun compute obligations. Exposure: Anthropic.
# Capital-Intensive Legacy Industries
Transformation capital competes with repair capital. Test: ROIC recovery + balance-sheet flexibility. Exposure: autos · chemicals · other repair-mode sectors.

::signals Where Conviction Should Rise | And where the burden of proof is climbing
# Scarcity owners with pricing power
Power, packaging, cooling and grid infrastructure sit closest to unavoidable constraints.
signal: Own the constraint; own the economics.
# Infrastructure with visible utilization
Hyperscale capex becomes attractive only when demand converts rapidly into recurring cash.
signal: Capacity earns its multiple only when demand fills it.
# Distribution with monetization
As models converge, customer ownership and workflow integration gain bargaining power.
signal: Where AI reaches the customer, value accrues.
# Security with recurring economics
Agentic adoption structurally expands identities, permissions and attack surfaces.
signal: Autonomy makes governance a permanent line item.
# Frontier-model economics — burden rising
Technological leadership increasingly has to outrun fixed compute commitments.
signal: Revenue must outrun compute obligations.
# Capacity built ahead of demand — burden rising
Unutilized infrastructure converts strategic ambition into depreciation and duration risk.
signal: Idle capacity is depreciation, not strategy.
# Legacy transformation stories — burden rising
Capital used to repair existing businesses competes directly with capital available for new profit pools.
signal: Repair capital competes with future profit pools.

::section Board-Level Implications | Govern AI as capital allocation, not a technology program
Five questions matter: Is AI capital earning above its cost? How much installed capacity is actually utilized? Where is the binding bottleneck? Is time-to-cash shortening? Is capital funding future profit pools — or repairing the past?

::pull The Board Test
Does the next dollar of AI investment generate cash faster than it expands the asset base? If not, scale may be increasing while value creation deteriorates.

::takeaways Five Takeaways | The rotation happens inside AI
- **AI is becoming a financial selection mechanism.** Technology leadership without superior returns will be repriced.
- **Scarcity is moving toward the physical stack.** Power, packaging, cooling and grids increasingly control the economics of deployment.
- **Capital productivity is replacing capex as the key test.** The decisive variable is the speed from investment to utilization to cash.
- **Higher-for-longer raises the cost of being early.** Idle capacity, delayed power and distant monetization become increasingly expensive.
- **The next rotation happens inside AI.** Models → Bottlenecks → Utilization → Distribution → Security → Cash.

::close Own what AI cannot function without. Own where AI reaches the customer. Own the control layer. Demand proof everywhere else — ==technology creates the opportunity; capital productivity decides who captures it==.

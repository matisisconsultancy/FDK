The overnight evidence changes the composition of global velocity more than its headline level. AI demand is now transmitting visibly from compute into exports, factories, memory, networking and power—while the global cost of capital remains restrictive.

The AI boom is no longer confined to technology valuations. It is becoming an ==industrial production cycle==.

::pull Compute is beginning to reorganise the physical economy around it.

::section The Regional Divergence | overnight evidence
South Korea delivered the strongest acceleration. September exports surged 83.5% y/y to a record $120.9 billion, semiconductor exports rose 263%, and manufacturing PMI climbed to 53.9, with export orders at the fastest pace in 15½ years.

Japan also strengthened: the BOJ's September Tankan showed large-manufacturer confidence at +24, the strongest since 2018, and manufacturing PMI held at 54.1. The offset is monetary—the BOJ has lifted its policy rate to 1.25%, with opinions flagging persistent inflation and further tightening.

Europe moved the opposite way. September harmonised inflation rose to 3.3% in Germany, 3.4% in France, 5.0% in Spain and 4.1% in Italy, alongside higher German unemployment. The euro-area flash is not due until October 2, so adjustments use only confirmed national data.

In the US, cooler August PCE at 3.4% (below 3.7% consensus) and an upward Q2 GDP revision to 2.2% improved the policy mix. But the 10-year Treasury still traded around 5.31% overnight, keeping the long-duration hurdle exceptionally high.

::section The Recalculation | GVI 108.0, no ranking changes
The +0.4 points of acceleration in South Korea and Japan are offset by -0.4 across the four euro-area economies with new inflation evidence. The weighted Global Velocity Index therefore remains 108.0. No ranking changes.

The United States leads at 94.3, China follows at 91.2, South Korea rises +0.3 to 88.8, and Japan gains +0.1 to 85.0. Germany eases -0.1 to 83.1, the United Kingdom holds at 82.3, France slips -0.1 to 81.3, with Switzerland at 80.7, the Netherlands at 80.4 and Sweden at 79.1 rounding the top ten. Denmark (77.8), Norway (77.1), Spain (74.9, -0.1), Austria (72.4) and Italy (71.7, -0.1) complete the table.

::signals Five-Pillar Readout | where velocity is moving
# Intelligence Infrastructure — accelerating
South Korea's semiconductor surge and Micron's results reinforce the same signal: high-bandwidth memory is one of the hardest constraints in the AI stack. Micron disclosed $32 billion of long-term commitments, up from $22 billion in June, with most 2027 HBM production contracted. HPE announced a $1.2 billion AI-server-rack order and raised its networking outlook.
signal: Memory and networking are confirmed corporate datapoints—material, but not yet enough to move a national score.

# Innovation Velocity — strong and broadening
AI innovation now generates measurable demand through semiconductors, autos, networking and advanced manufacturing across Asia. September factory activity expanded across South Korea, Japan, China and Taiwan.
signal: AI innovation is converting into real demand, not just new models.

# Enterprise Transformation — strongest in Asia
South Korean production and orders reached their strongest rates in roughly five years, and Japan's large manufacturers reported their strongest confidence since 2018.
signal: The buildout is becoming throughput.

# Smart Capital — still the principal decelerator
Softer US inflation has reduced immediate Fed-hike probabilities, but the long end remains above 5%, while European inflation revives ECB-tightening expectations. The Bank of England warned that AI-related debt issuance—roughly $450 billion, about twice 2025 levels—is increasing financial-system vulnerabilities.
signal: The long-duration capital hurdle remains the defining constraint.

# Discovery Capacity — stable
No new high-confidence research, talent or commercialisation release cleared the daily threshold.
signal: No material change today.

::section Confirmed but Not Yet Scored | capacity, not announcements
South Korea has unveiled elements of a $350 billion US strategic-investment package, including a $22.3 billion gas-power project to supply AI data centres and plans for up to $120 billion of nuclear investment. These remain announced programmes rather than operating capacity, so they do not enter today's score. The GVI rewards capacity created, not announcements.

::section The Evolutionary Market Dynamic | AI narrative → productivity
The overnight evidence strengthens a transition building for weeks: AI NARRATIVE → COMPUTE → BOTTLENECKS → INDUSTRIAL PRODUCTION → PRODUCTIVITY.

South Korea is the clearest manifestation: semiconductor scarcity first produced higher chip prices and profits; it is now feeding exports, orders and capex. Japan shows the same through confidence, Micron through contracted memory, HPE through networking orders.

The first leg of the Supercycle concentrated returns in model providers, hyperscalers and accelerators. The next leg should transmit value toward memory, networking, power systems, transformers, cooling, automation, advanced manufacturing and the enterprises that convert AI into higher margins.

But the financial regime is becoming harsher. That creates the defining tension of Q4: **industrial AI acceleration versus 5%+ long-duration capital**. The consequence is not the end of the cycle—it is ==selection==. Cash-generative companies keep investing; bottleneck owners retain pricing power; marginal debt-dependent projects become vulnerable.

::pull The next rotation: long-duration AI → cash-generative AI → physical bottlenecks → industrial AI → productivity winners.

::takeaways Five Takeaways | the industrial leg
- The industrial leg is now in measured data. South Korea's record exports and Japan's confidence are evidence of physical transmission, not expectation.
- Memory is moving from component to strategic bottleneck. Micron's backlog confirms scarcity shifting beyond GPUs toward HBM.
- Europe faces the wrong kind of acceleration. Energy-led inflation raises financing costs precisely when it needs to accelerate AI, grids and industrial investment.
- The next rotation remains inside the AI Supercycle. Capital should broaden from compute toward networking, power, grids, automation and productivity winners.
- ROIC will determine the winners. At 5%+ long rates, AI adoption without cash conversion is a liability: CAPACITY → UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW → ROIC > WACC.

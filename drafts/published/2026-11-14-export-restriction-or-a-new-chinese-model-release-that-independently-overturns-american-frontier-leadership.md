New technical evidence suggests that China's progress in frontier AI remains more dependent on American accelerated computing than its success in lower-cost models might imply.

In a November 12 investigation, reported more widely on November 13 and examined further on November 14, former OpenAI researcher Will DePue identified hardware fingerprints compatible with Nvidia's restricted Blackwell processors in six Chinese open-weight AI models.

::signal
- **New technical evidence** suggests China's frontier AI remains more dependent on American accelerated computing than its lower-cost success implies.
- **Will DePue** identified hardware fingerprints compatible with Nvidia's restricted Blackwell processors in six Chinese open-weight models from four developers.
- **The findings** establish how initial weights were generated, not where full training occurred, and prove no illegal exports.
- **The contradiction** is that China can expand consumption of inexpensive intelligence while remaining exposed to scarce, expensive American computing capacity.
- **Malaysia** has approved roughly $94.7 billion in data-center investments since 2021, exposing regional financing to greater scrutiny.
- **Alibaba's Qwen4-Max-Preview** adds a signal of continued product development but is not yet a decisive inflection.

::section The Models Identified | four developers

The findings cover four developers, with the researcher reproducing key initialization patterns on physical Blackwell hardware.

::patterns Developers and models | hardware fingerprints
# DeepSeek
V4-Flash, V4-Pro.
# Moonshot AI
Kimi K3, K3.1-Pro.
# MiniMax
M3.
# Ant Group
Ling-3.1-flash.

This establishes evidence about how the models' initial weights were generated — not conclusive proof of where or on which hardware their full training occurred. The findings do not establish illegal exports, and full independent replication remains outstanding.

That distinction is essential. The research nevertheless provides a substantial new technical basis for investigating the computing infrastructure behind China's leading models.

::section Why It Changes the Analysis | three layers

The emerging competition has three layers.

::patterns The three layers | structure of the contest
# American Computing Leadership
Nvidia's advanced accelerators remain strategically important to some leading Chinese model developers.
# Chinese Model Efficiency and Diffusion
Open-weight releases, competitive pricing and adaptable deployments allow Chinese AI to compete internationally.
# The Emerging Financial Contradiction
China can expand the consumption of inexpensive intelligence while remaining exposed to scarce, expensive American computing capacity.

This directly strengthens our governing thesis: the United States leads in critical frontier-computing infrastructure, while China challenges the economics and diffusion of intelligence.

It also exposes a potential vulnerability. If Washington restricts offshore computing access, Chinese model developers could experience higher training costs and deployment constraints.

Reuters reported on November 13 that Southeast Asian data-center operators anticipate greater regulatory scrutiny. Malaysia has approved approximately $94.7 billion in data-center-related investments since 2021, making the potential effects on regional infrastructure financing consequential.

::section A Second Development — Alibaba's Qwen4 | continuing product development

Alibaba's November 11 release of Qwen4-Max-Preview adds another relevant signal.

The model supports multimodal reasoning, agentic tasks, tool calling and a one-million-token context window. It remains a limited preview through November 25, without sufficient independently validated evidence to establish a change in global frontier rankings.

This is evidence of continuing Chinese product development, but not yet a separate decisive inflection.

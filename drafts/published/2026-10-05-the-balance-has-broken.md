Delfin has moved. The shareholder arithmetic has tightened. October 29 is now a test of whether MPS can still assemble the coalition required to keep the Lovaglio architecture alive.

The Italian banking Risiko has entered its decisive phase. Delfin — MPS's largest shareholder with roughly **17.6%** — has committed its stake to Intesa Sanpaolo's revised offer and aligned itself against the defensive resolutions. That matters more than Intesa's additional €800 million: the contest has moved from industrial logic to shareholder arithmetic.

Intesa has the lead. MPS still has a path — but it has narrowed. October 29 is now a three-trigger test across Banco BPM, Banca Generali and the capital restructuring supporting the defence. Approval of any one can jeopardise Intesa's offer; failure of all three leaves the Intesa path open.

::section What Changed | Delfin created the first hard block
Intesa raised the cash component of its bid to €1.25 per MPS share, alongside 1.6 Intesa shares. But price did not fundamentally change the contest. Delfin did.

Its commitment reduces the pro-MPS voting pool and makes Caltagirone and the institutional investors the next decisive actors.

::section The Voting Arithmetic | The decisive issue
The hurdle is a two-thirds majority of votes cast on each defensive resolution — not two-thirds of total MPS capital. That produces three useful thresholds.

With Delfin alone at 17.6% NO, MPS needs turnout above roughly **52.8%**. With Delfin plus disclosed Caltagirone at about 27.9% NO, MPS needs turnout above roughly **83.7%**. At the treasury-adjusted breakpoint of about 31.7% NO — with the Treasury's roughly 4.9% absent — that level of organised opposition would effectively exhaust MPS's mathematical room.

This is the arithmetic that now governs the contest.

::section Politics And Regulation | Private capital chooses. Regulators clear. Governments condition.
The Treasury will not vote its residual MPS stake. The remaining gates are the ECB / SSM, Consob, Italian Antitrust and Golden Power.

Across Europe the same model is emerging around UniCredit–Commerzbank: shareholders choose the transaction; regulators determine executability; governments negotiate strategic safeguards. That is Banking Union advancing transaction by transaction.

::section The Hidden Pattern | The share register has become the strategy
The first phase of the Italian Risiko was dominated by CEOs, ministries and institutional history. The decisive phase increasingly belongs to anchor shareholders, global asset managers, proxy advisers and regulators. Delfin's move crystallises that transition.

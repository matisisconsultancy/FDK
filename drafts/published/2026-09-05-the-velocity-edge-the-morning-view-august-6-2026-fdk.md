The overnight evidence points to a more consequential phase of the AI Supercycle. The US services economy remains strong, new orders are accelerating, and European companies are reporting record industrial profits, expanding cloud backlogs and stronger demand for enterprise AI deployment.

But employment is no longer moving at the same velocity.

The US services employment index has fallen back into contraction. Private payroll creation slowed sharply in July, and some businesses are already attributing reductions in headcount to AI implementation. At the same time, Asian semiconductor shares are selling off despite extraordinary underlying demand, while investors continue to question whether even strong AI revenues can justify the capital intensity, duration and valuations required to produce them.

::section The Fundamental Question | Who captures the value
What if the AI Supercycle is already increasing output, profits and productivity faster than employment, purchasing power and the financial system's capacity to distribute its benefits?

The market is no longer debating whether AI will produce economic value. It is beginning to price ==who captures that value== — and who is displaced while it is being created.

# The AI Productivity Shock Is Appearing in the Labor Market
The US services PMI held at 54.1 in July, while new orders rose to 57.2. Yet the employment component fell from 51.2 to 47.4, indicating contraction. ADP reported that private employers added only 44,000 jobs, down from 95,000 in June and below the 70,000 consensus forecast. The ISM reported that some companies were reducing employment alongside AI implementation, while businesses described strong demand for electrical equipment, longer delivery times and increasing financial stress among smaller suppliers.

This may be the first clear macroeconomic expression of the AI productivity cycle: stronger output, higher orders, fewer incremental workers. AI is beginning to separate business growth from employment growth — positive for operating margins and productivity, but more ambiguous for wage formation, household income and the breadth of consumer demand.

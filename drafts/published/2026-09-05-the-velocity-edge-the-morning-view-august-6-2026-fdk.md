The overnight evidence points to a more consequential phase of the AI Supercycle. The US services economy remains strong, new orders are accelerating, and European companies are reporting record industrial profits, expanding cloud backlogs and stronger demand for enterprise AI deployment.

But employment is no longer moving at the same velocity. The US services employment index has fallen back into contraction, private payroll creation slowed sharply in July, and some businesses are already attributing reductions in headcount to AI implementation.

At the same time, Asian semiconductor shares are selling off despite extraordinary underlying demand, while investors question whether even strong AI revenues can justify the capital intensity, duration and valuations required to produce them.

::section The Fundamental Question | Who captures value — and who is displaced
What if the AI Supercycle is already increasing output, profits and productivity faster than employment, purchasing power and the financial system's capacity to distribute its benefits?

The market is no longer debating whether AI will produce economic value. It is beginning to price who captures that value—and who is displaced while it is being created.

US services activity remained robust, but the combination of higher new orders, rising prices and contracting employment reveals a striking internal divergence. Asian technology markets moved sharply lower, while oil remained below $80 and the ten-year Treasury yield held near 4.61%. Expected US unemployment stands at 4.2%, with consensus for Friday's nonfarm payrolls at 80,000. Third-quarter US Treasury borrowing is expected to reach $739B, moderating to $628B in the fourth quarter.

# The AI Productivity Shock Is Appearing in the Labor Market
The US services PMI held at 54.1 in July, while new orders rose to 57.2. Yet the employment component fell from 51.2 to 47.4, indicating contraction. ADP reported that private employers added only 44,000 jobs, down from 95,000 in June and below the 70,000 consensus. The ISM noted that some companies were reducing employment alongside AI implementation, while describing strong demand for electrical equipment, longer delivery times and increasing financial stress among smaller suppliers.

This may be the first clear macroeconomic expression of the AI productivity cycle: stronger output, higher orders, fewer incremental workers. AI is beginning to separate business growth from employment growth — positive for operating margins and productivity, more ambiguous for wage formation, household income and the breadth of consumer demand. AI is becoming deflationary for headcount while remaining inflationary for capital, electricity and scarce industrial inputs.
signal: This is not a recession signal — it is a signal that the relationship between economic activity and labor absorption is changing.

# AI Demand Is Accelerating. Valuation Support Is Not.
South Korea's KOSPI fell 3.64%, Japan's Nikkei declined 1.57%, SK Hynix lost 6.95%, Kioxia dropped 9.61% and Tokyo Electron fell 4.61%. Yet the broader earnings picture remains exceptionally strong. Capital expenditure by Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Oracle is expected to approach $800 billion in 2026. S&P 500 second-quarter earnings are on course to increase 31.1%, while technology-sector earnings are expected to rise 72%.

The semiconductor selloff does not invalidate the AI infrastructure cycle. It reveals the difference between industrial acceleration, financial expectations, and the price already paid for future leadership. A company can be strategically right and financially overowned. The AI trade is broadening economically while narrowing financially.
signal: The next winners will prove that AI revenue is increasing faster than the capital required to generate it.

# Europe Is Discovering Its Real AI Advantage
Siemens reported its highest-ever quarterly industrial profit and orders: industrial profit rose 25% to €3.52 billion, revenue rose 7% to €20.79 billion and orders advanced 13% to €27.90 billion, with Digital Industries profit up 44%. SAP's cloud backlog increased 26% to €22.9 billion, Capgemini bookings rose 9.2%, Sopra Steria's organic growth accelerated to 5.3% and OVHcloud's public-cloud revenue increased 20.2%. Airbus expects about 70 critical applications to run on the European-controlled Scaleway platform by end-2028. The euro-area Composite PMI rose to 52.0, an eight-month high, with services at 51.7 and Spain's strongest performance in over eighteen months, though France remained in contraction.

Europe may not need to defeat the United States in frontier-model scale to capture a decisive share of AI value. Its opportunity lies in making intelligence operational inside factories, transportation systems, energy networks, defense platforms, healthcare, corporate software and critical infrastructure. The bottleneck is moving from access to models toward the integration of models with proprietary data, legacy systems, regulation, security and physical operations.
signal: The next phase of AI will be won not only by those who produce intelligence — but by those who make it work inside the real economy.

::takeaways Key takeaways | Where the Great Split is heading

- Europe's decisive advantage lies in operational AI — integrating intelligence into industry, energy, defense and critical infrastructure.
- Sovereignty strengthens rather than constrains that opportunity.

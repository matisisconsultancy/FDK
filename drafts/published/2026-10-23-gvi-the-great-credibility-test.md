The AI Supercycle is confronting its most consequential financial test. Semiconductor orders continue to accelerate and corporate investment remains substantial, yet sovereign credibility and confidence in AI returns are deteriorating simultaneously — a widening divergence between technological velocity and the financial capacity to sustain it.

France's sovereign-risk premium has reached levels last seen during the 2011 euro crisis, European consumer confidence is deteriorating, and American technology valuations are under pressure despite resilient employment. The fundamental question is changing: **can technological productivity accelerate faster than financial credibility deteriorates?**

::section The Hidden Pattern — Financial Credibility as a Factor of Production | the transmission to watch
Markets are differentiating more sharply between economies by their capacity to preserve productive investment under fiscal and political stress. France is the clearest example: improving industrial expectations should support confidence, yet yields rise — investors attach growing weight to fiscal sustainability and policy credibility.

The chain: **sovereign credibility down → risk premium up → financing tightens → investment hurdles rise → future productivity at risk.** If persistent, it becomes self-reinforcing as weaker growth erodes revenues and widens premiums further. Sovereign markets reprice in hours; corporate investment responds over quarters; productivity may not reflect the damage for years — a dangerous interval during which headline data stay resilient while future capacity is undermined. Widening Belgian, Italian and Spanish spreads show financial spillovers, not yet contagion; the critical escalation would be persistent increases in bank funding and corporate credit costs. Europe shows stage one.

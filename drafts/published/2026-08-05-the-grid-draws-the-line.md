AMD doubles data-center revenue. SpaceX nearly doubles sales. Caterpillar's backlog reaches $72.1 billion. Yet Texas suspends automatic progression for data-center connections—and markets punish capital expenditure that cannot prove its return.

The fundamental question is now visible: who controls structural velocity when demand for intelligence remains abundant—but electricity, grid permission, physical delivery and capital discipline become scarce? The overnight evidence resolves an important debate. The AI Supercycle is not weakening. It is becoming industrial—and therefore more selective.

::pull THE GREAT SPLIT
The Great Split now separates technological demand from industrial permission—and industrial permission from financial return.

::section The Overnight Evidence | four signals, one message
AMD reported second-quarter revenue of $11.5 billion, up 50%, with data-center revenue more than doubling to $6.72 billion. Yet its shares fell almost 9% after hours because an above-consensus outlook was still insufficient against elevated expectations.

SpaceX reported its first public quarterly results: revenue rose 92% to $7.8 billion, Starlink subscriptions reached 12 million and AI-related revenue expanded sharply. The shares nevertheless fell approximately 7.5% as investors focused on an estimated $18 billion of capital expenditure, much of it associated with AI infrastructure.

Caterpillar provided the opposite signal. Revenue increased 24% to $20.5 billion, construction sales rose 35%, and its backlog reached $72.1 billion as data centers, power generation and infrastructure translated digital demand into machinery orders.

Texas then clarified the new limit. The governor ordered a comprehensive audit of every data-center project advancing through the ERCOT interconnection process. No additional project may move forward until its compliance, power requirements and broader impact have been verified.

::signals The Five Strongest Industries | verified evidence and analytical judgment
# Semiconductors — 97.4
AMD reported quarterly revenue of $11.5 billion, up 50% year over year, with data-center revenue exceeding $6.7 billion and third-quarter guidance of approximately $13 billion—above consensus. AMD is increasingly competing through complete systems combining GPUs, CPUs, networking and data-center capacity, including a major AI-server commitment from Anthropic and up to 2.5 gigawatts of capacity with Core Scientific. Onsemi expects its AI-data-center business to more than double during 2026.

Semiconductors return to first place because the addressable architecture is broadening—from accelerators, high-bandwidth memory, fabrication and packaging into custom processors, networking, optical connectivity, storage, power conversion, edge intelligence and robotics control. AMD's post-results decline is not evidence of weak demand; it is evidence that the financial threshold has risen faster than revenue.
signal: Semiconductor velocity is accelerating even as semiconductor valuations are being disciplined.

# Aerospace & Defense — 97.2
SpaceX's quarterly revenue rose from $4.1 billion to $7.8 billion. Starlink generated more than half of revenue, subscriptions reached 12 million and the company plans to deploy 1,000 next-generation V3 satellites within twelve months. Starlink revenue reportedly grew 66% to approximately $4.3 billion and generated operating profit, while wider AI activities remained heavily capital-intensive. The traditional defense cycle remains supported by historic backlogs and multiyear sovereign procurement.

Aerospace and defense increasingly form one integrated architecture: launch systems, satellites, broadband, intelligence, autonomous platforms and sovereign procurement. Funded production deserves a premium; deployable software deserves a premium; speculative capability without manufacturing throughput does not.
signal: Space infrastructure has become economically observable rather than merely strategic.

# Cloud & Data Centers — 97.1
The Texas governor directed the utility commission and ERCOT to audit every data-center proposal advancing through interconnection. Projects cannot progress until compliance and impact have been verified, and noncompliant projects must be denied grid access. Cloud demand remains exceptionally strong—AMD's data-center growth and SpaceX's expanding compute reinforce the requirement for accelerated infrastructure.

Cloud loses the top position because power availability is no longer equivalent to connection approval. The strongest facilities will possess contracted customers, credible financing, verified load requirements, dedicated generation, water and cooling plans, and demonstrable community benefits. Speculative queue positions will lose value.
signal: Structural demand remains near maximum; the ability to convert announced capacity into operating capacity has become less certain.

# Energy & Power Infrastructure — 97.0
Power systems in northern, northeastern and eastern China reached record loads as extreme heat drove air-conditioning demand. Texas's audit reinforces the same global reality: the electricity system is becoming the binding constraint on data-center deployment. Caterpillar's power and energy business grew 17%.

Energy moves higher despite Brent falling toward $79 per barrel—the Great Split inside energy. Oil scarcity is easing; electricity scarcity is intensifying. Value migrates toward dispatchable generation, grid equipment, turbines, transformers, nuclear supply chains, batteries, microgrids and behind-the-meter systems.
signal: The grid is no longer merely carrying the AI economy—it is determining which parts of it are allowed to exist.

# Artificial Intelligence & Accelerated Computing — 96.6
AMD's data-center business more than doubled; SpaceX reported rapid AI-revenue growth but very high infrastructure expenditure; Palantir's earlier results showed domain-specific AI producing accelerating contracted revenue. AI's score declines modestly because capital expenditure is no longer accepted as evidence of monetization.

The market is differentiating four models: AI infrastructure sold externally, enterprise AI embedded in contracted workflows, consumer AI monetized indirectly, and strategic AI funded by another profitable business. AMD strengthens the first, Palantir the second, SpaceX reveals both opportunity and risk of the fourth.
signal: The technology remains at maximum velocity; the allocation of capital within it is becoming far more demanding.

::section The Conversion Penalty | a new valuation rule
AMD fell approximately 9% after hours despite doubling data-center revenue. SpaceX fell roughly 7.5% despite nearly doubling total revenue. This is a market inflection rather than an industry collapse. It establishes a new valuation rule: the market will pay for growth only when the path from growth to free cash flow is visible.

Financial Services & Fintech (95.4, −0.2) illustrates the pressure on funding structures. SoftBank faces approximately $30 billion of obligations during the second half of 2026 while relying increasingly on loans secured against its investment holdings. Collateral values can fall; funding obligations remain fixed; long-duration investments may require additional capital before generating cash. Financial velocity remains high—financial resilience has weakened marginally.

::section The Legitimacy Constraint | when permission becomes the bottleneck
Power, data centers and mineral projects require more than financing and permits—they require public acceptance. Texas is auditing the effects of computing infrastructure. In South Africa, proposed lithium development affects areas covering more than 73,000 hectares and over 100 farms, intensifying concerns about soil, water, displacement and the trade-off between mineral security and food-producing land. Agriculture (82.6, −0.3) remains the industry least able to insulate itself from competing demands for water, energy, land and infrastructure.

The market must now add a distinction beyond announced versus completed: announced, financed, authorized, connected, utilized and monetized. Every step removes uncertainty. Every incomplete step requires a larger discount.

::takeaways Key takeaways | what the overnight evidence resolves
- Demand is no longer the scarce variable—capital productivity explains the valuation, not the build-out.
- Grid access is becoming a license to operate; only powered, permitted facilities deserve an infrastructure premium.
- AI is pulling industrial capital upstream, from workload to data center to power to construction to machinery to materials.
- Space infrastructure is becoming digital infrastructure as aerospace, telecom, media and computing boundaries collapse.
- The capital-expenditure bar has risen again: the market now asks whether the return exceeds the cost of funding it.
- Own scarcity—but verify permission, utilization and liability.

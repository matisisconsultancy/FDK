The first phase of the AI trade is ending. Compute scarcity created the winners; capital efficiency will separate them.

The U.S.–China contest is accelerating that transition. America still controls much of the frontier. China is attacking the economics of that advantage through cheaper models, domestic substitution, offshore compute access and alternative financing. The market consequence is decisive: ==AI CAPEX is no longer the signal — the return on AI CAPEX is.==

The investment equation has moved from *GPUs → Capacity → Valuation* to *CAPEX → Utilization → Revenue → Free Cash Flow → ROIC*. The next winners will not be those building the most compute. They will be those making compute earn the most.

::pull OWN THE BOTTLENECK BECOMES OWN WHO MAKES THE BOTTLENECK EARN MORE.

::signals The Great Split | Two systems, two equations
# America: the capability bet
The American model is straightforward: *Capital → Frontier Compute → Superior Intelligence → Premium Workloads → Cash Flow → More Capital.* Its advantage remains formidable — advanced silicon, hyperscale infrastructure, frontier models, software ecosystems, enterprise distribution and deep capital markets. But that advantage is becoming extraordinarily expensive.

The financial question is no longer whether U.S. companies can keep spending. They can. It is whether AI revenue can grow faster than AI capital employed. If yes, the American frontier becomes one of history's great compounding machines. If not, today's infrastructure boom becomes tomorrow's ROIC compression.
signal: America seeks higher value per unit of compute — ROIC through capability.

# China: the efficiency bet
China is optimizing a different equation: *Efficient Models → Lower System Cost → Cheaper Intelligence → More Deployment → Higher Utilization.* DeepSeek compresses inference economics, Huawei attacks compute dependency, domestic memory and networking reduce system costs, and offshore capacity bridges remaining hardware gaps.

China does not need to maximize revenue per token. It needs to maximize economic output per dollar of AI capital.
signal: China seeks more utilization per unit of capital — ROIC through volume.

# Compute is becoming an asset class
GPUs are migrating from technology equipment toward financeable productive infrastructure. The relevant economics increasingly resemble *Asset Cost → Financing Cost → Utilization → Contracted Revenue → Cash Flow → Residual Value* — opening AI to private credit, infrastructure funds, sovereign capital, insurers and leasing vehicles.

The AI supercycle can now lever the balance sheet of the financial system, increasing the potential size and duration of the boom. It also introduces the next risks: leverage, refinancing, utilization and obsolescence. AI is becoming a credit cycle as well as an equity cycle.
signal: AI is becoming a credit cycle as well as an equity cycle.

# The rotation from scarcity to efficiency
The first phase rewarded scarcity: *GPU → HBM → Networking → Data Centers.* The second rewards efficiency around scarcity: inference optimization, memory efficiency, networking utilization, cooling, power efficiency and workload orchestration.

The thesis changes from owning the bottleneck to owning who makes the bottleneck earn more — a much broader, and far more selective, trade.
signal: A broader trade, but a more selective one.

# Intelligence deflation redistributes profits
China's biggest contribution to the global AI economy may ultimately be price compression. As intelligence becomes cheaper, *Inference Price ↓ → Consumption ↑↑ → Model Margins ↓ → Infrastructure Utilization ↑ → Application Economics ↑.*

Model scarcity falls, infrastructure volume rises, application value rises. The biggest winners may not be the companies producing the model. They may increasingly be the companies owning scarce infrastructure — or using commoditized intelligence to generate measurable productivity and cash flow.
signal: Economic rents migrate from the model to infrastructure and applications.

::patterns The Great Split Becomes a Financial Contest | ROIC, two ways
# 🇺🇸 United States
Frontier CAPEX → superior intelligence → premium workloads → higher revenue per compute unit. **ROIC through capability.**

# 🇨🇳 China
Lower-cost stack → cheaper intelligence → mass deployment → higher utilization. **ROIC through volume.**

# The decisive question
No longer *who wins AI?* but *which system converts AI capital into cash flow faster?* That is the metric financial markets will eventually price.

::section The Next Market Divide | Inside the AI trade itself
The AI trade will increasingly split companies into two categories.

**AI Compounders:** AI investment → productivity/revenue → free cash flow → reinvestment → higher ROIC.

**AI Capital Traps:** AI investment → capacity → price competition → weak utilization → depreciation → lower ROIC.

This distinction will matter more than whether a company calls itself an AI winner. The next Great Split is inside the AI trade itself.

::section What Markets Should Watch | Five numbers that matter more than another benchmark
Five numbers increasingly matter more than another model benchmark: AI CAPEX / incremental AI revenue, compute utilization, revenue per megawatt, free cash flow after AI CAPEX, and cost per completed task.

Together they answer one question: is AI creating economic value faster than it is consuming capital? Everything else is secondary.

::takeaways Key Takeaways for Investors | The valuation premium moves to capital efficiency
- The indiscriminate AI CAPEX trade is ending — the next phase rewards returns, not announcements.
- U.S. frontier leadership remains the strongest capability trade, but its capital intensity means monetization must now catch up with investment.
- China is the global AI deflation engine, pressuring model pricing while expanding inference volumes.
- Infrastructure remains structurally advantaged, but selection matters — favor assets with scarcity, utilization and pricing power across HBM, networking, optics, power and cooling.
- As models commoditize, economic rents migrate toward companies turning cheap intelligence into productivity.
- Credit becomes the next frontier and the next risk — financing can extend the cycle, but leverage will expose weak utilization quickly.
- The decisive metric becomes ==Free Cash Flow / AI Capital Employed==.

::pull The first phase asked who can get the compute. The second, who can finance it. The phase now beginning asks: who can make it earn?

::close America bets that better intelligence commands higher returns; China bets that cheaper intelligence creates overwhelming volume. Both expand the AI economy — but markets will not reward both equally. ==The next AI rotation will be from capacity to cash flow.==

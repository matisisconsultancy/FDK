Markets enter Tuesday with the AI trade firmly back in command. The Nasdaq closed Monday at a record 27,122.09, +2.26%, the S&P 500 gained 1.49%, the Philadelphia semiconductor index jumped 4.3%, and Meta rose 11.4% as investors responded to strong early demand for its Muse autonomous assistant. AMD rose roughly 10% and crossed $1 trillion in market value for the first time. Asian technology markets followed: MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan was up about 0.75%, while South Korea at one point gained around 2%.

But the rally is occurring inside a much less forgiving macro regime. Brent is still around $101/bbl, the dollar index is near a seven-week high at 100.4, USD/JPY is around 157.47, and markets now price approximately a 56% probability of another Fed hike in October, up from 43.5% a week ago. ECB Chief Economist Philip Lane said the latest energy shock may last longer than previously expected and that inflation may only converge back toward target from around mid-2027.

Overnight, the physical scale of the AI competition moved higher again. Alibaba says it plans models of 5–10 trillion parameters, unveiled a new proprietary AI chip delivering three times the performance of its predecessor, says a cluster could support as many as 500,000 cards, and wants Alibaba Cloud's global data-center capacity above 20 GW by 2032. At almost the same moment, Texas suspended new state permits for data centers while it audits a queue exceeding 470 GW — more than five times the state's peak electricity demand. The contradiction is the story.

::section The Fundamental Question | Two clocks, two speeds
What happens when intelligence scales in weeks, while electricity, grids, data centers, nuclear plants, financing and permits scale in years? The AI economy is developing two radically different clocks.

The digital clock moves at software speed: models improve, agents diffuse, usage rises, tokens multiply. The physical clock moves at industrial speed: power plant, grid connection, data center, cooling, chips, financing, permits.

The emerging equation is therefore **AI demand velocity ÷ physical capacity velocity = scarcity rent**. The larger that ratio becomes, the more value migrates away from generic intelligence and toward the scarce infrastructure required to deliver it.

This is the deeper evolution of *The Great Split*. It is no longer simply AI winners vs. AI losers. It is increasingly **those who can scale intelligence vs. those who can scale the physical system beneath it**. The second group may ultimately control the economics of the first.

::signals The Five Most Important Overnight Signals | Where the story turned
# The Agentic Economy Has Produced Its First Clear Market Demand Signal
Meta's Muse assistant has seen strong early demand following its U.S. launch. Muse can autonomously interact with applications to send emails, sell items, book travel and perform other persistent tasks. Meta shares surged 11.4% Monday. The first generation of generative AI waited for humans to ask questions; agentic AI performs tasks, and that changes consumption. A chatbot interaction may require seconds of compute; a persistent agent can operate for minutes, hours or continuously.

This is potentially the beginning of ==Agentic Elasticity==. Better and cheaper AI does not necessarily reduce total compute spending — it can do the opposite. As the marginal cost of an intelligent task falls, the number of tasks worth automating expands. This is the Jevons Paradox applied to cognition.
signal: Cheaper intelligence means more intelligence consumed — bullish for infrastructure, less straightforward for application margins.

# Alibaba Has Turned China's AI Strategy Into a Full-Stack Industrial Program
Alibaba says upcoming Qwen models could scale to 5–10 trillion parameters, versus 2.4 trillion for its current flagship. It unveiled the Zhenwu V900 AI chip, claiming three times the performance of its predecessor, and says systems could scale to clusters of as many as 500,000 cards, targeting more than 20 GW of global data-center capacity by 2032. CEO Eddie Wu said supply constraints across the AI data-center chain are limiting Alibaba Cloud's ability to grow as quickly as customer demand.

That sentence may matter more than the model announcement. Model capability is no longer the binding constraint; infrastructure availability increasingly is. China is responding by vertically integrating the stack — models, domestic silicon, supernodes, cloud, power, data centers. The U.S. remains far ahead across frontier silicon and hyperscale ecosystems, but the strategic unit of competition is moving from the chip to the complete intelligence factory.
signal: This is not a model race — it is a system race.

# Texas Has Made Permission a Hard Constraint
Governor Greg Abbott barred the state environmental agency from issuing new permits for data centers until regulators finish a wide-ranging audit of their power, tax and water impact. More than 470 GW of data centers and other large users were waiting for connections when the state recently froze new grid hookups — more than five times Texas's peak electricity demand. Texas became an AI magnet because it had land, power, gas, favorable regulation and speed; now the growth itself is creating political constraints.

The economic unit investors should value is not one megawatt of theoretical capacity, but ==one permitted, powered, financed and connected megawatt==. Those are very different assets, and existing infrastructure with secured power, interconnection and permits may command increasing strategic premiums relative to announced capacity.
signal: The causal chain now runs AI demand up, permitting down, capacity scarcity up.

# Europe Is Moving From AI Regulation to AI Infrastructure Regulation
The European Commission proposed an EU-wide disclosure framework for data centers of at least 500 kW, requiring standardized reporting on energy and water efficiency, local water stress and their ability to support energy systems, including through waste-heat reuse. Data centers currently account for around 2.5% of EU electricity consumption, with capacity expected to expand from 12 GW in 2025 to 28 GW by 2030.

Transparency is useful, but Europe must avoid solving the wrong sequencing problem. The continent does not need only better measurement — it needs faster creation of the infrastructure feeding data centers. A European AI strategy requires reporting *plus* permitting, power, grid and capital.
signal: Europe's competitive metric should become time to power — months from investment decision to a fully connected, operating facility.

# Sovereign Capital Is Merging With Power Infrastructure
Seoul has selected a proposed 6.3-GW gas-fired power complex in Texas, reportedly valued above $20 billion, as the first project within a $350 billion U.S. investment package negotiated alongside bilateral trade arrangements. The plant is intended to support AI data centers and semiconductor facilities. South Korea is also discussing nuclear investments, including Korean-designed APR1400 reactors, and a potential stake in Westinghouse.

Countries are no longer negotiating only tariffs; they are negotiating the physical production architecture behind AI — power, nuclear, gas, semiconductors, shipbuilding, capital. The sovereign-capital model is moving from financial allocation toward ==strategic capacity formation==, blurring the lines between infrastructure investing, industrial policy and foreign policy.
signal: Capital is deployed to increase energy security, industrial capacity and geopolitical leverage — not only financial return.

::patterns Five Hidden Patterns Beyond the News | The structure under the headlines
# Demand Curve Steeper Than Forecasts
Persistent autonomous agents may transform consumption from session-based to continuous. If one user eventually operates several agents simultaneously, total inference demand may expand far faster than user counts suggest. The relevant metric becomes compute hours per person — not simply AI users.

# The Race Converges on Vertical Integration
Nvidia, Meta, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Alibaba, Huawei and OpenAI are all moving beyond isolated layers of the stack. As bottlenecks proliferate, relying entirely on external suppliers exposes growth to somebody else's scarcity. The new advantage is control of the bottleneck migration.

# Electricity Is Both an AI Input and an Inflation Input
AI capex raises power demand, which raises energy cost, inflation, interest rates and the AI hurdle rate. ECB Governing Council member Fabio Panetta warned Monday that central banks must understand how AI productivity gains are distributed. The first phase of the AI revolution may generate inflation before later productivity gains become broadly disinflationary.

# Geopolitics Regulates the Speed of AI Capital Formation
The Trump–Xi summit later this week is expected to include AI alongside trade. U.S. officials proposed a notification mechanism for AI incidents reaching national-security significance, though export controls were excluded and Beijing's position remains unclear. AI now sits in the same category as critical minerals, semiconductors, energy and trade access.

# The Great Split Becomes a Speed-of-Construction Split
Software can scale globally overnight; infrastructure cannot. The winners of the next phase may be less differentiated by who announces the largest ambition than by who can turn it into operating capacity fastest. The scoreboard: MW permitted, connected, financed, operating; utilization; revenue per MW; tokens per watt.

::section Implications for Investors | Scarcity owner vs scarcity customer
Yesterday's equity rally confirms that investors still assign enormous value to AI adoption. But the infrastructure evidence says the opportunity set is narrowing toward businesses capable of converting demand into scarce, operating capacity. The most attractive economics increasingly sit where demand elasticity meets supply rigidity — accelerated computing, HBM and memory, advanced packaging, optical networking, power generation, nuclear, grid infrastructure, transformers, storage, cooling and permitted data-center capacity.

The key distinction is no longer AI winner vs. AI loser. It is **scarcity owner vs. scarcity customer**. The first captures the rent; the second pays it. The market will increasingly need to distinguish between AI companies whose growth improves free cash flow and those whose growth merely raises dependence on expensive infrastructure.

::section Implications for Executives | Build the Scale Constraint Map
Every board should now construct a *Scale Constraint Map* — not a generic AI strategy, but a map of the few constraints capable of stopping the strategy. For most enterprises, those constraints will sit across compute availability, electricity access, model access, data rights, cybersecurity, capital, permitting and organizational execution.

The strategic question is: **which input cannot scale as fast as our AI ambition?** Find that input first, because the slowest layer determines the speed of the whole system. That is classic strategy — and it is becoming the defining management problem of the AI Supercycle.

::section Implications for Europe and Policymakers | A capital-formation problem
Europe should pay particular attention to today's contrast. Alibaba targets 20 GW. Texas already has 470 GW of large-load projects seeking connection. The entire EU had roughly 12 GW of data-center capacity last year and expects about 28 GW by 2030. These numbers are not directly comparable — Texas's queue includes other large loads and proposed rather than operating capacity — but the scale difference is instructive.

Europe's AI problem is becoming a capital-formation problem, requiring simultaneous improvement in power supply, cross-border grids, capital markets, permitting, AI factories and physical-AI deployment. The opportunity remains especially strong where Europe already possesses large installed systems: mobility, railways, manufacturing, energy, robotics, healthcare, logistics. Europe should not attempt merely to recreate Silicon Valley — it should build the best environment in the world for ==AI applied to the physical economy==. But physical AI requires physical infrastructure. There is no shortcut around that fact.

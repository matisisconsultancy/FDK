The AI Supercycle has entered a decisive new phase. This morning, ASML confirmed extraordinary semiconductor-equipment demand extending toward 2030, while the IMF warned that rising sovereign borrowing costs threaten financial stability and the International Energy Agency identified an energy shortage that could persist into 2027.

The central contradiction is becoming unavoidable: the world's demand for productive intelligence is accelerating faster than its capacity to finance, power and deploy that intelligence at sustainable economic returns.

Will the AI Supercycle generate enough economic value to overcome the rising cost of capital—or will financial scarcity concentrate the next generation of economic power in the hands of a few corporations and sovereign investors? This is the defining question behind **The Great Split**. The technology is advancing and demand remains extraordinary, but capital markets are beginning to differentiate between technological potential and financial productivity.

::signals Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy | capital, energy and sovereign pressure
# Accelerated Computing — the scarcity premium moves upstream
ASML reported €11.7 billion in quarterly sales and €5 billion in net income, with customers forecasting orders for 2029 and 2030. It is weighing further capacity expansion and expects annual revenue of €44–45 billion; shares gained ~3% in early European trading. The AI cycle is increasingly constrained by the physical capacity to manufacture advanced computing equipment. Scarcity is not uniform: equipment makers gain pricing power while some infrastructure developers face higher construction costs and tougher financing.
signal: Markets may favor indispensable equipment suppliers and cash-generating infrastructure over businesses whose valuations depend on capacity not yet deliverable.
# Energy — the physical constraint becomes financial
The IEA warned diesel shortages could persist into 2027. Middle Eastern and Russian diesel exports fell 1.3 million barrels per day in September; refining margins neared $95 per barrel. Houthi forces threatened further attacks on Saudi infrastructure after damage to Aramco's headquarters, though no production interruption has been established. For AI infrastructure, reliable power is essential to expected utilization and cash flows: energy disruption raises operating costs, sustains inflation, lifts financing costs and lowers returns.
signal: Energy resilience is becoming a determinant of which infrastructure projects get financed — and a component of capital productivity.
# Sovereign Capital — the new competition for global savings
The IMF intensified its debt-sustainability warning in Bangkok. Public debt is nearing 100% of global GDP; average G7 10-year yields rose more than 110 basis points between February and September. The IMF projects average effective government financing costs to rise from 2.3% in 2021 to 3.7% by 2031. Governments financing refinancing, defence, infrastructure and energy security now compete with technology companies for long-duration capital.
signal: Institutions able to commit patient capital may gain negotiating power over infrastructure ownership and industrial partnerships.
# China — industrial acceleration meets weak demand
Producer prices rose 5% year-on-year in September while consumer inflation reached only 0.9% and core 1%. China's industrial economy can expand rapidly while domestic demand remains insufficient to absorb production, and higher energy and commodity prices compress margins. America must reconcile exceptional technology investment with demanding financing; China must translate manufacturing strength into domestic demand.
signal: The Great Split is a divergence between different models of capital formation, production and economic capture.
# Europe — the banking system confronts the next transition
The STOXX 600 advanced ~0.5%, supported by ASML, yet Europe's 20 largest banks have fallen ~7% on average since early August. BlackRock reported $15.49 trillion in assets under management. The strategic question is which financial structures can channel European savings toward industrial AI, energy resilience and productive infrastructure.
signal: Europe needs stronger financial institutions not merely to consolidate banking, but to finance its next industrial economy.

::patterns The Hidden Pattern | a competition between two capital systems
# Capital-productive economy
Combines technological leadership, secure energy, patient financing and rapid commercialization. Its cycle reinforces itself: capital → computing capacity → productive deployment → cash flow → reinvestment.
# Capital-dependent economy
Expands investment commitments faster than revenues. Its vulnerability runs in reverse: higher financing costs → delayed deployment → weaker cash conversion → greater refinancing needs. The critical difference is not technological ambition but the relationship between time to revenue, return on capital and the cost of financing.

Today's evidence suggests three priorities: favor demonstrated cash generation over speculative capacity; price energy security and financing maturity into infrastructure valuations; and distinguish technological leadership from sustainable profitability. The most important rotation may not be from technology into defensives, but within the AI economy itself—from businesses requiring continuous capital injections toward those converting technology into recurring, capital-efficient earnings.

The first phase of AI rewarded the accumulation of computing power. The second rewarded the financing of infrastructure. The next will reward ==the conversion of intelligence into sustainable financial returns== — and that is where The Great Split will become most consequential.

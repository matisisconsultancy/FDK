No secured power. No committed demand. No financing edge. No pricing power.

The evidence is ordered by consequence. First, the U.S. 10-year Treasury is around **5.20%** after touching 5.2251%, while the 30-year is near **5.48%** after reaching 5.5016% — the highest levels in roughly two decades. Second, Brent remains near **$105.6**, keeping the energy-inflation channel open. Third, SoftBank has raised **$11.1 billion** to finance OpenAI at dollar coupons reaching **9.75%**. Yet AI investment continues.

The consequence is clear: AI growth now has to compete for capital on materially tougher terms.

::stats
5.20% :: U.S. 10-year Treasury (peak 5.2251%)
5.48% :: U.S. 30-year yield (peak 5.5016%)
$105.6 :: Brent crude per barrel
$11.1B :: SoftBank bonds to fund OpenAI

::pull THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
Can AI cash flows compound faster than the hurdle rate rising against them? When medium-term U.S. sovereign debt yields roughly 5% before investors assume corporate or execution risk, every AI project, infrastructure asset and equity story must make a stronger case for capital.

::signals Five Signals | Ordered by consequence
# Rates — From duration to discipline
U.S. long yields are at multi-decade highs. Long-duration growth is being repriced against a 5%+ sovereign benchmark. Favor current earnings, pricing power and low refinancing dependence.
signal: ==Earnings visibility > distant optionality==

# Energy — From input cost to valuation pressure
Brent is trading near $105.6/bbl, despite tentative hopes for U.S.–Iran progress. Energy remains high enough to sustain inflation and policy pressure. Prefer energy-resilient assets; avoid low-margin businesses unable to pass through higher input costs.
signal: ==Energy → inflation → rates==

# AI — From capacity to monetization
SoftBank has issued $11.1 billion of bonds to fund OpenAI, with dollar tranches priced at 8.625%, 9.25% and 9.75%. AI demand remains strong, but financing is becoming more selective. Favor distribution, subscriptions, contracted demand and visible utilization over uncommitted capacity.
signal: ==Monetization > megawatts==

# U.S.–China — From decoupling to leverage
Washington and Beijing continue negotiations over AI, trade and critical inputs, while the broader relationship remains strategically interdependent rather than fully decoupled. Dependencies are increasingly being used as bargaining instruments. Favor supply-chain redundancy and assets positioned at critical technological and material chokepoints.
signal: ==Interdependence becomes leverage==

# Europe — From broad exposure to selective ownership
French 10-year borrowing costs now trade more than 110 basis points above Germany, the widest spread since the euro-area crisis, even as Europe continues investing in strategic infrastructure. Europe is becoming more attractive by sector and less attractive as a single macro trade. Favor grids, electrification, automation, semiconductor equipment, secure infrastructure, rail and infrastructure finance.
signal: ==Own the enablers, not the fragility==

::section The Velocity Insight | Where value is decided
The first AI race was about models. The second was about capacity. The third is about economics.

Who owns the customer? Who has secured demand? Who has reliable power? Who can finance the build-out? Who keeps the margin? That is where the next phase of value creation will be decided.

::pull THE HIDDEN PATTERN
Higher rates are not stopping AI investment; they are separating bankable growth from capital-intensive ambition.

::patterns Investor Action Hierarchy | Own what AI cannot avoid — buy optionality last
# [1] Core Overweight: Mandatory Bottlenecks
Semiconductors, HBM, memory, advanced networking, grids, firm power, resilient data centres. Structural demand, difficult substitution, strongest pricing power.

# [2] Selective Overweight: Monetization Engines
Agentic distribution, AI security, enterprise deployment, infrastructure finance. Closest to customers, transactions and recurring revenue.

# [3] Selective Only: Funded Optionality
Frontier models, new compute platforms, emerging AI infrastructure. Require secured customers, strong balance sheets, visible utilization and credible unit economics.

# [4] Underweight: Dependent Capacity
Uncontracted compute, leveraged infrastructure, weak intermediaries and businesses reliant on external power or financing.

::takeaways Investors' Takeaways | Positioning for the third race
- Own mandatory bottlenecks before optionality.
- Prefer cash conversion over capacity announcements.
- Treat power and resilience as earnings variables.
- Demand contracted utilization from AI infrastructure.
- In Europe, overweight industrial enablers; underweight sovereign and financing fragility.

::close ==Own what AI cannot avoid. Then own what monetizes it. Buy optionality last.==

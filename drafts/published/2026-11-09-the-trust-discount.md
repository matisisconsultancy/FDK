November 9 marks a consequential transition in the AI Supercycle: the world's most strategically important technology is becoming a national-security asset before the financial system has resolved how to value, finance and govern it.

Nvidia fell roughly 6% — putting nearly $300 billion of market capitalization at risk — after its board launched an independent investigation into whether CEO Jensen Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. Yet the broader market rebounded, distinguishing the economics of an industry from the risks of one of its most important companies.

::section Nvidia — The $300 Billion Governance Shock
Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation into reports that Huang may have been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, following scrutiny of his relationship with a former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore company tied to possible diversion of restricted American chips to China. Huang acknowledged an extramarital relationship and apologized.

Nvidia says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised; China rejected the allegations as unfounded. With a market capitalization near $4.5 trillion and leadership unusually concentrated around its founder, the market is beginning to price the dependence of a strategic technology company on a single individual. The distinction that matters is between temporary reputational damage and a durable change in Nvidia's competitive economics.

::section Alphabet — The Challenge Moves Into Secure Computing
Google unveiled two new Tensor Processing Units. The 8tx targets government and regulated industries needing confidential computing, pairing Google's architecture with AMD processors and hardware-level encryption at up to twice the prior speed; the 8i Max targets memory-intensive reasoning and research. Google Cloud revenue more than doubled to $30.71 billion in the September quarter.

As Nvidia confronts governance uncertainty, a major rival is expanding into precisely the secure capabilities governments value. For investors, the decisive measure becomes the total cost of commercially useful computation — not raw processor performance.

::section Wall Street — A New Valuation Divide
Ten of eleven S&P 500 sectors rose; Broadcom and Marvell advanced ~8% and ~9%. Morgan Stanley and UBS called last week's selloff excessive, while Citigroup cut U.S. equities from overweight to neutral. The next rotation may not move away from AI but among competing financial models within it — those selling computing, financing it, commercializing intelligence and deploying it for productivity.

::section Europe — Banking and Industrial Capital Return
The STOXX Europe 600 closed at 656.52. Crédit Agricole proposed combining its Italian operations with Banco BPM — a 20% premium, a €1 billion extraordinary dividend and ~€800 million in annual synergies — lifting Banco BPM 10.7%. UniCredit reported Q3 net profit up 24% to €3.3 billion, a 25.5% return on tangible equity and a ~15% CET1 ratio; shares rose 6.3%. European banking consolidation is becoming a contest over who controls the institutions financing the continent's next industrial cycle.

::section Energy and China — The Margin Contradiction
Brent fell to $93.75 after President Trump signaled further strikes on Iran were unlikely while negotiations progress. China's October PPI rose 5.7% — the strongest since June 2022 — while CPI slowed to 0.7%, as weak demand prevented companies passing higher input costs through. America must finance extraordinary computing investment while preserving returns; China must sustain industrial expansion without matching pricing power.

::section Where Global Capital Is Moving
In the first nine months of 2026, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle generated $542.9 billion in operating cash flow against $556.9 billion in capex — aggregate free cash flow of −$14.0 billion, versus +$143.5 billion a year earlier, with buybacks down 83%. The objective must shift from maximizing capacity to maximizing its economic productivity, governed by Economic Profit = (ROIC − WACC) × Invested Capital.

KKR reported record adjusted net income near $1.6 billion and assets above $800 billion, confirming capital remains abundant; the question is where it is deployed. Enel and Leonardo signed a cooperation agreement on energy and satellite services protecting critical infrastructure. Industrial AI offers the largest long-term prize: for a company with €20 billion of invested capital, a one-point gain in after-tax ROIC adds roughly €200 million in annual profit. BNSF's 19% quarterly profit rise shows the upside of better operating performance; Alstom's troubles show the cost of execution failure.

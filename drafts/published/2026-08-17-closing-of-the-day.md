What if the most important signal from today is not where markets closed—but where capital has begun to move?

The answer emerging from August 17 is increasingly clear: the AI Supercycle is entering its **capital-intensive phase**. For three years, markets largely rewarded the companies producing intelligence: semiconductors, accelerated computing, memory, models and cloud infrastructure. The next phase will increasingly reward the companies capable of deploying intelligence into energy systems, factories, construction sites, transportation networks and machines.

That shift changes the economics of AI—and potentially the architecture of modern capitalism.

::section The Day Beneath the Market | Markets are beginning to price two economies at once

At first sight, today's market action was hardly revolutionary. U.S. equities remained close to record territory but struggled for direction. The deeper pressure came from bonds: long-duration Treasury yields moved higher as oil and geopolitical risk revived inflation concerns, with the 30-year U.S. yield reaching its highest closing level in roughly nineteen years.

Europe was similarly uneasy. The early optimism faded and several major markets finished lower, with Spain's IBEX among the weaker performers. Gold moved above $4,400/oz, while Bitcoin traded around the $63,000–64,000 range.

Yet underneath those asset-price movements something considerably more important happened. Capital formation around AI moved another step from software toward infrastructure—and from infrastructure toward the physical economy.

The extraordinary example came from NVIDIA. It agreed to provide as much as **$105 billion** of guarantees supporting OpenAI's leasing of the enormous Ohio data-center project being developed by SoftBank-owned SB Energy. NVIDIA will separately invest $1.5 billion in SB Energy. The project could ultimately reach 8 GW of data-center capacity, while the surrounding energy build-out could involve another 10 GW of power generation.

This is no longer simply semiconductor demand. It is ==industrial capital formation organized around intelligence==.

::pull THE CHAIN
AI demand → accelerated computing → AI factories → electricity → grids → financing → physical infrastructure → industrial intelligence → productivity → new cash flows.

::section The Capital Rotation | From financing chips to financing intelligence infrastructure

The crucial development is not merely that AI investment remains enormous. It is that its financial architecture is changing.

NVIDIA has already announced partnerships involving Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR intended to mobilize more than **$500 billion** of third-party capital for AI infrastructure. AI is beginning to require financing structures historically associated with railways, telecommunications networks, electricity systems and large infrastructure projects. Equity alone cannot finance the next stage.

The AI Supercycle is therefore migrating toward corporate equity + private equity + private credit + infrastructure funds + project finance + sovereign capital + utilities + long-duration contracts. The AI trade is becoming a *capital-formation trade*.

Today provided another signal. SoftBank invested **$200 million** in Swiss company Gravis Robotics, whose autonomous technology transforms conventional heavy construction machinery into intelligent robotic systems. The amount is modest compared with hyperscale data centers. The signal is not.

Construction is one of the world's largest, most fragmented and least-digitized industries. Once intelligence can operate excavators, loaders, cranes and other heavy machinery autonomously, AI stops being primarily an information technology. It becomes ==productive capital== — the transition from AI that answers → AI that decides → AI that acts.

::section Tomorrow's Market | Watch the rotation, not the index

The central mistake investors can make now is to interpret AI through the performance of the Nasdaq alone. The Nasdaq measures only part of what is happening. The more consequential question is: where does the next dollar of AI-driven capital expenditure go?

Increasingly, the answer extends beyond semiconductors. It moves toward memory, power, grid equipment, cooling, optical networking, industrial automation, robotics, construction, logistics, transportation and autonomous systems. The physical economy is becoming the new frontier of the AI Supercycle.

Independent investment research increasingly points in the same direction: estimates suggest the global robotics opportunity could expand toward **$2.5 trillion** in annual sales by 2035, compared with roughly $100 billion in 2025. Industrial robot installations already exceeded 540,000 units annually in the latest reported global figures—more than twice the level of a decade earlier. But robotics should not be considered a standalone industry. It is better understood as the execution layer of Industrial AI.

::section The Velocity Dashboard | August 18 — five structural movements to watch

Tomorrow, watch less for whether the Nasdaq rises 50 basis points and more for confirmation of five structural movements. **Bond yields:** whether long-duration yields continue challenging the valuation of capital-intensive growth. **Oil:** whether geopolitical risk reinforces the higher-for-longer inflation regime. **AI infrastructure financing:** further evidence that institutional capital is moving into the AI build-out. **Memory and semiconductor breadth:** whether capital continues rotating beyond the narrowest AI leaders. **Physical AI:** robotics, industrial automation, power equipment and autonomous systems for evidence that the market is beginning to recognize the next layer of value creation.

The global financial system is approaching a decisive inflection point. The AI Supercycle continues to accelerate technologically, but the financial architecture required to sustain it is undergoing its most consequential test yet.

The defining development of Monday, November 16, is not simply the renewed technology selloff. It is the recognition that the next phase of artificial intelligence will be determined as much by the allocation, price and security of capital as by advances in computing power.

Technological capability, financial capacity and sovereign resilience are becoming three separate — and increasingly unequal — sources of economic power. This is the next phase of **The Great Split**.

::signal
- **Nvidia's succession** has become a repricing of the entire AI financing system, with analysts expecting greater scrutiny after Jensen Huang's departure.
- **Capital turned restrictive** precisely as the AI cycle demands extraordinary financing: the U.S. 10-year trades near 5.12%, above 5%.
- **Nvidia's market cap** fell from roughly $5.8T in early October to $3.9T by Friday, November 13, even as demand stays strong.
- **The $500B financing platform** remains engaged, but lenders may favour resilient hyperscalers over smaller, leveraged computing providers.
- **Brent eased toward $95** on an expected supply surplus, yet unresolved Strait of Hormuz risks keep physical energy security vulnerable.
- **The central question** is who captures AI's returns — and who must finance the infrastructure required to create them.

::section The Fundamental Question | Technology versus capital
Will the AI Supercycle generate the greatest productivity dividend in modern history — or will the cost of financing intelligence concentrate its returns in an increasingly small number of companies and sovereign powers?

The development of intelligence is a technological process; its deployment is industrial; its financing is a capital-allocation process; and the capture of its benefits is competitive. These processes need not advance at the same velocity. The economic value of AI can be extraordinary while its financial returns remain unevenly distributed.

::signals Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy | Monday, November 16
# Nvidia's succession reprices the AI financing system
The $500 billion platform — backed by Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR — has not been suspended, Reuters reports. But under interim CEO Colette Kress, JPMorgan expects financing to take longer or require more compensation for risk, and Morgan Stanley sees funding migrating toward established hyperscalers. Bank of America estimates ~$70B of equity investments and ~$230B of guarantees as of August — concepts distinct from the $500B platform.
signal: Founder-driven expansion may give way to institutional discipline — and greater infrastructure concentration.

# Nvidia's earnings and Anthropic's IPO test two models
Nvidia reports after the close on November 17, guiding to ~$108B revenue (±2%) with ~90% expected earnings growth; options imply a ~7% move. Anthropic's expected debut provides a second test. One monetizes computing demand; the other, frontier models and applications.
signal: The next rotation will distinguish revenue growth from capital productivity.

# Restrictive rates change the ownership economics of the Next Economy
Treasury yields hold above 5%; Fed futures price a likely December hike. German Bund yields near 3.38%, with ECB forward markets implying a 3.23% deposit rate by late 2027 versus 2.50% today. Higher discount rates reduce the present value of long-duration infrastructure.
signal: Financial strength enables productive investment — which generates further financial strength.

# Europe's next crisis may be a competition for sovereign capital
Italian 10-years yield ~4.43%; the French OAT–Bund spread stands at 154.5 bps, below October's 177.7 peak but elevated. Germany is preparing a major expansion of infrastructure and defence investment that must compete with refinancing and social spending.
signal: Europe's divergence depends on converting capital into productive industrial transformation.

# The new oil surplus collides with persistent geopolitical risk
Brent traded near $95.18 at 09:00 GMT; the IEA expects a Q4 surplus of ~1.7 million bpd. Yet Hormuz maritime-security risks remain unresolved, and China's October refinery throughput fell 8.1% year-on-year. Computing depends on reliable electricity, not merely lower crude.
signal: The next energy premium favours reliable, economically delivered power.

::patterns Three Scenarios for the Next Six Months | Indicative FDK weights
# [25%] The Productive Reacceleration
Strong Nvidia results, successful AI commercialization and improving infrastructure returns broaden market participation.

# [50%] The Great Capital Selection
Financing becomes more selective, favouring resilient hyperscalers, profitable infrastructure suppliers and companies with strong cash conversion.

# [25%] The Sovereign–Technology Squeeze
Restrictive rates, AI financing stress and energy disruptions reinforce one another, delaying investment and widening risk premiums.

::takeaways Five Takeaways for Investors and Boards | What to act on
- Treat the cost of capital as a competitive variable; strong balance sheets sustain investment when leveraged rivals cannot.
- Separate technological leadership from financing leadership, as Nvidia's transition illustrates.
- Anticipate infrastructure-ownership concentration toward established hyperscalers and resilient operators.
- Integrate sovereign and industrial analysis; Europe depends on financing, reliable energy and productivity.
- Prepare for value migration within the AI Supercycle — toward infrastructure, power systems and enterprise applications.

The critical market threshold is not another correction in AI equities. It is sustained deterioration in the terms on which the productive infrastructure of intelligence can be financed.

::close
The next great redistribution of global wealth will not be decided by who spends the most to build intelligence — it will be decided by who can finance its productive deployment, capture its economic returns and ==compound those returns without letting the cost of capital consume the future it promises to create==.

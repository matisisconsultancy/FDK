The AI Supercycle is producing a monetary paradox. Industrial AI may ultimately be deflationary. But building the Industrial AI economy is proving inflationary first.

The latest signal comes from Japan. The Bank of Japan has raised rates to 1.25%, entering its estimated neutral-rate range just as AI investment, energy scarcity and fiscal spending are increasing global demand for capital.

Japan now joins the Fed, ECB and Bank of England in confronting the same fundamental question: *has the AI Supercycle raised the neutral rate during the build-out phase?*

::stats
1.25% :: Bank of Japan policy rate, now within its neutral range
4 :: major central banks — BoJ, Fed, ECB, BoE — facing the same question
2 :: opposing forces — capital formation vs. productivity diffusion

::section The Emerging Chain | how capital formation lifts R*
The mechanism is becoming legible: AI Expectations → Capital Formation → Compute + Energy + Infrastructure Demand → Competition for Savings → Higher Long-Term Yields / R* → Higher Hurdle Rates.

Only later comes the countervailing force: AI Diffusion → Productivity → Lower Unit Costs → Industrial AI Deflation.

Central banks are still operating predominantly in the first half of this cycle. That explains an apparently contradictory world: ==productivity can accelerate while interest rates remain high—or even rise==.

::signals The Policy Response | how central banks are reading it
# The ECB's Counterweight
The ECB is introducing an important qualification. Christine Lagarde is resisting a mechanical response to energy inflation: a supply shock should not automatically trigger tighter policy if higher prices simultaneously weaken consumption and growth.

That distinction will matter enormously for AI. A temporary shortage of GPUs, electricity or data-centre capacity is different from AI investment triggering persistent wage, credit and demand inflation.
signal: Distinguish a temporary supply shock from persistent demand-driven inflation.

::patterns The Great Split Moves Into the Cost of Capital | winners vs. vulnerable assets
# AI Cash-Flow Generators
The winners will convert compute and infrastructure into cash flow quickly enough to outrun a higher WACC.

# AI Capital Consumers
The vulnerable assets will depend on repeated refinancing, expensive power and long-duration assumptions.

::section The European Risk | a widening competitiveness gap
For Europe, the danger is even greater: less AI investment + slower productivity diffusion + imported higher global yields = a widening competitiveness gap.

Europe therefore needs to connect the full chain: Savings → Capital Markets → Energy + Grids → AI Infrastructure → Industrial AI → Productivity → Cash Flow → Reinvestment.

::pull The decisive variable is time.

The monetary equation of the AI Supercycle is becoming: AI Capital Formation → Higher R*; AI Productivity Diffusion → Lower Unit Costs.

If productivity arrives quickly enough, Industrial AI ultimately becomes disinflationary. If capital requirements outrun productivity for too long, the AI Supercycle itself can keep the equilibrium cost of money structurally higher.

::takeaways Key takeaways | what to watch
- The BoJ's move to 1.25% places a fourth major central bank inside the neutral-rate debate.
- Central banks remain in the first half of the cycle — capital formation, not productivity diffusion.
- Investors face a widening split between AI cash-flow generators and AI capital consumers.
- Europe risks a competitiveness gap unless it connects savings to productivity through AI infrastructure.

::close AI may eventually make intelligence cheaper. But first, ==building intelligence is making capital more expensive==.

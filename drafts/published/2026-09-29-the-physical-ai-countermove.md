Material inflection: yes.

AMD has agreed to acquire Fei-Fei Li's World Labs for **$8.2 billion**, bringing one of the leading teams in spatial intelligence and world models directly inside a major U.S. compute platform. Li will become AMD's EVP and chief scientist.

The strategic message is bigger than the transaction: America is extending the frontier from language models into the physical world.

::stats
$8.2B :: AMD's acquisition of World Labs
354,000 :: industrial robots installed by China in 2025 (59% of world total)
~38,500 :: industrial robots installed by the U.S. in 2025
EVP :: Fei-Fei Li becomes AMD's chief scientist

::pull The competition is moving beyond "who has the best model?" toward ==who controls the full loop from intelligence to action?==

::section The Great Split | frontier intelligence vs deployment velocity

World models allow AI to understand, simulate and eventually act inside three-dimensional environments—the foundation for robotics, autonomous systems, industrial simulation and Physical AI. That directly sharpens the U.S.–China split.

The **United States** pairs frontier models, advanced compute and world models to reach higher-value Physical AI. **China** pairs efficient models, manufacturing scale, robots and factories for faster Physical-AI diffusion.

China still holds the deployment advantage: it installed 354,000 industrial robots in 2025, 59% of the world total, versus roughly 38,500 in the U.S. But AMD's move shows that America is not leaving Physical AI to China's industrial scale.

::patterns Two ends of the same problem | how each power builds the loop

# U.S. Frontier Intelligence
AMD is collapsing chips → models → simulation → robotics workloads into a tighter stack. Nvidia is already pursuing the same direction.

# Chinese Deployment Velocity
China approaches from the opposite end: factories → robots → deployment → data → increasingly capable domestic AI.

::section The AI Supercycle Implication | from data centers into the physical economy

Physical AI multiplies infrastructure intensity. World models → simulation → robot training → inference at the edge → more accelerators, sensors, networking and power.

The Supercycle therefore expands from data centers into the physical economy.

::takeaways Key takeaways | where the race is headed

- **U.S. frontier leadership is broadening.** The frontier is moving from LLMs toward spatial intelligence and autonomous systems.
- **China still owns the larger deployment substrate.** Its robot and manufacturing base remains a major strategic advantage.
- **Physical AI becomes the next capital-formation race.** Compute, simulation, robotics and industrial infrastructure begin to converge.
- **The decisive advantage will be integration.** Not the best chip, model or robot individually—but the fastest intelligence-to-action system.

::pull America is now pushing the frontier from intelligence into action. China is pushing intelligence into the installed industrial base.

The next phase of the AI race will therefore be fought less on chatbots—and increasingly on machines, factories and autonomous systems.

::close ==The frontier is becoming physical. And that makes the AI Supercycle bigger.==

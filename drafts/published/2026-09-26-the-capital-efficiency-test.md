The AI race has moved from capability to capital efficiency. From here, control will determine returns. Microsoft is embedding permissions and usage controls into enterprise agents. OpenAI is confronting the cost of autonomous failures. Palo Alto Networks is monetizing the security consequences. Nscale shows why contracted AI capacity is not the same as productive capacity.

The financial message is decisive: the next AI winners will convert intelligence into cash flow faster than capital intensity, delay and liability rise. With the U.S. 10-year Treasury at **5.158%**, capital discipline has become the dividing line between AI growth and AI value creation.

::pull THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
Who can turn AI capital into durable free cash flow and ROIC above the cost of capital — without allowing execution risk, infrastructure intensity or liability to absorb the return? Three tests now define Corporate Velocity: ==control the risk, convert the cash, compound the returns==. Fail the first and autonomy becomes liability. Fail the second and backlog remains promise. Fail the third and capex fails to create economic value.

::section The Investor Hierarchy | control, convert, compound
With the U.S. 10-year Treasury at 5.158%, only AI investments that convert rapidly into cash flow and sustain ROIC above WACC deserve a premium.

**1. Control the Risk.** AI autonomy creates value only when permissions, observability, infrastructure resilience and execution quality contain downside risk. Favor companies where governance and execution reduce the probability of capital impairment. Watch: Microsoft · Palantir · Palo Alto Networks. Protect capital before scaling capability.

**2. Convert the Cash.** Demand, backlog and contracted capacity matter only when they become productive utilization, revenue and free cash flow. Favor companies with high utilization, visible deployment and accelerating cash conversion. Watch: TSMC · Schneider Electric · Amazon. Pay for operating capacity — not announced capacity.

**3. Compound the Returns.** Cash generation creates value only when incremental ROIC sustainably exceeds WACC and can be reinvested at attractive rates. Favor companies with durable excess returns and credible reinvestment opportunities. Monitor carefully companies where capex grows faster than cash flow. Excess returns prove the model.

::takeaways Five Investor Takeaways | favor discipline
- **ROIC, not capex:** Large AI spending is not evidence of value creation.
- **Cash, not contract volume:** Risk-adjust backlog for financing, power, utilization and time-to-revenue.
- **Control has economic value:** Governance lowers expected losses and protects margins.
- **Scarcity deserves a premium:** TSMC and Schneider Electric monetize hard bottlenecks rather than merely fund them.
- **5.158% changes the math:** At today's yield, idle capacity and delayed projects deserve materially higher hurdle rates.

::takeaways Board of Directors Takeaways | capital-allocation strategy
- **Replace AI capex targets with return targets:** Track AI ROIC, utilization, payback and free-cash-flow conversion.
- **Put time-to-cash on the board dashboard:** Every month between investment and monetization carries an explicit capital cost.
- **Risk-adjust backlog:** Separate announced demand from financed, powered, utilized and cash-generating capacity.
- **Treat AI governance as financial control:** Permissions, observability and containment protect margins and reduce contingent liabilities.
- **Set a hard capital rule:** No major AI investment should proceed without a credible path to returns above WACC.

::section Methodology | how the index reads Velocity
The FDK Global Velocity Index — Corporate Benchmark measures how rapidly globally significant corporations convert technological, financial and organizational capabilities into future economic value. The GVI Corporate Score combines Innovation Velocity 14% · Capital Formation 12% · Productivity Acceleration 10% · AI Adoption & Deployment 10% · Infrastructure Position 10% · Ecosystem Strength 10% · Resilience 9% · Talent Intensity 8% · Strategic Optionality 9% · Execution Quality 8%.

The methodology has two layers: ~70% measured evidence — revenue, margins, free cash flow, capex, backlog, contracted demand, installed capacity, utilization, adoption and execution milestones; and ~30% FDK proprietary data analytics — cross-company analysis of capital productivity, cash-conversion velocity, ecosystem control, infrastructure scarcity, resilience, execution quality, strategic optionality, concentration risk and the probability that announced or contracted capacity becomes productive, cash-generating assets.

The framework explicitly distinguishes scale from Velocity. Large capex is not enough. Large backlog is not enough. Technological leadership is not enough. The decisive test is whether capability becomes Productivity → Free Cash Flow → ROIC → Strategic Optionality. Daily GVI change measures structural Corporate Velocity, not stock-price performance.

The evidence today is unusually revealing. The OECD now identifies AI infrastructure investment as one of the principal reasons the world economy has absorbed the Middle East energy shock better than expected, raising its 2026 global-growth forecast to **2.9%**. Yet simultaneously the US 10-year Treasury yield has reached **5.11%**, Japan's 10-year sovereign yield **3.06%** — its highest since 1996 — and Brent remains around **$102**.

The central equation is therefore tightening: AI is accelerating the real economy fast enough to withstand the shock — but that very resilience is keeping inflation and the cost of capital higher. That is today's dominant Velocity signal.

Structural acceleration remains intact over the 30-day regime, but the cost of financing that acceleration has moved materially higher. Model confidence sits at 96%, with net model impulse approximately flat. Today produces one country-level revision: ==South Korea rises +0.1 to 88.5==.

::section The Velocity Paradox | AI on both sides of the equation
Today's evidence exposes the most consequential paradox yet in the AI Supercycle. AI investment is increasingly powerful enough to raise economic growth itself: Korean semiconductor exports explode, American business activity reaches a five-year high, German activity accelerates, and the OECD revises world growth upward.

But precisely because the real economy is stronger than expected, inflation remains harder to suppress. That keeps bond yields elevated, which raises the hurdle rate on the very AI infrastructure driving the growth. The circular chain is becoming: AI CAPEX → PRODUCTIVITY + DEMAND → GROWTH → CAPACITY PRESSURE → INFLATION → HIGHER RATES → HIGHER AI HURDLE RATE.

::signals Country signals | measured evidence, not forecasts
# South Korea — AI crosses from cycle to macro Velocity
Official Korean customs data show exports in the first 20 days of September up **78.3% year on year to $71.4 billion**, the highest 20-day performance on record, with semiconductor shipments up **259.4%**. The OECD raised South Korea's 2026 growth forecast from 2.6% to 3.7%, and the ADB from 2.6% to 3.2%, both citing strong exports, production and AI-semiconductor demand. The forecasts earn no GVI points; the measured export and production impulse does. The causality chain is now visible: global AI capex → HBM/memory demand → Korean exports → industrial production → earnings → investment → GDP acceleration. The constraint remains inflation, with Korea's 2026 projection raised to 3.0%.
signal: South Korea rises from 88.4 to 88.5 — the transition from an AI trade to an AI economy.

# United States — accelerating into a higher hurdle rate
The Composite PMI surged from 56.0 to **58.4** in September, its highest since July 2021, with new orders at their strongest since March 2022 and backlogs the highest since May 2022. But the input-price index jumped to 66.4, its highest since October 2022. The 10-year yield jumped nearly 14bp to 5.106%, the two-year reached 4.947% intraday, and markets moved the implied probability of another October Fed increase toward 70%. The positive productive evidence and negative Smart Capital effect offset.
signal: United States remains 94.3 — economic Velocity up, cost of capital up.

# Germany — the strongest European operating signal in months
The Composite PMI rose from 51.8 to **53.8**, the highest since October 2025; services returned to expansion at 52.9, manufacturing held at 53.8 and employment rose for a second month. But input costs rose at the fastest rate in four months, driven by fuel and energy, and the GVI requires persistence before reversing accumulated structural constraints.
signal: Germany stays 83.2 but moves closer to an upgrade threshold.

# Japan — capital available, but increasingly expensive
SoftBank completed an **$11.1 billion** dollar/euro bond issuance to finance its OpenAI investment — the largest high-yield bond sale by an Asia-Pacific issuer, with dollar coupons of 8.625%, 9.25% and 9.75%, and a five-year CDS spread above 400bp versus around 280 in June. The 10-year JGB rose to 3.06% and the 30-year to 4.12%. Global markets can still mobilise enormous capital for frontier AI — but the required return is becoming extremely high.
signal: Japan remains 84.9; Smart Capital momentum deteriorates.

# United Kingdom — digital finance advances as the macro mix worsens
Lloyds, NatWest and Barclays completed the world's first interbank transactions using tokenised commercial-bank deposits, including mortgages; a further HSBC test demonstrated programmable payments, with plans to issue digital bonds settled through tokenised deposits in early 2027. Meaningful Innovation Velocity evidence — but it earns no +0.1 while transactions remain pilots. The Composite PMI slipped from 52.5 to 51.7.
signal: UK remains 82.3 — the two signals offset.

# France — recovery, but not yet escape velocity
The Composite PMI rose sharply from 48.5 to 51.2, the strongest pace in 25 months and back into expansion. But employment declined for a fifth month, new orders barely increased, business expectations weakened and prices accelerated — preventing any reversal of Saturday's Smart Capital downgrade.
signal: France remains 81.4 — activity recovery confirmed, self-sustaining acceleration not yet.

# China — localisation strengthens, evidence provisional
Chinese authorities are reportedly surveying use of Broadcom networking switches across state data centres, with the FT reporting they may account for as much as 90% of deployed switches; Reuters could not verify. The direction fits China's full-stack architecture, but this is precisely the evidence the GVI should not yet score.
signal: China remains 91.1 — zero numerical weight until procurement changes become measurable.

::section Global macro validation | AI is now visible in GDP
The OECD's new Interim Economic Outlook raised 2026 global growth from 2.8% to 2.9%, raised US growth to 2.2%, and explicitly identified investment in data centres and semiconductors as a major source of resilience — including through Japanese and Korean technology exports.

But it warned that AI investment is becoming increasingly dependent on external financing, creating vulnerability if expected returns fail to materialise. It sees 2027 world growth at only 3.0% and G20 inflation at 3.6%, materially above its previous projection. AI is no longer merely a technology-sector phenomenon — it is becoming a macroeconomic variable.

::section Implications | the next Great Split | 
For investors, the next Great Split should increasingly separate AI capacity that produces cash flow from AI capacity that merely consumes capital. Korea's export data show what productive conversion looks like; SoftBank's financing costs show the price of getting it wrong.

For executives, the decisive KPI is no longer AI adoption or even AI revenue. It is incremental operating cash flow generated per dollar of fully loaded AI capital employed.

For policymakers, the OECD evidence confirms AI is now large enough to influence national growth trajectories. The challenge is combining technology, energy, skills, capital markets and macroeconomic stability without allowing the resulting investment boom to destabilise inflation or public finances.

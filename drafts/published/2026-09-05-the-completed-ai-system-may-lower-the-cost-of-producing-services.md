During the transition, AI can reduce the labor required to satisfy each additional unit of demand. It is becoming deflationary for headcount while remaining inflationary for capital, electricity and scarce industrial inputs.

This is not a traditional recession signal. Demand is still expanding. It is a signal that the relationship between economic activity and labor absorption is changing.

The Great Split is moving from financial markets into factor incomes: capital receives the productivity upside immediately; labor must renegotiate its role inside the new productive system.

::section AI Demand Is Accelerating. Valuation Support Is Not. | The market withdraws unconditional capital
Industrial momentum remains powerful, but the market is withdrawing unconditional capital from companies whose growth no longer compensates for duration, capital intensity and execution risk.

South Korea's KOSPI fell 3.64%, Japan's Nikkei declined 1.57%, SK Hynix lost 6.95%, Kioxia dropped 9.61% and Tokyo Electron fell 4.61%. Yet the broader earnings picture remains exceptionally strong. Capital expenditure by Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Oracle is expected to approach $800 billion in 2026. S&P 500 second-quarter earnings are on course to increase 31.1%, while technology-sector earnings are expected to rise 72%.

::signals The FDK Interpretation | Where industrial reality and financial pricing diverge
# The selloff does not invalidate the cycle
The semiconductor selloff does not invalidate the AI infrastructure cycle. It reveals the difference between industrial acceleration, financial expectations, and the price already paid for future leadership.

The market can believe simultaneously that accelerated-computing demand will remain extraordinary and that individual securities have discounted too much of that future too early.
signal: A company can be strategically right and financially overowned.

# Broadening economically, narrowing financially
The AI trade is broadening economically while narrowing financially. More companies are benefiting from data-centre construction, semiconductors, memory, power, networking and enterprise deployment. But fewer companies are receiving unconditional permission to spend, finance and scale without demonstrating incremental margins and cash-flow conversion.
signal: Fewer companies now hold unconditional permission to spend.

# The next winners
The next winners will not merely report AI exposure. They will prove that AI revenue is increasing faster than the capital required to generate it.
signal: Prove revenue outpaces the capital that generates it.

::section Europe Is Discovering Its Real AI Advantage | Intelligence made operational inside the real economy
Siemens reported its highest-ever quarterly industrial profit and orders. Industrial profit increased 25% to €3.52 billion, revenue rose 7% to €20.79 billion and orders advanced 13% to €27.90 billion. Profit at Digital Industries increased 44%, with Siemens identifying industrial AI and automation as important growth drivers.

SAP's cloud backlog increased 26% to €22.9 billion. Capgemini bookings rose 9.2%, Sopra Steria's organic growth accelerated to 5.3% and OVHcloud's public-cloud revenue increased 20.2%. Airbus expects about 70 critical applications to run on the European-controlled Scaleway platform by the end of 2028.

Euro-area business activity also reached an eight-month high. The Composite PMI rose to 52.0, services reached 51.7 and Spain recorded its strongest performance in more than eighteen months. France remained in contraction.

# The Real Terrain
Making intelligence operational inside factories, transportation, energy, defense, healthcare, software and critical infrastructure.

Europe may not need to defeat the United States in frontier-model scale to capture a decisive share of AI value. The bottleneck is moving from access to models toward the integration of models with proprietary data, legacy systems, regulation, security and physical operations. This is Europe's natural competitive terrain.

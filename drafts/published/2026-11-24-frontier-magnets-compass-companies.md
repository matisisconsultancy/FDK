A century ago, Captains of Industry — Ford, Edison, Rockefeller, Sloan — built modern capitalism by mastering industrial production, establishing the foundations of a new order Alfred D. Chandler would call managerial capitalism.

Today another transformation is underway. Jensen Huang, Dario Amodei, Elon Musk and Mark Zuckerberg represent an emerging class whose influence extends far beyond the corporations they manage. Their defining capability is the power to establish a credible destination for the future — and generate the gravitational force that mobilizes capital, talent, industries and governments to reach it.

We call them **Frontier Magnets**. The institutions that translate their vision into platforms, ecosystems and enduring value are **Compass Companies**. One creates the pull. The other gives it direction.

::signal
- **Frontier Magnets mobilize capital** beyond the corporations they manage, establishing credible destinations that coordinate independent investors, talent and governments.
- **Compass Companies give direction**, converting that gravitational pull into technologies, platforms and measurable economic value.
- **Huang's November 23 reinstatement** at NVIDIA repriced the company sharply despite no change in its productive assets.
- **The decisive distinction is value, not attraction**: conviction must become productivity, cash flow and returns above the cost of capital.
- **Europe's greatest risk** is not missing the AI revolution but participating in a future whose direction is set elsewhere.
- **Two proposed instruments** — the FDK Frontier Magnet Index and the FDK Compass Company Index — measure mobilization and institutional durability.

::section From the Visible Hand to the Gravitational Field | How influence changed
Chandler's giants organized resources through managerial authority. Frontier Magnets influence investment decisions across organizations they neither own nor control.

Huang cannot command the hyperscalers' investment committees, yet NVIDIA's roadmap shapes capital allocation across semiconductors, AI factories, electricity infrastructure and industrial computing. Amodei does not control the enterprises adopting frontier AI, yet Anthropic's direction shapes expectations for the industrialization of cognition. Their competitive power derives from establishing technological destinations around which independent actors coordinate their investments.

::pull The twentieth-century giant mastered organizational scale. The Frontier Magnet seeks to master something more elusive — coordinating expectations, investment and innovation across entire ecosystems.

::section Why Huang's Return Matters | Charisma versus institution
The market reaction to Huang's November 23 reinstatement repriced NVIDIA despite no change in productive assets — evidence that markets value leadership continuity in strategically central companies. But it exposes a paradox: the individual who creates extraordinary confidence can become a source of extraordinary institutional fragility.

The ultimate achievement of a Frontier Magnet is not becoming permanently indispensable. It is building a Compass Company that sustains strategic direction beyond the individual — the lesson connecting Jobs and Apple to Huang and NVIDIA. Where Jobs redefined how people interact with computing, Huang has the opportunity to influence how entire economies produce, distribute and apply intelligence. Success will depend on governance and sustainable returns, not charisma alone.

::section The New Economics of Leadership | Three dimensions
The relationship introduces three dimensions conventional analysis overlooks: **capital attraction** — leaders catalyzing commitments far beyond their balance sheets; **ecosystem coordination** — companies establishing standards that shape how others invest; and **financial transmission** — expectations that reshape valuations, infrastructure investment and national competitiveness.

This can create a self-reinforcing cycle: a credible destination attracts capital, capital finances complementary capabilities, deployment generates productivity and cash flows, reinforcing confidence. But it works in reverse too. When expectations exceed returns, capital becomes fragility; founder dependence amplifies uncertainty; infrastructure outpaces viable demand. A Frontier Magnet creates conviction. A Compass Company must convert it into measurable performance.

::section The Competition for Economic Gravity | The hidden pattern
The AI Supercycle changes the nature of competition. The most consequential companies no longer compete only within established industry boundaries. Computing capacity affects energy demand; frontier models reshape software economics; autonomous systems influence manufacturing and mobility; platforms accelerate intelligent applications. The next frontier is not simply superior technology but establishing the architecture around which others organize their investments — a new source of both opportunity and systemic risk, as concentration around a few leaders can amplify volatility.

::section Europe: The Strategic Imperative | From adoption to leadership
Europe possesses extraordinary industrial capability but has struggled to scale those strengths into globally influential technology companies. It must pursue two objectives at once: transform industrial leaders into AI-native Compass Companies, and create the conditions for European Frontier Magnets to emerge. This requires deeper capital markets, competitive energy infrastructure and accelerated industrial AI deployment — above all where AI meets the physical economy: manufacturing, energy, transportation, high-speed rail, logistics and automation.

::takeaways Key Takeaways for Investors | Where the next rotation forms
- Follow the gravitational forces of capital — identify Frontier Magnets whose vision moves investment far beyond their own corporations.
- Invest in direction, not just momentum — prioritize Compass Companies that set standards and sustain superior returns on invested capital.
- Anticipate second-order effects across energy, infrastructure, industrial automation, robotics and enterprise AI, not just today's leaders.
- Price leadership and institutional resilience separately — founder dependence introduces governance, succession and valuation risk.
- Position ahead of the next capital rotation, rewarding companies that convert deployment into productivity, free cash flow and durable advantage.

::takeaways Key Takeaways for Governments | A new dimension of competitiveness
- Recognize that economic leadership now depends on attracting Frontier Magnets and developing globally influential Compass Companies.
- Move from subsidizing technologies to mobilizing ecosystems — computing, energy, infrastructure, talent, research and private capital.
- Make capital formation strategic: deep financial markets finance the companies that define the next paradigm.
- Preserve competition and institutional independence — strategic leaders must not justify concentrated power or weakened governance.

::close The future will not belong simply to those who anticipate it. ==It will belong to those capable of mobilizing the world to build it.==

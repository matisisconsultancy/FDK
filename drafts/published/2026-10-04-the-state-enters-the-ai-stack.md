Sunday has no major Western cash-equity session, so today's repricing is forming through policy, geopolitics and capital allocation ahead of Monday.

Friday left the U.S. 10-year at 5.281% despite a sharply weaker 29,000 September payroll gain. The Nasdaq still rose 1.2%, the S&P 500 0.7%, and Brent traded near $102.77. In Europe, the France–Germany 10-year spread widened to around 150 basis points, its highest since the euro-area debt crisis.

Since then, three weekend developments have pushed the Next Economy further upstream: Washington is preparing a $4.2 billion loan to Vistra to expand nuclear output; Broadcom has agreed to provide Anthropic with up to $42 billion to finance compute; and Iran said Sunday that the Strait of Hormuz will not fully reopen until its conditions with Washington are met. ==AI is no longer being built by technology companies alone.==

::pull THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
When governments finance the power and chipmakers finance the compute, is AI still a technology industry — or is it becoming strategic infrastructure? That is today's Great Split.

::signals Five Signals Shaping The Next Economy | states, utilities, chipmakers and sovereign balance sheets enter the capital stack
# Power: The State Moves Inside The AI Economics
The U.S. government plans to lend Vistra about $4.2 billion to increase output at existing nuclear plants. Vistra's fleet includes six reactors producing more than 6.5 GW, and Washington explicitly links rising electricity demand to AI data centers, electrification and other power-intensive industries.

This is more consequential than another power deal. The state is beginning to finance the infrastructure required to make accelerated computing possible. ==The electron is becoming industrial policy.==
signal: The AI race increasingly depends on who can finance power before compute demand arrives.

# Compute: Semiconductor Companies Become Banks
Broadcom has agreed to lend Anthropic up to $42 billion, potentially financing around one-third of Anthropic's $125.2 billion five-year TPU lease commitment. Broadcom could ultimately convert some debt instruments into Anthropic equity.

Chipmakers no longer merely sell capacity. They increasingly finance the customer purchasing it. That accelerates demand — but also merges technological exposure with credit exposure. The GPU and TPU supply chain is becoming a financial supply chain.
signal: The Split will distinguish companies that can fund customers without weakening their own balance sheets from those that cannot.

# Sovereign Capital: Patient Money Moves Upstream
Singapore's roughly $400 billion Temasek says it remains positive on AI infrastructure and intends to raise AI-related exposure from about 6% to as much as 15% of its portfolio over five years, arguing the buildout is anchored by some of the world's strongest corporate balance sheets. Meanwhile, global equity funds attracted $34.76 billion last week — but investors withdrew $2.63 billion from technology funds while adding to financials and utilities.

Capital is not abandoning AI. It is moving underneath AI — from applications toward power, financing, infrastructure, memory, networking, utilities and long-duration balance sheets.
signal: The market is rotating from AI beta to AI ownership of the physical system.

# Europe: The Resilience Bill Arrives Before The Productivity Dividend
Euro-zone inflation jumped to 3.8% in September, driven largely by energy and food, while France's sovereign spread over Germany has widened to around 150 basis points. Today, Germany is simultaneously committing €1 billion in new military aid to Ukraine, €350 million for energy-infrastructure repairs, and deeper joint production of drones and other defence technologies.

Europe must finance AI, grids, defence and resilience at the same time that sovereign capital is becoming more expensive. ==Europe does not have a shortage of investment priorities. It has a shortage of cheap balance sheet.==
signal: Nations that convert resilience spending into productivity gain autonomy; those that fund it through leverage import the bond market into industrial policy.

# Energy: Supply Targets Matter Less Than Secure Barrels
Iran said today that Hormuz will not reopen fully until conditions agreed with Washington are met. Before the war, the Strait carried roughly one-fifth of global oil and LNG flows. OPEC+ has agreed in principle to keep November production targets unchanged — yet its seven core members pumped about 25 million barrels per day in August, roughly 5 million bpd below pre-war February levels, because Middle Eastern export disruption has prevented stated increases from fully reaching markets. The Houthis also said they targeted an Aramco facility near Riyadh; Reuters observed smoke and fire nearby, but Saudi authorities had not confirmed the attack at publication.

The oil market has entered a regime in which nominal capacity and accessible capacity are different assets.
signal: The new scarcity is not oil in the ground — it is secure energy at the point of use, and AI is becoming one of its largest marginal claimants.

::section The Hidden Pattern | one structural change
The week's separate stories resolve into one structural change: AI is simultaneously becoming financialized and sovereignized. Broadcom finances compute. Washington finances nuclear power. Temasek finances the ecosystem. Governments finance grids, defence and industrial resilience. OPEC and geopolitical actors influence the energy cost beneath all of it.

The technology stack is therefore becoming inseparable from the capital stack and the sovereign stack. Yesterday, the winners owned superior models. Tomorrow, the strongest platforms may be those that can assemble compute, power, financing, security, distribution and patient capital.

::section What To Watch This Afternoon — The Monday Gap | the signals capable of repricing Monday before markets reopen
There is no regular Western cash-equity session today. The decisive signals are therefore those capable of repricing Monday before markets reopen.

**OPEC+.** The group has agreed in principle to leave November targets unchanged. Watch the formal communiqué and any language on actual Gulf export constraints rather than headline quotas.

**Hormuz.** Qatar continues to mediate between Washington and Tehran. The critical issue is sequencing: whether security in the Strait can be restored before a broader nuclear or sanctions agreement.

**Saudi energy infrastructure.** Any Saudi or Aramco confirmation of physical damage following the Houthi claim would immediately matter for the Monday oil-risk premium.

**Nuclear finance.** Watch the terms attached to Washington's planned $4.2 billion Vistra financing when the package is formally detailed. The cost and structure of that capital could become a benchmark for future AI-linked power financing.

**The two market thresholds:** 5.28% on the U.S. 10-year and ~150 bps on France versus Germany. If both move higher as oil remains above $100, Monday begins with simultaneous pressure on technology duration and European sovereign duration.

::takeaways Key takeaways | the institutional turn in the AI Supercycle
- The decisive change in the AI Supercycle is no longer technological — it is institutional.
- Chip companies now finance customers; governments now finance nuclear power; sovereign funds are increasing AI exposure.
- Geopolitical control determines which energy molecules reach the market, making energy security the ground beneath AI.
- Intelligence has crossed the line from product to infrastructure — raising the hurdle but deepening the cycle.
- The next winners will possess the balance sheet, energy access and institutional architecture to keep AI running when capital and geopolitics turn hostile.

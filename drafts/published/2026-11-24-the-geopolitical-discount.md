What happens when financial markets restore their confidence in the leadership of the AI Supercycle, but governments simultaneously restrict the global circulation of the computing power underpinning it?

Two developments on November 23–24 expose this fundamental contradiction. Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO has reignited investor confidence. Simultaneously, Washington has blacklisted seven Southeast Asian companies, including an Alibaba subsidiary, materially escalating the enforcement of American semiconductor restrictions.

::signal
- **Jensen Huang's reinstatement** as Nvidia CEO reignited investor confidence, driving a +6.51% Nasdaq rally on November 23.
- **Washington blacklisted seven** Southeast Asian companies, including an Alibaba subsidiary, escalating semiconductor-export enforcement.
- **An emerging K-shaped divergence** splits the Supercycle: a US leadership premium against a Chinese geopolitical discount.
- **An estimated 60–70%** of Chinese high-end training capacity sits offshore, now exposed to restricted access.
- **China extended its suspension** of restrictions on US-bound gallium, germanium and antimony until January 27, 2027.
- **The material change is regulatory and financial**, not a new Chinese frontier model overturning American leadership.

::section From Export Controls to Capital Exposure | the big take
The American Bureau of Industry and Security has introduced licensing requirements, with a presumption of denial, for the seven designated companies operating across Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand.

Alibaba disputes the designation of its Singapore subsidiary, Apex Enterprise Solutions, and denies that it diverted restricted chips. The regulatory action does not constitute a criminal conviction.

The strategic significance extends well beyond semiconductor exports. Geopolitical risk is now reaching computing-capacity contracts, collateral, infrastructure utilization and corporate balance sheets. Sources: Reuters, Bank of America research cited by Reuters Breakingviews, Financial Express and Fortune. The offshore training figure is an industry estimate; the prepayments are not confirmed financial losses.

::patterns The Hidden Pattern | the market is repricing two different forces
# The Leadership Premium — United States
Restored confidence in Nvidia's leadership, renewed investor appetite for accelerated computing, and potentially lower cost of equity capital.
# The Geopolitical Discount — China
Restricted offshore computing access, higher infrastructure and substitution costs, potentially higher risk premiums, and increasing divergence in valuation multiples and capital allocation.

::pull This is an emerging K-shaped divergence within the AI Supercycle itself: American computing companies could benefit from renewed investor confidence, while Chinese AI companies face increasing uncertainty over access to the infrastructure required to remain competitive.

::section A Countertrend | selective separation, continued negotiation
A particularly important countertrend emerged on November 24: China reportedly extended its suspension of certain restrictions on U.S.-bound gallium, germanium and antimony exports until January 27, 2027. This suggests that selective technological separation can coexist with continued negotiations over critical industrial supply chains.

No newly verified Chinese frontier-model release has overturned American leadership in today's review. The material change is regulatory and financial.

::section The Final Take | the governing thesis
The return of Jensen Huang may restore confidence in the leadership of the AI Supercycle. It does not remove the structural forces fragmenting its technological and financial architecture.

For The Velocity Edge, the governing thesis is strengthened: America leads critical elements of the AI frontier, while China challenges the economics of its deployment. The next market rotation will increasingly differentiate technological leadership, capital efficiency and geopolitical resilience.

::close The geography of intelligence is becoming the ==geography of capital==. Investors must price not only the power of AI, but the freedom to deploy it.

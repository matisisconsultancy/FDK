The AI boom is no longer just about who builds the best technology. It is about who controls the scarce inputs that make technology scale.

Oil and long-term yields are rising again this morning while equities soften. Brent is back around $107, and the market is reassessing whether higher rates are becoming structural rather than temporary. That changes the investment regime.

Higher rates do not simply compress valuations. Scarce power does not simply raise costs. More compute does not automatically create value. Together, they create a new hierarchy: ==control first, returns second, scale third==. The next phase of the AI Supercycle will be won less by those who build the most — and more by those who can finance, power and monetize scale better than everyone else.

::pull THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
What happens when the most capital-intensive technology cycle in history collides with scarce power and expensive capital? The answer is not simply slower growth. It is concentration. The winners will increasingly control capital, power, compute, customers and cash flow. Everyone else pays somebody else's price.

::signals The Structural Shift | five forces reshaping the cycle
# Scarcity Becomes the New Moat
The U.S. long end remains around 5%+, while investors increasingly question how high the neutral rate may settle. That is more than a valuation problem. It raises barriers to entry, strengthens balance-sheet leaders, weakens optionality for capital-dependent challengers, and accelerates consolidation.
Scarcity does not just raise prices — it redistributes power. The second-order effect is that higher rates may make AI more oligopolistic, not less important.
signal: Higher rates make AI more concentrated, not less relevant.

# Power Moves From Cost to Strategic Asset
The deeper bottleneck is no longer compute alone. AI, electrification, defence and reshoring all compete for the same physical infrastructure: generation, grids, storage, cooling and transmission. Oil above $100 reinforces the pressure, but the structural issue is broader.
Compute without power is stranded capital. As power becomes scarcer, bargaining power moves upstream — utilities, grids, storage, cooling and power-connected infrastructure capture more of the economics. The next AI winners may increasingly sit outside traditional technology.
signal: Compute without power is stranded capital.

# The AI Trade Moves From Capacity to Returns
Markets have rewarded announced capacity. That phase is becoming harder. Corporate bond investors are already becoming more selective toward AI-linked issuance as hyperscaler borrowing rises and return visibility becomes more important.
The question changes from *how much can you build?* to *what return can you earn?* That favors contracted demand, memory, networking, enterprise AI and power-secured infrastructure. The AI race is becoming a return-on-capital race — and capital structure becomes market share.
signal: Capital structure becomes market share.

# China's AI Acceleration May Intensify Global Friction
China's industrial-profit growth slowed again in August as strong technology manufacturing failed to offset weak domestic demand. AI raises productivity and industrial capacity, but weak domestic absorption pushes more of that capacity outward.
The chain becomes AI productivity → excess capacity → export pressure → trade friction → industrial policy. The result may be more geopolitical fragmentation before global productivity gains fully materialize. The Great Split is increasingly visible inside economies as well as between them.
signal: Excess capacity travels outward as trade friction.

# Europe's Opportunity Moves to the Physical Stack
Europe's strategic vulnerability is becoming clearer. The value layer in mobility and industry is shifting toward AI software, compute architecture, data and autonomy. Europe risks owning the machine while renting the intelligence.
But Europe still controls major parts of the physical stack: grids, rail, automation, semiconductor equipment, mobility infrastructure and energy systems. Do not buy Europe as one macro trade — buy the bottlenecks Europe controls.
signal: Own Europe's bottlenecks, not its macro.

::section The Hidden Pattern | efficiency gives way to control
The Next Economy is being reorganized around three scarce resources: ==capital, power and compute==. AI increases demand for all three. Geopolitics restricts them. Higher rates reprice them.

Scarcity then creates second-order effects: higher barriers, greater concentration, stronger pricing power, more vertical integration and more industrial policy. The system is moving from efficiency to control. That is the deeper evolution of The Great Split.

The AI Supercycle is becoming a mechanism for ==redistributing economic power== across countries, industries and financial markets.

China's industrial profits are accelerating. Japan is integrating energy generation with AI infrastructure. Italy's UniCredit has secured the remaining approvals for its Commerzbank offer. The ECB is confronting the consequences of energy inflation and debt-financed expansion.

The next hierarchy of global competitiveness will reflect the capacity of economies to integrate **intelligence, energy and capital** into a single architecture of growth.

::signals The Five Signals Reshaping Global Velocity | one tap per signal
# China — The industrial dividend of intelligence
Industrial profits accelerated to 20.1% in October. Electronics profits doubled over ten months, contributing 9.7 points to overall growth, yet automotive profits contracted 26%.
signal: China's industrial economy is becoming increasingly asymmetric.

# Japan — Energy meets intelligence
JERA is negotiating with American technology companies to develop data centres near power-generation facilities. The JERA–Dell–RHAELM partnership illustrates an emerging model integrating energy and computing.
signal: Energy producers are evolving into intelligence infrastructure providers.

# United States — Computing sovereignty reshapes investment
Nvidia has suspended certain server shipments to four Southeast Asian markets following tighter US export controls. Fitch expects these rules to make Asian data-centre investment increasingly selective.
signal: Computing access is becoming a condition of capital access.

# Italy and Germany — The strategic value of financial scale
UniCredit confirmed on November 26 that all regulatory conditions for its Commerzbank offer had been satisfied, with settlement expected by December 7 — a milestone in European banking integration, though synergies remain unrealised.
signal: Financial scale is becoming strategic infrastructure.

# Europe — Monetary continuity, fiscal fragmentation
Leaders began consultations on Pablo Hernández de Cos's candidacy for ECB president but failed to agree the EU's 2028–2034 budget. The ECB's latest accounts reveal support for further tightening.
signal: Monetary resolve meets fiscal fragmentation.

::section The Hidden Pattern — The Great Industrial Dispersion | China industrial profit growth, Jan–Oct 2026 YoY
Electronics rose +100.0%, non-ferrous metals +71.7% and chemicals +50.3%, against all-manufacturing +17.5% — while electrical equipment fell −8.0%, power and heat supply −18.4%, automotive −26.0% and non-metallic minerals −46.7%.

The AI Supercycle is not lifting every industry. It is **redistributing profitability**. The next rotation may occur within industries and economies rather than between them.

::section Second-Order Effects — The Next Capital Rotation
Three structural opportunities are emerging: energy companies evolving into intelligence infrastructure providers; industrial corporations capturing disproportionate productivity gains; and financial institutions acquiring the scale to fund the Next Economy. Conversely, higher financing costs, restricted computing access and fragmented energy markets could accelerate consolidation among vulnerable businesses. The most consequential opportunities may lie at the intersection of asset classes previously considered unrelated.

::takeaways Five Takeaways for Investors, Boards and Governments
- Investors: focus on the intersection of energy infrastructure, Industrial AI and financial services.
- Asset managers: position for structural earnings dispersion, not a uniform technology recovery.
- Boards: evaluate investments against incremental ROIC, financing costs and access to critical resources.
- Executives: build advantage through the integration of intelligence, proprietary data and industrial execution.
- Governments: treat energy architecture, capital-market integration and AI deployment as a unified strategy.

::section The Financial Verdict
Main accelerator: realised industrial profitability across the AI supply chain. Main decelerator: monetary tightening, technological fragmentation and selective infrastructure financing. What's next: a more differentiated market in which access to strategic resources, financial resilience and capital productivity determine valuation premiums.

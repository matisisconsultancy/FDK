Three developments matter today, and together they sharpen the race. Brussels has reset the clock. Finland has raised the power-and-capital benchmark. Italy has gained political continuity—but still lacks the hard infrastructure proof.

The competition is moving from consortium announcements to a much tougher test: **site + power + capital + offtake + execution**.

::signals The Three Developments | what moved today
# EU — The Tender Clock Has Moved To 3 December
EuroHPC now lists 3 December 2026 at 17:00 CET as the deadline for the AI Gigafactory call. The facilities must demonstrate not only massive processor scale but also adequate power infrastructure, energy efficiency, water efficiency and circularity.

The next eight weeks are no longer about political positioning. They are about converting national coalitions into bankable bids. For Italy, Spain, France, Germany and the Nordics, the burden of proof is now explicit: committed equity → secured MW → development-ready site → technology architecture → anchor demand → credible delivery timetable. The tender is increasingly selecting infrastructure platforms, not technology manifestos. Companies joining a consortium now need to know whether they are shareholders, customers, power providers, lenders or technology suppliers—ambiguous participation will not create a financeable asset.
signal: The tender now rewards physical readiness, not political ambition.

# Finland — The Nordics Just Raised The Bar
atNorth today announced FIN05 in Salo, Finland: a 28.6-hectare campus with 75 MW secured for Phase 1, a path to 230 MW gross power, connection through a Fingrid substation, and approximately €2 billion of atNorth investment. The company estimates customer-owned servers and installation could add as much as €10 billion more. Long-term PPAs, grid-flexibility solutions and heat reuse are part of the design. The first phase is planned around 60 MW of IT capacity, with power availability targeted for Q3 2028.

This is consequential even though atNorth has not disclosed FIN05 as an official EuroHPC Gigafactory bid—because Finland is demonstrating what the winning physical architecture may look like: power first, grid first, site first, capital second, compute follows. Where Italy and Spain are still heavily focused on consortium architecture, the Nordics are increasingly presenting development-ready power infrastructure. In AI infrastructure, secured megawatts can become more valuable than announced billions. If customer hardware approaches even a fraction of the estimated €10 billion potential, capital intensity around Salo could rival or exceed several national Gigafactory programmes.
signal: Follow secured power and customer CAPEX, not national headlines.

# Italy — The Project Is Becoming A National Project
Italy's AI Gigafactory bid today received explicit support from PD Senate leader Francesco Boccia, creating unusual majority-opposition convergence around the project. The emerging coalition includes Eni, Enel, Leonardo, Saipem, Snam and Terna alongside research institutions, with Palazzo Chigi coordinating the bid.

Large AI infrastructure has a 10–20 year economic life, so political continuity affects permitting, public procurement, sovereign support and ultimately the cost of capital. Cross-party support can reduce policy-duration risk. But Italy should not overread the signal: political consensus does not solve the remaining gaps—no final site, no disclosed secured MW, no finalized capital structure, no contracted compute offtake, no publicly defined ownership architecture. Today's development strengthens Italy politically, but does not yet strengthen it financially. That distinction is critical.
signal: Italy gained political durability; it must now show financial durability.

::takeaways Key takeaways | where the race stands
- The race has entered its execution phase—the 3 December deadline puts a premium on physical readiness over political ambition.
- Power is becoming the scarce strategic asset; Finland's 75 MW secured immediately outweighs another unsecured multibillion-euro announcement.
- The Nordics are emerging as Europe's AI power reservoir: cheap low-carbon electricity, cooler climate, grid access and development-ready sites.
- Italy gained political durability but must now show financial durability—its next credible move must concern site, power, equity or offtake.
- The winning model is clear: secured power → contracted compute → bankable cash flow → lower WACC → more capital → more compute.

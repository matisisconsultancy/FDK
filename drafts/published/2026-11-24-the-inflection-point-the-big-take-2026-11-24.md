Global financial markets are entering a new regime in which technological leadership, corporate governance, national security and monetary policy are becoming inseparable determinants of asset valuations.

Nvidia gained **31.5%** on Monday, recovering more than **$952 billion** in market capitalization following the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as chief executive; the S&P 500 added more than **$2.8 trillion**. Yet this morning Nvidia fell back in premarket trading, while Anthropic slipped below its **$83** IPO price days after the largest public offering in financial history.

These developments reveal three dynamics: the systemic importance of corporate leadership, the influence of political and regulatory risk on technology valuations, and capital potentially rotating toward alternative sources of intellectual property and industrial productivity.

::signal
- **Markets are entering a new regime** where leadership, governance, national security and monetary policy jointly determine valuations.
- **Nvidia recovered $952B Monday** on Huang's reinstatement, then fell 5.2% in Tuesday premarket as governance scrutiny renewed.
- **Anthropic traded near $82**, below its $83 IPO price, despite raising ~$93.5B in a record offering.
- **A December Fed hike is almost fully priced**, with the dollar near a 20-month high and core PCE at 3.1%.
- **Sanofi announced a $41B acquisition** of Insmed as Brent fell below $89, easing commodity pressure.
- **The next rotation may favour industries** that capture AI's productivity dividend without inheriting its full infrastructure burden.

::signals Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy | what the past 24 hours reveal
# The Rise of Compass Companies and the Key-Person Premium
Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang on November 23 after consultations that included U.S. government representatives; a national-security committee has been established. Nvidia's influence spans semiconductors, cloud, electricity and global capital, so its leadership uncertainty reverberates far beyond its share price.
signal: The founder premium becomes a governance discount if institutions cannot preserve strategic continuity independently of one leader.

# Anthropic and the Great Repricing of Intelligence
Anthropic traded near $82, below its $83 IPO price and far below its $111.50 opening on November 19, having raised ~$93.5 billion. The divergence from Nvidia's recovery reveals markets differentiating between suppliers of computing, developers of frontier intelligence and the enterprises that monetize it.
signal: The most valuable asset may not be intelligence itself, but privileged access to its productive applications.

# The AI Rally Becomes a Monetary Policy Variable
A December Fed rate increase is now almost fully priced; the dollar is near a 20-month high, the two-year yield stands near 4.67% and September core PCE was 3.1%, above target. The buildout phase stimulates demand immediately, while productivity gains require time—so higher rates can raise the financing cost of the very infrastructure expected to improve productivity.
signal: Advantage belongs to enterprises able to finance the transition from inflationary buildout to disinflationary productivity.

# Europe's Convergence of Industrial AI and Strategic Capital
Germany's Ifo climate index rose from 90.1 to 90.6; Sanofi announced a $41 billion cash acquisition of Insmed, completion expected in the first half of 2027. Country risk and corporate competitiveness must be evaluated separately—Germany's composite PMI reads 55.0 against France's 46.2.
signal: Globally competitive European firms can retain strategic financing capacity despite weak domestic macroeconomics.

# Cheaper Oil and Expensive Electricity Can Coexist
Brent fell below $89 as recovering supply and surplus expectations outweighed Hormuz concerns; copper declined ~3%. Lower crude provides a disinflationary impulse but does not reduce grid connection delays or electricity-delivery costs for data centres.
signal: The scarcity premium may migrate from energy commodities toward the infrastructure that delivers reliable power.

::pull The next competitive frontier will concern who controls the intelligence, who controls the transaction, who owns the customer relationship and who carries the resulting financial liability.

::section The Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines | second-order effects
Four effects deserve attention: the **politicization of corporate valuations**; the **migration of economic rents** toward proprietary data and applications; the **AI–monetary paradox**, where stronger technology markets encourage restrictive conditions that hurt weaker participants; and the **regulatory convergence of technology and finance**. This morning Britain's Financial Conduct Authority warned that companies offering AI agents may fall within financial regulation when their products perform regulated activities.

::patterns Three Scenarios for the Next Six Months | indicative FDK weights
# [50%] The Selective Supercycle
AI investment continues, but capital increasingly rewards resilient balance sheets, commercial adoption and proven operating economics. This remains the central scenario.
# [25%] The Productive Broadening
Industrial AI adoption accelerates, credit conditions stabilize and earnings growth spreads beyond technology infrastructure.
# [25%] The Geopolitical–Monetary Squeeze
Higher rates, technology restrictions and governance uncertainty compress valuations and delay infrastructure investment.

::takeaways Five Takeaways for Investors and Boards | what to act on
- Introduce leadership continuity into valuation models: key-person dependence and governance resilience are material for Compass Companies.
- Position for value migration: proprietary data, enterprise applications and distribution may capture an increasing share of returns.
- Distinguish duration risk from default risk: falling sovereign yields do not guarantee improving credit for leveraged infrastructure developers.
- Differentiate European corporate opportunity from sovereign vulnerability: competitive firms can retain attractive economics despite weak public finances.
- Treat geopolitical and regulatory exposure as financial variables: restrictions and AI-agent rules can alter markets, costs and multiples.

::close The Great Split will not separate those who believe in artificial intelligence from those who do not — it will separate those with the ==financial and industrial architecture to capture its returns== from those who merely finance its expansion.

The most consequential development in global markets this morning is the collision between two powerful forces: the extraordinary acceleration of artificial intelligence and the renewed increase in the cost of sustaining it.

America's October inflation exceeded expectations — headline CPI +0.4% MoM against a 0.3% forecast, core +0.3% against 0.2%. The two-year Treasury yield jumped roughly 18 basis points to 4.73%; the ten-year approached 5%. A December Fed increase is now considered virtually certain.

Yet the AI Supercycle keeps demonstrating extraordinary commercial momentum — alongside an equally extraordinary financing burden. The emerging divide is not between AI winners and losers, but between companies capturing the profits of acceleration and those assuming the financial obligations required to make it possible. ==This is the next evolution of The Great Split.==

::signal
- **Inflation is repricing AI** — higher discount rates reduce the present value of distant AI cash flows just as financing costs rise.
- **CoreWeave shows the asymmetry** — $3.66bn quarterly revenue and a $132.5bn backlog, but $846m net interest expense versus $207m operating profit.
- **SoftBank posted a $7bn loss** — including a $3.7bn charge, with financing costs more than doubling to about ¥462bn.
- **Scarcity is migrating to power** — Siemens Energy's record €164bn backlog signals where the next economic rents may accumulate.
- **Europe faces a financing asymmetry** — 4% inflation, restrictive credit and sovereign risk threaten geographic divergence in productive investment.
- **The rotation follows economic profit** — capital will favour returns above a rising cost of capital, not capex growth alone.

::signals Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy | the forces in play
# The Discount-Rate Shock
America's inflation problem is becoming an AI valuation problem. The two-year yield saw its largest one-day move since April 2025; the ten-year approached 5%. Higher financing costs raise the returns required to justify further AI investment — a transmission chain the Supercycle may itself be large enough to influence.
signal: The next premium rewards generating incremental cash flow faster than the cost of capital rises.

# CoreWeave and SoftBank
Revenue velocity and financial velocity are diverging. CoreWeave's backlog reached $132.5bn and active capacity exceeded 1.7 GW, yet net interest expense of $846m topped operating profit fourfold, with Q4 interest expense seen at $1.05–1.15bn. SoftBank raised $56.5bn in the quarter while posting a $7bn loss.
signal: The winners may be those monetizing AI demand without the proportionate infrastructure financing burden.

# TSMC and Nvidia
TSMC's board approved prospective investments of up to $500m each in Anthropic and OpenAI — suppliers becoming financially integrated with the model developers they serve. Nvidia fell ~9.6% over Nov. 9–10 on unproven allegations, with Microsoft briefly overtaking it. The structural risk is financial concentration.
signal: Semiconductor leadership will be set by pricing power, efficiency and governance — not mere AI exposure.

# Energy
Europe's most consequential AI opportunity may sit outside the AI industry. Siemens Energy's €164bn backlog, E.ON's ~€4.2bn of grid investment and RWE's €4.58bn EBITDA point to durable infrastructure demand. Brent traded near $92.5 as the geopolitical premium eased, though 37 vessels were reportedly attacked in October.
signal: The next migration of AI value may move toward the productive infrastructure that supplies the Supercycle.

# Europe
The Great Split is becoming a contest over financial and industrial execution. Eurozone inflation hit 4.0% (energy +20.8%); the ECB reported net 9% tightening of corporate credit standards. The OAT–Bund spread eased to ~138bp. Tomorrow's EU AI Gigafactories deadline aims to mobilize over €30bn.
signal: Europe's next hierarchy depends less on existing capabilities than on financing and deploying them productively.

::patterns Four Hidden Patterns | beneath the headlines
# Rates Raise Concentration
Cash-generative firms keep investing through higher rates; the externally financed face higher hurdles. The Supercycle may concentrate precisely as technological capability becomes more widely available.
# Scarcity Moves to Power
The next economic rents may accumulate in power generation, grid access and electrical equipment — the infrastructure required to make intelligence commercially usable.
# Revenue ≠ Value
CoreWeave shows revenue and financial value creation are becoming different cycles. A correction could occur even as the Supercycle grows, if incremental returns fail to justify incremental investment.
# A Divide in Economic Profit
The economy is splitting between businesses earning returns above their cost of capital and those expanding without equivalent financial productivity — from AI to banking, industry and energy.

::section Three Rotations to Anticipate | where capital may move
First, from AI infrastructure *quantity* toward AI infrastructure *profitability*. Second, from technology expenditure toward productive infrastructure — networks, equipment, automation, efficient computing. Third, from broad geographic exposure toward financing resilience, favouring those able to sustain productive investment under restrictive conditions.

The central three-month scenario remains **disciplined reallocation** (50%), ahead of a financing squeeze (30%) and a productivity breakthrough (20%). The decisive distinction is whether the financial system begins rewarding better investment — or restricting the productive capital future growth requires.

::takeaways Five Key Takeaways | for investors, boards and policymakers
- **Equity investors** — follow economic profit, not revenue velocity.
- **Credit investors** — the Supercycle is becoming a financing-quality test: coverage, refinancing, utilization.
- **Corporate boards** — financial resilience is now a competitive advantage; preserve capacity to invest through tightness.
- **European policymakers** — productive capital formation must become the central industrial priority.
- **Global investors** — The Great Split is becoming a divide between growth and value capture.

::close The future has become more expensive to finance precisely as its possibilities have become more extraordinary. Technology determines the possibilities; capital the scale; productivity the returns. ==The next winners will not simply build the future — they will make the future pay.==

The U.S. 10-year is near 5%, Brent remains above $100, Hormuz traffic is still impaired, Washington and Beijing are negotiating AI guardrails and critical minerals, and China's newest DRAM platform claims more than 50% higher gross dies per wafer. The constraint on the Next Economy is no longer technology alone. It is the ability to connect compute, energy, capital, regulation and demand fast enough to convert innovation into economic output.

The next phase will not necessarily reward those with the most GPUs, capital or industrial scale. It will reward those that can turn technological acceleration into cash flow before infrastructure, financing or policy become binding constraints.

The fundamental question: *who can convert technological acceleration into economic power before the surrounding system slows it down?*

~5% :: U.S. 10-year Treasury yield resetting hurdle rates
>$100 :: Brent crude, with Hormuz traffic still impaired
>50% :: higher gross dies per wafer on China's newest DRAM platform

::pull INVESTOR VERDICT
Own the control points; underweight the assets that pay for scarcity, wait for permission or refinance at 5% money.

::patterns The Investor Action Box | own bottlenecks, avoid victims
# Long / Accumulate
Semiconductors, HBM and memory — structural scarcity and sovereign demand. Optical networking and interconnect, as larger compute clusters require more bandwidth. Contracted data centres with secured power, utilisation and long-duration customers. Grid equipment, nuclear, gas and storage. AI security and evaluation. Banks, insurers and infrastructure-finance platforms.
# Underweight
Uncontracted AI capacity — gigawatts without committed demand are expensive optionality. Legacy industrials with weak pricing power. Highly leveraged long-duration assets, as 5% sovereign yields reset hurdle rates. Narrative technology, where cash conversion matters more than multiple expansion.
# Avoid
Stranded scale: no secured power, no contracted demand, no regulatory certainty and no credible return above WACC.
# The Portfolio Test
Does the asset control a bottleneck — or pay for one?

::signals Five Signals | from capability to coordination
# AI: from technology competition to system governance
Washington and Beijing are discussing AI behaviour, critical minerals, tariffs and energy together. AI has moved beyond the technology stack and into geopolitical architecture.
signal: The competitive edge increasingly comes from shaping the rules that govern strategic systems.
# China: from production strength to demand constraint
AI is increasing industrial productivity while domestic demand remains comparatively weak. Higher productivity can amplify overcapacity rather than solve it: more productivity → more output → lower unit costs → more export pressure → more trade friction.
signal: China's next constraint is not production. It is demand absorption.
# Semiconductors: from technological sovereignty to economic sovereignty
China's domestic memory industry is improving yield, density and manufacturing economics. Producing a chip is not enough; sovereignty becomes durable only when production is competitive at scale.
signal: The decisive metric moves from technological feasibility to cost, yield and volume economics.
# AI infrastructure: from corporate capex to institutional finance
Funding is broadening from hyperscalers toward banks, sovereign funds, private credit and insurers. AI infrastructure is beginning to resemble a financeable asset class: contracted capacity → predictable cash flow → collateral → lower cost of capital → more scale.
signal: The underwriting test is now whether capacity can generate infrastructure-like returns.
# Energy: from input cost to competitive architecture
Hormuz remains impaired and oil remains above $100 while energy increasingly enters trade and strategic negotiations. Energy scarcity now transmits directly into inflation, rates, WACC and industrial competitiveness.
signal: Energy security is becoming a determinant of economic velocity.

::section The Hidden Pattern | coordinated velocity vs. fragmented scale
The United States has technology and capital, but growing infrastructure and governance constraints. China has industrial scale and execution capacity, but insufficient domestic demand. Europe has capital, engineering and industry, but fragmented finance, energy and regulation.

The difference is not scale. It is the ability to connect assets and convert them into output. That is the new Great Split: ==coordinated velocity vs. fragmented scale==.

::section Where Capital Moves Next | scarcity, pricing power, cash-flow visibility
The market should increasingly reward assets at the control points of the system: compute, memory, bandwidth, power, grids, secure infrastructure, AI governance and capital intermediation.

The winning combination is **scarcity + pricing power + cash-flow visibility**. At 5% Treasury yields, aspiration is not enough. The market wants ==cash flow, contracts, control==.

::section The Velocity Insight | the system is multiplicative
The first phase of the AI revolution was about capability. The second was about capacity. The third is about whether the surrounding system can absorb both.

A chip without power is useless. Power without grid access is stranded. A data centre without financing cannot scale. A model without deployment cannot monetise. Productivity without demand cannot compound.

The economic system is multiplicative: **compute × energy × capital × infrastructure × regulation × demand**. One weak link can slow the entire chain.

- Scale provides resources; capital provides optionality; technology provides capability — but execution determines whether they compound.
- At 5% money, the market rewards cash flow, contracts and control over narrative.
- The new Great Split is coordinated velocity versus fragmented scale.

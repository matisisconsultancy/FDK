The AI profit pool is migrating away from intelligence itself. As models proliferate, scarcity is moving along a chain — Memory → Packaging → Networking → Power → Chip Design → Security — while higher rates increase the cost of scaling every layer.

The emerging divide is **Scarcity + Economic Capture** versus **Capital Intensity + Weak Conversion**. Technology leadership remains essential, but the next phase will increasingly reward companies that can turn scarce positions into durable cash economics before the cost structure required to scale them absorbs the advantage.

The fundamental question: ==who can turn an indispensable position into durable economic value before capital intensity erodes the return?==

::signals The Two Decisive Signals | Where the economics are moving
# The Economic Center of AI Is Moving Beyond Models
Model capability is proliferating faster than the harder-to-reproduce layers surrounding it. TSMC captures advanced manufacturing scarcity. Micron captures AI-memory scarcity. Schneider Electric captures power architecture. Synopsys monetizes design complexity. Palo Alto Networks benefits from the governance burden created by autonomous agents.
signal: AI value is moving toward the infrastructure and control layers required to industrialize intelligence.
# Resilience Is Becoming Strategic Power
AI is becoming more capital intensive precisely as long-duration capital remains expensive. Companies with strong cash generation can invest through volatility, absorb delays and maintain strategic flexibility. Companies carrying large fixed commitments face a harder conversion test.
signal: Higher-for-longer changes competitive structure — not merely valuation.

::patterns Second-Order Effects | How the game changes
# Capital as Barrier
Capital access becomes a barrier to entry, and funding provenance becomes a quality test.
# Backlog Quality
Backlog quality overtakes backlog size: Announced → Contracted → Funded → Delivered → Cash-Generating.
# Asymmetric Contracts
What strengthens visibility for the supplier can increase rigidity for the buyer.
# Broadcom–Anthropic
Best understood as a diagnostic case of new strategic interdependence — not evidence that supplier financing is inherently weak.

::section Investment Verdict | The ranking identifies velocity; the verdict identifies what still has to go right
**QUALITY CONFIRMED — Microsoft · TSMC · Schneider Electric.** The strategic position is established and monetization is visible. The burden is durability.

**EXPECTATIONS EXPOSED — Nvidia · Meta · Micron · Synopsys.** The economics are compelling, but continued execution must justify increasingly demanding expectations. The burden is sustained outperformance.

**CONVERSION TO PROVE — Amazon · Alibaba.** Structural capacity is substantial; the question is how efficiently incremental investment translates into durable cash returns. The burden is conversion.

**DEPENDENCY RISK — Anthropic.** Technology leadership remains exceptional, but fixed commitments and counterparties increasingly shape the economics. The burden is proving that growth can outrun financial rigidity.

**BROADCOM — DUAL-EDGED INFLECTION.** Broadcom does not fit cleanly into a single category today. Its expanding role across silicon, infrastructure and financing increases strategic value, while customer concentration and supplier-funded ecosystem growth raise a new quality test. The burden is proving that ecosystem leverage compounds faster than concentration risk.

::pull THE SHARPER DISTINCTION
The best company is not necessarily the best-priced security. The critical question is what the market still requires the company to prove.

::section Investor Action Framework | GVI → Economic Capture → Verdict → Valuation → Action
**Increase conviction** when the remaining burden is falling faster than expectations are rising.
**Hold conviction** when economic quality remains high but valuation already assumes continued excellence.
**Demand proof** when capital commitments rise ahead of cash conversion.
**Reassess** when the problem migrates from valuation into Structural Velocity itself.
Verdict defines the burden. Price determines whether that burden is worth bearing.

::section Board-Level Implications | The governance test
Boards should ask: What indispensable position do we hold? How much capital does it require to scale? How much demand is economically monetizable? Who is funding that demand? How quickly does investment become free cash flow? Can the strategy withstand slower monetization?

The governance test: ==Is the capital structure supporting strategy — or beginning to determine it?==

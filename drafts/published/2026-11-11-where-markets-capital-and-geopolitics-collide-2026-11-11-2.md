The most consequential development in markets this morning is the emergence of two radically different financial outcomes from the same technological revolution: companies supplying the physical infrastructure of artificial intelligence are converting demand into profits and cash flow, while companies financing computing capacity are accumulating extraordinary capital commitments before achieving sustainable net profitability.

The Great Split is no longer simply between technological leaders and followers. It is emerging between those converting acceleration into present profits and those financing infrastructure on the expectation of profits tomorrow — a distinction that may define the next rotation of global capital.

::signal
- **Siemens Energy posted a record FY2026**, with €8.8B pre-tax free cash flow and a €164B backlog; shares rose 8.3%.
- **CoreWeave revenue surged ~168%** to $3.66B, yet it booked a $709M net loss and $846M net interest expense.
- **US October CPI ran hot** at +0.4% headline, pushing the Fed toward further tightening just as AI needs long-duration capital.
- **Foreign investors pulled $49.77B** from seven Asian equity markets in October, Taiwan alone losing $30.68B.
- **Brent rebounded to $92.77** after renewed Iran uncertainty and 37 ship attacks near Hormuz in October.
- **The next bottleneck is financing**, not equipment — at a cost consistent with sustainable returns.

::signals Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy | the fault lines of financial differentiation
# CoreWeave Reveals the Cost of Scaling Intelligence
Q3 revenue rose to $3.66B from $1.365B, with $207M operating income — but a $709M net loss and $846M net interest expense. Debt reached ~$46.9B. FY2026 guidance is $13.0–13.3B revenue against $37–39B capex, with adjusted profitability expected only in H1 2028.
signal: Backlog is not profitability; the next bottleneck is financing at a sustainable cost.
# Siemens Energy Captures the Infrastructure Dividend
FY2026 revenue reached €44.2B, up 15.5%; orders +18.8% to €68.1B; book-to-bill 1.54; backlog a record €164B (≈€15B data-centre). Margin before special items doubled to 12.4%, with a 14–16% target for 2027. Suppliers capture profit during the build; operators carry the long-term risk.
signal: An electricity premium is accruing to those controlling scarce power infrastructure.
# The Return of Inflation Raises the Hurdle
October US CPI rose 0.4% headline, 0.3% core, electricity +0.9%; BLS could not publish annual comparisons. Fed officials signaled further tightening. AI's long-duration financing collides with a rising cost of capital.
signal: The discount-rate divide separates present earnings from distant promises.
# The $50 Billion Asian Capital Flight
Foreign investors withdrew $49.77B from seven Asian markets in October — Taiwan $30.68B, South Korea $14.59B — with a further $22.6B in November. India drew $5.68B. TSMC's board approved up to $500M each in Anthropic and OpenAI, becoming both supplier and investor.
signal: Industrial competitiveness and equity-market attractiveness can diverge sharply.
# Energy Security and Europe's Sovereign Divide
Brent rebounded to $92.77 amid Iran uncertainty and 37 Hormuz-area ship attacks. Eurozone inflation hit 4.0% (energy +20.8%), while Germany eased to 2.8%. Italian output rose 1.2% in September but fell 0.4% over the quarter.
signal: Europe's edge is productivity per euro invested, not matching US capex.

::patterns Three Scenarios for the Next Six Months | FDK central assessment
# Productive Reacceleration
Computing utilization, AI commercialization and energy infrastructure improve enough to support stronger returns — broader earnings growth, better cash conversion and renewed capital formation.
# [Central] Two-Speed AI Economy
Infrastructure suppliers generate substantial profits while capital-intensive operators face selective financing — greater valuation dispersion, consolidation and migration of economic value. The current working scenario.
# Sovereign–Infrastructure Squeeze
Restrictive policy, energy disruption and rising borrowing costs weaken long-duration returns — wider credit premiums, postponed projects and deepening industrial divergence.

::takeaways Five Key Takeaways | for investors and boards
- Separate the financing of intelligence from its profitability: revenue and backlog do not eliminate debt-service, depreciation and utilization risk.
- Identify companies already capturing the dividend: Siemens Energy shows the physical foundations of computing can generate current cash.
- Treat the cost of capital as a strategic competitive variable amid persistent inflation and restrictive policy.
- Anticipate international reallocation: the $49.77B Asian outflow shows competitiveness and market attractiveness can diverge.
- Evaluate the Great Split through capital productivity — converting investment into sustainable profit, not merely expanding capacity.

::close
The next great redistribution of global wealth will not be decided by who builds the most intelligence — it will be decided by ==who captures its cash flows, who bears its financial risks, and who can compound its economic returns faster than the cost of capital.==

::signals What the model admitted | Tap each signal to expand the read.
# Samsung–Broadcom: an MOU of more than $200 billion
Samsung confirmed a memorandum of understanding with Broadcom estimated at more than $200 billion over five years through 2030. The collaboration covers high-bandwidth memory for AI accelerators, semiconductor production using Samsung's two-nanometre-and-below processes, and advanced 2.3D and 2.5D packaging.
signal: The agreement remains an MOU — recognised by execution probability and integration, not headline value.
# SK–Nvidia: a $500 billion-plus initiative anchored by a 2 GW AI factory
SK Group and Nvidia separately confirmed a $500 billion-plus initiative supported by letters of intent. The architecture includes a planned 2 GW AI factory in South Korea, using Nvidia's Vera Rubin accelerated-computing platform and SK Hynix HBM4 memory, with the first facility scheduled for 2027.
signal: Reuters reported that the broader South Korean agreements total approximately $950 billion — around $750 billion of SK-related partnerships plus Samsung's $200 billion Broadcom arrangement.

::section Institutional interpretation | National Velocity is an orchestration story.
::para
Today's evidence marks a qualitative transition. South Korea's earlier strength was concentrated in memory manufacturing. The new agreements extend its position across HBM → advanced foundry processes → packaging → accelerated computing → AI factories → sovereign and physical AI deployment. That sequence matters more than the nominal $950 billion headline. National Velocity is created when complementary assets are orchestrated into a productive system.

::para
The United States continues to control the leading accelerated-computing platforms and much of frontier-model development. South Korea is demonstrating a different form of advantage: it is becoming indispensable at the industrial layers through which intelligence must be manufactured and deployed. China remains the closest systemic challenger, but the DeepSeek funding report exposes an important distinction between technological achievement and capital continuity.

Is South Korea still supplying the AI economy — or is it beginning to ==design its industrial architecture?==

::takeaways Implications | What each reader should carry forward.
- **For investors:** the AI value chain is shifting toward integrated suppliers that combine memory, logic, packaging, networking and committed customers. Headline deal values should be discounted until contractual and construction milestones are visible.
- **For executives:** securing isolated components will no longer be enough. Competitive resilience will depend on long-duration partnerships spanning the complete AI-factory architecture.
- **For policymakers:** the South Korean model demonstrates that national advantage does not require leadership in every technological category. It requires the ability to orchestrate critical capabilities at industrial scale.

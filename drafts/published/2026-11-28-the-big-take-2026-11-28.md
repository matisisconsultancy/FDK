What if the next great global capital rotation is driven by the widening geographical divide between the economies financing the AI Supercycle and those capturing its industrial profits?

The AI Supercycle is not losing industrial momentum. Financial markets are becoming **more selective** about where its economic returns will ultimately materialize. This does not yet establish a durable structural rotation — but it strengthens the case for distinguishing where AI capital is invested from where the resulting profits are captured.

::signal
- **Asia captures the dividend:** Chinese electronics-sector profits rose ==+100%== year on year (Jan–Oct), contributing ~9.7 points to industrial-profit growth.
- **Capital turns selective:** Global equity funds shed $22.73B in the week to November 25 while European funds drew +$7.29B.
- **Korea confirms the cycle:** A Reuters survey forecasts November exports up 96.8% YoY, with semiconductor shipments +294.4% over November 1–20.
- **Europe stirs:** CEO confidence climbed to 47 from 40, and executives expect ~57% of R&D investment to stay in Europe.
- **Monetary friction:** ECB one-year inflation expectations rose to 3.5%, which could limit easing as Europe finances grid and industrial modernization.

::section Accelerated Computing: Asia Captures the Industrial Dividend | China and Korea lead
China's November 27 industrial-profit statistics provide the strongest new economic evidence: electronics contributed approximately 9.7 percentage points to overall industrial-profit growth (NBS, Jan–Oct 2026, YoY). A Reuters survey forecasts South Korean November exports rising **96.8%** year on year, with preliminary data showing semiconductor shipments up **294.4%** during November 1–20. Complete November figures are due December 1.

Economies supplying advanced components may generate current profits before the companies financing computing infrastructure realize mature returns. For Europe, semiconductor equipment, industrial automation, advanced materials and electricity technologies represent important opportunities for value capture.

::section The Great Split: Global Capital Becomes More Selective | Flows, prices and earnings diverge
LSEG Lipper reported global equity-fund withdrawals of **$22.73 billion** during the week ending November 25, yet the November 27 session delivered a substantial rebound — Nasdaq +2.31%, semiconductors +4.70%, Nvidia +0.80%.

Financial flows, share prices and industrial earnings are moving on different timetables. For investors, the priority is to distinguish temporary changes in risk appetite from sustainable differences in cash-flow generation and return on invested capital. The next rotation will depend on where technological demand produces durable economic value — not simply where equity prices recover fastest.

::section Europe: The Return of Industrial Confidence? | A cautiously encouraging signal
The latest European Round Table for Industry survey shows CEO confidence rising from **40 to 47** (neutral threshold: 50), with the share of CEOs increasing European investment plans climbing from 8% to 23%. Surveyed executives expect Europe to receive approximately **57%** of their companies' R&D investment over the next three years.

However, broader euro-area economic sentiment declined to 95.8 in November, below its long-term average. Europe's industrial leadership may be preparing for renewed investment, but the improvement is not yet broad-based. The opportunity lies in connecting advanced industrial capabilities with AI-native productivity improvements.

::section Europe 2030: The Financial Architecture Advances | Infrastructure outpaces public policy
The European Commission has cleared Deutsche Börse's proposed **€5.35 billion** acquisition of Allfunds, subject to outstanding approvals. Together with SIX's European Growth Program, this illustrates the gradual development of a more integrated European financial-market architecture: savings → institutional capital → deeper markets → potentially lower financing costs → productive investment.

The transmission is economically plausible, but no reduction in the cost of capital has yet been demonstrated. Meanwhile, negotiations over the EU's 2028–2034 budget remain unresolved. Europe's private financial infrastructure is advancing while its public capital-allocation framework continues facing delays.

::section Energy, Monetary Policy and Productivity | Inflation expectations tick higher
The ECB's November 27 Consumer Expectations Survey shows renewed pressure: one-year inflation expectations rose to **3.5%** from 3.1%, three-year to 3.0%, five-year steady at 2.5%. Higher near-term expectations could limit monetary easing as Europe seeks to finance electricity infrastructure and industrial modernization.

The physical constraint remains unchanged: no new material Europe-wide grid commissioning, operating AI Gigafactory award or independently verified aggregate AI productivity acceleration has been established today.

::pull Could the economies capturing the largest industrial profits from the AI Supercycle ultimately generate superior risk-adjusted returns to those financing the largest infrastructure investments?

::section The Geographical Return Asymmetry | A testable research hypothesis
NER-C proposes comparing the incremental economic return spread — the AI Value-Capture Spread (incremental ROIC minus WACC) — across semiconductor manufacturers, computing-infrastructure owners and industrial AI adopters by country, controlling for investment timing, depreciation, subsidies, exchange rates and semiconductor cycles. Research status: testable, not yet empirically validated.

::takeaways Five key takeaways | Where returns, not just investment, will concentrate
- **Investors:** Follow the geographical distribution of profits, not just the scale of announced AI investment.
- **Boards:** Evaluate AI initiatives through incremental returns, cash-flow conversion and financial resilience.
- **Financial institutions:** Monitor credit concentration, governance and customer-financing risks across the AI value chain.
- **Governments:** Competitive industrial specialization can generate AI-related wealth without leadership in frontier models.
- **European policymakers:** Integrate capital markets, competitive electricity and industrial AI deployment into a coherent strategy.

::close The next great capital rotation may be determined not by the economies investing the most in intelligence, but by those ==capturing the highest economic returns== from its production and deployment.

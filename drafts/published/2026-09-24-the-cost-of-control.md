U.S. five-year Treasury yields are above 5%. That is the key benchmark: roughly 5% is what investors can earn on medium-term U.S. government debt before taking corporate, execution or equity risk. SoftBank is paying up to 9.75% to finance OpenAI. Microsoft is committing more than $10 billion to Gulf AI infrastructure. Meta is building proprietary hardware to reduce platform dependence. Washington and Beijing are negotiating over rare earths, technology access and AI governance.

The central thesis is simple: as AI becomes more capital-intensive, value shifts from those that merely use intelligence to those that can own its critical infrastructure and still generate returns above the cost of capital.

AI is becoming easier to access. The infrastructure required to monetize it is becoming harder—and more expensive—to own. That moves the competitive test from AI capability to economic capture: ==who owns the critical layers, who keeps the margin, and who earns ROIC above WACC?==

::stats
5% :: Five-year Treasury yield — the risk-free reference rate
9.75% :: Dollar coupon SoftBank pays to finance OpenAI
$11.1B :: SoftBank bond issuance for OpenAI
$10B+ :: Microsoft commitment to Gulf AI and cloud infrastructure

::pull The significance of a 5% five-year Treasury yield is not that every project must earn 5%. It means investors already have a roughly 5% sovereign alternative before taking additional risk.

::signals Five Signals | From capability to economic capture
# AI Finance — From growth to return discipline
SoftBank is issuing $11.1 billion of bonds to finance OpenAI, with dollar coupons reaching 9.75%. AI capital is no longer cheap. The U.S. five-year Treasury yield above 5% is the risk-free reference rate for medium-term dollar capital; a corporate borrower or AI infrastructure project must then pay an additional premium for credit risk, execution risk, illiquidity and equity risk.
So **5% Treasury ≠ 5% WACC** — and ==ROIC must exceed WACC==.
signal: Return on Invested Capital must exceed the Weighted Average Cost of Capital.

# Agentic AI — From models to distribution
Meta is moving into proprietary AI hardware to reduce dependence on third-party platforms. Value is migrating from model capability toward the customer interface. Agents that execute transactions shift economics toward whoever owns identity, distribution and checkout.
The chain runs: **agent → transaction → distribution → margin**.
signal: Favor platforms positioned closest to monetization.

# Infrastructure — From capacity to resilience
Microsoft is investing more than $10 billion across Gulf AI and cloud infrastructure. AI infrastructure is becoming critical infrastructure. Secure power, redundancy and connectivity determine uptime; uptime determines utilisation and revenue.
The chain runs: **capacity → resilience → uptime → cash flow**.
signal: Resilient infrastructure should command a premium over nominal capacity alone.

# U.S.–China — From decoupling to leverage
Rare earths, AI governance, tariffs and market access are converging into one negotiation. Strategic dependencies are becoming bargaining instruments. Control of constrained inputs and market access increases negotiating power without requiring full separation.
The chain runs: **dependency → leverage → pricing power**.
signal: Assets located at geopolitical bottlenecks acquire strategic as well as financial value.

# Europe — From ambition to investable economics
Europe's proposed AI gigafactories require materially more recurring demand than current public procurement can support. Europe's constraint is not simply access to compute — it is the ability to aggregate demand, power and capital at continental scale. Subsidized capacity without sufficient utilisation creates weak economics; infrastructure serving existing industrial demand can monetize earlier and with greater visibility.
==Europe: infrastructure before frontier.== Do not buy "European AI" indiscriminately — favor the layers where Europe already possesses industrial depth and monetizable demand.
signal: Europe's investment edge is industrial conversion, not model imitation.

::patterns The Great Split | Control Owners vs. Control Renters
# Control Owners
Own critical inputs. Secure energy. Own distribution. Finance at scale. Retain pricing power.
# Control Renters
Buy critical inputs. Depend on external infrastructure. Rent distribution. Borrow at higher cost. Absorb higher input costs.

::section Investor Action Box | One-line verdict
Prioritize economic quality: **pricing power → cash flow → funded optionality → dependency → stranded scale.**

**1 — Core overweight: pricing-power assets.** Semiconductors, HBM, memory, advanced networking, grids, firm power and resilient data centres. They sit at unavoidable parts of the AI production chain where demand is strongest and substitution is hardest. Portfolio role: core exposure to the AI Supercycle.

**2 — Selective overweight: cash-flow converters.** Agentic distribution, AI security, enterprise deployment and infrastructure-finance platforms. They turn AI adoption directly into transactions, recurring revenues or financing income. Portfolio role: monetize the build-out.

**3 — Selective only: funded optionality.** Frontier models, new compute architectures and emerging AI infrastructure. Own only where there are strong balance sheets, secured customers, visible utilisation, credible unit economics and a clear path to ROIC > WACC. Portfolio role: capture upside without underwriting unlimited duration.

**4 — Underweight: dependency.** Uncontracted compute, leveraged infrastructure, weak intermediaries and businesses dependent on third-party power, models, distribution or financing. They pay the economics captured elsewhere in the stack.

**5 — Avoid: stranded scale.** No secured power. No committed demand. No financing edge. No pricing power. Large announced capacity does not equal economic value — ==scale without returns = capital destruction==.

::section Europe — Portfolio Bias | Overweight the enablers, be selective on the builders
Favor electrification, grids, industrial automation, semiconductor equipment, secure data-centre infrastructure and financial intermediation. Demand a much higher proof threshold from stand-alone AI factories, national champions and capacity built primarily around subsidies rather than customers.

Europe does not need to win every layer of AI to create shareholder value. It needs to monetize the layers where its existing industrial position already gives it an advantage.

::takeaways Key takeaways | The third race is about returns
- The first AI race was about models, the second about capacity, the third is about returns.
- 5% Treasury ≠ 5% WACC — projects must clear a real, risk-adjusted hurdle.
- Value accrues to Control Owners of inputs, energy, distribution and financing.
- Europe's strongest opportunity is industrial conversion, not frontier imitation.

::close Europe becomes investable when AI stops being a sovereignty slogan and becomes an ==industrial earnings story==.

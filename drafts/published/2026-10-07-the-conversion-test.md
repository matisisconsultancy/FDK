Advanced-wafer capacity remains tight. HBM remains strategically scarce. Grid access increasingly determines when compute becomes productive. Financing structures are expanding because the capital burden of deployment is rising.

The benchmark reduces the current AI cycle to one chain — **Scarcity → Investment → Financing → Utilization → Returns** — and three financial tests: revenue growth greater than capital-commitment growth; FCF growth greater than fixed-obligation growth; and incremental ROIC greater than incremental cost of capital.

This is why Microsoft and TSMC remain the two structural leaders; why Amazon, Micron and Synopsys remain accumulation candidates; and why Anthropic can remain a Global Velocity Leader at 97.4 while ranking outside the Top 10 at #11.

::section The Velocity Edge — The Next Bottleneck | Part II
The AI race is no longer only about securing compute. It is about making the system economically productive. Microsoft and TSMC remain the structural anchors. Amazon is the benchmark's most important capital-architecture experiment. Schneider is becoming a defining Industrial-AI platform. Micron and Synopsys occupy increasingly valuable memory and design layers. Broadcom is extending from custom silicon into ecosystem finance.

Nvidia remains the dominant accelerated-computing platform, but its investment case increasingly depends on whether customer economics can sustain the infrastructure cycle.

::section What's Next | Likely sources of the next benchmark moves
The next benchmark moves are most likely to come from Amazon (FCF and incremental ROIC), Schneider (PTC execution and debt reduction), Micron (HBM pricing durability), Broadcom / Anthropic (financing quality), and Power (grid-related deployment delays).

Executive and policy implications point the same way. Boards should require one measurable chain for every major AI commitment — **Capacity → Utilization → Productivity → Revenue → Margin → FCF → ROIC** — owning what creates durable differentiation, securing access to what is scarce, and financing commoditizing assets externally when economics are superior. The relevant production system runs Semiconductors → Memory → Networks → Power → Data Centers → Software → Industrial Applications → Capital: policy should target system completeness, not isolated capacity.

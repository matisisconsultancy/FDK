We spend the week measuring the future in trillions of dollars, gigawatts, compute, interest rates and geopolitical power.

On weekends, we will ask a different question: what kind of world are we actually building?

We begin on the shore of Lake Zurich, at Le Corbusier's final building.

Small in scale, radical in conception, the Pavillon Le Corbusier reminds us of something easily forgotten in an age obsessed with AI infrastructure: technological revolutions eventually become cultural revolutions.

::pull WHY IT MATTERS
Intelligence without imagination produces efficiency—not civilization.

::section The Argument | why culture follows technology
The twentieth century transformed steel, concrete, electricity and the automobile into new ways of living.

AI will do the same.

But intelligence without imagination produces efficiency—not civilization.

That is why today we launch **The Velocity Edge — Life & Arts**.

Every weekend, we will explore where the future becomes human: architecture, art, literature, poetry, philosophy, psychology, interiors, design, cities, fashion, food and travel.

::section An Open-Source Community of Ideas | we want readers to build it with us
Think of Life & Arts as an open-source community of ideas: economists alongside poets; engineers alongside architects; investors alongside artists; psychologists alongside entrepreneurs; scientists alongside writers.

Challenge an argument. Send a poem. Recommend a building. Introduce an artist. Suggest a book. Share a photograph. Tell us about an idea we have missed.

We are not looking for consensus. We are looking for *collision*.

Because genuinely new ideas often emerge not within disciplines, but between them.

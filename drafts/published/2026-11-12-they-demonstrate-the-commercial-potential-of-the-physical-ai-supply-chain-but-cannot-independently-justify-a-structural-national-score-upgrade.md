The global AI investment cycle is revealing a consequential financial divide: demand for intelligence keeps expanding, but economic returns are increasingly shifting toward the physical infrastructure required to sustain it.

Three developments define today's Global Velocity assessment. Germany's **Siemens Energy** reported record orders of **€68.1 billion** and pre-tax free cash flow of **€8.8 billion**. Japan's producer-price inflation accelerated to **7.1%**. South Korea announced another major semiconductor milestone even as youth and manufacturing employment weakened and foreign investors withdrew capital.

The hidden pattern is the growing divergence between technological investment, financial returns and the diffusion of prosperity across the broader economy. Evidence cut-off: November 12, 2026.

::section Germany — Physical AI Infrastructure Becomes a Profit Centre
Siemens Energy's fiscal 2026 results: orders up 18.8% on a comparable basis to **€68.1 billion**, revenue **€44.2 billion**. Profit before special items more than doubled to roughly €5.5 billion, with margin rising from **6.0% to 12.4%**. Pre-tax free cash flow rose from **€4.66 billion to €8.80 billion**, and the order backlog reached **€164 billion**. The United States accounted for €27.7 billion in orders, up 70.8% on a comparable basis.

For fiscal 2027, management expects 14–16% comparable revenue growth and a 14–16% margin — forecasts, not realised returns. The €164bn backlog is not cash already received or guaranteed profit.

GVI implication: positive company-level Enterprise Transformation and Smart Capital signals for Germany.

::section Japan — Industrial Inflation Becomes the Next Capital Constraint
October producer-price inflation rose to **7.1%** year over year, from 6.8%, up 0.7% month over month. Yen-based import inflation reached **24.8%**, versus 23.8% in September; petroleum and coal products rose 15.9% and chemicals 15.5%. Manufacturers with limited pricing power face margin compression, while more persistent wholesale inflation could strengthen expectations of further Bank of Japan tightening.

GVI implication: negative Smart Capital input-cost momentum — high confidence in the observation, moderate confidence in persistence.

::section South Korea — Capital Formation Outpaces Employment Diffusion
Samsung's 6 trillion won HBM packaging facility in Asan is expected to be followed by roughly 40 trillion won in advanced-equipment investment, with the new line commencing in June 2028 — anticipated spending, not completed investment. Yet October employment rose **139,000** while manufacturing fell 7,000 and employment among those aged 15–29 fell **196,000**; the youth employment rate dropped 1.7 points to 42.9%. Foreign investors withdrew a net **$14.59 billion** from Korean equities in October — portfolio flows, not productive-investment withdrawals.

GVI implication: positive Intelligence Infrastructure execution; negative employment-diffusion and portfolio-flow signals.

::section China — Export Strength Masks Weaker Domestic Absorption
Total vehicle sales fell **9.2%** year over year in October, after an 8.4% decline in September. Full-year vehicle exports are projected above 11 million units while domestic passenger demand remains weak. Industrial scale can grow faster than domestic profitability and income.

GVI implication: negative domestic Enterprise Transformation, partly offset by prior positive export momentum. No new national numerical revision.

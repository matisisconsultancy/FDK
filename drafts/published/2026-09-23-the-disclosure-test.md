The most consequential development is no longer another takeover rumor. It is Consob.

Market report — not yet confirmed by Consob: Italian reporting says the securities regulator has widened its examination of MPS beyond the economics of the Banco BPM and Banca Generali counter-offers, requesting communications between certain directors and the bank and examining whether the board and the market received complete information on time, with particular attention to the Generali relationship.

That matters because the 29 October MPS shareholder meeting is no longer simply a referendum on whether Luigi Lovaglio can build a third banking pole. It is becoming a test of whether the defensive architecture is legally clean, procedurally robust and executable after a possible change of control.

::signals What Changed In The Last 24 Hours | Four developments reset the map
# Consob deepens the MPS scrutiny
According to reporting cited by Alliance News, Consob has sought additional material concerning communications between MPS directors and the bank, following earlier requests concerning the Banco BPM and Banca Generali offers, the passivity rule and the relationship with Generali. This remains reported regulatory activity, rather than a new public Consob ruling.

The question is shifting from whether MPS's counter-offers are strategically credible to whether they are procedurally unassailable. The distinction is critical because an Intesa takeover of MPS could occur before completion of the MPS counter-offers. MPS has maintained the offers would remain irrevocable after a change of control; Intesa has challenged that interpretation.
signal: The battle is now over procedure, not strategy.

# MPS/Mediobanca continues to touch BPM stock
MPS's subsidiary Mediobanca disclosed another small market purchase of Banco BPM shares on 22 September: 55,000 shares at an average €16.2817. The absolute size is economically immaterial and should not be interpreted as stake-building, but it confirms that the MPS offer remains operationally active rather than merely rhetorical.
signal: The signal is executional, not financial.

# Banca Generali simplifies its perimeter
Saxo Bank completed the acquisition of Banca Generali's remaining 49% of BG Saxo, becoming sole owner and renaming the company Saxo Italia. The transaction implements an agreement already reached earlier, so it should not be read as a response to the MPS bid. But it leaves Banca Generali with a marginally cleaner corporate perimeter at exactly the moment when its ownership is under strategic review.
signal: A simpler perimeter modestly reduces transaction complexity.

# The alliance premium retreats
Banco BPM's 4.3% Monday rally on speculation about a possible UniCredit–Crédit Agricole arrangement was partially reversed yesterday. The lack of public confirmation is beginning to matter.
signal: Show us the alliance — or give back the premium.

::section The Decisive Issue | The passivity rule becomes the real battleground
The Italian Banking Risiko has moved through five stages: Assets → Capital → Time → Governance → Regulatory Validity.

MPS can theoretically win shareholder approval for its Banco BPM and Banca Generali offers on 29 October. But the decisive issue is increasingly what happens afterwards if Intesa succeeds in acquiring control of MPS before those transactions close. MPS says the offers remain binding. Intesa believes a new controlling shareholder and board could reconsider them. Consob is probing the information architecture around the transactions.

::pull THE MPS DEFENCE BECOMES STRONGER IF IT BECOMES IRREVOCABLE — BUT THE MORE IRREVOCABLE IT BECOMES, THE MORE REGULATORY SCRUTINY MATTERS.

::patterns Updated Strategic Map | The eight players and their standings
# [STRONG OW] Intesa Sanpaolo
Intesa's offer remains straightforward: 1.6 Intesa shares plus €1 cash for each MPS share, conditional on reaching at least 66.67%, subject to waiver, with substantial branch disposals already designed to address competition issues. Every additional procedural question surrounding MPS's defence increases the relative value of Intesa's simpler architecture. The advantage is no longer only capital strength — it is transactional simplicity.

# [HIGH CONVEXITY] MPS
A credible industrial thesis survives: MPS + Mediobanca + Banco BPM + Banca Generali, with exchange ratios of 1.567 MPS shares per Banco BPM share and 6.958 per Banca Generali share. But the reported Consob inquiry adds a fourth layer — procedural risk — atop industrial, shareholder and execution risk.

# [EVENT OW] Banco BPM
Closed Tuesday at €16.09, down 1.74%, but still 2.38% above last Friday's official level — the Monday rumor premium has not disappeared. Three strategic destinations remain: MPS, Crédit Agricole, or a negotiated structure potentially involving UniCredit. That creates scarcity.

# [STRATEGIC OW] Crédit Agricole
Controls 29.3% of Banco BPM. Reports carried by Reuters said the French bank considered the MPS proposal unattractive and preferred a Banco BPM–Crédit Agricole Italia combination; neither party confirmed. Its greatest asset remains the ability not to choose yet.

# [OVERWEIGHT] UniCredit
Shareholders formally approved up to €5 billion equivalent of convertible AT1 instruments (97.39%) and up to 10.603 million new shares linked to Commerzbank-referenced Total Return Swaps (97.70%). This gives Andrea Orcel flexibility across two fronts — Germany, converting Commerzbank ownership into governance, and Italy, preserving Banco BPM optionality.

# [GOVERNANCE] Commerzbank
Germany has moved from preventing UniCredit's advance to negotiating safeguards. Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil has told UniCredit that Commerzbank should remain listed, headquartered in Frankfurt and committed to the Mittelstand. The debate is no longer whether UniCredit can own Commerzbank, but what price in governance it must pay to control it.

# [OVERWEIGHT] Generali
Fell 3.3% to €44.26 within a weak session, with no public evidence tying the move to a new transaction. Its structural position is unchanged: an MPS/Mediobanca strategic asset, controlling shareholder of Banca Generali, an antitrust variable for Intesa, and central to MPS's proposed €4 billion extraordinary distribution.

# [EVENT POSITIVE] Banca Generali
The BG Saxo disposal marginally simplifies the perimeter; the stock closed at €65.95, +1.46%. Its transaction spread is materially wider than Banco BPM's — improving relative asymmetry.

::section Capital-Markets Reaction And Valuation | Three signals from yesterday's close
**Banco BPM — the spread is narrowing.** MPS closed at €11.85 and Banco BPM at €16.09. Because the proposed €1.208 extraordinary MPS distribution is reserved to existing MPS shareholders before settlement, the cleaner comparison uses an adjusted MPS value of ~€10.64. Applying the 1.567 ratio gives an adjusted implied Banco BPM value of ~€16.68 vs €16.09 market — roughly a 3.6% residual premium, substantially tighter than earlier in the contest.

**Banca Generali — the execution discount remains large.** The same adjusted MPS currency and the 6.958 ratio imply ~€74.05 vs €65.95 market, approximately a 12.3% spread. That is not an endorsement of the transaction; it is the market's price for execution uncertainty — and it is large.

**MPS — the Intesa offer is almost fully priced.** At Intesa's €6.738 reference and the 1.6 shares + €1 cash structure, the offer mechanically values MPS at about €11.78, versus €11.85 market — only ~0.6% above the mechanical value. The market is pricing very little pure Intesa deal risk, while preserving a small option value for MPS's alternative path.

::section Three Scenarios | Probabilities updated
**1 — Continental Bifurcation | 40% | unchanged.** Intesa completes MPS after acceptable remedies; UniCredit converts Commerzbank ownership into negotiated control; Banco BPM eventually moves toward Crédit Agricole or another negotiated architecture; Generali remains independent. The cleanest structural outcome.

**2 — MPS Coalition Third Pole | 24% | ↑ 1 point.** MPS obtains the 29 October mandate and proceeds with Banco BPM and Banca Generali. The slight increase reflects continued operational execution and a modestly simpler Banca Generali perimeter; only one point because Crédit Agricole resistance and reported Consob scrutiny remain substantial obstacles.

**3 — Negotiated Realignment | 36% | ↓ 1 point.** The existing offers force a multi-party settlement — some combination of Intesa–MPS, Crédit Agricole–Banco BPM, UniCredit–Commerzbank, an independent Generali and a revised role for Banco BPM. The probability slips because the reported UniCredit–Crédit Agricole dialogue has produced no public confirmation and some Monday premium reversed.

::section The Hidden Pattern | A battle over capital-allocation legitimacy
The superficial interpretation is still M&A: who buys MPS, who gets Banco BPM, who controls Banca Generali, who wins Commerzbank. But each transaction asks the same deeper question: who has the legitimacy to allocate the next euro of capital?

Intesa argues that scale plus execution earns that right. MPS argues that strategic independence plus coalition-building earns it. UniCredit argues that Continental Scale earns it. Crédit Agricole argues that patient ownership and optionality earn it. Governments argue that national interest gives them veto rights. Brussels argues those vetoes must become narrower.

Consob's reported intervention is therefore not peripheral. It goes directly to the heart of the system: capital allocation requires not only money — but procedural legitimacy. Meanwhile the European equation is becoming **Continental Scale + National Safeguards + Supranational Supervision** — not the abolition of national banking systems, but their federation through capital.

::takeaways What To Watch | Next 24–72 hours
- **Consob first** — any official confirmation, further disclosure request or clarification on board communications, Generali and the passivity rule immediately changes MPS execution risk.
- **MPS second** — whether the bank responds publicly to the reported widening and whether Mediobanca continues disclosed Banco BPM purchases.
- **Crédit Agricole–UniCredit third** — the Monday report now needs evidence; a denial removes BPM optionality, confirmation transforms the map.
- **Banco BPM fourth** — €16 is becoming the critical market reference; a renewed move above Monday's highs without a catalyst would signal an emerging competitive process.
- **Banca Generali fifth** — the BG Saxo disposal is complete; the next event is Generali's formal strategic stance on MPS's proposal.
- **Berlin sixth** — any concrete agreement on Commerzbank headquarters, listing, employment and supervisory-board governance moves UniCredit from economic to executable control.

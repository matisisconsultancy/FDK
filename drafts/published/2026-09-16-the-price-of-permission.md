The Federal Reserve decides tonight with the U.S. 10-year Treasury at 4.99% and markets pricing a 92% probability of a rate increase. Brent remains near $108 despite a huge U.S. inventory build. Yet Anthropic has just secured access to a 2.16-GW Australian AI campus devoted to inference, SK Hynix is discussing producing memory in the United States with Intel, and New York wants data-centre developers to invest at least $1 million in local communities for every megawatt they consume.

The AI Supercycle is not running out of capital. It is discovering that capital alone is no longer sufficient. The Great Split is becoming ==PERMISSIONED CAPACITY== versus **STRANDED SCALE**.

If the Next Economy increasingly requires permission from central banks, governments, communities and regulators before capital can become productive capacity, is speed still the ultimate advantage — or is the scarce asset now the ability to scale *with permission*? The underlying principle is becoming clear: **capital creates optionality; permission creates capacity.**

::signals Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy | Tonight's cost-of-capital audit
# Tonight's Fed decision determines whether 5% becomes a ceiling — or a floor
The U.S. 10-year yield is around 4.99% after breaching 5% on Tuesday. Futures imply a 92.4% probability of a 25-basis-point increase. The S&P 500 lost about 0.5% Tuesday, its second consecutive decline. Morgan Stanley now expects a hike today *and* another quarter-point move in December, citing slow disinflation, energy prices and AI-related investment demand.

The policy rate matters, but the dot plot matters more. A single hike is insurance against an energy shock; a *sequence* changes the capital regime. The decisive equation becomes **productivity velocity vs. discount-rate velocity** — if AI cash flows rise faster than the cost of capital, the Supercycle survives 5% money.
signal: Tonight is the first full cost-of-capital audit of the AI Supercycle.

# Anthropic's 2.16-GW deal says inference is accelerating even as frontier AI debates slowing
Anthropic has agreed to lease capacity at a proposed 2.16-GW campus near Brisbane, expected to begin operating in 2027 and dedicated to inference rather than training. The project remains subject to Australian Foreign Investment Review Board approval. It arrives days after CEO Dario Amodei called for frontier developers to slow model-capability gains.

The market assumed slower models means fewer chips and less infrastructure. Anthropic is telling us the opposite: **frontier velocity can slow while deployment velocity accelerates.** The centre of gravity is shifting from training the next model toward running intelligence continuously — rewarding low latency, energy efficiency, networking and utilisation.
signal: This is the transition from AI discovery to AI industrialisation.

# HBM sovereignty may be coming to Ohio
SK Hynix is in exploratory discussions with Intel about manufacturing memory chips in the U.S. for the first time — potentially leasing part of Intel's Ohio complex or forming a venture with major cloud customers. No decision has been made. Washington has threatened tariffs of up to 100% on some South Korean and Taiwanese producers unless they build in the U.S.; Seoul regards advanced memory as strategically sensitive.

Phase I of semiconductor sovereignty was bringing leading-edge logic home. Phase II becomes **bringing the memory stack home too** — because AI systems cannot be sovereign if only one part of the accelerated-computing architecture is domestic. The complete stack now spans processors, HBM, networking, packaging, electricity and data centres.
signal: Bottlenecks attract industrial policy.

# Data-centre communities are starting to charge for the externality
New York Governor Kathy Hochul is recommending municipalities seek at least $1 million per megawatt of utility demand from proposed data centres. New York earlier became the first U.S. state to impose a moratorium on large new facilities; only about one-third of Americans approve of the current pace of construction, per a Reuters/Ipsos poll. Texas is weighing tougher water-use disclosure.

A new line item is appearing in underwriting: **social licence.** At $1 million per megawatt, a 1-GW project implies $1 billion of potential community investment. Infrastructure won't stop — it will migrate toward jurisdictions where power is abundant, communities accept development and permitting is predictable.
signal: Economic geography is becoming an AI variable.

# Oil is down this morning — but the physical system remains broken
Brent has slipped to roughly $108 after industry data showed an unexpected 7.1-million-barrel crude build, against expectations for a draw. But Saudi Arabia has suspended loadings at Yanbu following damage to the East–West pipeline and is offering additional crude to Asia via ship-to-ship transfers off Oman. Only four vessel transits crossed Hormuz on Tuesday, versus a 10-day average of 18. European diesel futures hit a record.

An inventory build is a stock variable; the energy shock is a network problem. Saudi Arabia is redesigning the route — pipeline failure to Yanbu interruption to Oman transshipment to higher complexity. The same law that governs AI infrastructure now applies: capacity must possess route optionality, political permission, physical security and redundancy.
signal: The cost of resilience is becoming part of the cost of production.

::patterns Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines | The Permission Stack emerges
# The AI slowdown debate is really about where intelligence is produced
Leaders debate slowing frontier progress even as Anthropic commits to one of the largest inference campuses ever proposed. There is no contradiction once the Supercycle splits into two layers — *frontier intelligence* (ever-more-capable models) and *industrial intelligence* (deploying existing capabilities billions of times). Capital may rotate from the first toward the second. The Supercycle does not contract; it broadens.

# The new bottleneck is the Permission Stack
Every major development this morning requires approval from a different institution: the Fed prices capital, governments shape semiconductor geography, communities influence data-centre economics, energy regulators control grid access, safety regulators govern autonomy, and foreign-investment boards decide whether projects proceed. Permission now sits between money and capacity.

# Governments are competing on the price of permission
Canada offers the counterexample to New York. Prime Minister Mark Carney's permanent Productivity Mega Deduction lets companies expense most new capital immediately — cutting the marginal effective tax rate on new investment from roughly 13% to 6.4% as Ottawa courts more than C$1 trillion of foreign capital. New York pays more for the social licence; Canada pays less for the capital licence. Capital will compare both.

# European banking consolidation has crossed a threshold
Germany no longer asks how to stop UniCredit. Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil has told Andrea Orcel his conditions: Commerzbank should remain listed, keep its Frankfurt base and continue financing the Mittelstand. A combined group would hold over €1.3 trillion of assets. Europe has moved from resisting continental scale to **negotiating the conditions of continental scale.** Banking Union is becoming industrial policy.

# Physical AI is discovering the same constraint
Tesla began limited commercial Cybercab deployment in Austin on September 3. NHTSA has now ordered Tesla to explain by September 30 how the steering-wheel-free vehicle complies with federal safety rules. Being technically capable of deploying is not the same as having permission to deploy at scale: autonomy to certification to liability to approval to fleet deployment. Regulation becomes part of the production architecture.

::section The Great Split — September 16 | Permissioned Capacity vs. Stranded Scale
The Great Split has moved through several layers: AI versus non-AI; Bottleneck Owners versus Buyers; Returns Above the Hurdle Rate versus Capital Below It; Governable Scale versus Uncontrolled Velocity; Underwritable Velocity versus Unpriced Acceleration. Today the next distinction becomes visible: ==Permissioned Capacity== versus **Stranded Scale**.

Permissioned Capacity has secure power, grid access, regulatory approval, local acceptance, financing, strategic alignment and customers. Stranded Scale has land, capital, ambition — and an architectural rendering. At 5% money, the market will increasingly distinguish between the two.

::section Likely Market and Competitive Implications | Do not value announced capacity as operating capacity
The immediate configuration is cautious rather than disorderly. MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan is modestly positive, Japan slightly weaker, U.S. futures nearly flat ahead of the Fed. The dollar sits near a two-week high.

Beneath the indices, capital should keep moving toward assets whose permission risk is resolved or structurally advantaged: HBM, networking, grid-connected data centres, firm power, nuclear, transformers, secure digital infrastructure and banks able to finance very large programs. Australia strengthens its case through land, energy and policy ambition; Canada competes on taxation; the U.S. uses industrial policy to bring memory closer to domestic AI; the Nordics retain power availability and predictability.

The vulnerable assets look different: unpermitted data-centre projects, developments waiting years for grid connections, highly leveraged long-duration investments, frontier-AI valuations unsupported by deployment cash flow, and industrial businesses unable to pass energy costs through prices. The new allocation rule is becoming: **do not value announced capacity as operating capacity.**

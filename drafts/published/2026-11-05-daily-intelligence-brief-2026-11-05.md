The AI Supercycle is entering a more sophisticated phase. Competition increasingly depends not only on access to computing capacity, but on the creditworthiness, contractual structures and technologies that make that capacity financially viable.

Three developments on November 4 provide unusually clear evidence: Google agreed to provide contractual credit support for a data-centre leased to Anthropic; Arm reported 27% revenue growth; and the European Commission launched a €525 million semiconductor-design initiative. Credit is making investment possible, intellectual property is capturing value, and industrial policy is building missing capabilities.

The next stage will be determined by the financial architecture supporting intelligence: ==who provides capital, who assumes risk, who owns essential technologies and who ultimately captures the cash flows==.

::signal
- **Google is backstopping credit**, not buying capacity — supporting TeraWulf financing tied to ~$19B in Anthropic lease commitments over 20 years.
- **Arm posted record revenue** of $1.44B, up 27%, with licensing up 50% and data-centre royalties roughly doubling.
- **Europe launched a €525M** Chip Design Platform to address a segment where European firms hold less than 1% of global revenue.
- **Qualcomm revenue fell 8%** to $10.32B, but automotive jumped 89% to $1.99B as handsets dropped 26%.
- **Guarantees redistribute risk**, they do not remove it — financing benefits and project returns remain unproven.
- **The Great Split is deepening** between those who combine technology with credible financing and durable returns, and those who cannot.

::section The Credit Architecture of AI | Google, Anthropic and TeraWulf
TeraWulf entered credit-support agreements with Google for financing its Justified Data Campus in Hawesville, Kentucky — a ~401 MW project backed by a 20-year Anthropic lease, with phased delivery from late 2027 to Q2 2028, built under a new Fluor EPC agreement.

Google is not purchasing $19 billion of capacity, nor guaranteeing the full lease value. The SEC filing describes credit support covering specified Anthropic obligations, with options to assume leases or pay termination amounts following qualifying defaults.

The evolution matters: one major technology company's balance sheet is being used to strengthen infrastructure serving another AI company. Lenders can evaluate the combination — land, power, demand and credit support — rather than the developer alone. But as such structures proliferate, the aggregate contingent commitments connecting technology companies, developers and customers become less visible.

::pull Credit support strengthens the financing proposition; it does not establish that a project has secured funding or will generate satisfactory returns.

::section Accelerated Computing | Arm demonstrates the power of IP
Arm reported record fiscal Q2 revenue of $1.44 billion (+27%), with licensing of $773 million (+50%) and royalties of $670 million (+8%). Data-centre royalties roughly doubled. Partners shipped more than ten billion Arm-based chips in the quarter.

The company estimates its architecture represented more than half of hyperscaler server-chip deployments in 2025, up from ~15% in 2024, and expects over 60% by end-2026 — forward-looking estimates. Management expressed greater confidence in up to $2 billion of AGI CPU revenue across fiscal 2027–2028, a target rather than realized revenue.

Arm monetizes intellectual property through licensing and royalties, offering capital efficiency. Its move into its own processor products introduces new manufacturing and execution risks even as it captures more value directly.

::section Industrial and Physical Computing | Qualcomm's shifting geography
Qualcomm reported fiscal Q4 revenue of $10.32 billion (−8%), with sharply divergent businesses: handsets $5.14 billion (−26%), automotive $1.99 billion (+89%), IoT $1.89 billion (+4%). It reaffirmed a ~$5 billion data-centre revenue objective for fiscal 2027 and expects non-handset revenue to expand more than 60% — management forecasts, not achieved results.

Automotive growth is evidence of expanding semiconductor demand, not proof of equivalent AI-attributable productivity. No new independently verified European economy-wide productivity data establish a material acceleration from industrial AI deployment.

::section Europe's Most Consequential Development | The €525M design initiative
The European Commission launched the European Chips Design Platform, mobilizing €525 million — approximately €440 million in grants and €85 million for the platform and infrastructure — to support firms that currently hold less than 1% of global chip-design revenue.

A design company requires less physical capital than a fabrication facility while potentially controlling valuable intellectual property. Europe should not measure semiconductor sovereignty solely by installed manufacturing capacity, but also by the economic value created through European-owned designs. The €525 million is meaningful policy action; its ultimate economic return remains to be established.

::patterns Three financial scenarios | What to monitor through early 2027
# Productive Credit Expansion
Credit support improves terms, deployment proceeds on schedule and demand supports cash generation — broadening participation in the Supercycle.

# Selective Financial Concentration
The best financing terms increasingly depend on access to investment-grade sponsors and customers, favouring those with stronger contractual relationships.

# Contingent Risk Accumulation
Infrastructure commitments expand faster than cash generation, and interconnected guarantees draw greater scrutiny to counterparty concentration and financial-stress transmission.

::takeaways Five key takeaways | For investors, boards and policymakers
- The financing architecture of AI is evolving beyond conventional debt and equity, as Google's TeraWulf support shows.
- Intellectual property remains a key source of value capture, reinforced by Arm's earnings even as it expands into silicon.
- Embedded and physical computing create new growth, shown by Qualcomm's automotive performance, though productivity benefits remain unproven.
- Europe took a meaningful step toward strengthening semiconductor design with its €525 million initiative.
- Capital productivity remains the ultimate test: advantage counts only when it yields sustainable economic returns.

::close The future of intelligence will be built with capital. ==Its enduring financial winners will be those who can underwrite the risk, control the technology and capture the returns.==

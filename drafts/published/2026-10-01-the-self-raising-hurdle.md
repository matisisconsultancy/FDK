The U.S. 10-year Treasury touched 5.34%, its highest since 2002, despite softer inflation having reduced expectations of another immediate Fed increase. Brent moved back above $100 after China suspended fuel exports. Yet global manufacturing is strengthening on AI-related capital spending, while software and enterprise AI continued to attract capital.

That is tonight's decisive contradiction: **AI promises tomorrow's deflation — but its construction is creating today's inflation.**

::stats
5.3445% :: U.S. 10Y peak — 24-year capital hurdle
~$101 :: Brent — physical inflation returns
52.9 :: Eurozone Manufacturing PMI — strongest since May 2022
$42B :: Broadcom → Anthropic financing — the AI credit loop

::section The Five Signals | Part I
AI now operates on both sides of the economic equation. The front end sees a Fed pause; the long end sees persistent capital scarcity. Markets have spent years pricing the productivity chain. The bond market is forcing them to price the construction chain.

::signals The Five Signals | AI on both sides of the equation
# 5.34% changes everything
The 10-year briefly reached 5.3445% before easing toward 5.27%. The 2-year fell toward 4.81%. The front end sees a Fed pause; the long end sees persistent capital scarcity.
signal: The most important market price is no longer Fed funds — it is the long-term cost of capital.

# AI is already driving the industrial cycle
Factory activity strengthened across Europe and much of Asia in September, with AI-related capital goods lifting the eurozone manufacturing PMI to 52.9, its strongest since May 2022. South Korean and Taiwanese manufacturing also benefited from semiconductor and AI demand.
signal: AI spending is propagating beyond hyperscale computing into capital goods.

# Energy has returned as the physical constraint
Brent jumped above $101 after China suspended most fuel exports, adding to an already tight diesel market affected by Russia and Middle East disruptions.
signal: Diesel transmits directly into freight, agriculture, construction and industrial costs.

# Markets prefer AI productivity to AI duration
Wall Street weakened under the bond selloff, but software rallied strongly after Accenture's results. The market is distinguishing businesses that monetize AI now from infrastructure whose economics sit years in the future.
signal: Near-term cash conversion is beating fixed-duration exposure.

# AI finance is becoming circular
Broadcom has agreed to lend Anthropic up to $42 billion, potentially financing roughly one-third of Anthropic's $125.2 billion five-year TPU lease commitment — while also becoming a major supplier to Anthropic.
signal: Growth financed by the supplier is economically different from independent demand.

::pull The hidden pattern: AI CAPEX raises inflation and WACC today; AI DEPLOYMENT lowers unit costs and brings disinflation tomorrow.

::section Where Capital Is Moving | Part II
Capital is not leaving AI. It is moving toward the layers where scarcity can be converted into cash flow fastest. The architecture of the future is explicit: POWER + LAND + CAPITAL + COMPUTE = AI INFRASTRUCTURE.

::patterns Where Capital Is Moving | Scarcity into cash flow
# Memory And Compute Remain Scarce
Micron's strong outlook reinforced the persistence of AI-memory demand, even as its shares weakened while investors weighed enormous future investment requirements.

# Power Is The Location Algorithm
JERA, Dell, RHAELM and Apollo are developing a $15 billion, 400-MW AI data center in Chiba. JERA will provide land beside an existing power station and guarantee electricity for 15–25 years.

# Credit Is Strategic Infrastructure
Broadcom financing Anthropic shows how suppliers can accelerate demand by financing customers — but it also creates counterparty and concentration risk.

# Industrial AI Broadens The Cycle
September factory surveys suggest AI spending is propagating into capital goods, semiconductors, electrical equipment and industrial systems — not remaining confined to hyperscale computing.

::pull For Europe, the bottleneck is no longer capital availability. It is ==Capital-Conversion Velocity== — and at 5%-plus yields, every year of execution delay becomes more expensive.

::section What Matters Next | Part III
Five signals now define the next session. U.S. employment on Friday will test whether the economy is cooling enough to validate the Fed-pause narrative; a strong report could push the 10-year back through 5.3%. The long bond remains the key: a sustained 10-year above 5.25% keeps the underwriting regime intact. Watch diesel as closely as Brent, track AI credit spreads and counterparty exposure after Broadcom–Anthropic, and watch whether industrial breadth turns the Supercycle into a genuine macroeconomic capital-formation cycle.

::takeaways Key takeaways | For every seat at the table
- For investors: favor AI that combines scarcity + pricing power + contracted demand + near-term cash conversion.
- For CEOs: the KPI is no longer AI adoption — it is productivity created per dollar of AI capital.
- For boards: scrutinize supplier financing and circular capital structures.
- For Europe: accelerate power, grids and Industrial AI — at today's yields, permitting time is financial cost.
- For policymakers: the Supercycle may require a two-stage monetary framework — capital-formation inflation first, productivity disinflation later.

::section The Next 90 Days | The Great Split
The debate is no longer whether AI demand is real. It clearly is. The debate is becoming: who finances it, at what cost, for how long, and when does productivity arrive? This creates the next Great Split — ==AI that creates productivity faster than it absorbs capital== versus AI whose capital intensity outruns its economics.

The paradox is now visible. The more successful the AI investment boom becomes, the more it demands capital, electricity, memory, construction and credit — and that can raise the hurdle rate for AI itself. **The Law of Self-Raising Hurdles:** a technological Supercycle can ultimately lower economic costs while initially raising the cost of the capital required to build it.

::close October opened with the clearest signal yet: the Fed did not tighten — the market did. The Supercycle is no longer competing only against obsolescence. It is competing against ==5.3%==.

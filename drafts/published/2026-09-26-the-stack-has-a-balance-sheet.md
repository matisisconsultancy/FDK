Three numbers define the AI trade entering the weekend: 5.16% Treasuries, $104 oil, and a +3.66% move in Microsoft. Capital is expensive. Energy is costly. The market still pays for monetization.

The AI race has moved from building the stack to making the stack pay. Models proved capability. Compute proved demand. The next phase must prove economics — and the fundamental question is now simple: who can turn compute into cash flow fast enough to outrun energy and capital costs?

::signals Five Signals | The evidence entering the weekend
# Capital — 5% Changes the Screen
The U.S. 10-year closed above 5.15%. Long-duration AI assets must now justify a materially higher risk premium.
signal: ROIC minus WACC is the test.

# Agents — Monetization Moves Forward
Microsoft expanded Copilot into coding, always-on agents and deeper Office integration. The market is shifting from model capability toward paid enterprise productivity.
signal: The premium is moving toward monetization.

# Energy — The Electron Enters the Economics
Brent settled at $104.32, with Hormuz uncertainty unresolved. Energy raises both compute costs and, through inflation and rates, capital costs.
signal: The electron is now inside the valuation.

# Europe — AI Meets the Power Constraint
Europe wants data-center capacity to rise substantially by 2030, while gas inventories remain well below last year's level. The opportunity sits increasingly in grids, electrical equipment, semiconductor equipment, nuclear and automation.
signal: AI ambition requires competitive power.

# Sovereign Capital — Resilience Moves Up the Agenda
Qatar has created Doha Investment, a new domestic investment arm within its sovereign-capital architecture. The broader signal is strategic: sovereign capital is increasingly directed toward domestic infrastructure, energy security and resilience, rather than portfolio diversification alone.
signal: The race now extends into national balance sheets.

::section The Hidden Pattern | Capital productivity is the common denominator
The common denominator is capital productivity. Agents must become paid workflows. Compute must become utilized capacity. Power must remain affordable. CAPEX must become cash flow.

The AI Supercycle is moving from capacity creation to ==economic conversion==.

::section The Great Split | Productive vs. speculative AI capital
The divide is no longer AI versus non-AI. It is **productive AI capital versus speculative AI capital**.

Productive capital combines real demand, secured power, high utilization, strong balance sheets and returns above the cost of capital. Speculative capital depends on future adoption, future power, continuous refinancing and lower future rates.

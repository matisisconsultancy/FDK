The Italian Banking Risiko has moved again. The most important development is not another bid — it is the ==redistribution of decision-making power==.

Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti confirmed yesterday that the Italian Treasury, which owns 4.86% of MPS, will not participate in the October 29 shareholder vote on the defensive Banco BPM and Banca Generali acquisitions. His message was explicit: the government will follow the market's decision and expects the market to reward the better offer.

Simultaneously, Luigi Lovaglio reframed the vote. MPS shareholders, he argued, can authorize the defensive strategy on October 29 and still later tender their shares to Intesa. He also stated the two offers can work independently: Banco BPM creates scale and most of the synergies; Banca Generali transforms the group's wealth-management profile and brings Generali into the shareholder base.

And this morning, a third signal. Crédit Agricole CFO Clotilde L'Angevin described a combination between Banco BPM and Crédit Agricole Italia as a "very interesting" scenario, while reiterating the French group will remain patient. The consequence is decisive: the October 29 vote is no longer government versus market — it is **private capital versus private capital**.

# The State Withdraws From The Vote
The Treasury's abstention materially changes the shareholder arithmetic. MPS's disclosed ownership includes Delfin at 17.53%, Caltagirone at 10.26%, MEF at 4.86%, BlackRock at 4.66% and Banco BPM at 3.74%. The defensive resolutions require approval by at least two-thirds of votes cast. At the April meeting, 64.11% of capital was represented; if participation were similar and the Treasury's 4.86% stayed outside, the approval hurdle would mechanically correspond to roughly 39½% of total capital — an illustration, not a forecast, but it shows why Delfin, Caltagirone, BlackRock, Banco BPM and international free-float investors now become the real constitutional assembly of MPS.
signal: The swing block is now private, institutional and international.

# MPS Makes The Defence Modular
Lovaglio's statement that the two offers can work individually is more consequential than it appears. It lowers the strategic dependence of the defence on achieving the entire €34 billion architecture at once. Banco BPM is the scale leg; Banca Generali is the wealth-management and Generali-alignment leg. MPS is quietly moving from a single grand bargain toward a portfolio of defensive options.
signal: The defence no longer needs both assets to create option value.

# Crédit Agricole Hardens The BPM Alternative
Crédit Agricole's public preference for a BPM–Crédit Agricole Italia combination is now considerably harder to dismiss as background optionality. With 29.3% of Banco BPM, the French group remains the institution with the greatest ability to determine whether MPS can obtain the asset it needs most.
signal: The veto is becoming a strategy.

::section The Decisive Issue | two sequential veto points
The Risiko now has two sequential veto points. The first is October 29, when MPS must persuade its own shareholders to authorize defensive capital allocation. The second is Banco BPM itself, where Crédit Agricole's 29.3% stake keeps the French group central to any realistic control outcome.

That creates a new asymmetry: MPS can authorize an offer without Crédit Agricole, but MPS cannot easily complete the strategic architecture against Crédit Agricole. The defence no longer needs both assets to create option value — but the full Third Pole still needs both to realize the original strategic vision.

::patterns Updated Strategic Map | who wins from the new arithmetic
# Intesa Sanpaolo — Strong Overweight
The government's abstention is incrementally positive because MPS's defence must now win exclusively through shareholder economics. Intesa's offer remains 1.6 newly issued shares plus €1 cash per MPS share, subject to a 66.67% minimum acceptance it can waive. The comparative advantage is no longer merely scale — it is execution simplicity versus coalition complexity.

# MPS — High Convexity / High Vote Risk
Lovaglio is no longer asking shareholders to decide definitively between MPS and Intesa. He is asking them to preserve strategic optionality. That is sophisticated — but if the vote is not final ownership selection, shareholders must weigh the value of keeping alternatives alive against dilution, complexity and regulatory friction. The industrial thesis survives; the shareholder burden of proof is rising.

# Banco BPM — Event-Driven Overweight
Banco BPM closed Wednesday at €16.285, +1.21%, while MPS gained only 0.37%. The scarcity is structural: plausible destinations include MPS, Crédit Agricole Italia, a negotiated European solution and potentially renewed UniCredit involvement. Scarcity rises with the number of credible buyers.

# Crédit Agricole — Strategic Overweight
Crédit Agricole is moving from passive kingmaker toward explicit strategic architect. Its preferred scenario is now publicly identifiable, yet it remains patient and need not force timing. That gives it something unusually valuable: control over the clock.

# UniCredit — Overweight
No material new Commerzbank development emerged overnight — itself strategically useful. UniCredit does not need Banco BPM to justify its scale strategy. Its primary transformation remains Germany, where its Commerzbank position approaches control and Berlin has shifted toward negotiating safeguards rather than blocking. It retains Italian optionality without making Italy existential.

# Commerzbank — Event-Driven
Commerzbank closed at €41.39 on September 23, UniCredit at €83.47. At the 0.485 exchange ratio, the mechanical offer value is roughly €40.48 — so Commerzbank trades about 2.2% above it. The market still prices an improved ratio, a governance settlement or a residual control premium.

# Generali — Overweight
Generali remains embedded in every major Italian outcome. There is no new formal decision this morning on the Banca Generali proposal; Generali has said it could offer growth opportunities but remains unsolicited and subject to evaluation. Strategic scarcity outweighs daily volatility.

# Banca Generali — Event-Driven Positive
Banca Generali closed Wednesday at €65.85, down 0.15%. Unlike Banco BPM, it has not developed a multi-bidder premium — making the spread significantly wider and the payoff more binary.

::section Capital-Markets Reaction And Valuation | the market's own verdict
The market continues to differentiate sharply between the two MPS target assets. MPS closed at €11.894 and Banco BPM at €16.285. Before settling its offers, MPS proposes an extraordinary distribution of €1.208 per share; adjusting for that gives an ex-distribution value of roughly €10.686. Applying the 1.567 exchange ratio implies a Banco BPM value of about €16.74 — only around 2.8% above market. The BPM spread is becoming extremely tight: the market is saying MPS does not own the upside in BPM.

The same calculation for Banca Generali is very different. The 6.958 ratio on the adjusted MPS currency implies roughly €74.35 versus a €65.85 close — a spread of about 12.9%. That gap is pure execution risk. For MPS itself, Intesa's current terms mechanically imply roughly €11.74 using Intesa's €6.71 September 23 close; MPS closed at €11.894, around 1.3% above — a small but real price for optionality.

::patterns Three Scenarios | probabilities revised this morning
# [42%] Continental Bifurcation ↑
Intesa completes MPS; UniCredit converts Commerzbank ownership into negotiated control; Banco BPM gravitates toward Crédit Agricole Italia or another consensual structure; Generali stays independent. The probability rises because the Treasury will not support the MPS vote and Crédit Agricole has reinforced its alternative.

# [20%] Modular MPS Defence ↓
MPS wins authorization but does not necessarily complete both offers — one leg survives. Lovaglio's own comments make this more realistic than the original all-or-nothing Third Pole, but the probability falls because Crédit Agricole's stance hardens the BPM leg and the Treasury will not vote in favour.

# [38%] Negotiated Realignment ↑
Competing bids force bilateral and multilateral settlements: Intesa–MPS, Crédit Agricole Italia–Banco BPM, UniCredit–Commerzbank, Generali independent, with MPS shareholders extracting value through defensive assets before control changes. This rises because the system is becoming modular.

::section The Hidden Pattern | privatising the decision, not the strategy
Rome is effectively saying: we will not choose the winning capital allocator through our MPS shareholding. That is consistent with Giorgetti's formulation that the market should reward the superior offer. But regulators still define the perimeter — MPS has ECB authorization for the Mediobanca merger, while the Banco BPM and Banca Generali offers remain subject to supervisory, competition, insurance and Golden Power processes.

The emerging model is not laissez-faire consolidation. It is **private capital selection + national safeguards + European supervision** — Banking Union in practice. The government is privatising the decision, not the strategy: allowing market prices and private shareholders to determine who gets the capital, while retaining the ability to set the rules under which that capital can move.

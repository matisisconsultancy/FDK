A decisive inflection has occurred in the U.S.–China AI race. For the first time, the leadership crisis at the company dominating advanced computing is converging with a shift in American semiconductor policy expectations and an observable repricing of Chinese AI assets.

On November 12, Nvidia confirmed Jensen Huang had resigned as chief executive and director. CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO, while Harvey Jones assumed the newly established board chairmanship. Nvidia says its product roadmap and customer commitments remain intact.

The hidden pattern: America controls the most strategically valuable computing chokepoint, while China's diffusion strategy remains more dependent on that chokepoint than headline narratives about independence suggest.

::signal
- **Jensen Huang resigned** as Nvidia CEO and director on November 12; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, Harvey Jones chairs the new board.
- **Reuters found over $3.2 billion** of Chinese AI projects built around restricted Nvidia H100, H200 and H800 processors, across 150-plus procurement documents.
- **Markets split sharply:** Z.AI fell 12% and MiniMax 8.4% on November 13, while chipmaker Cambricon rose more than 20%.
- **Alibaba and Tencent** lost roughly $75 billion in combined market value this week, per the Financial Times.
- **SMIC warned** AI chip supply chains could stay constrained through 2027, with equipment delays exceeding 24 months.
- **Nvidia reports November 17** under interim leadership; no new Chinese model reverses American frontier leadership.

::section The Nvidia Inflection | From key-person risk to systemic technology risk
Huang's resignation removes the executive who built Nvidia's technological ecosystem and advocated retaining commercial access to China. The immediate risk is not that accelerated computing suddenly becomes uncompetitive. It is that leadership succession, governance scrutiny and export-policy uncertainty converge at a company central to global AI investment.

The allegations involving Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive suspected of Chinese intelligence connections, remain unproven; the Justice Department has reached no conclusion, and Nvidia says it has no reason to believe its secrets were compromised.

But the political consequences are measurable. House Speaker Mike Johnson has endorsed stronger chip restrictions, and the future of Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is reportedly under discussion inside the White House. The fundamental market risk is a transition from selective controls to a broader regime governing hardware ownership, offshore computing and access to advanced intelligence.

::section The Chinese Computing Contradiction | $3.2 billion of exposed investment
A Reuters investigation—reviewing more than 150 procurement notices, investment documents and disclosures—identified over **$3.2 billion** of Chinese projects incorporating or anticipating Nvidia H100, H200 and H800 accelerators. Some facilities operate, others are under construction, still others remain planned. The amount includes supporting infrastructure, not simply chip purchases.

The findings expose substantial potential costs if restrictions tighten: delayed commissioning, costlier procurement, or redesign around Chinese processors complicated by software portability and developer ecosystems. The distinction matters—China can possess competitive AI models without yet possessing a fully independent system to train and deploy them.

::section The Market Rotation | China's AI economy begins to split
According to the Financial Times, Alibaba and Tencent have lost roughly **$75 billion** in combined market capitalization this week, while accelerator maker Cambricon has advanced more than **20%** on expectations of domestic semiconductor demand. Chinese model developers face computing-access risk; domestic chip manufacturers may benefit from substitution.

::pull This is a potential evolutionary mechanism, not an established outcome — substitution succeeds only where Chinese alternatives become technologically and economically viable.

The possible second-order effect is a new capital cycle: tighter American controls, higher replacement costs, accelerated Chinese investment in domestic accelerators and software, and a long-term reduction in dependence on U.S. infrastructure.

::section The Semiconductor Counterpoint | Scarcity could persist through 2027
SMIC, China's largest contract chipmaker, said on November 13 that AI-related supply chains could remain constrained through **2027**. It cited equipment delivery delays exceeding **24 months** on some orders and an expected **~30%** rise in depreciation expenses during 2026. Domestic demand may rise sharply, but manufacturing capacity cannot expand at the same speed—temporarily weakening China's diffusion economics even as it strengthens the case for independence.

::section The Investment Verdict | The next AI rotation
Today's evidence exposes a critical asymmetry: model efficiency does not eliminate dependence on advanced computing infrastructure. Five signals now deserve immediate attention.

::patterns What changes the thesis | Five signals for investors
# Nvidia leadership
Disruption of product execution, customer financing or strategic partnerships.
# U.S. export legislation
Enacted limits on remote compute access, chip tracking or eligible Chinese customers.
# Chinese chip substitution
Verified performance and fully loaded cost competitiveness in production deployments.
# Chinese model economics
Higher inference costs or deployment delays caused by restricted computing.
# AI market financing
Material changes in infrastructure commitments, financing spreads and capital productivity.

The next immediate event is Nvidia's **November 17** earnings call under new interim leadership, alongside congressional scrutiny including anticipated testimony from Huang. No newly verified Chinese frontier-model release reverses American leadership—the decisive development is institutional and financial, not a new benchmark.

The AI Supercycle is no longer simply repricing intelligence. It is repricing the right to produce, access and economically exploit it. The next rotation may favor businesses holding scarce, defensible computing assets, firms delivering lower system-level costs, and enterprises converting intelligence into measurable productivity.

::close The decisive advantage will belong to the system that can ==secure computing sovereignty without sacrificing capital productivity==.

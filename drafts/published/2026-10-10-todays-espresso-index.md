In 1986, *The Economist* introduced the Big Mac Index to make purchasing power parity understandable through the price of a familiar product across countries. The Velocity Edge's **Espresso Index** applies a similarly intuitive idea to a radically different economic question.

The Big Mac Index compares the purchasing power of currencies. The Espresso Index seeks to compare the ==productive power of intelligence== across economies. Its starting point is the emergence of the token economy: a world in which accelerated computing, GPU architectures, optimized acceleration libraries and increasingly capable AI models are reducing the cost of producing intelligence while expanding the speed at which machines and organizations can learn.

But the true revolution is not simply faster computation. It is the compounding of learning. Accelerated computing reduces the cost of experimentation. More experimentation generates data. Data improves models and operational decisions. Better models enable more autonomous actions. Those actions generate additional feedback, potentially accelerating the next learning cycle.

::pull COMPUTE → TOKENS → LEARNING → ACTION → DATA → BETTER INTELLIGENCE

The process is circular rather than linear, and its economic consequences may be unprecedented. The central question of the Espresso Index is therefore: *how fast can an economy convert accelerated intelligence into cumulative learning, productive action and financial returns?* It is not a measure of national intelligence or an official economic statistic. It is a proposed proprietary indicator of the speed at which the token economy translates technological capability into economic progress.

::section The Next AI Divide Is The Speed Of Learning | the big take

The AI Supercycle has already demonstrated that it can generate extraordinary infrastructure profits. Third-quarter S&P 500 earnings are expected to rise approximately **31%**, with roughly two-thirds of the increase concentrated in technology and major AI-exposed companies. Semiconductor earnings are forecast to grow approximately **136%**. These figures describe a powerful but concentrated earnings cycle.

The next stage is more consequential. Can the learning generated by AI spread across the economy fast enough to produce a broad productivity dividend? That is where the Espresso Index differs from conventional AI rankings. It is not designed simply to reward technological scale; it aims to capture the velocity of economic conversion.

::pull ACCELERATED COMPUTING → LOWER INTELLIGENCE COST → FASTER LEARNING → INDUSTRIAL DEPLOYMENT → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW

The speed of that chain—and its ability to reinforce itself—will increasingly determine national competitive advantage.

::signals The Second-Order Effect | why the rankings may change faster than conventional economic rankings

# Learning becomes self-reinforcing
The most consequential feature of the token economy is that learning can compound. Countries that combine compute, research, industrial applications and capital can generate a recursive advantage: more deployment → more feedback → better models → cheaper execution → more deployment. This could widen economic differences even as foundational AI models become more widespread.
signal: The recursive loop, not raw compute, is the durable moat.

# A rotation from producers to deployers
The United States retains the strongest combined position in frontier development and private capital. China possesses exceptional potential to convert intelligence into physical output. South Korea and Taiwan occupy indispensable semiconductor layers, while Europe's opportunity lies in connecting its engineering base to accessible intelligence. The financial consequence is a possible rotation from countries producing intelligence toward those deploying it most productively.
signal: That rotation is not yet established—it is what future releases should test.

::takeaways Five Key Takeaways For Investors | learning velocity over installed scale

- The next AI premium is **learning velocity** — look beyond installed compute toward the speed at which organizations turn inference into better decisions and recurring revenue.
- Distinguish **AI producers from AI converters** — the U.S. dominates capital formation; China is strong in industrial deployment. Different sources of return.
- Watch the **second tier** — South Korea, Japan, Taiwan and leading European industrials can capture value through semiconductors, automation and specialized systems.
- Europe needs an **industrial conversion strategy** — Germany, France, the Netherlands, Spain and Italy gain if energy, capital, data and procurement operate as a more integrated market.
- **Earnings diffusion** is the decisive catalyst — with two-thirds of forecast U.S. Q3 growth concentrated in tech, watch for verified margin and ROIC improvements in AI-adopting industries.

::section The Espresso Verdict | the economics of compounding intelligence

The Big Mac Index made the purchasing power of currencies intuitive. The Espresso Index aims to make the productive velocity of intelligence measurable. In the industrial economy, advantage was built through capital accumulation, manufacturing scale and organizational efficiency. In the AI-native economy, those advantages increasingly interact with accelerated computing and machine learning systems capable of improving through repeated feedback.

The decisive economic unit may therefore shift from the *quantity* of intelligence an economy can access toward the *speed* at which that intelligence compounds into productive capability. Not simply who has the most GPUs. Not merely who produces the best models. But: ==which economy learns fastest—and turns that learning into the highest sustainable return on capital?==

::close Intelligence is increasingly available to everyone; the capacity to compound it into productive power will not be. ==The future will belong to those that learn, adapt and compound the fastest.==

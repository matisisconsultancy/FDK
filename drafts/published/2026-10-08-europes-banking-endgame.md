The MPS contest has entered its final strategic phase. Francesco Gaetano Caltagirone has formally moved against the extraordinary resolutions at the October 29 MPS shareholders' meeting.

Together with Delfin, the two largest strategic shareholders now represent a base opposition bloc equal to **27.79% of MPS share capital**. That does not mathematically close the vote — but it changes the nature of the contest.

Lovaglio can no longer prevail simply by defending the architecture he has already presented. He must now produce a materially better economic reason for shareholders to remain independent.

::pull CALTAGIRONE HAS CHANGED THE ARITHMETIC. LOVAGLIO MUST NOW CHANGE THE ECONOMICS.

If he cannot, Intesa's advantage moves from momentum toward control. And once control shifts, the consequences move immediately beyond MPS.

::stats
27.79% :: Delfin + Caltagirone base opposition (of total share capital)
83.37% :: Break-even turnout at the 27.79% NO bloc
93.06% :: Required turnout if Caltagirone's full 13.49% votes NO
95.14% :: Maximum theoretical turnout with Treasury (4.86%) absent

::section The Arithmetic | Why 27.79% against requires 83.37% turnout

MPS does not need two-thirds of its entire share capital to approve the extraordinary resolutions. It needs **two-thirds of the votes cast at the meeting**. The result therefore depends on two variables: how much share capital actually votes, and how much of that voting capital votes NO.

The current base opposition positions are Delfin at 17.53% and the Caltagirone Group at 10.26% — together 27.79% of total share capital. Assume all 27.79% attends and votes NO. Let T equal the percentage of total capital represented by votes cast. The YES vote can be no greater than T − 27.79, and to pass it must equal at least two-thirds of all votes cast:

T − 27.79 ≥ ⅔T, which rearranges to ⅓T ≥ 27.79, therefore **T ≥ 83.37%**.

The intuition is simpler. At 83.37% turnout, the 27.79% NO bloc is exactly one-third of votes cast (27.79 ÷ 83.37 = 33.33%), and the remaining 55.58% is exactly two-thirds (55.58 ÷ 83.37 = 66.67%). So 83.37% is the break-even turnout. Below it, a fixed 27.79% NO bloc exceeds one-third and the resolutions cannot reach two-thirds.

::pull 27.79% is the NO bloc as a share of total capital. 83.37% is the minimum total capital that must vote for a two-thirds YES majority to remain possible.

The Treasury's 4.86% stake is expected not to participate, cutting maximum representation from 100% to 95.14%. At 95.14% turnout with 27.79% NO, YES = 67.35, which is 70.79% of votes cast — still passable. If Banco BPM's roughly 3.74% also stays away, maximum representation falls to 91.40%; YES = 63.61, or 69.60% — still above two-thirds. So under the base case, even Treasury and Banco BPM non-participation do not mathematically block Lovaglio. They simply make the coalition he needs exceptionally demanding.

::section The Caltagirone Variable | Why 13.49% would change the equation

MPS separately reports that the Caltagirone Group held 13.49% of share capital at the May dividend-payment date. That figure should not yet be treated as confirmed voting power for October 29 — the conservative voting case remains 10.26%. But if the full 13.49% were confirmed as exercisable against the resolutions:

Delfin 17.53% + Caltagirone 13.49% = **31.02%**. The same formula gives T ≥ 3 × 31.02, therefore T ≥ 93.06%. At 93.06% turnout, 31.02% NO is exactly one-third and 62.04% YES exactly two-thirds.

With the Treasury absent, theoretical maximum participation is only 95.14% — leaving Lovaglio just **2.08 percentage points of turnout headroom** before the mathematics close. And if Banco BPM's 3.74% also stays away, maximum turnout falls to 91.40%, below the required 93.06%. In that specific case, the resolutions would be mathematically blocked.

::pull 10.26% Caltagirone → extremely difficult. 13.49% Caltagirone → potentially decisive.

::signals The Strategic Fault Lines | How the coalition fragments

# Delfin and Caltagirone: same vote, different endgame
The two shareholders now reinforce one another against Lovaglio, but they should not be treated as a permanent bloc. Delfin has committed its MPS position to Intesa. Caltagirone has opposed Lovaglio without formally binding itself to the Intesa transaction. Delfin has chosen a transaction; Caltagirone has preserved optionality. They can vote together on October 29 without wanting the same outcome on October 30.
signal: The old shareholder coalition is fragmenting as the ownership architecture around Italian savings is rebuilt.

# Lovaglio's only real question
His problem is no longer strategic imagination — it is credibility. The current defence asks shareholders to underwrite Banco BPM, Banca Generali, Mediobanca integration, Generali exposure and a more complex capital structure. Intesa offers cash, Intesa equity and execution certainty. Lovaglio has three levers — more value, less complexity, more time — but time without a better economic offer is only delay.
signal: He must prove independence creates more risk-adjusted value than Intesa's offer.

# Why Generali matters
Generali can provide financial flexibility, but it is also a principal strategic asset underpinning the wider MPS–Mediobanca architecture. The more Generali is used to defend MPS today, the less remains to justify MPS independence tomorrow. If Intesa acquires MPS, Mediobanca and its Generali exposure move into a larger banking perimeter — the route Delfin has chosen and Caltagirone has not.
signal: Generali is the central contradiction, and the next fault line after October 29.

::section The Institutional Float Decides — Unless the Arithmetic Closes | Where the vote is won

Under the 27.79% base case, institutional investors remain decisive. Lovaglio requires exceptionally high turnout and overwhelming support from the remaining voting capital. They will judge the independent strategy on ROTE, EPS accretion, CET1 consumption, synergies, integration costs, dividend capacity, execution probability and terminal value.

The argument cannot be that MPS *deserves* to remain independent. It must be that **MPS independence is worth more than Intesa's offer**. But if the opposition bloc grows large enough that required turnout exceeds the maximum capital able to participate, institutional support can no longer solve the problem. At that point, Lovaglio must change the opposition arithmetic itself — changing a vote, changing participation, or changing the proposal sufficiently to fracture the NO coalition.

::section What Happens If Lovaglio Fails | The European endgame

An Intesa victory would not end consolidation — it would release the next sequence. MPS moves toward Intesa; Mediobanca enters a larger banking perimeter; Generali becomes more strategically important; Banco BPM becomes scarcer; Crédit Agricole must decide whether influence is enough; UniCredit must choose between domestic depth and continental scale; and Brussels confronts the unfinished Banking Union.

For Crédit Agricole, the question becomes whether to convert influence in Banco BPM into control. For UniCredit, whether the next euro of capital should buy greater domestic depth or deepen the Commerzbank strategy. For Brussels and the ECB, the question becomes institutional: Europe can supervise banks across borders, but it has not yet demonstrated that it can accommodate cross-border ownership at true continental scale. ==Common supervision without common ownership is an incomplete Banking Union.==

::pull The MPS vote is not the endgame. It is the trigger for the next one.

::patterns What's Next | Three variables that decide everything

# [13.49%] The true Caltagirone voting position
At 10.26%, the base opposition is 27.79% and break-even turnout is 83.37%. At 13.49%, the bloc becomes 31.02% and break-even turnout rises to 93.06%. The single most important arithmetic distinction.

# Who else does not vote
Every absent shareholder lowers maximum available turnout. When maximum possible turnout falls below three times the fixed NO bloc, the resolutions are mathematically blocked. That is the general rule.

# October 15
Lovaglio must decide whether to deliver more value, less complexity, more time — or all three. Everything now flows from those three variables.

::takeaways Key takeaways | The transparent arithmetic
- The threshold is two-thirds of votes cast — not two-thirds of MPS share capital.
- A fixed NO bloc can block the resolutions by becoming more than one-third of whatever capital actually votes.
- With 27.79% voting NO, turnout must reach at least 83.37%; at exactly that level, 27.79% is one-third and 55.58% is two-thirds.
- With 31.02% voting NO, turnout must reach at least 93.06%.
- Treasury non-participation caps theoretical turnout at 95.14%; with Banco BPM also absent it falls to 91.40%.
- So 27.79% NO does not mathematically block the vote even with Treasury and BPM absent — but 31.02% NO would.
- The general formula: minimum required turnout = 3 × fixed NO bloc. If maximum possible turnout falls below that number, the two-thirds majority is impossible.

::close Lovaglio does not need two-thirds of MPS — he needs two-thirds of whoever votes. At 27.79% opposition he still has a route; at 31.02% it becomes extraordinarily narrow; add enough non-participation and it disappears entirely. ==The question is no longer only how many shares oppose Lovaglio — it is how large that opposition becomes as a share of the vote that actually shows up.==

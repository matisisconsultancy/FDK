The return of Jensen Huang to NVIDIA has triggered an extraordinary recovery across global technology markets. NVIDIA surged roughly 31% on Monday, the S&P 500 advanced 4.7% and the Nasdaq gained 6.5%, with Asian semiconductor markets extending the rally overnight.

But the more consequential development lies beneath the recovery. As equity valuations rebounded, US Treasury yields rose and the dollar strengthened. The same technological revolution supporting superior earnings expectations is also reinforcing demand for capital, electricity and computing infrastructure. The AI Supercycle is becoming simultaneously an engine of productivity and a source of financial differentiation.

The next rotation may not be between technology and traditional industries. It may be between corporations and economies capable of converting accelerated computing into sustainable returns — and those increasingly exposed to higher financing costs.

::signals The Five Overnight Signals | what moved and why it matters

# NVIDIA: the return of the compass company
NVIDIA's board confirmed Huang's reinstatement, acknowledging input from senior US government officials. The market response demonstrates the extraordinary financial significance of leadership at strategically indispensable corporations. The premium attached to technological leadership now encompasses ecosystem control, strategic continuity and geopolitical relevance — not simply earnings. But the rebound does not eliminate governance risk or establish the long-term profitability of every AI investment.
signal: Leadership at indispensable firms carries a geopolitical premium, not only an earnings one.

# Accelerated computing: capital productivity is the next battleground
The next phase of competition will revolve around computing efficiency, inference economics, industrial deployment and the conversion of capacity into cash flow. The crucial transition is from building intelligence infrastructure to monetizing intelligence at scale — intensifying the gap between highly utilized infrastructure and speculative capacity.
signal: The contest shifts from building compute to monetizing it.

# Europe: the great split becomes visible
Germany's composite PMI reached 55.0 while France fell to 46.2; the eurozone held at 51.7. France simultaneously faces elevated sovereign costs, with its 10-year near 4.83% and its spread over Germany above 150 basis points. Higher sovereign yields feed into corporate borrowing, bank valuations and infrastructure financing.
signal: Europe's industrial divergence risks becoming a financial divergence.

# Energy: lower oil, higher structural demand
Oil declined toward three-month lows as increased Middle Eastern supply and restored Libyan flows improved the near-term outlook, though geopolitical risk remains material. For the AI Supercycle, electricity availability, grid expansion and the cost of dependable power may matter more than short-term oil relief.
signal: The investment frontier is extending from semiconductors toward energy and grids.

# Global markets: the rebound meets the discount rate
The equity recovery has coincided with rising Treasury yields. Long-duration growth assets depend not only on future earnings but on the rate at which those earnings are discounted. An AI-led recovery with persistently higher real financing costs favours demonstrable profitability and resilient balance sheets over continuous external funding.
signal: Higher discount rates reward cash flow over promise.

::patterns The Great Split | three divergences defining a more selective regime

# The Productivity Divide
Companies that translate AI deployment into higher margins, earnings and free cash flow versus those accumulating technology expenditure without measurable returns.

# The Capital Divide
Corporations and infrastructure providers with durable financing capacity versus businesses exposed to refinancing risk, obsolescence and weak utilization.

# The Sovereign Divide
Economies able to mobilize capital for productive transformation versus those constrained by fiscal fragility and structurally higher borrowing costs.

::pull The next rotation could occur within the AI Supercycle, not away from it — toward productive deployment and economically indispensable systems rather than undifferentiated expansion.

::section The Strategic Agenda | what each actor must prioritize
For investors, distinguish earnings acceleration from valuation expansion: free cash flow, return on invested capital, debt maturity and credit spreads should guide selection. Favour profitable AI platforms, electricity grids and storage, and contracted infrastructure credit; treat European sovereign debt and highly leveraged infrastructure as exposed to repricing.

For corporate boards, translate AI spending into measurable operational productivity rather than treating technology investment as an objective in itself.

For policymakers, strengthen electricity infrastructure, technological capability and capital-market depth while preserving sovereign fiscal credibility. For Europe, industrial competitiveness and sovereign financing costs are becoming inseparable.

Today's catalysts include Dell's earnings and US consumer confidence, followed on November 25 by US GDP and PCE inflation — tests of the rebound against the monetary outlook. FDK positioning: ==Selective Risk-On== — constructive on AI-driven productivity and essential infrastructure, more cautious on duration, weak balance sheets and fiscally vulnerable sovereigns.

::close The next great divide will not be between those who invest in AI and those who do not. It will be between those who turn acceleration into ==financial returns== and those who pay the rising price of capital without capturing its value.

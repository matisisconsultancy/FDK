Today's signal is bigger than another move in equities. The world's major central banks are converging toward a new tightening cycle precisely when the AI Supercycle is becoming more physical, energy-intensive and capital-intensive. The Fed has delivered its first rate increase since 2023; today the Bank of Japan raised rates to 1.25%, a 31-year high; markets expect further tightening across several major economies.

The fundamental question is therefore: can AI productivity rise faster than the cost of financing the physical infrastructure required to produce it? That is now the equation driving markets.

5% money changes everything. Capital remains available, but every data center, AI factory, nuclear plant, railway, defense facility and acquisition must now compete against an increasingly attractive risk-free return.

::signals Today's Signals | the forces repricing the world
# The denominator has changed
U.S. Treasury yields moved higher again today as investors absorbed the new global rate regime. Wall Street was modestly lower in late trading, with the Dow down about 0.36%, the S&P 500 about 0.10% and the Nasdaq approximately flat. Europe performed worse: the STOXX 600 fell 1.1% to 635.45, with autos down 3.4%.
signal: Every asset must now clear an increasingly attractive risk-free return.

# Japan closes the zero-rate era
The BOJ increased its policy rate to 1.25%, the highest in 31 years, by a 7–2 vote. Governor Ueda signaled that policy has entered a more pre-emptive phase focused on preventing inflation from overshooting. Curiously, the yen weakened because the two dissenters created uncertainty over how rapidly subsequent tightening will occur. For decades, cheap yen fed the global carry trade and exported capital abroad; now higher Japanese rates mean better domestic returns and greater competition for Japanese savings.
signal: The world has lost its last major near-zero-cost funding anchor.

# Oil has become monetary policy
Oil remains above $100, despite some easing today as China reportedly pressed Iran to restrain Houthi actions. More important than crude alone is diesel: U.S. diesel has exceeded $6 per gallon and European diesel around $210 per barrel amid refinery disruption and historically tight inventories. Diesel directly feeds trucking, agriculture, construction, mining and industrial logistics.
signal: Energy flows straight into physical costs, inflation, yields, WACC and the AI hurdle rate.

# AI capital formation refuses to stop
This is the counter-signal that matters most. Crusoe has just raised $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion post-money valuation, with demand for specialized AI-cloud and data-center capacity remaining enormous. And an even larger architecture became visible: South Korea and the United States are discussing projects within a $350 billion framework, including a proposed $22.3 billion, 6.3-GW Texas gas-power complex to serve AI data centers and fabs, plus potentially as many as eight U.S. nuclear reactors.
signal: AI investment is migrating from GPU-and-data-center toward power, nuclear, gas, grid and industrial capacity.

# Europe's Great Split becomes visible
Today's European selloff exposed the other side. Volkswagen fell 5.6% after cutting its profit outlook amid restructuring costs, job-reduction provisions and weak Chinese performance. Porsche lost 4.9%. Europe possesses extraordinary engineering assets, but capital must migrate faster from low-return legacy capacity toward industrial AI, power, grid, defense, autonomy, rail, robotics, accelerated computing and intelligent mobility.
signal: Europe's problem is no longer lack of capital — it is Capital-Conversion Velocity.

::section The Next 90 Days | three forces on a collision course
First, monetary tightening: the Fed, ECB and BOJ have moved toward higher rates, and markets anticipate further increases. Second, physical inflation: oil above $100, record diesel prices and disrupted energy infrastructure keep upward pressure on the cost base. Third, AI productivity: trillions of dollars of infrastructure investment now need to demonstrate that intelligence can generate enough incremental output, margin and cash flow to outrun the higher cost of capital.

That creates the central equation for Q4: **AI productivity velocity** versus **WACC + energy + physical scarcity**. Whichever side accelerates faster will determine the next market regime.

::section The Velocity Insight | return on intelligence
The first phase of the AI Supercycle was about intelligence. The second was about accelerated computing. The third became about power. The fourth became about capital. We are now entering the decisive phase: ==Return on Intelligence==.

The market will increasingly ask not how many GPUs, nor how many gigawatts, nor even how much AI capex — but how much productivity and free cash flow does each additional dollar of AI capital create? That is the metric capable of separating infrastructure from overinvestment.

::pull THE GREAT SPLIT
The dividing line is no longer AI versus non-AI. It is becoming capital that produces productivity faster than WACC rises — versus capital whose return arrives too late.

Nvidia is reducing the size of its direct OpenAI financing guarantee while considering a $3 billion equity investment in the power-and-data-centre platform behind the same project. Anthropic's prospective IPO is being valued against 2028 revenues. Hyperscalers have already issued almost $220 billion of bonds this year. The AI Supercycle is not slowing. *Data cut: 13:00 CEST | Sunday, August 16, 2026. Sunday edition: U.S. and European cash markets are closed. Today's Pulse combines Friday's closing prices with new developments reported through Sunday morning.*

The most important AI development this weekend is not another processor. It is a change in who carries the financial risk of the processor economy.

Nvidia is reportedly discussing an investment of up to $3 billion in SB Energy, the SoftBank subsidiary developing OpenAI's enormous Ohio data-centre campus. Roughly half could be invested when the project agreement is signed and half in a planned SB Energy IPO that could raise at least $5 billion. The discussions sit alongside approximately $100 billion of credit support being considered for the project.

But this follows Friday's report that Nvidia expects to provide an initial guarantee of less than $120 billion, compared with as much as $250 billion previously discussed, after investors raised concerns about the chipmaker's exposure. The proposed Ohio campus is a 10-gigawatt project. This is not retreat. It is *risk redistribution*.

The financing structure is moving from one corporate balance sheet toward vendor support + infrastructure equity + IPO capital + institutional credit + project finance + long-duration leases. That transition tells us where the AI Supercycle is heading.

::signals Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy | risk, valuation, capital and energy
# AI Financing Is Moving From Concentrated Guarantees to Distributed Risk
Nvidia has reportedly reduced the initial Ohio guarantee to less than $120 billion from as much as $250 billion, while separately considering a $3 billion investment in SB Energy. SB Energy could seek at least $5 billion through an IPO, and the parties are discussing around $100 billion of credit support. Nvidia has also created financing platforms with six major institutional investors targeting more than $500 billion of third-party AI infrastructure capital.

The capital requirement has become too large to remain concentrated on technology-company balance sheets. Risk is being unbundled along a chain: equity sponsor → project company → vendor support → customer lease → private credit → infrastructure debt → public equity → institutional refinancing. AI infrastructure is becoming a capital market, not merely a capex programme. The question shifts from "Does AI demand exist?" to "Who owns the residual risk if utilization, pricing or technological value falls short?"
signal: AI infrastructure is becoming a capital market — the residual risk, not the demand, is now the decisive variable.

# AI Valuation Is Moving Further Into the Future
Anthropic is preparing investors for a potential IPO using a projected $190–200 billion of 2028 revenue, compared with a run rate above $47 billion reported in May. Bankers are reportedly considering multiples based on forward forecasts rather than current earnings. Anthropic has projected at least $10.9 billion of Q2 revenue and its first quarterly operating profit.

This is a textbook duration trade. What is unusual is the combination of extraordinary future growth assumptions, extraordinary present capital intensity, and historically expensive long-duration money. The valuation chain — 2028 revenue → future margin → infrastructure efficiency → capital requirement → discount rate → present value — compounds at every step. AI revenue expectations are accelerating; so are real yields. One increases terminal value, the other discounts it.
signal: The Great Split is now inside the valuation formula itself.

# AI Capital Formation Is Starting to Raise Its Own Hurdle Rate
Alphabet, Amazon and Meta have issued almost $220 billion of bonds in 2026, already more than double their 2025 total. U.S. 30-year real yields are near 3%, with German and UK real yields near decade highs. Reuters reports investors increasingly identify heavy AI borrowing alongside government issuance as a driver of competition for long-duration capital.

AI is now large enough to affect demand for capital itself. The feedback loop: AI investment → bond issuance → competition for savings → higher real yields → higher WACC → higher required AI ROIC. This is the first endogenous financial constraint of the AI Supercycle. Capex can eventually become self-limiting even if technological demand remains strong — not because investors stop believing in AI, but because AI requires too much capital simultaneously with governments.
signal: The more successful capital formation becomes, the higher the return threshold on the next dollar.

# Capital Moves Toward Europe as Industrial Capital Turns Geopolitically Selective
European equity funds attracted $13.52 billion in the latest week, far above U.S. inflows of $2.58 billion. Yet German companies invested only €4.3 billion directly in the United States in H1 2026 — nearly two-thirds less than a year earlier and almost 80% below 2024. The STOXX 600 remains within roughly 1% of its record, with Q2 earnings expected to increase 23.4%, the strongest in almost four years.

Financial capital is moving into Europe; European industrial capital is becoming more cautious about moving into America. That is The Great Split applied to cross-border capital. Europe has an unusual opening — to attract global financial capital while retaining more of its industrial capital at home — but only if it converts that into accelerated computing, energy infrastructure, advanced manufacturing, defence, mobility and Industrial AI.
signal: Europe's question is whether it can turn a capital-flow advantage into a capital-formation advantage.

# Energy Security Is Becoming Explicit Sovereign Industrial Policy
India ordered domestic refiners to target a maximum 63,810 tonnes of LPG production per day while maintaining storage and transport buffers; before the Iran war disrupted the Strait of Hormuz it sourced roughly 90% of LPG imports from the Middle East. The U.S. is preparing additional pressure on Iran, potentially targeting oil shippers, Chinese refiners and financial intermediaries; China bought more than 80% of Iran's shipped oil per 2025 Kpler data. Brent settled Friday at $88.52, up 6% for the week, with Hormuz traffic severely constrained.

Energy security is now managed as national resilience architecture — domestic production targets, inventories, transport capacity, diversified sourcing. Every country building sovereign accelerated-computing capacity must answer the same question: can intelligence keep operating when energy supply is politically disrupted? The sovereign AI stack becomes chips + data + grids + generation + storage + logistics + capital.
signal: Without the energy layer, technological sovereignty is incomplete.

::patterns Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines | structural currents beneath the tape
# The AI Value Chain Is Becoming a Risk-Transfer Chain
The old chain was technological: model → processor → data centre → application. The emerging chain is financial: manufacturer → vendor finance → developer → infrastructure fund → bank → bond market → sovereign investor. If hardware becomes collateral for loans, investors must price the resale and utilization value of a rapidly depreciating asset — fundamentally different from lending against railways, airports or pipelines.

# The Great Split Is Now Earnings Versus Consumption
S&P 500 earnings grew roughly 52% in Q2, while July retail sales fell 0.6%, sentiment declined to 51.0 and July payrolls contracted. AI raises productivity and margins; the consumer supplies final demand. Profit acceleration without income propagation is powerful financially, but unstable economically.

# The Long Bond Is Becoming the Real AI Benchmark
The Fed can pause in September; that does not make AI capital inexpensive. Thirty-year real yields remain near 3% and the latest long-bond auction cleared at 5.22%. The equation is now: real long yield + credit spread + project risk + technological depreciation = AI hurdle rate.

# Central Banks Are Building Their Own Barbell
Central banks bought a net 289 tonnes of gold in Q2 — five times Q1 and a second-quarter record worth an estimated $45 billion — while China added 20 tonnes in July and 45% of central banks expect to add more within a year. Private markets buy future productivity; central banks buy monetary resilience. The common denominator is scarcity.

# Europe's Physical Economy Is Acquiring a New Risk Premium
Europe's fifth heatwave of the year is widening the insurance gap: Moody's estimates last summer's heat-related losses at about €43 billion versus only ~€500 million of insured payouts, and a Padua survey found over 80% of hospitality operators reporting ~20% revenue declines. Heat affects productivity, power, water, transport and insurance — and therefore industrial ROIC.

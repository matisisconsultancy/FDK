The AI Supercycle has encountered a new test of its financial architecture: the extraordinary concentration of technological capabilities, strategic decisions and capital commitments within a small number of companies.

On November 12, Nvidia confirmed that co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang had resigned, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO, Harvey C. Jones became the first separate board chair, and a search for a permanent chief executive began. The same day, Applied Materials, Cisco and Foxconn each reported powerful growth. Today, November 13, European leaders convene in Dublin for their first dedicated strategic discussion of AI.

The evidence is not consistent with a simple narrative of collapsing AI demand. It points toward increasing differentiation between operating fundamentals, technological dependencies and financial valuations.

::signal
- **Nvidia's governance shock** — Huang resigned as CEO and director; Kress is interim chief, a material corporate event that does not verify the underlying allegations.
- **Supplier earnings stayed strong** — Applied Materials grew revenue 53%, Cisco 24%, and Foxconn 46%, all predating the leadership change.
- **Markets diverged sharply** — Nvidia fell ~4.3% on November 12 while AMD rose ~5.9% and Broadcom ~5.5%, without confirmed order transfers.
- **Supply stays tight** — SMIC expects AI-related semiconductor supply constrained through 2027, with some equipment lead times exceeding 24 months.
- **Europe elevated AI** — Dublin's summit is a political milestone, but no binding outcome, Gigafactory award or operational infrastructure is verified.
- **The central question** — valuation shocks differ from operating deterioration, and concentration is becoming a priced investment risk.

::section Nvidia: The Governance Shock | at the heart of the AI economy
Nvidia's economic position extends beyond processors to hardware architectures, networking, software environments, AI frameworks and a deep partner ecosystem built over years. A company can hold considerable advantages while facing a material reassessment of management continuity and execution.

The transmission runs: leadership uncertainty → reassessment of execution risk → higher equity risk premium → valuation changes → possible capital-allocation adjustments. Nvidia's next results on **November 17** are an important near-term test. The distinction for investors is governance-related repricing versus deterioration in operating economics.

*NER-C: Major verified governance inflection. No verified equivalent disruption to operating semiconductor demand or capacity.*

::section Applied Materials: The Investment Engine | record orders challenge the collapse narrative
Applied Materials reported Q4 revenue of **$10.41B (+53% YoY)**, GAAP operating income of $3.66B (35.1% margin) and operating cash flow of $3.30B. Semiconductor-systems revenue reached ~$8B (+64%); Q1 FY2027 guidance is ~$11.1B ±$0.5B. The quarter ended October 25, predating Nvidia's change — strong evidence the cycle entered November from substantial industrial demand.

Geographically, Taiwan ($2.92B, 28%) surpassed China ($2.64B, 25%, down from 29%) as the largest market; South Korea $1.81B (17%), US $1.32B (13%), Europe $520M (5%).

*NER-C: Major new earnings evidence. The physical investment cycle remains strong; long-term customer capex requires monitoring.*

::section Cisco and Foxconn: The Great Split | different positions in the same supercycle
Cisco reported Q1 FY2027 revenue of **$18.5B (+24%)**, GAAP operating income +66% (30.2% margin) and networking revenue +43%. It raised its FY2027 hyperscaler AI infrastructure revenue expectation to **$9B**, more than double FY2026.

Foxconn reported Q3 revenue of **NT$3.02T (+46%)**, operating profit NT$115.4B (+64%, 3.83% margin) and net profit NT$77.5B (+34%). Manufacturing and integration carry heavy component, production and working-capital costs — a different financial architecture from IP- or equipment-intensive businesses. The supercycle expands the profit pool, but participation does not guarantee equal access to profits.

*NER-C: The Great Split is visible in business-model economics; no permanent profit hierarchy established.*

::section China and Productivity: The Constraints Persist | supply and the evidence gap
On November 13, SMIC Co-CEO Zhao Haijun said AI-related supply chains would likely remain constrained through 2027, with memory nearing balance in mid-to-late 2028 and some equipment lead times beyond 24 months. Investment constraints can remain material even where manufacturers have financial capacity to expand.

Separately, these results do not establish economy-wide productivity gains attributable to AI. Higher computing consumption does not measure realized economic benefit. BloombergNEF's forecast of ~2,354 TWh of data-centre electricity demand by 2035 and ~181 GW of proposed on-site gas generation are pipelines, not commissioned capacity.

*NER-C: New industrial earnings evidence. Economy-wide AI productivity baseline unchanged.*

::section Europe 2030: The First Leaders' AI Summit | Dublin elevates competitiveness
Today's informal European Council in Dublin is the EU leaders' first dedicated AI discussion, with Mistral CEO Arthur Mensch, covering dependencies, infrastructure, clean energy, private investment and market concentration. No binding outcome is verified. The Cloud and AI Development Act remains in legislative process.

The EuroHPC Gigafactories timetable is unchanged: Lot 2 due December 3, 2026 and Lot 1 due February 4, 2027 (17:00 Luxembourg). The program envisages up to seven facilities, up to €10B public support and at least €20B anticipated private investment — intended framework, not completed expenditure.

*NER-C: Major new political-agenda development. No binding summit outcome, award or operational inflection.*

::takeaways Five key takeaways | for investors, boards and policymakers
- The AI Supercycle faces a governance shock, not a verified demand collapse.
- Financial value is distributed unevenly across the technology stack.
- Technological concentration is becoming a more important investment risk.
- Europe's opportunity lies in strategic specialization combined with resilience.
- Capital productivity remains the ultimate measure of success.

::close
The AI Supercycle has been built on concentration. Its next financial advantage may come from ==mastering interdependence==—preserving technological leadership while reducing the economic cost of strategic vulnerability.

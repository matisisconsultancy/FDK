Five numbers define the regime. The U.S. 10-year Treasury touched 5.23%. U.S. computer-related capital-goods orders rose 20.1% year on year. Anthropic committed $11.6 billion to Akamai cloud infrastructure. Brent remains above $100. French sovereign spreads widened beyond 110 basis points over Germany.

They describe the same collision: **AI demand ↑ → capex ↑ → capital required ↑**, while **energy risk ↑ → yields ↑ → hurdle rate ↑**. AI demand is accelerating inside a more expensive capital regime.

The decisive question is: *who can turn AI capex into free cash flow fast enough to outrun the hurdle rate?* That is today's Great Split — ==Productive AI Capital vs. Capital Consumption==.

::pull THE GREAT SPLIT
The market is separating scarcity, contracted demand and cash flow from capex, leverage and distant monetization.

::signals Five Signals | the regime in detail
# Earnings — AI Still Outruns Duration
AI-linked equities remain resilient despite Treasury yields above 5%.
signal: Retain exposure where earnings revisions and free cash flow support the multiple.

# Capex — AI Is Now an Industrial Cycle
Core U.S. capital-goods orders rose 1.6%, with computer-related orders up 20.1% year on year.
signal: Manage compute, power and networking as one industrial capital-allocation system.

# Contracts — Visibility Earns the Premium
Anthropic's $11.6 billion Akamai commitment validates long-duration AI demand.
signal: Favor capacity backed by secured customers, power and utilization.

# Energy — Power Is Part of the Discount Rate
Brent remains above $100, affecting AI through both operating economics and financing costs.
signal: Secure power is a strategic asset.

# Europe — Capital Conversion Is the Bottleneck
European sovereign spreads are widening as strategic investment needs in AI, grids, defense and infrastructure rise.
signal: Europe must measure time to productive capacity, not announced capex.

::section The Hidden Pattern | selective acceleration
The regime is **Selective Acceleration**. AI demand remains powerful, but capital is becoming less forgiving.

The market is separating *scarcity + contracted demand + cash flow* from *capex + leverage + distant monetization*. The next phase will reward companies that convert capital into economic output fastest.

::patterns Capital Allocation Hierarchy | four tiers
# [Tier 1] Own the Bottleneck — Core Overweight
Accelerated computing, HBM/memory, networking, optics, grid, power and connected capacity. Overweight while scarcity, utilization and pricing power remain intact.

# [Tier 2] Own the Conversion — Selective Overweight
Hyperscalers and AI infrastructure with secure power, contracted demand and strong balance sheets. Add only while incremental FCF keeps pace with incremental AI capex.

# [Tier 3] Own Monetization — Selective
Agentic AI and applications with distribution, proprietary data and pricing power. Build exposure only when monetization grows faster than compute cost.

# [Tier 4] Avoid Capital Traps — Watch / Underweight
Leveraged, long-duration AI dependent on refinancing, uncertain utilization or unresolved power. Stay underweight while economic proof depends on lower rates or future capital raises.

::section For Boards of Directors | the governance agenda
Treat AI as capital allocation — not technology procurement. Put compute, power and financing on the same dashboard. Measure time-to-cash-flow, not only deployment speed. Stress-test higher rates, higher energy costs and lower utilization. Eliminate single points of failure across cloud, models, power and capital.

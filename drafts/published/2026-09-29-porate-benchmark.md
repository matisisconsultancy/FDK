The AI Supercycle has entered its capital-allocation phase. The financial test is no longer whether companies can deploy more AI capital. It is whether that capital can generate cash before financing costs, depreciation and technological obsolescence consume the return.

With the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield at **5.24%** at the September 28 close, the hurdle has moved higher. Every dollar committed to compute, data centers, power infrastructure or frontier models now has to convert into utilization and free cash flow fast enough to preserve economic spread over the cost of capital.

At the same time, the return pool is fragmenting. Model capability is proliferating and inference costs are falling, while advanced packaging, power, cooling and grid access remain constrained. Scarcity is therefore migrating toward the physical layers where pricing power is stronger and capacity takes longer to reproduce.

::section The Fundamental Question | Beneath today's ranking
Which corporations can convert scarce AI capacity into cash fast enough to widen ROIC above the cost of capital before depreciation and obsolescence close the gap?

The next AI winners will be the companies that capture the widest spread between ROIC and cost of capital — and sustain it as the stack commoditizes. The ranking is increasingly separating companies that merely participate in AI from those positioned to capture scarcity rents or convert AI investment into durable economic returns.

::signals Two Decisive Structural Drivers | What separates leaders now
# Physical scarcity is redistributing AI economics
The next constraints increasingly sit in advanced packaging → memory → networking → power → cooling → grid access. These layers cannot scale at software speed, creating a transmission mechanism of scarcity → pricing power → utilization → cash conversion → ROIC. TSMC captures manufacturing scarcity, Schneider Electric captures power architecture, Siemens Energy sits against generation and grid constraints, and Vertiv monetizes thermal density.
signal: The second-order AI trade is everything compute cannot operate without.

# Capital productivity is the decisive financial filter
The next divide will not be between companies that invest in AI and those that do not. It will be between capital that compounds — capex → utilization → cash flow → ROIC → reinvestment — and capital that merely accumulates — capex → capacity → depreciation → financing cost → margin pressure. The question is no longer how much is being spent, but how rapidly each incremental dollar becomes productive free cash flow.
signal: That is the emerging hurdle rate of the AI Supercycle.

::section The Backlog Repricing | Nominal versus economic backlog
Not all backlog has equal economic value. The chain runs announced → contracted → financed → powered → utilized → cash-generating. At the 5.24% September 28 close, every delay between commitment and monetization carries a real duration cost.

Powered backlog is worth more than announced backlog. Utilized capacity is worth more than installed capacity. Cash-generating infrastructure is worth more than financed infrastructure. The next divide is **nominal backlog versus economic backlog**.

::signals Three Forward Signals | Where monetization is decided

::section Ranking vs. Investment Verdict | GVI measures structural velocity; valuation does not affect rank
GVI answers which corporations are shaping the Next Economy fastest. The investment verdict identifies what still has to go right — it does not prescribe portfolio action.

**Quality Confirmed** — Microsoft · TSMC · Schneider Electric: high and rising structure, visible economic capture; durability must be proven. **Expectations Sensitive** — Nvidia · Palantir · Meta: strong economics that must keep outperforming. **Economics to Prove** — Amazon · Anthropic: high structure, cash conversion incomplete. **Higher-Risk Optionality** — Alibaba: capture less certain. Palo Alto Networks remains best read as a second-order monetization beneficiary of rising agentic-security intensity.

The investor action framework runs GVI → economic capture → investment verdict → valuation → portfolio decision. Increase conviction when structural velocity and economic capture improve faster than expectations; maintain when economics stay strong but expectations are demanding; reduce when expectations rise faster than velocity or cash conversion; reassess when structural velocity itself breaks. The investor decides whether the price compensates for the burden.

::takeaways Key Takeaways | The economic spread, not the spend
- Physical scarcity is becoming the first great redistribution mechanism of AI economics.
- The value of AI increasingly moves toward the layers that cannot scale at software speed.
- Capital productivity — not capex scale — is becoming the decisive financial test.
- Backlog quality increasingly depends on conversion into powered, utilized, cash-generating capacity.
- The next market leaders should be those that combine scarce assets with superior capital-to-cash conversion.

::close The first AI winners proved that intelligence could scale; the next phase decides who captures the return. Scarcity determines where pricing power forms — capital productivity determines who keeps it. ==Follow the cash, not the capex.==

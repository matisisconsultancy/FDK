The Italian banking contest has moved one layer deeper.

First it was about ownership. Then equity currency. Then time. Yesterday it became about the Execution Premium. Today, we can see what execution actually costs.

A new report based on MPS correspondence with Consob says the proposed MPS–Banco BPM combination assumes roughly 5,000 voluntary early-retirement exits to generate up to €400 million of personnel savings, while antitrust remedies could require between 57 and 115 branch disposals, depending on the market-share threshold applied. The same correspondence reportedly says MPS intends to acquire from Mediobanca the entire 13.3% Generali stake before the October 29 shareholder meeting. These details have not been separately published in full by MPS; they were reported by *la Repubblica* from documents it says it reviewed.

::pull SCALE NOW HAS A PRICE
That matters because the strategic debate is no longer abstract. Scale is paid through redundant costs, branch remedies, shareholder dilution, governance concessions, and — increasingly — political capital.

::section The Brussels Counter-Current | scale versus its cost
At the same time, Brussels is moving in the opposite rhetorical direction. On 18 September, EU finance ministers and central bankers discussed a European Commission report calling for less political interference in bank mergers and fewer barriers to cross-border banking.

ECB Vice President Boris Vujcic argued that European banks need greater scale and deeper capital markets to compete with U.S. institutions.

That is today's fundamental contradiction: **Europe wants bigger banks. National systems must absorb the cost of making them bigger.**

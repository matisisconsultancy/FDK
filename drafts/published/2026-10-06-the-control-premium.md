The AI market is crossing from an investment cycle into an economic-selection cycle. The next winners will not be determined by who has the largest model, the most GPUs or the biggest capex budget. They will be determined by who can turn scarce infrastructure, software reach and distribution into higher margins, stronger cash generation and superior returns on capital. That is the structural break.

For the last two years, investors were paid for owning AI capacity. From here, they will increasingly be paid for owning AI systems that monetize capacity better than competitors. Three forces are converging: power is becoming the gating constraint, capital is becoming part of the competitive model, and integration is becoming the real moat.

The investment consequence is decisive. The premium is moving from capacity to conversion. Companies that can raise Structural Velocity while also improving ROIC and free-cash-flow conversion should command the next premium. Companies that require ever more capital simply to maintain growth should not.

::section Anthropic — The Financial Hurdle | GVI 97.4 | #11 overall
Anthropic remains in the Global Velocity Leader regime because its score exceeds the 97.0 threshold, yet it ranks #11 overall, below Alibaba at #10 with 98.1, and therefore sits outside the relative Top 10. Its financial hurdle is increasingly concrete: it is committing to large, multi-year compute obligations while revenue, margins and cash generation still need to catch up with that fixed-cost base.

The key risk is not weak demand for its models. It is revenue growing quickly, but not quickly enough to outrun infrastructure commitments and financing costs. The proof points are explicit — revenue growth must outpace fixed compute commitments, gross margins must improve as inference and training scale, free cash flow must inflect before financing dependence becomes structural, customer concentration must decline, and long-duration capacity commitments must remain sufficiently reversible.

Anthropic's hurdle is not proving AI relevance. It is proving that revenue, margins and cash flow can scale faster than compute obligations. Its #11 position reflects a widening gap between exceptional innovation velocity and still-unproven financial conversion.

::section The Two Hard Tests | power and capital
**Power.** The AI buildout increasingly depends on electricity availability as much as compute capacity. The chain runs Silicon → Memory → Networking → Power → Data → Software, and a delay in one layer can defer returns across the rest.

**Capital.** Amazon and Broadcom show that access to compute can increasingly be separated from ownership. That can improve flexibility, but only when financing costs remain below the value created. Scale is valuable only when the economics survive the financing structure.

For Schneider, PTC pushes the perimeter toward Power → Automation → Engineering Data → Industrial Software → AI. The strategy deserves a higher GVI score; the security requires stronger financial proof — cross-selling, software-margin expansion, debt reduction and post-deal ROIC. The structural thesis weakens materially if capital intensity stays high, cash conversion deteriorates and pricing power fades.

::takeaways Five Market Takeaways | from capacity to conversion
- **Integrated platforms are taking the premium** — the market is shifting from isolated AI assets toward companies that combine infrastructure, software and distribution into one economic system.
- **Power is becoming the real deployment bottleneck** — compute without electricity cannot monetize; grid access now determines how quickly capex turns into revenue.
- **Financing quality matters as much as financing access** — external capital can accelerate growth or simply shift obligations forward.
- **Strategic expansion raises the return hurdle** — Schneider is the clearest example: stronger positioning, less room for execution error.
- **Return on capital remains the final test** — the winners will turn strategic advantage into durable cash generation.

::close The Next Economy will be won by the corporations that turn strategic position into ==superior returns with the least capital friction==.

The AI race is moving from technological leadership to economic capture. The next winners will control the bottlenecks, convert them into cash, and sustain returns above the cost of capital.

With the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield at **5.17%**, capital is no longer forgiving. The market is entering a more discriminating phase: velocity identifies the leaders, economic capture determines the quality, valuation determines the return.

The question is no longer simply who has the best AI. It is: ==who keeps the economics after compute, power, security, infrastructure and capital costs are paid?==

::signals What Comes Next | Where the rent migrates
# The bottleneck moves outside the GPU
GPUs were the first scarcity. The next constraints increasingly sit in packaging, memory, networking, power, cooling and grid access. As model capability becomes more abundant, economic rent migrates toward layers that cannot expand as quickly.

Implication: TSMC, Schneider Electric and the wider power/cooling ecosystem gain strategic leverage.
signal: The next AI premium goes to whoever removes the next constraint.
# Intelligence gets cheaper. Distribution gets more valuable.
Model competition is intensifying, inference costs are falling, and capability gaps should continue narrowing. The second-order consequence: the strategic scarcity may shift from who owns intelligence to who owns the customer — favoring Microsoft (enterprise workflows), Meta (consumer distribution), Amazon (cloud + commerce) and Alibaba (China's digital ecosystem).
signal: Distribution could become more durable than model leadership itself.
# Agents create the next infrastructure wave
Training powered the first compute boom; persistent agents could drive the second. More agents mean more inference, more networking, more power and more security — creating simultaneous demand across Nvidia, Schneider Electric, cloud infrastructure and cybersecurity.
signal: The agent economy expands the AI stack rather than replacing it.

::section The Capital-Efficiency Test | Two forms of AI capital
The next divide is increasingly between two forms of AI capital.

**Capital that compounds:** Capex → Utilization → Cash Flow → ROIC → Reinvestment.

**Capital that accumulates:** Capex → Capacity → Depreciation → Financing Cost → Margin Pressure.

The distinction is decisive. The market should increasingly reward AI capital productivity — not AI spending.

::section The Backlog Repricing | Nominal versus economic
Not all backlog has equal economic value: Announced → Contracted → Financed → Powered → Utilized → Cash-Generating. Each step removes risk and increases economic quality.

The market should increasingly distinguish between nominal backlog and economic backlog. Powered, prepaid, high-utilization demand deserves a premium. An unpowered promise does not.

::signals Three Explicit Downside Scenarios | The transmission channels
# The capex glut
AI capacity grows faster than utilization. Transmission: excess capacity → pricing pressure → slower FCF conversion → weaker ROIC. Warning signal: AI capex materially outpacing incremental revenue and free cash flow.
signal: The most exposed are those financing capacity before demand becomes productive.
# The rate shock
Long rates remain elevated — or move higher. Transmission: higher discount rates → higher project hurdle rates → multiple compression → weaker infrastructure economics. At a 5.17% 10-year yield, duration already matters.
signal: A further increase accelerates the divide between self-funded leaders and externally financed challengers.
# Model commoditization
Frontier-model capability converges faster than monetization. Transmission: falling model pricing → margin compression → value migration toward distribution, infrastructure and proprietary workflows. Potential beneficiaries: Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, TSMC, Schneider Electric.
signal: If intelligence commoditizes, customer ownership and bottleneck ownership become more valuable.

::section The New Investors' Playbook | Risk → Cash → ROIC → Valuation
**1. Control the risk.** Governance, resilience and execution must protect capital first. Positive signals: Microsoft, Palantir, Palo Alto Networks.

**2. Convert the cash.** Capacity, adoption and backlog must become utilization and free cash flow. Positive signals: TSMC, Schneider Electric, Microsoft.

**3. Earn excess ROIC.** Incremental returns must sustainably exceed company-specific WACC. Growth without excess ROIC is capital accumulation — not value creation.

**4. Price the expectations.** Only after the first three tests should valuation determine the investment verdict.

::section Methodology | GVI = Structural Velocity
The FDK Global Velocity Index — Corporate Benchmark measures one thing: how rapidly a corporation is increasing its capacity to create future economic value. It does not directly rank securities by valuation or expected return.

The score combines ten weighted pillars: Innovation Velocity 14% · Capital Formation 12% · Productivity Acceleration 10% · AI Adoption & Deployment 10% · Infrastructure Position 10% · Ecosystem Strength 10% · Resilience 9% · Talent Intensity 8% · Strategic Optionality 9% · Execution Quality 8%. Each pillar is scored 0–100.

Composite formula for corporation i: GVIᵢ = 0.14(INNOV) + 0.12(CAPFORM) + 0.10(PROD) + 0.10(AIADOPT) + 0.10(INFRA) + 0.10(ECOSYS) + 0.09(RESIL) + 0.08(TALENT) + 0.09(OPTION) + 0.08(EXEC). Daily change ΔGVIᵢ,t = GVIᵢ,t − GVIᵢ,t−1. A score moves only when new evidence materially changes structural velocity; a stock-price move alone does not change GVI. The Top-10 Composite is ΣGVIᵢ,t / 10 for the ten highest-ranked corporations.

Evidence architecture: ~70% measured evidence — revenue, margins, free cash flow, capex, backlog, capacity, utilization, adoption and execution milestones; ~30% FDK proprietary data analytics — capital productivity, cash-conversion velocity, ecosystem control, bottleneck scarcity, resilience, optionality, concentration risk and probability of conversion into productive cash-generating assets. Economic capture is embedded inside the pillars — especially Productivity, Capital Formation, Infrastructure, Ecosystem and Execution — not calculated as a second score.

Valuation remains outside the GVI. GVI = Structural Velocity. Economic Capture = Quality of Monetization. Valuation = Price Paid for Future Velocity. Investment Verdict = the intersection of all three. The ranking is determined by GVI Score only; valuation never changes the GVI ranking.

The most consequential development in global markets is the collision between an extraordinary appetite for risk and a rapidly deteriorating financing environment. Global equity funds attracted **$50.99 billion** in the week to November 11 — including **$35.13 billion** into European equities, the largest weekly inflow since June 2021 — even as US Treasuries suffered their worst weekly selloff in more than 18 months.

Investors are still financing the future. But the financial system is raising the price of doing so, precisely as the world's largest investment requirements — AI, sovereign refinancing and physical modernization — begin to converge on the same institutional savings.

::signal
- **Capital is chasing the future** while the bond market demands higher returns — the central contradiction of the current regime.
- **The 10-year Treasury yield** rose ~28bp to around 5.14%, the sharpest selloff in 18 months, as October PPI ran at 6.5% year-on-year.
- **Global equity funds drew $50.99bn**, with a record €-equivalent $35.13bn into European equities since June 2021.
- **Anthropic targets up to $64.4bn** of equity against reported computing commitments near $900bn — the AI Supercycle's public-market test.
- **Friday's FAA shutdown** forced over 4,700 cancellations, exposing the economic cost of deferred infrastructure modernization.
- **Europe may need €500bn** for competitive AI, yet Dublin's summit closed without a funded investment settlement.

::pull THE CENTRAL THESIS
The emerging divide concerns the opportunity cost of capital itself — which companies, sectors and sovereigns can generate returns sufficient to attract investment when the claims on global savings are multiplying.

::signals Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy | the forces competing for capital
# The Bond Market's Revolt
The 10-year yield rose ~28bp while October PPI hit 6.5% year-on-year, the highest since November 2022. The repricing is occurring just as corporate and sovereign financing needs rise together; Reuters reports €40–60bn of further tech-related issuance expected in European markets, a crowding-out risk, not yet an established outcome.
signal: Favour high-quality earnings, strong coverage and pricing power — do not mistake abundant liquidity for permanently cheap capital.

# Anthropic and Nvidia
Anthropic's proposed IPO reportedly covered roughly six times, targeting up to $64.4bn plus a $26bn private placement, with a Nasdaq debut expected November 19. Concern centres on whether commercial revenues can support ~$900bn of reported computing commitments. Nvidia remains under investigation following Jensen Huang's resignation.
signal: The next AI leaders will be distinguished by financial independence and demonstrable returns, not computational scale.

# The FAA Shutdown
A communications failure forced a nationwide suspension of departures on November 13, lasting over four hours, with more than 4,700 cancellations and ~10,000 delays. Reporting attributes it to aging equipment overloaded during an upgrade; no cyberattack indicated.
signal: Infrastructure resilience is becoming an investable source of economic value, not merely a maintenance obligation.

# Europe's Unfunded Ambition
Dublin's AI summit exposed unresolved financing: Commission estimates suggest at least €500bn is needed. Eurozone Q3 GDP rose 0.4% with flat employment; Italy stagnant, France -0.1%. S&P affirmed Italy at BBB+ with a positive outlook, while Rome's proposed 2027 bank levies drew industry opposition.
signal: Europe will not close its AI gap through announcements alone — it must improve the productivity of capital deployment.

# Energy, China and Industrial Mobility
Brent settled at $95.84, down ~2.5% on the week, with the IEA pointing to a growing surplus. China's trade surplus topped $1 trillion over January–October, semiconductor exports up over 100%. Siemens Mobility's FY26 orders rose 56% to €26.57bn, backlog ~€65bn.
signal: Durable industrial opportunities will be measured by lifecycle economic returns, not initial capital expenditure.

::patterns Four Hidden Patterns | beyond the headlines
# Competition for Long-Duration Capital
AI, sovereign refinancing and infrastructure increasingly need the same long-horizon funding. The effect may arrive gradually — through wider spreads, shorter maturities and lower appetite for marginal borrowers.

# Capital Moves Toward Europe — But The Problem Persists
The $35.13bn weekly inflow shows appetite, not solved financing. Portfolio inflows and productive capital formation are different phenomena.

# The Physical Economy's Share of the AI Dividend
Technology suppliers may profit while infrastructure developers absorb financing costs. The next productivity dividend may come as much from preventing disruption as from new capacity.

# The Cost of Economic Progress
The Great Split is becoming a divide between productivity winners — strong cash flow, low financing friction — and capital-constrained economies facing higher spreads and delayed modernization.

::patterns Three Scenarios for the Next Phase | illustrative, three-month horizon
# [45%] Selective Capital Reallocation
AI demand stays strong; investors favour financial resilience, industrial productivity and credible infrastructure cash flows. The central scenario.

# [35%] The Capital-Cost Squeeze
Persistent inflation, heavy issuance and rising spreads crowd out marginal investment, particularly weaker borrowers.

# [20%] Productivity-Led Expansion
AI monetization and industrial efficiency lift returns enough to absorb higher capital costs.

::takeaways Five Key Takeaways | for investors, boards and policymakers
- **Equity investors:** follow capital productivity, not the scale of capital expenditure.
- **Credit investors:** competition for long-duration financing is structural — duration, quality and maturity matching are critical.
- **Corporate boards:** infrastructure resilience is now a financial imperative, not deferred maintenance.
- **European policymakers:** financial integration must deliver productive investment, not announcements.
- **Global investors:** the Great Split is becoming a divide in the cost of progress.

::close The Next Economy is not constrained by a lack of ambition but by the ==rising opportunity cost of financing it== — technology determines what can be built, capital what can be financed, and productivity what deserves to be.

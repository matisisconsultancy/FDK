China is asking individuals to build AI companies. Europe should ask its factories, railways, ports and utilities to become their first customers.

The Chinese campaign lowers the cost of starting a company so more people can discover where AI creates value. A maintenance engineer in Turin can build a tool that helps technicians diagnose equipment failures. The breakthrough comes when a factory pays to use it, a second factory in Stuttgart can verify the result without repeating the review, and a lender can finance delivery against a signed order.

::signal
- **Europe's edge is demand**, not just compute: make industrial purchasing power accessible across borders and financeable on real contracts.
- **AI adoption is uneven** — 20% of EU firms in 2025, Denmark at 42%, Italy near 16.5% — so proven solutions must travel.
- **Procurement is the lever**: public procurement exceeds 14% of EU GDP and can give small suppliers verifiable reference customers.
- **Evidence must cross borders**: a second buyer reuses the first deployment's tests and reviews only site-specific integration.
- **Finance the purchase order**: milestone payments and lending against signed orders link European capital to industrial deployment.
- **The payoff is a rerating**: recurring earnings gains in industrials and specialist software broaden the European equity case.

::section The Policy Choice Europe Faces | match the ambition
China's AI Plus strategy makes the spread of AI an explicit policy goal, and the one-person company movement adds a decentralized mechanism: individuals with models and agents can test opportunities a ministry or incumbent would struggle to identify. Europe can match that by opening its industrial base to founders who know where factories lose time, transport networks lose capacity and utilities lose energy.

Today a small supplier spends much of its early capital repeating integration work, negotiating data terms and answering similar security questions for each buyer. Europe needs frontier compute and model capability — but also a market where those capabilities reach ordinary businesses quickly. The practical test of the single market is whether the second deployment costs less and happens faster than the first.

::section Four Decisions for Policymakers | from purchasing power to deployment
**Use procurement to create the first customer.** The Commission should convene buyers from several member states around a few industrial problems — machine maintenance, freight and port operations, energy management — with committed budgets for paid, competitive trials. Each buyer specifies the operating result it wants, the evidence it will accept and the route to production. A focused use of public procurement could give small suppliers reference customers with verifiable results.

**Make the evidence travel.** Buyers in each sector should agree common performance tests, security documentation and model-switching requirements, so a second buyer reuses the first deployment's evidence and focuses only on site-specific integration and safety. The Apply AI strategy, AI Factories and testing facilities give Europe a base. A founder should carry an evaluation from Turin to Stuttgart as readily as a set of financial statements.

**Finance the purchase order.** Buyers can shorten the cash gap with milestone payments; banks and lenders can finance eligible signed orders or receivables where acceptance criteria, payment terms and buyer credit are clear. The EIB Group and national promotional institutions could test targeted risk-sharing with private lenders, giving the Savings and Investments Union a concrete transmission mechanism from European capital to deployment.

**Mobilize people who know industry.** Clusters, technical universities and employer associations should recruit maintenance engineers, production supervisors and logistics planners into short programs built around funded problems, with secure test data and a customer decision date. Measure them by paid deployments and repeat sales.

::section How This Changes the Investment Case | where the earnings appear
**Listed industrials** could see the first earnings effect — fewer downtime hours, less scrap, lower energy use, higher output from existing assets. The market can revise earnings when gains recur across sites and survive integration and support costs; the strongest players retain part of the savings through differentiated products and margins.

**Industrial software** could split in two. Suppliers embedded in critical workflows compound advantage through relationships, data and repeatable deployments; providers of generic features face pressure as models improve and platforms bundle. The question is whether recurring revenue grows while install-and-support cost per customer falls.

**Credit** can enter earlier once contracts are easier to underwrite. Standard acceptance criteria and dependable payment terms let lenders separate delivery risk from customer credit risk, letting founders reserve equity for growth. The test is whether private lenders keep financing orders as public risk-sharing steps back.

**Europe's equity case** could broaden through earnings — AI exposure via industrial companies and specialist software whose margins, cash conversion and returns on invested capital benefit from adoption, with a wider rerating depending on those results appearing across sectors and countries.

::takeaways The Numbers to Watch | the evidence that matters
- Time from paid trial to production contract — whether procurement is turning proof into revenue.
- Share of suppliers winning a second customer in another member state — whether the single market is cutting expansion cost.
- Deployment cost and time at the second site — whether founders can build scalable product businesses.
- Verified savings after integration and oversight costs — whether users translate adoption into operating profit.
- Private financing against signed contracts — whether the market can fund deployment on commercial terms.

::pull The fundamental question: can Europe turn a first purchase order into a continent-wide revenue opportunity?

The most consequential development in global markets this morning is the transmission of the AI selloff from equity valuations into leveraged positions, credit-risk pricing and international capital flows. This is a potentially decisive inflection point.

Wall Street's November 5 collapse erased roughly **$3 trillion** from S&P 500 market capitalization. The market is no longer questioning only the valuation of AI-related earnings; it is beginning to reassess the creditworthiness of the companies financing the investment cycle. An equity correction redistributes wealth — a funding shock can redistribute future productive capacity.

::signal
- **The AI correction is becoming a financing shock**, spreading from equity valuations into leverage, credit spreads and cross-border capital flows.
- **Wall Street erased ~$3 trillion** on November 5; the Nasdaq fell 5.52% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped more than 12%.
- **The shock reached Asia**, with Korea's KOSPI off more than 8% and triggering a circuit breaker, Japan's Nikkei sharply lower.
- **SoftBank's 5Y CDS hit 462 basis points**, its highest since October 2022, reflecting a reassessment of AI financiers' creditworthiness.
- **India drew ~$1.6 billion** of net foreign equity buying, its largest single day in nearly six years, as capital rotates from crowded AI trades.
- **Remain constructive on AI's productivity**, but financial resilience and control of scarce capabilities now decide where capital accumulates.

::signals Five Signals Shaping The Next Economy | from valuation to financing
# The AI Credit Transmission
Thursday's US collapse triggered forced selling across Asian tech; Korea temporarily eased margin rules and the KOSPI recovered part of its losses. Hong Kong's Aspex ($14bn) lost ~16% in October. SoftBank's 5Y CDS rose 17.4bp to 462 — the chain runs equity losses → collateral pressure → forced selling → wider spreads → costlier financing → delayed investment.
signal: The danger is markets constraining AI investment before technological demand deteriorates.

# Semiconductors — A Hidden Scarcity Cycle
The selloff obscures a structural shift: Yole expects advanced-packaging revenue to exceed traditional packaging for the first time in 2026, with capex near $28bn. China's YMTC targets up to 70% domestic equipment for a planned NAND plant — a target not yet achieved.
signal: The next winners may be set by cost per unit of intelligence, not chip volume.

# Energy And Monetary Policy
Brent rebounded toward $100 (~$99.58) but remained on course for a ~12% weekly decline amid Strait of Hormuz disruptions. The Bank of England raised rates to 4.00% (8–1), seeing UK inflation near 5% by mid-2027; eurozone inflation hit 4.0%, energy inflation 20.8%.
signal: A lower spot oil price does not immediately deliver easier monetary conditions.

# Europe — Sovereign–Credit Divide
The ECB's October survey showed a net 9% tightening of corporate credit standards. ACER reported 15 of 18 countries expect higher 2030 grid investment — the Netherlands nearly five times 2025 levels — colliding with restrictive financing.
signal: Europe's edge depends on integrating capital markets, energy infrastructure and industrial execution.

# The Global Capital Rotation
Indian equities advanced as the Nifty 50 gained ~0.5% and foreign investors bought ~$1.6bn — the largest single day in almost six years — helped by limited AI exposure and lower oil. But one day is not yet a durable trend.
signal: The rotation may broaden from financing intelligence to applying it across the real economy.

::patterns Three Scenarios For The Next Phase | illustrative three-month judgments
# [45%] The Selective Reset
AI valuations stabilize while financing remains available to strong borrowers; rotation toward cash flow and productivity.
# [35%] The Financing Feedback Loop
Wider credit spreads and weaker equities begin delaying investment; further pressure on leveraged infrastructure and suppliers.
# [20%] The Productivity Reacceleration
Strong demand and improving monetization restore confidence in returns; broader recovery across AI and industrial technology.

::takeaways Key Takeaways | for investors, boards and policymakers
- Today's US October payrolls (~+59,000 expected, unemployment near 4.2%) at 14:30 CET is the critical macro test.
- Equity investors: the next AI opportunity will be determined by financing quality, not demand alone.
- Credit investors: watch the transmission from equity valuations into funding conditions.
- Corporate boards: financial resilience is becoming a strategic asset — preserve capacity to invest through volatility.
- The Great Split is now about access to capital under stress.

::close
The AI Supercycle is entering its most consequential test: whether the capital markets that accelerated the revolution can keep financing it as the price of risk rises — and ==the next great market rotation will follow that distinction==.

The AI Supercycle has crossed a consequential threshold. Artificial intelligence is no longer simply the most powerful driver of technological investment — it has become one of the most closely scrutinized sources of potential financial instability.

The Federal Reserve's November 13 Financial Stability Report, a Treasury selloff, a record European capital rotation, two defining AI market tests and an overnight infrastructure shock point toward one conclusion: the next financial regime depends on whether AI can generate enough **independent economic returns** to support its financing without amplifying systemic vulnerability.

::signal
- **The Fed named AI a stability risk**, with 87% of surveyed contacts citing it versus 50% in May 2026.
- **Advanced-computing debt reached $680B** through October, and interconnected obligations could exceed $3 trillion.
- **Treasury yields surged**, the 10Y closing near 5.14% in the sharpest weekly move in over eighteen months.
- **US PPI rose 6.5% year-on-year**, the fastest since November 2022, despite a softer 0.3% monthly print.
- **Capital rotated rather than retreated**, with $50.99B into global equity funds and $35.13B into Europe.
- **Physical fragility returned**, as a five-hour US flight ground stop coincided with Brent at $95.84.

::signals The Five Signals | Where markets, capital and geopolitics collide
# The Fed: AI Becomes a Financial Stability Question
The Financial Stability Report shows 87% of surveyed contacts citing AI, up from 50% six months earlier. Advanced-computing debt issuance reached roughly $680 billion through October 2026 — about five times all of 2023. Combined obligations across credit, guarantees, SPVs and future leases could exceed $3 trillion. That is not a loss forecast or a single consolidated figure; the instruments differ in maturity, status and risk bearer. The central vulnerability is that ostensibly different exposures may depend on the same expectations about future AI demand.
signal: Treat the quality, transparency and independence of AI cash flows as critical valuation variables.

# The Bond Market: The Price of Financial Endurance
October PPI rose 0.3% monthly but 6.5% year-on-year, the fastest since November 2022. The ten-year yield finished Friday near 5.14%, up almost 30 basis points on the week — the most severe Treasury adjustment in over eighteen months. AI infrastructure spending stokes near-term demand for power, equipment, labor and capital before productivity benefits appear, tightening financial conditions. Resilient hyperscalers can invest through internal cash flow; leveraged developers face a harder equation.
signal: Distinguish falling tech valuations from a deeper deterioration in financing conditions.

# Europe: A $35B Rotation Meets a €500B Challenge
European equity funds absorbed $35.13 billion, their strongest weekly inflow since June 2021; the STOXX 600 closed at 664.14, up over 2% for the week. At the Dublin AI summit, Ursula von der Leyen estimated Europe needs at least €500 billion for computing, chips and frontier AI. But fund inflows are not corporate capex, and ambitions are not implementation — Europe must connect sovereign credibility, private capital and industrial productivity.
signal: Separate a financial rotation into Europe from genuine productive competitiveness.

# Nvidia and Anthropic: Two Defining Tests
Nvidia remains under pressure after Jensen Huang's November 12 resignation, with Colette Kress serving as interim CEO; shares fell a sixth straight week. Its November 17 earnings test whether operating demand offsets governance uncertainty. Anthropic's offering — gross value near $64 billion, expected to price November 18 — reportedly drew roughly 6× demand, but oversubscription is not completed fundraising. Reuters identified over $3 billion in Chinese computing projects involving restricted Nvidia technology; the Trump–Xi Shenzhen meeting on November 18–19 could shift export expectations.
signal: Evaluate technological advantage separately from governance, financing interdependence and valuation.

# Energy and Infrastructure: Supply Recovers, Resilience Does Not
The IEA now expects Q4 oil supply to exceed demand by about 1.7 million barrels per day; Brent settled at $95.84. Yet physical risk persists — Iran reported another tanker incident in the Strait of Hormuz. Late Friday, the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop for about five hours after a communications outage; officials cited overwhelmed legacy equipment, not a cyberattack. A prospective oil surplus does not eliminate geopolitical or infrastructure risk.
signal: Favor critical infrastructure that earns through reliability, not simply rising budgets.

::section The Hidden Pattern | The Great Split becomes a financial mechanism
The AI Supercycle is producing two opposing forces: expanding technological abundance and increasing financial concentration. The Great Split is evolving from a technological divide into a **financial divide** — between those able to sustain productive acceleration and those whose financing and infrastructure dependencies constrain them.

The mechanism can self-reinforce. Resilient companies invest when rivals retrench, securing scarce infrastructure and strategic relationships. But financing constraints can delay investment, postpone returns and erode the capacity to compete. The next capital rotation could accelerate concentration even as intelligence becomes more widely available.

::patterns Three Scenarios, 90 Days | FDK analytical weights, not calibrated probabilities
# [45%] Selective Capital Discipline
AI demand stays substantial while financing turns selective; stronger businesses expand as weaker projects restructure or consolidate.
# [30%] Productivity-Led Broadening
AI monetization improves and credit stabilizes, spreading benefits toward industrial infrastructure, applications and broader equities.
# [25%] Systemic Capital Transmission
Financing losses and contractual interdependence amplify stress while persistent inflation and high yields limit monetary relief.

::takeaways Key takeaways | For investors, boards and policymakers
- AI has become a financial-system question: assess leverage, guarantees and correlated exposure.
- The bond market has reasserted authority — yields above 5% make financing discipline essential.
- Boards must map AI partnerships as a network of obligations, not isolated deals.
- Record European inflows are an opportunity, not an achievement, until they fund productive investment.
- The Great Split will be decided by financial endurance, not announcement size.

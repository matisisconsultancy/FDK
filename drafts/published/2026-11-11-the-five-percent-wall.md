November 11 opens with an extraordinary divergence. The US ten-year Treasury yield crossed 5% after October inflation exceeded expectations, and markets now regard a December Fed increase as virtually certain. Yet the physical expansion of artificial intelligence continues at pace.

These developments do not show the AI Supercycle ending. They show that its next phase will be governed by a more demanding equation: technological growth must generate economic returns fast enough to compensate for rising financing costs, infrastructure commitments and the accelerating replacement cycle of computing assets.

::signal
- **The US ten-year Treasury crossed 5%** after October CPI rose 0.4% month-on-month, making a December Fed increase almost fully priced.
- **CoreWeave posted 168% revenue growth** and a $132.5B backlog yet reported a $709M net loss on $846M of net interest expense.
- **Anthropic disclosed $900.9B** in computing-related commitments as it seeks a fully diluted valuation near $1.4 trillion.
- **TSMC's October revenue rose 59.7%** year-on-year, while Siemens Energy reported a record €164B order backlog.
- **SoftBank posted a ~$7B quarterly loss**, underscoring the risk in AI capital allocation.
- **The coming rotation rewards financial resilience** and demonstrable returns on capital over expansion dependent on cheap money.

::signals The Five Overnight Signals | What moved and why it matters

# The Five-Percent Treasury Reclaims Control
The BLS reported October consumer prices up 0.4%, with core up 0.3% — both above consensus. The ten-year reached roughly 5.012% intraday. A five-percent sovereign benchmark changes the hurdle rate against which the entire infrastructure cycle must compete. Financially resilient platforms may fund investment internally; developers dependent on repeated debt issuance face more demanding conditions.
signal: The Supercycle may keep accelerating, but the price of financing it is no longer neutral.

# CoreWeave: The Financial Paradox In The Accounts
Revenue reached $3.661B, up ~168%, with backlog near $132.5B and FY2026 capex guided to $37B–$39B. Yet operating income was $207M against $846M in net interest expense and a $709M net loss. Commercial and operational success can precede financial success — a large backlog does not eliminate construction risk, counterparty concentration or the cost of servicing debt.
signal: The next AI credit premium is earned by converting backlog into cash flow, not by expanding backlog.

# TSMC And Anthropic: The Age Of Interdependent Capital
TSMC's October revenue rose 59.7% year-on-year. Anthropic filed an amended IPO at $70–$78 per share, a valuation approaching $1.4 trillion, alongside a conditional $26B private placement from Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and a TSMC affiliate — and disclosed ~$900.9B in purchase and lease commitments. Suppliers increasingly finance the customers who buy their products, raising the correlation of financial risks.
signal: Avoid counting the same strategic demand multiple times across different investment narratives.

# Siemens Energy: The Physical Economy Captures Digital Value
A record €164B backlog, stronger 2027 margins, a €9B buyback and a more-than-tripled dividend reflect surging demand for gas turbines and grid equipment. RWE and E.ON reinforce the picture. Without reliable power and grid connections, computing capacity cannot operate — so economic value migrates toward the physical inputs of digital productivity.
signal: Europe's advantage may lie not in producing more intelligence, but in controlling the infrastructure that makes it productive.

# Energy And Geopolitics: Peace Reprices Faster Than Risk
Brent rebounded to ~$92.53 as markets weighed de-escalation between the US, Israel and Iran. Yet 37 vessels were attacked in October, the highest monthly total of the conflict, with new November incidents near Oman and at Russia's Ust-Luga. Futures can price peace overnight; mine clearance, insurance and shipowner confidence cannot be restored at that speed.
signal: Differentiate a temporary decline in crude from a durable improvement in physical energy security.

::section Europe's Great Financial Divide | Two Europes in the bond market
A November 11 FT analysis identifies growing differentiation between highly indebted sovereigns — notably France and Belgium, with French ten-year yields briefly exceeding 5% — and more resilient borrowers such as Spain and Ireland. Italy's costs have risen but improving fiscal credibility has set it apart.

The danger is transmission: sovereign spread widening could pressure bank funding, raise corporate credit costs and weaken productive investment. Europe needs substantial investment in electricity, industry and productivity precisely when the conditions for financing it are becoming more demanding.

::takeaways Key takeaways | For investors, boards and policymakers
- The discount rate is again a central valuation variable: judge AI firms on the cost and timing of cash flows, not revenue growth alone.
- Credit markets are the most consequential test of AI infrastructure: separate commercially valuable assets from vulnerable capital structures.
- Boards must replace capital expenditure with capital productivity as the primary measure of AI success.
- Electricity infrastructure is becoming a source of European competitive advantage — sovereign stress must not crowd out future productivity.
- The Great Split will reward financial endurance: technological capability paired with financing flexibility and strong customer economics.

::close The five-percent Treasury is not a verdict against technological progress — it is a reminder that technological opportunity and financial value are not the same thing. ==The next economy will be built on intelligence; the next market leaders will be those who can make intelligence earn more than the capital required to create it.==

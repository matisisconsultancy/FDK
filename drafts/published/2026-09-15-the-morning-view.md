What happens when the largest technology-investment cycle in modern history collides with a monetary regime still calibrated primarily to the economic structure of the past? That is increasingly the question underneath global markets.

This Sunday morning, the important signal is not another daily movement in equities, bonds or currencies. It is the convergence of two repricing mechanisms that will become increasingly visible over the coming week: **NVIDIA and the economics of the AI Supercycle**, and **Jackson Hole and the price of capital**.

NVIDIA is expected to report quarterly results on August 26, while the Federal Reserve's Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium follows August 27–29. These are not independent events. Together they represent the two variables increasingly determining the architecture of global capital allocation: ==the productivity of intelligence and the cost of financing it==.

::section The AI Supercycle Enters Its Capital-Discipline Phase | From technology story to capital-formation story

The argument surrounding artificial intelligence is changing. The question is no longer whether AI exists, whether adoption is accelerating, or whether accelerated computing will require enormous infrastructure. Those questions have largely been answered.

The harder question is: how quickly can extraordinary capital expenditure become extraordinary economic output? Goldman Sachs estimates that annual AI capital expenditure could reach approximately **$765 billion in 2026**, rising toward **$1.6 trillion annually by 2031** under its baseline assumptions.

That scale changes the nature of the investment cycle. AI is moving from a technology story into a capital-formation story. And once that happens, financial markets inevitably begin distinguishing between three categories: capital deployed → intelligence produced → economic value captured. The distance between those three stages will increasingly determine valuations.

::section NVIDIA Becomes A Referendum On The Velocity Of Capital | Beyond a single company

NVIDIA's results this week matter beyond NVIDIA. Markets will examine whether demand for AI infrastructure remains sufficiently powerful to validate the enormous investment commitments being made across hyperscalers, sovereign AI programs, data centers and AI factories.

The deeper issue is capital velocity. The AI economy increasingly operates through a recursive chain: Capital → Accelerated Computing → AI Factories → Tokens → Inference → Industrial Applications → Productivity → Cash Flow → More Capital.

::pull If every stage accelerates, the economic system compounds. If one stage becomes constrained, the market begins repricing the entire chain.

That is why NVIDIA earnings increasingly function as something close to a global industrial indicator for artificial intelligence.

::section The Great Split Is Becoming More Visible | A K-shaped capital market

Capital is not simply moving between technology and traditional industries. It is increasingly distinguishing between assets capable of participating in the Next Economy and assets whose economic structures remain anchored in the previous one. The result is a ==K-shaped capital market==.

On one side: AI infrastructure → productivity expectations → higher margins → stronger cash generation → cheaper capital → more investment. On the other: low productivity → weak margins → expensive capital → underinvestment → declining competitiveness. The divergence becomes recursive. Success lowers the cost of future success; stagnation raises the cost of escaping stagnation.

Interpreting today's AI valuations exclusively through the language of a "bubble" risks missing the larger transformation. There can absolutely be corrections. There will be failed companies. There will be excessive valuations. But the underlying movement of capital can remain structurally rational even while individual securities become temporarily irrational.

::section Jackson Hole: The Other Side Of The Equation | The price of capital meets the Next Economy

The 2026 Jackson Hole symposium takes place August 27–29 under the theme "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy." Markets will focus on inflation, interest rates, financial conditions and the future path of monetary policy. But there is a larger question central banks will increasingly confront: what happens when productivity, capital formation and economic capacity are being transformed by AI faster than conventional macroeconomic models can measure them?

The traditional monetary framework interprets the economy largely through employment, wages, inflation, output gaps, consumption and credit. The Next Economy adds another variable: the accelerating production of intelligence.

::pull Central banks may confront an economy in which capital expenditure is inflationary before productivity becomes deflationary.

Understanding the time lag between those forces may become one of the defining monetary-policy challenges of the decade.

::section Europe Begins To Connect Capital With Industrial AI | From regulation to industrialization

The European Commission has launched the process to support as many as **seven AI gigafactories**, with sovereign AI infrastructure targeted to become operational around the middle of 2028. And this week another important signal emerged: the Commission cleared the creation of the joint venture involving ACS AIID, Telefónica, Banco Santander and SETT connected to Spain's bid for a European AI gigafactory.

This matters enormously, because it begins connecting four forms of capital that Europe has historically struggled to assemble simultaneously: **industrial capital + telecommunications infrastructure + financial capital + public strategic capital**. That architecture may prove far more important than the individual project.

Europe does not need simply more AI laboratories. It needs an industrial system capable of deploying intelligence at scale. The European race is moving from AI regulation toward AI infrastructure and ultimately toward ==Industrial AI==. That is the transition investors should watch.

# AI capital expenditure is becoming macroeconomically significant
Goldman Sachs' baseline estimate of roughly $765 billion of AI CapEx in 2026 means AI infrastructure can no longer be treated as a narrow technology-sector phenomenon.
signal: AI CapEx is now a macro variable, not a sector story.

# NVIDIA becomes the first major test of capital productivity
The market increasingly wants evidence not merely of GPU demand but of the durability of the entire AI investment chain.
signal: The question shifts from demand to the durability of the chain.

# Jackson Hole puts the cost of capital back at the center
AI investment requires extraordinary financing precisely when long-duration assets remain highly sensitive to rates and bond yields.
signal: Financing conditions will shape the pace of the Supercycle.

# Europe is moving from AI policy toward AI industrialization
The Spanish ACS–Telefónica–Santander–SETT structure is an early illustration of the public-private capital architecture required to build European AI infrastructure.
signal: The public-private capital stack is finally assembling in Europe.

# The Great Split is migrating from markets into the real economy
The decisive distinction is increasingly not "technology versus non-technology" but AI-accelerated versus structurally unaccelerated economic systems.
signal: The divide now runs through the real economy itself.

::patterns Five Hidden Patterns | Beneath the headlines

# I. AI is becoming an infrastructure asset class
Semiconductors were the beginning. The opportunity set is expanding toward electricity, grids, cooling, optical networks, memory, data centers, industrial software and physical infrastructure.

# II. Energy becomes part of the intelligence stack
The relevant economic unit is increasingly not merely the cost of computing infrastructure. It is intelligence produced per unit of capital and energy.

# III. Capital markets are becoming technology-policy mechanisms
Investors are increasingly deciding which companies, industries and countries receive the capital required to migrate toward the Next Economy.

# IV. Industrial AI may become more consequential than the model race
The next stage of value creation will depend less on producing ever-larger foundation models and more on applying intelligence across factories, mobility, logistics, energy, finance, healthcare and infrastructure.

# V. Geography is returning
AI looked initially like a cloud phenomenon. AI factories make it physical again. Power grids, fiber, land, water, permitting, industrial ecosystems and financing capacity now determine where intelligence can actually be produced.

::takeaways Key takeaways | The Velocity insight

- The debate over whether AI valuations are too high is less interesting than the question underneath: where will the capital mobilized by the AI Supercycle produce the highest incremental economic return?
- The discussion moves from valuation to capital allocation — from models to Industrial AI, from technology to infrastructure, from infrastructure to geography, and ultimately to economic power.
- NVIDIA will tell markets something about the return on intelligence; Jackson Hole will tell markets something about the price of capital.
- Between those two variables lies much of the emerging architecture of the Next Economy.

::close The countries, corporations and investors capable of accelerating ==Capital × Intelligence × Execution== simultaneously will not merely grow faster — they will determine where future value is created.

This is one of those days when three apparently separate market stories converge into a single economic equation. Data cut: 07:45 CEST.

Later today, Nvidia reports results that could determine whether the extraordinary expansion of AI infrastructure continues to validate the capital being committed underneath it. Before that, at 14:30 CEST, the United States publishes July PCE inflation. And overnight, the third component of the equation moved sharply in the opposite direction, as oil fell on renewed Iran–Oman negotiations over the Strait of Hormuz.

Markets are therefore simultaneously repricing the return on artificial intelligence, the cost of capital, and the cost of the physical system on which artificial intelligence depends. That is the complete equation.

::pull THE FULL EQUATION
AI is no longer simply competing with other technologies for corporate budgets. It is competing with governments, households and the rest of the economy for capital, electricity and duration.

::section The Fundamental Question | Numerator vs denominator
Can the economic value created by artificial intelligence compound faster than the cost of the capital, energy and infrastructure required to produce it? That sounds simple. It is not.

The AI Supercycle now operates through three simultaneous prices. **Price One — Intelligence:** algorithmic efficiency and model competition keep pushing the marginal cost of intelligence downward. **Price Two — Infrastructure:** accelerated computing, memory, electricity, transmission, cooling, powered land and data-center capacity remain scarce. **Price Three — Capital:** AI infrastructure must increasingly compete for long-duration financing against governments, defense, energy systems and the broader corporate economy.

The winning economics therefore become: Falling Intelligence Cost × Expanding Usage × Rising Productivity must exceed Infrastructure Inflation + Capital Cost + Technological Depreciation. ==That is the real hurdle rate.==

::signals The Five Most Important Overnight Signals | recursive system
# Nvidia Has Become the Earnings Report for the AI Economy
Nvidia reports today with revenue expected near $92.2 billion and data-center sales set to more than double, while investors scrutinize the transition from Blackwell to Vera Rubin. Tuesday's positioning was constructive but cautious: Nvidia gained 2.2%, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index 1.4%, the Nasdaq 0.66% and the S&P 500 0.32%, though Nasdaq futures were about 0.5% lower early this morning.

The market already assumes Nvidia will beat expectations. Execution is therefore no longer the upside catalyst — it is the minimum requirement. The relevant question is what happens after Rubin, and today's result should be read through one new metric: economically useful intelligence produced per dollar of AI infrastructure.
signal: Nvidia's earnings are now a macro reading of the AI capital cycle, not a sector event.

# Hormuz Relief Has Become Duration Relief
Iran and Oman have resumed negotiations over the Strait of Hormuz, including a temporary joint navigational corridor and mine clearing. Brent fell more than 3% Tuesday and another 2.6% to around $86.28, while U.S. 10-year yields fell roughly 6.5 basis points Tuesday to near 4.63%. But only five commodity ships crossed Tuesday, against a ten-day average of 15.

The recursive chain is now visible: Hormuz risk ↓ → oil ↓ → inflation premium ↓ → Treasury yields ↓ → WACC ↓ → technology-duration valuations ↑. Lower oil acts indirectly as AI financial easing. But five vessels are not normalization — the market is buying the possibility of normalization before the infrastructure has actually normalized.
signal: Oil and Nvidia increasingly belong inside the same financial model.

# PCE Will Test Whether the Denominator Can Really Fall
July core PCE is expected at 3.3% year-on-year and +0.2% month-on-month, with Fed policy at 3.50%–3.75%. Boston Fed President Susan Collins warned that further tightening may soon be appropriate absent sustained progress. Meanwhile consumer confidence fell to 89.4 in August and July new-home sales dropped 10.5% to a 607,000 annual rate.

The consumer is weakening before inflation is defeated. The Great Split is becoming visible inside the U.S. economy: AI investment velocity rising sharply while household purchasing velocity declines. The Fed increasingly faces two economies — one capital-rich and accelerating, the other consumption-dependent and rate-sensitive.
signal: If core PCE holds near 3.3%, the Fed cannot cut for the consumer without raising the hurdle rate on capital.

# Europe Is Beginning to Grow Again — Just as the ECB Prepares to Tighten
Germany's Q2 GDP was revised up to +0.3% quarter-on-quarter, following 0.4% in Q1, with exports up 2% and the Ifo index at 88.8, its strongest in a year. Yet Reuters reports ECB policymakers preparing for a September hike, probably taking the deposit rate from 2.25% to 2.50%.

For years the problem was insufficient growth; now it may become how to finance a recovery without extinguishing it. Europe must simultaneously fund defense, energy security, grids, rail, accelerated computing, AI factories and demographic obligations — while entering that cycle with materially higher sovereign yields.
signal: Can productivity growth rise faster than the cost of capital being mobilized to produce it — the same question Nvidia faces, at continental scale.

# AI Has Reached the Sovereign Balance Sheet
Morgan Stanley estimates $350–400 billion of AI-related public investment-grade debt could be issued in 2026, with $1.4 trillion of AI CapEx planned for 2027 and some $92 billion financed since mid-year. At the same time, U.S. corporate tax receipts are down roughly 23% — partly because 2025 legislation allows immediate expensing of large capital investments — while deficits run near 6% of GDP and interest on ~$40 trillion of debt exceeds $1 trillion annually.

The chain becomes: AI CapEx ↑ → corporate debt issuance ↑, corporate tax receipts ↓ → government funding pressure ↑ → competition for duration ↑ → long yields ↑ → AI WACC ↑. Artificial intelligence can raise future productivity while making the short-term sovereign financing problem harder.
signal: The AI Supercycle now has a fiscal dimension markets have barely begun to price.

::patterns Five Hidden Patterns Beyond the News | structural shifts
# Numerator and Denominator, Same Day
Nvidia tests future cash-flow velocity, PCE tests the discount rate applied to those cash flows, and Hormuz tests the physical input cost beneath both. Equity Value = Future AI Cash Flow / Cost of Capital, with energy affecting both sides.

# Nvidia as Central Capital Allocator
Beyond selling accelerated computing, Nvidia is arranging $500 billion of financing, guaranteeing up to $105 billion on OpenAI's lease and investing across the ecosystem. The feedback loop is formidable — but at what point does ecosystem reinforcement become financial interdependence?

# From Technology to Balance-Sheet Quality
Two projects may deploy identical computing, but secured power, long-term contracts, low leverage and short payback make one durable and the other fragile. The AI race is becoming a balance-sheet race.

# The Scarce Asset Is Productive Duration
Capital is not disappearing, but competing long-duration claims are exploding — AI, deficits, defense, grids, transport, demographics. The scarce asset is capital willing to wait, and patient capital will grow more selective.

# Fiscal Dominance as a Market Variable
With Jackson Hole opening this week, the question of who ultimately absorbs government debt is unavoidable. Either central banks restrain sovereign costs — pushing the adjustment into currencies and inflation — or long-duration corporate infrastructure bears higher financing costs. Both reach the AI economy.

::section Implications for Investors, Executives and Policymakers | a three-dimensional model
Today should be read through a three-dimensional AI valuation model: **Operating Velocity** (can revenue, inference and free cash flow keep compounding?), **Capital Velocity** (how much infrastructure capital is required per next dollar of revenue?), and **Financial Velocity** (at what WACC can that investment be financed?). The most attractive assets combine rapid revenue growth, high utilization, short payback, durable scarcity, strong balance sheets and modest dependence on financial engineering.

For executives, boards should adopt a harder standard. The question is no longer *how much AI capacity are we deploying?* but *how much economically useful intelligence does every euro or dollar of capital produce?* AI must move from the technology dashboard to the capital-allocation dashboard.

For policymakers, Europe has an opportunity invisible six months ago. It must connect world-class positions in industrial engineering, automation, power systems and semiconductor equipment to accelerated computing, AI factories, grids and patient capital. The objective is maximum productive intelligence per euro of strategic European capital — converting AI investment into industrial advantage rather than becoming a customer of someone else's intelligence infrastructure.

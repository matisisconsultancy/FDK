The AI Supercycle is entering a new financial phase. The decisive competitive variable is shifting from the amount of computing capacity corporations can deploy to the speed at which they can convert that capacity into sustainable economic returns.

The defining financial variable is no longer simply growth. It is the speed of economic conversion. The next source of alpha will be found where improving corporate velocity shortens the time between capital deployment and cash generation faster than markets anticipate.

::signal
- **The market is repricing time-to-return**, not just growth: the next alpha is where velocity beats expectations.
- **On October 30** the Nasdaq fell 2.86% and the semiconductor index declined 7.2%.
- **Cash-flow divergence defines the cycle**: Microsoft generated $18.6bn quarterly free cash flow, Alphabet reported negative $9.1bn.
- **Earnings stayed resilient**: 82% of 299 reporting S&P 500 companies beat expectations despite the selloff.
- **Infrastructure accelerated** — Vertiv grew revenue 61%, SK Hynix achieved an 80% operating margin.
- **Industry decelerated**: November 2 surveys showed slowing expansion in Japan, South Korea and Taiwan despite resilient AI demand.

::patterns Five Hidden Patterns | The next market rotation
# Time Is The New Scarcity
Beyond processors and memory, time-to-monetization is emerging as a competitive dimension. Companies lifting revenue per unit of deployed computing capital may earn superior incremental returns without proportionate new investment.
# Capex As A Valuation Divider
Microsoft (+$18.6bn quarterly FCF), Alphabet (-$9.1bn quarterly FCF) and Amazon (-$42.4bn trailing-12-month FCF) occupy three positions. Markets may increasingly distinguish those generating cash from infrastructure from those still financing it.
# Suppliers Capture Operating Leverage
Vertiv reported 61% revenue growth and 90% operating-profit growth; Amphenol posted 65% revenue growth, $12.2bn in orders and $1.6bn quarterly free cash flow. Leadership could broaden toward power and connectivity.
# A More Uneven Economy
South Korea's October manufacturing PMI fell to 51.3 from 53.9, Taiwan's to 54.9 from 56.7 — both expansionary but weakening, deepening the K-shaped structure and concentrating capital.
# Credit As The Next Channel
The architecture linking suppliers, hyperscalers, investors and lenders means equity pressure could migrate into credit spreads and infrastructure financing. Balance-sheet resilience may drive the next rotation as much as technological leadership.

::section The Next Alpha Frontier | Where capital could move
The emerging transmission mechanism runs: Innovation Velocity → Infrastructure Utilization → Productivity Acceleration → Earnings Revisions → Cash-Flow Conversion → Equity Repricing. The opportunity lies in anticipating the sequence before analysts revise.

FDK's six-month scenarios, unchanged from the November 1 framework: **selective differentiation (45%)**, capital favoring sustainable earnings and cash; **financing-led consolidation (35%)**, investment delays and credit pressure; **productivity breakout (20%)**, broader equity leadership. Five indicators matter most: earnings revisions, incremental ROIC, infrastructure utilization, free-cash-flow guidance and credit spreads. Outside the benchmark, SK Hynix, Vertiv and Amphenol are the strongest challengers to the established hierarchy.

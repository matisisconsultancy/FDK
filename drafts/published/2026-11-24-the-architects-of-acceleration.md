The next phase of the AI Supercycle will increasingly be shaped by three strategic assets: visionary leadership, integrated capital markets and the industrial capacity to deploy intelligence at scale.

Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO triggered an extraordinary market recovery. Switzerland's SIX launched a cross-border initiative connecting European growth companies with investors. Germany's industrial acceleration contrasts sharply with France's deepening contraction, while the US strengthens its scientific discovery infrastructure through the Genesis Mission.

The hidden pattern: the geography of global capital allocation is increasingly determined by the interaction between technological leadership, institutional execution and financial scalability.

::section I. Four Developments Reshaping Global Velocity |

**United States — The rise of Compass Companies.** Nvidia's recovery shows how certain corporations and leaders influence expectations across entire industrial ecosystems. Huang's return reduces immediate leadership uncertainty, but continuing investigations and governance questions prevent an unconditional reduction in long-term risk.

**Switzerland, Spain and the UK — Europe's capital-market opportunity.** SIX's European Growth Program connects 31 listed growth companies across the Swiss Exchange, Madrid's BME and London's Aquis — a potential step towards financing European scale-ups without excessive reliance on American capital. Actual new capital raised remains to be measured.

**Germany versus France — Europe's industrial divergence.** Germany's composite PMI reached its highest level since March 2022 while France's contraction intensified. The 8.8-point gap, the widest in six years, reinforces the prospect of differentiated European returns.

**United States — Scientific discovery becomes industrial infrastructure.** The DoE's November 23 Genesis Mission review identifies 297 projects across 369 institutions, $2.4 billion in industry commitments and major advances in scientific computing — milestones that do not yet establish equivalent gains in scientific productivity.

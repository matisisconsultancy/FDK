Was today simply another technology rally—or did markets reveal the condition under which the AI Supercycle can keep accelerating? The answer is increasingly clear: **AI remains extraordinarily sensitive to the price of capital and energy.**

After weeks in which oil above $100 and the U.S. 10-year around 5% threatened the economics of long-duration investment, today both constraints eased simultaneously. Brent dropped back below $100, Treasury yields retreated, and capital rushed immediately toward semiconductors and AI infrastructure.

That reaction matters more than the rally itself.

::section The Day: The Pressure Valve Opened | Part I

Wall Street closed sharply higher: S&P 500 +1.49%, Nasdaq +2.26%, Dow +0.72%. AMD crossed $1 trillion in market capitalization for the first time; Intel and Arm jumped more than 12% during the session and the semiconductor index surged.

At the same time, the two variables that have been constraining the AI trade moved in the opposite direction. Brent fell below $100, after trading above $103 previously, while the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield slipped below 5%. Earlier readings put Brent near $99.8 and the 10-year around 4.96%.

The chain of causality was unusually clean: geopolitical de-escalation expectations → oil down → inflation pressure down → bond yields down → discount rate down → AI-duration assets up → semiconductors lead.

::pull Today wasn't fundamentally an AI-news day. It was a cost-of-capital day disguised as an AI rally.

The market demonstrated how much latent demand for AI exposure remains underneath the macro constraint.

::signals Capital Allocation: The Great Split Reopens | Part II — five signals
# Compute Reclaims Leadership
AMD's move through the $1 trillion valuation threshold is symbolically important. The market is broadening its AI infrastructure premium beyond a single semiconductor champion: Nvidia → AMD → Arm → Intel → infrastructure ecosystem.
signal: The AI trade is gradually becoming an industrial system, rather than a single-stock phenomenon.
# $100 Oil Remains the Macro Hurdle
Brent's retreat below $100 immediately relieved pressure on equities, confirming the threshold we identified earlier this month. Above it, inflation expectations, rates, electricity costs and infrastructure economics begin working against AI valuations. Below it, the constraint loosens.
signal: $100 oil is not simply an energy price. It is a hurdle rate for the AI Supercycle.
# 5% Treasuries Are the Second Hurdle
The U.S. 10-year's retreat below 5% mattered almost as much as oil. AI infrastructure requires enormous capital today against cash flows extending far into the future. If WACC rises faster than AI productivity, valuations compress; if WACC falls while AI investment accelerates, capital concentration resumes.
signal: AI ROIC − WACC = Value Creation. The equation remains brutally simple.
# Europe Gets Relief, Not a Reprieve
European equities participated strongly: the STOXX 600 gained roughly 1%, Germany's DAX closed around +1.1%, with Infineon and Siemens among the strongest performers. But the ECB recently raised rates 25 basis points, with euro-area inflation forecast averaging 3.0% in 2026, and warned that gas-price shocks can transmit into consumer inflation faster than in the past.
signal: America gets AI scale plus deeper capital markets plus declining energy pressure; Europe still faces energy vulnerability plus tighter capital plus insufficient AI scale.
# Financial Infrastructure Is Also Accelerating
The ECB launched Pontes, connecting its TARGET payment infrastructure with blockchain-based markets and allowing eligible transactions to settle in central-bank euros rather than private stablecoins. Deutsche Bank, Santander and Clearstream are among the initial participants.
signal: AI transforms computation, tokenisation transforms settlement, energy transforms infrastructure — the next economy is being rebuilt simultaneously.

::section The Circular Chain | The constraint recreates itself

Oil down → inflation expectations down → bond yields down → WACC down → AI valuations up → capital formation up → compute infrastructure up → power demand up → energy constraint returns.

And that last arrow is the one investors should watch. The AI Supercycle increasingly contains the mechanism capable of recreating its own constraint.

::takeaways Five Takeaways | The system in summary
- **AI leadership survived the 5% rate test** — capital returned immediately once yields eased.
- **$100 Brent is emerging as a critical macro threshold** — energy is now part of the AI valuation equation.
- **The AI trade is broadening** — AMD's trillion-dollar milestone and the semiconductor rally point toward an expanding compute ecosystem.
- **Europe received macro relief, but its structural energy problem remains** — today's rally does not eliminate the competitiveness gap.
- **The next battle is no longer models versus models** — it is compute × capital × energy × infrastructure × geopolitical resilience.

::close For much of the boom the scarce asset was compute, then power, then the cost of capital and energy simultaneously. Tonight both constraints loosened and the market immediately repriced AI upward. Tomorrow's question is not whether AI keeps accelerating — ==it is who can still afford to accelerate with it.==

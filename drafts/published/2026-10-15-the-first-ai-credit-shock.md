The AI supercycle is entering a new financial regime. Demand for intelligence continues to accelerate, but rising financing costs are exposing the gap between infrastructure expansion and economic returns.

The next market rotation will be determined not by who invests most in AI, but by who converts investment into cash flow fastest.

::patterns Five Signals Beneath the Headlines | What the tape is really saying
# Semiconductors
Selling pressure reflects growing concerns about valuations, financing and returns—not necessarily weakening AI demand.
# AI Revenues
Rapid growth strengthens the technology thesis but raises expectations for profitability and capital efficiency.
# Credit Markets
Higher borrowing costs threaten leveraged data-center operators and infrastructure developers.
# Energy
Europe's battery investments demonstrate how storage can become a strategic advantage in AI infrastructure economics.
# Central Banks
Persistent inflation and a potentially hawkish ECB complicate financing just as AI investment requirements accelerate.

::section The Hidden Pattern: The AI Return Spread | The decisive variable
The decisive financial variable is increasingly the spread between productive returns and the cost of financing them.

::pull AI Return Spread = Return on Productive AI Capital − Cost of Capital

As financing costs rise, the market begins separating three categories of companies:

**Capital creators:** Businesses generating measurable productivity, margins and free cash flow from AI.

**Capital absorbers:** Infrastructure operators with expanding investment requirements but uncertain monetization.

**Capital casualties:** Highly leveraged businesses whose expected returns cannot justify their financing costs.

The AI boom is not necessarily ending. Its financial architecture is being repriced.

::section Second-Order Effects: The Great Rotation | Four consequences for market leadership
First, capital concentration intensifies. Companies with strong balance sheets can acquire infrastructure and capabilities from financially constrained competitors.

Second, infrastructure valuations diverge. Power availability, utilization, financing structures and contracted revenues become more important than announced computing capacity.

Third, enterprise AI gains strategic importance. Investors increasingly favor measurable productivity improvements over speculative capacity expansion.

Fourth, financial markets become more selective. Credit spreads and free-cash-flow conversion begin challenging revenue growth as indicators of AI investment quality.

The resulting rotation could favor enterprise software, automation, energy optimization and capital-efficient AI platforms over highly leveraged infrastructure expansion.

::takeaways Five Investor Takeaways | How to position
- Prioritize returns over growth: Revenue acceleration without cash conversion is insufficient.
- Watch credit before equities: Widening financing spreads may reveal stress before earnings disappoint.
- Differentiate AI infrastructure: Contract quality, utilization and energy economics determine resilience.
- Favor productive deployment: Enterprise AI with demonstrable economic benefits offers a potentially stronger investment proposition.
- Preserve strategic optionality: Balance-sheet strength creates acquisition opportunities when weaker competitors face refinancing pressure.

::section The Financial Verdict | A more demanding phase
The first AI credit shock would not necessarily signal the end of the supercycle. It would mark the beginning of a more demanding phase of capital allocation.

The market's fundamental question is changing from *How much AI capacity can be built?* to *How much economic value can that capacity produce?*

::close The next generation of AI winners will be defined not by the capital they mobilize, but by the ==intelligence they monetize==—and the returns they retain after financing costs.

The most consequential development this morning is not another correction in semiconductor valuations. It is the sudden removal of the individual who helped integrate the technological, commercial and financial architecture of the AI Supercycle.

On November 12, Jensen Huang stepped down as Nvidia's chief executive and left its board. CFO Colette Kress was appointed interim CEO, while former Synopsys chief Harvey Jones became board chair. Nvidia shares closed 4.3% lower; AMD and Broadcom each gained roughly 6%. The espionage allegations remain unproven, but the institutional consequences are already real — and the timing could hardly be more consequential.

::signal
- **Huang is gone; the technology remains.** Nvidia's computing leadership survives, but the financial terms of its expansion are now under review.
- **The cost of capital is rising.** The US 10-year reached ~5.14% and the 2-year ~4.89% as inflation persists.
- **Private AI demand stays extraordinary.** Anthropic's up-to-$64.4bn IPO is reportedly ~6× oversubscribed.
- **Europe faces a financing divide.** EU leaders meet in Dublin as Germany agrees €210.4bn of new 2027 borrowing.
- **The Great Split is shifting.** Advantage now moves from owning computing to financing it with independence and productivity.
- **Capital may migrate.** From concentrated AI exposures toward financially autonomous platforms and cash-generative infrastructure.

::section The Fundamental Question | Healthier competition — or exposed dependence?
Is Huang's departure the beginning of a healthier, more competitive AI economy — or the shock that exposes how dependent the Supercycle has become on an interconnected system of supplier financing, customer commitments and extraordinary valuations?

A more competitive ecosystem could broaden supplier choice and reduce computing costs. A financing disruption could delay projects, widen credit spreads and reduce investment before underlying demand deteriorates. The critical distinction is whether technological acceleration can become financially self-sustaining — or remains dependent on the architecture that created it.

::signals Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy | Verified evidence, Velocity interpretation
# Nvidia — the end of the single-architect model
Huang's departure is confirmed; Kress is interim CEO and reportedly advocated greater financial discipline in some customer and infrastructure arrangements. Nvidia's advantage combined computing architecture, software, industrial partnerships and ecosystem financing — and the succession introduces uncertainty into how these are coordinated. AMD, Broadcom and alternative architectures could benefit if customers diversify, but replacing a supplier does not replace its software ecosystem.
signal: The next rotation rewards credible technological substitution, not simply penalizing Nvidia's leadership.

# Anthropic — strong demand meets public-market discipline
The offering is reportedly ~6× oversubscribed: 825 million shares at $70–$78, proceeds up to $64.4bn, a fully diluted valuation near $1.4 trillion, trading expected November 19 under ANT. Strong demand does not eliminate valuation risk. If major suppliers become more selective, frontier firms must rely more on independent capital markets.
signal: Anthropic could become the first public-market benchmark for frontier AI's financial independence.

# The Federal Reserve — the Supercycle collides with the cost of money
October CPI rose 0.4% headline and 0.3% core, lifting yields and the dollar; the yen briefly weakened beyond ¥160. Higher sovereign yields hit corporate borrowing, credit and future earnings — acute for AI, where commitments precede returns. Hyperscalers retain financing access; leveraged developers face pressure. Sumitomo Mitsui raised its outlook to ¥1.9 trillion while tightening data-centre financing standards.
signal: Financing quality is becoming as important as computing demand in the next hierarchy of AI returns.

# Energy and geopolitics — repricing supply risk
Brent traded near $95.5 after settling at $95.82, up ~3.3%, following Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure and reported tanker incidents near Hormuz. The economy faces two energy risks: hydrocarbon security and reliable electricity for computing. Even if crude stabilizes, grids, transformers and turbines may stay constrained.
signal: The next energy premium reflects reliable productive capacity, not commodity prices alone.

# Europe — the AI summit meets the sovereign financing divide
EU leaders gather in Dublin with ambitions for seven gigafactories but unresolved financing disputes; the Chips Act looks unlikely to hit its 20% 2030 target. Germany's 2027 deal provides €561bn spending and €210.4bn new borrowing. France contracted 0.1% in Q3 with October inflation at 3.4%; Spain faces a November 29 election.
signal: Europe must move from announcing technological sovereignty to demonstrating competitive deployment.

::patterns Four Hidden Patterns | Beyond the headlines
# Unbundling power
The Supercycle is beginning to separate technological power from financial power, distributing opportunity across suppliers, operators and competing technologies.

# Concentrated finance
Computing may diversify even as capital concentrates: the strongest balance sheets capture the most investment if conditions stay restrictive.

# Capital productivity
Sovereign competitiveness now turns on whether borrowing and gigafactories create productive assets generating sufficient returns.

# Dependence vs. autonomy
The Great Split moves toward a division between financial dependence and economic autonomy — converting capability into returns without disproportionate dependencies.

::section Three Scenarios for the Next Market Phase | Illustrative three-month judgments
**Selective Reallocation — 50%.** Nvidia retains its position, but customers and investors diversify; capital migrates toward resilient platforms, alternative suppliers and productivity-led industries.

**The Financing Shock — 30%.** Higher rates and changed supplier support widen spreads, delay marginal projects and compress infrastructure valuations.

**Productivity-Led Reacceleration — 20%.** An orderly transition, stronger AI returns and broadening competition reduce computing costs.

::close The AI Supercycle has shown intelligence can scale at extraordinary speed; it has yet to show the financial architecture supporting that scale can remain equally resilient under stress. ==The next winners will not simply possess the technologies of the future — they will control the economics of their deployment.==

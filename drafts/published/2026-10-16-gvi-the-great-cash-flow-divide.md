The AI Supercycle has reached a decisive inflection point: technological profitability is accelerating while financial markets are becoming increasingly selective about who can finance the next phase of expansion.

TSMC's record earnings, the sharp correction in AI-related credit and equities, and Europe's weakening industrial output reveal an emerging division between economies that capture the profits of intelligence and those that struggle to finance its productive deployment. The next Great Split will be determined by the conversion of intelligence into cash flow, not by the accumulation of computing capacity.

::signals Five Signals Reshaping Global Velocity | Earnings strong, financing tightening
# The Semiconductor Paradox: Record Profits, Falling Valuations
TSMC's Q3 revenue reached NT$1.494 trillion, up 50.9% year-on-year; net income rose 68.7% to approximately NT$763 billion; gross margin hit 66.4%, with advanced processes at 79% of wafer revenue. Q4 guidance is $52.3bn–$54.3bn. Yet South Korea's KOSPI fell 7.57% on October 15, with Samsung down ~9.5% and SK Hynix ~11.1%. The distinction is between operating performance and financial valuation.
signal: Earnings can accelerate while investors demand lower multiples — Intelligence Infrastructure strong, Smart Capital more selective.

# United States: Inflation Redefines the Investment Hurdle
September producer prices rose 1.3% monthly and 6.3% annually; energy led (+10.8%, gasoline +20.3%), but core ex-food, energy, trade rose 0.5%. Retail sales gained 0.8% nominal, yet the control group rose just 0.1%. On October 15, Treasury yields fell as investors sought safety despite hot inflation.
signal: Higher nominal spend is not stronger real demand; Smart Capital faces pressure, no structural downgrade.

# Europe: The Industrial Great Split Becomes Measurable
August industrial production: Netherlands +2.6% monthly / +9.8% annual; Sweden +6.5% / +2.7%; Germany +0.4%; France −0.3%; Spain −0.8%; Italy −1.3% / 0.0%. At euro-area level, capital-goods production rose 3.0% annually even as total output fell 1.1%.
signal: The challenge is uneven conversion of capability into growth, not absence of capability.

# United Kingdom: Digital Services Support Resilience
Real GDP rose 0.1% monthly and 0.7% over three months; services +0.9%, production −0.2%, construction −1.0%, manufacturing +1.2% in August. The UK can participate in the Supercycle through software, financial services and enterprise adoption without replicating the full semiconductor ecosystem.
signal: Enterprise Transformation improves qualitatively; national score unchanged.

# Global Capital: AI Credit Becomes a Stress Indicator
The correction exposed sensitivity among heavily indebted infrastructure operators even as TSMC confirmed strong demand. Capital now differentiates three groups: cash-generative leaders financing expansion internally; contracted operators dependent on utilisation and debt service; and speculative developers reliant on future demand and refinancing.
signal: Capital availability may become the transmission mechanism linking markets to the physical growth of intelligence.

::section Financial Verdict | Index at 108.0
**Main accelerator:** continuing semiconductor demand, exceptional advanced-manufacturing profitability, selectively improving output. **Main decelerator:** producer-price inflation, growing financial selectivity and Europe's uneven industrial performance. **Principal signal:** AI equities and credit are being repriced despite strong earnings among critical suppliers. **Principal uncertainty:** whether the next infrastructure phase can generate cash returns sufficient for its capital requirements.

The sequence that separates productive investment from capital-intensive expectation: **CAPITAL → COMPUTE → UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → FREE CASH FLOW → ROIC > WACC.**

::close
The next scarcity is not computing power. It is the capacity to turn computing power into ==cash-generative productivity== before the cost of capital consumes its economic value.

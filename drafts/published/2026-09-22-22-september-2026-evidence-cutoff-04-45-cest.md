# United States — AI moves upstream into power
Google and Georgia Power have agreed to finance upgrades at the Vogtle and Hatch nuclear plants that could add approximately 96 MW of generation. The architecture matters more than its national scale: AI demand → hyperscaler contract → nuclear uprate → incremental electricity capacity. This is strong Intelligence Infrastructure evidence, but the 96 MW scale is insufficient for another US country-score increase, and regulatory approval is still required.

US momentum nevertheless improves at the margin because lower oil and Treasury yields are reducing the immediate financial pressure on AI infrastructure. The Nasdaq's record close and 4.3% semiconductor rally confirm that repricing, but equity prices themselves do not alter the structural GVI.
signal: Hyperscalers are moving upstream into generation—but scale and approval still gate the score.

# Europe — efficiency enters the production function
The European Commission has proposed a common efficiency-label system for data centres of at least 500 kW. Operators would report energy and water efficiency, local water stress and their ability to support energy systems through measures such as waste-heat reuse. EU data-centre capacity is roughly 12 GW in 2025, expected to reach around 28 GW by 2030, already consuming approximately 2.5% of EU electricity.

No national score receives points because this remains an EU-wide proposal. But it introduces an important GVI concept: intelligence measured per unit of electricity, water and grid capacity.
signal: Efficiency, not installed compute, becomes the metric that matters.

# Spain — stronger direction, no points yet
Spain has outlined a 12-month AI roadmap including a proposed AI gigafactory, new models developed with the Barcelona Supercomputing Centre, and stronger cybersecurity and environmental requirements for data centres. This strengthens Innovation Velocity and Discovery Capacity momentum, but not today's 75.0 score. The projects remain prospective; the GVI requires financing, procurement, construction or measurable operating capacity before recognising them structurally.
signal: Ambition is rising; recognition awaits financing and construction.

::section Institutional interpretation | The efficiency constraint
The fundamental question is becoming: how much economic intelligence can a system produce from each marginal unit of energy, capital and physical infrastructure?

The AI race is migrating through a sequence of bottlenecks: Accelerated Computing → Memory → Optical Bandwidth → Power → Grid → Capital → Efficiency. The technological frontier continues to advance, but the economic frontier increasingly depends on system efficiency.

The US power agreement illustrates hyperscalers moving upstream into generation. Europe is beginning to measure energy and water efficiency explicitly. Spain is trying to combine compute, sovereign models and environmental constraints. Markets immediately reward AI again when oil and bond yields retreat.

For investors, the premium should migrate toward permitted, powered, contracted and highly utilised capacity—and toward bottlenecks such as memory, optics, transformers, grids, cooling and energy. For executives, the crucial KPI is becoming ==economic value created per megawatt of intelligence deployed==. For policymakers, AI competitiveness can no longer be separated from energy policy: the objective is not simply more data centres, but more productive intelligence without destabilising grids, water systems or household energy costs.

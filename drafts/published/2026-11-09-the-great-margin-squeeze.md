The next constraint on global economic acceleration may not be technological capacity or even access to capital. It may be the widening gap between the cost of producing economic value and the ability to capture it.

China's producer-price inflation accelerated to **5.7%** in October while consumer inflation moderated to just **0.7%** and industrial purchasing prices rose **7.9%** — a widening divergence between upstream costs and downstream pricing. The emerging regime is defined by three simultaneous forces: accelerating technological capacity, rising pressure on operating margins and increasingly selective access to financial capital. Evidence cut-off: November 9, 2026, 04:45 CET.

::signal
- **China's PPI-CPI gap widened** to 5.0pp as producer inflation hit 5.7% and consumer inflation fell to 0.7%.
- **Industrial purchasing prices rose 7.9%**, led by fuel and power up 18.6%, pressuring manufacturer margins that cannot recover costs.
- **China extended** its additional 24% US tariff suspension for two months, easing policy risk but not strategic-material uncertainty.

::section China — The Industrial–Consumer Price Divide | Upstream pressure without demand
China's NBS published October figures on November 9. Industrial purchasing prices are rising faster than factory-gate prices; core consumer inflation remains weak at 0.8%; and increases concentrate in key inputs — fuel and power up 18.6%, chemical raw materials 16.8%, non-ferrous metals and electrical wires 16.7%.

This creates a margin challenge for manufacturers unable to recover costs through prices, productivity or product mix. Strong technology exports can support production and cash generation while weak domestic demand constrains profitability elsewhere. *GVI implication:* negative Enterprise Transformation and Smart Capital cost-pressure signals — not a numerical competitiveness downgrade.

::section The Strategic Counterweight — China Extends Its Tariff Truce | Policy relief, not supply certainty
China extended the suspension of its additional 24% tariff on selected US imports for two months from November 10; the separate 10% tariff remains. The extension reduces immediate escalation risk but does not confirm that rare-earth and related export-control suspensions — scheduled to expire November 10 — have been extended. ==Tariff stability and supply-chain certainty are not equivalent==, and for semiconductor and advanced-manufacturing businesses, access to critical materials can matter as much as finished-product tariffs.

::section South Korea — AI Capacity Remains Operationally Resilient | Installed stock, not new additions
Deputy Science Minister Ryu Jemyung confirmed that 10,000 Nvidia GPUs financed through a 2025 supplementary budget had already been delivered and were operating, with additional contracted 2026 deliveries expected before year-end despite uncertainty surrounding Nvidia's leadership. This is evidence of operating computing capacity rather than announced capex, and is recorded under Intelligence Infrastructure — not treated as an overnight increase.

::section Global Capital — The AI Repricing Enters A Second Phase | Earnings quality is the test
Following last week's correction, Japan's Nikkei gained roughly 2.5% and Taiwan recovered similarly, while MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan rose 0.8% — an incomplete rebound. Markets are now evaluating whether higher infrastructure spending can generate earnings and cash flows sufficient to justify capital commitments; results from CoreWeave, Cisco and Applied Materials this week will add evidence. Brent crude declined toward $97.49 per barrel as expectations of further US strikes on Iran diminished, but this does not reverse October's realised producer-price pressures.

::section Second-Order Effects — The Next Capital-Allocation Test | Input costs to competitive advantage
The decisive question is whether rising input costs and weaker downstream demand begin altering the allocation of productive capital. Margin pressure can slow reinvestment; financial differentiation can favour firms with pricing power and strong balance sheets; and AI adoption may become a competitive response if productivity gains exceed added costs. The transmission runs: input costs → operating margins → cash generation → investment capacity → productivity → competitive advantage — and a successful technological transition can interrupt that negative sequence. That is the decisive investment test.

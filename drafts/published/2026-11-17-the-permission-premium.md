The AI Supercycle is entering a new phase. Financial markets are no longer pricing only the cost of producing intelligence—they are beginning to price the political permission, financing credibility and physical infrastructure required to deploy it.

On Monday, Washington introduced new restrictions on advanced AI chips and remote access to computing capacity, extending export controls into overseas data centers serving Chinese companies. The commercial value of computing is becoming conditional on sovereignty.

The Next Economy is developing three barriers to entry: permission to access technology, access to patient capital, and access to reliable infrastructure.

::signals The Five Signals | overnight developments
# Washington extends the AI battlefield to computing access
The rule names Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil as jurisdictions requiring additional licensing; US-operated data centers in those countries are exempt. Overseas centers serving significant Chinese demand must obtain licenses within 90 days. The announcement precedes Trump's expected meeting with Xi in Shenzhen. Computing access is becoming a regulated economic asset: a facility's revenue now depends on the identity and location of its customers. Alibaba fell nearly 5%, Baidu nearly 7%, Malaysia's YTL Power about 5%.
signal: The next premium belongs to whoever can deploy power reliably across regulatory boundaries—not whoever owns the most.

# Nvidia's financing phase is rewritten before earnings
The $500B initiative remains under discussion among Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR, but analysts expect tighter scrutiny of leveraged neocloud operators. Bank of America estimates ~$70B in AI equity investments and $230B in guarantees as of August—commitments, not realized losses. SEC filings show 138 funds exited data-center stocks against 119 initiating. Nvidia reports tonight: ~$109B revenue, ~90% earnings growth expected.
signal: Separate the operating franchise from the quality and risk distribution of its financing commitments.

# The Fed and the dollar enter the AI function
Chair Warsh acknowledged possible productivity gains alongside risks of labor displacement and disappointing returns. The dollar reached 103.69, its strongest since April 2025; the euro fell to ~1.0985, the yen near 160. A December rate increase remains expected. Monday's selloff persisted despite lower oil and yields—valuation, positioning and financing uncertainty are contributing independently.
signal: The Fed must assess how the financing of AI changes the economy before productivity gains arrive.

# Europe: banking integration advances as sovereigns diverge
UniCredit received unconditional EC competition approval and US Fed approval for its ~€45B Commerzbank acquisition—an important step, not yet a completed merger. Meanwhile, France's pensions are expected to exceed €430B next year as Le Pen presents proposals ahead of 2027. Sovereign credibility increasingly sets the cost at which banks and businesses access capital.
signal: Assess whether consolidation becomes productive capital—not merely larger balance sheets.

# The EBRD warning makes the Great Split visible
Across roughly 40 economies, the Transition Report finds that in almost half, electricity grids cannot support even a single data center. Yet many can still capture value by applying AI in industry, healthcare, agriculture and logistics without owning frontier infrastructure.
signal: The divide will separate countries that turn AI into productivity from those whose physical and financial constraints prevent it.

::section The Hidden Pattern | permission as economic condition
Capturing the value of intelligence now requires three conditions. The **regulatory condition**: the legal right to acquire and deploy computing. The **financial condition**: financing on terms consistent with sustainable returns. The **physical condition**: electricity, grid access and reliable operations.

These can become self-reinforcing. Regulatory certainty improves revenue visibility, which supports financing, which enables deployment, which generates returns that sustain further investment. The reverse dynamic is equally powerful. The next global hierarchy will be shaped by the interaction of three scarce resources: technological access, patient capital and reliable electricity.

::takeaways Five takeaways | for investors, boards and policymakers
- **Investors:** regulatory permission is now a valuation variable—customer eligibility and licensing certainty must enter models explicitly.
- **Investors:** AI financing requires discrimination; demand alone does not establish investment quality.
- **Boards:** protect strategic access as carefully as technological capability through diversified customers, jurisdictions and funding.
- **European policymakers:** financial integration must become productive capital formation while preserving sovereign credibility.
- **All:** the Great Split is entering its most consequential phase—advantage depends on combining access, certainty and profitable deployment.

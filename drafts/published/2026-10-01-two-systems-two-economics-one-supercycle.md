America is building increasingly powerful intelligence. China is building increasingly abundant intelligence.

The U.S.–China AI race is no longer primarily a contest over who has the best model. It is becoming a contest between two fundamentally different economic systems for producing and deploying intelligence.

America is maximizing the *value* of intelligence. China is maximizing the *volume*. That distinction changes the investment thesis: America pushes the frontier higher, China pushes the deployment cost lower. One creates ==scarcity value==, the other creates ==abundance== — and together they are making the AI Supercycle larger.

::pull THE GREAT SPLIT
America compounds through **CAPABILITY × CAPITAL**. China compounds through **EFFICIENCY × SCALE**.

::section America: Intelligence As A High-Value Capital Asset | The value loop
The American system starts with one assumption: superior intelligence creates disproportionate economic value. Its loop runs **CAPITAL → COMPUTE → CAPABILITY → HIGH-VALUE TASKS → CASH FLOW → MORE CAPITAL**.

The U.S. combines the deepest capital markets with frontier laboratories, advanced semiconductors, hyperscale cloud and enormous AI factories. Its objective is not cheap intelligence first — it is *better* intelligence first: better reasoning, coding, science, agents, autonomous research and Physical AI.

The economic bet is enormous: if intelligence becomes more capable, its economic value rises faster than its cost. That justifies the extraordinary CAPEX. ==America is effectively financializing the frontier.==

::section China: Intelligence As An Industrial Input | The volume loop
China starts from almost the opposite assumption: intelligence becomes most powerful when it is cheap enough to put everywhere. Its loop runs **EFFICIENCY → LOWER COST → DEPLOYMENT → SCALE → DATA → OPTIMIZATION → STILL LOWER COST**.

DeepSeek attacks model economics. Huawei attacks compute constraints. CANN attacks CUDA dependence. Chinese memory and networking attack infrastructure bottlenecks. Robotics and manufacturing turn cheaper intelligence into physical output. And offshore compute access can potentially bridge whatever frontier gap remains.

The Chinese objective is not necessarily to build the world's single best model. It is to make powerful intelligence ==cheap enough to become an industrial commodity==.

::signals The Strategic Divide | Scarcity versus abundance

# America Monetizes Scarcity
Create intelligence few others can produce, deploy it into high-value tasks, capture premium economics, and reinvest the rents into the next frontier. The American system asks: how valuable can the next unit of intelligence become?
signal: Premium economics funds the next frontier.

# China Industrializes Abundance
Make intelligence increasingly sufficient, compress its cost, deploy it across enormous volumes, and turn scale into data, learning and lower unit economics. China asks: how many units of intelligence can the economy absorb?
signal: Scale turns sufficiency into a flywheel.

# China No Longer Needs Parity
China does not need Ascend = Nvidia, CANN = CUDA, or DeepSeek = the best U.S. frontier model. It needs only that **Chinese stack economics ≤ economic value of Chinese AI**. A weaker accelerator can be compensated by better interconnect, more efficient models, cheaper energy, lower-cost inference and manufacturing scale. The objective shifts from technological parity to economic sufficiency.
signal: The hurdle is sufficiency, not parity.

# The American Risk Is Not Losing The Frontier
The biggest American risk may not be China building a better model — it may be China making frontier superiority less economically decisive. If one system delivers 100 capability for $100 while another delivers 90 for $10, enormous categories — manufacturing, customer service, logistics, coding, mobility, robotics, industrial automation, edge AI, emerging markets — may choose the second. China does not need to eliminate the gap; it needs to make the gap worth less.
signal: The threat is a cheaper good-enough, not a better best.

# The Chinese Risk Is The Opposite
If frontier intelligence begins producing non-linear returns, being slightly behind becomes extremely expensive. Better AI could accelerate AI research, drug discovery, chip design, software engineering, robotics and scientific discovery — making the American capability advantage recursive: **FRONTIER AI → AI BUILDS BETTER AI → FRONTIER MOVES FASTER**. If that mechanism dominates, abundance below the frontier may not compensate for falling behind at the top.
signal: Recursive capability could outrun commoditization.

::section The Real Battle: Compounding | Which system compounds faster
The decisive question is no longer who has the best model. It is which system compounds faster.

The **American loop**: better intelligence → higher-value applications → higher margins → more capital → more compute → better intelligence.

The **Chinese loop**: cheaper intelligence → more deployment → more users and machines → more data and optimization → lower costs → cheaper intelligence.

America compounds through **CAPABILITY × CAPITAL**. China compounds through **EFFICIENCY × SCALE**. That is the Great Split.

::patterns Digital America vs. Physical China | The next phase

# Digital America
America is strongest where intelligence creates high-value digital outputs: science, coding, finance, agents and knowledge work. America may invent intelligence faster.

# Physical China
China possesses an extraordinary transmission mechanism into factories, robots, vehicles, logistics, energy systems and infrastructure — a potentially powerful advantage in Physical AI. China may embody intelligence faster.

# Build
Huawei + CANN + domestic memory + sovereign infrastructure. Build what you cannot safely rent.

# Rent
The reported Tencent–Oracle arrangement shows China can access frontier American compute offshore where possible. Rent what you cannot yet build.

::pull TWO EQUATIONS
American AI power: **CAPABILITY × COMPUTE × CAPITAL × VALUE PER TASK**, optimizing the *value* of intelligence. Chinese AI power: **EFFICIENCY × SYSTEM COST × INDUSTRIAL SCALE × DEPLOYMENT VELOCITY**, optimizing the *abundance* of intelligence.

::section The Second-Order Effect | Why both can scale at once
Here lies the most important investment conclusion: the two systems do not cancel one another — ==they accelerate one another==.

America raises capability. China lowers cost. Higher capability creates more valuable use cases; lower cost makes more use cases economical. The addressable market expands from both directions.

The loop becomes: **Capability ↑ • Cost ↓ → Applications ↑↑ → Inference ↑↑ → Infrastructure ↑ → Capital Formation ↑ → Capability ↑ + Cost ↓.** That is the AI Supercycle.

::takeaways Key takeaways | Two theories of economic power
- Stop treating U.S.–China AI as one race — the systems optimize different economic variables.
- America owns the capability frontier, strongest where marginal intelligence creates disproportionate value.
- China is building the economic frontier — making sufficiently powerful intelligence cheap, deployable and ubiquitous.
- China's objective is not parity but making American superiority less economically decisive.
- America's counterweapon is recursive intelligence: frontier AI accelerating AI research itself.
- Physical AI may become China's strongest battlefield, converting cheap intelligence into industrial output at scale.
- The infrastructure thesis strengthens under either outcome — more capability and lower costs both drive more compute, memory, networking and power.

::close America is building the frontier. China is building the market. If both succeed — as increasingly appears possible — ==intelligence becomes both more powerful and cheaper at the same time==, the most expansionary outcome of all.

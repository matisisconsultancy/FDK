The monetary thesis is now decisive: AI is tightening the economy before it is transforming it. The build-out is already pulling on power, compute, infrastructure and financing. The offset — higher output, lower unit costs and broader efficiency gains — is still arriving.

That means the first-order effect of the AI Supercycle is not disinflation. It is pressure on capital, inputs and rates. The sequence runs from AI CapEx to demand and input pressure, to a higher cost of capital, to less room for easing. Only later does AI diffusion drive higher output, lower unit costs and, finally, disinflation.

The evidence now lines up across three channels.

::pull ONE UNDERLYING RACE: HOW FAST CAN AI CAPITAL BECOME LOWER-COST OUTPUT?

::signals The Three Channels | Demand, inflation, supply response
# Fed — Demand
AI CapEx flows into compute, power and infrastructure, lifting investment and financing demand, which translates into rate pressure. Williams notes that the aggregate efficiency effect is not yet clearly visible.
signal: Capital demand runs ahead of any measurable efficiency offset.
# BoE — Inflation
Energy and input costs feed into prices, then into expectations and wages, forcing a monetary response. Bailey warns that policymakers may need to act before second-round effects are fully embedded.
signal: Central banks may move ahead of embedded second-round effects.
# Singapore — Supply Response
AI adoption drives skills and job redesign, raising output per worker and lowering unit costs. Singapore is retraining more than 80,000 financial-sector workers to accelerate the transition.
signal: The supply-side offset depends on how fast labor is redeployed.

::section Investor Implications | Positioning while the capital cycle leads
Stay selective on duration while capital demand runs ahead of measurable gains. Own the build-out selectively: compute, power, networks, data centers, cooling and semiconductor equipment. Favor businesses already converting AI into margins, cash flow and higher returns on capital.

Watch the inflection in output per hour, unit costs, margins, labor utilization and returns on AI spending. When those metrics turn decisively, markets can begin pricing AI as an economic transformation cycle rather than primarily a capital cycle.

::section Europe's Test | Compress the chain
Europe must compress one chain: capital → compute → adoption → output. The risk is not simply investing too little. It is investing heavily while converting too slowly. Europe needs fewer years between AI CapEx and measurable economic return.

::pull THE VELOCITY INSIGHT: THE NEXT MONETARY REGIME WILL BE SHAPED BY HOW QUICKLY AI MOVES FROM RAISING THE COST OF CAPITAL TO LOWERING THE COST OF PRODUCTION.

::takeaways The Investor Verdict | Four rules for the capital cycle
- Own the enablers selectively.
- Demand cash flow.
- Respect rate risk.
- Rotate when output gains become data.

::close The next monetary regime will be shaped by one variable — ==how quickly AI moves from raising the cost of capital to lowering the cost of production==.

Agentic AI is shifting control from apps, search and marketplaces toward the agent that captures intent, selects outcomes and executes decisions.

These are not isolated product moves. They point to a new control layer — and the next platform war is about who controls the decision before the transaction begins.

::section The Evidence | What the moves reveal

Apple is tightening Mac data access as autonomous agents demand broader permissions. OpenAI is pushing always-on agents designed to operate across applications and computers. Meta is moving AI beyond apps into glasses and ambient devices.

Google is extending AI from search into shopping and transactions. Amazon is increasing AI's role in discovery, comparison and automated purchasing. Alibaba, Alipay and Tencent already connect commerce, payments, communication and services inside integrated ecosystems.

At the same time, capital spending is accelerating across accelerators, memory, networking, data centers and power.

::pull FROM EVIDENCE TO THESIS
Traditional architecture runs *Intent → Search → App → Marketplace → Transaction*. The emerging architecture collapses to *Intent → Agent → Execution*. If the agent decides what to search, which product to select, which merchant receives the order and how payment is settled, bargaining power moves upstream — and ==margins eventually follow==.

::section Margin-Pool Migration | The structural transition

Search economics give way to orchestration economics. Marketplace economics give way to agent economics. App distribution gives way to machine-callable services. Advertising rents give way to selection rents.

This is margin-pool migration. The next platform war is about who controls the decision before the transaction begins.

::signals The Three Bifurcations | How value re-sorts

# Interface vs. Orchestration
Apps remain — but increasingly as callable services. The agent decides which app, which merchant, which product, which payment rail. Apple aims to become the operating system for agents; Google to move from search to execution; Amazon to own the shopping decision; Meta to own ambient context.
signal: Own the orchestrator — or compete to be called by it.

# Attention vs. Authority
The internet monetized attention. Agentic AI monetizes permission to act. That elevates identity, trust, payments, security and context. The most valuable agent may not be the smartest — it may be the one users trust to execute.
signal: Authority to act outranks attention to see.

# Discovery vs. Selection
Digital platforms monetize visibility. Agents can evaluate thousands of alternatives and return one. The economics shift from being seen to being selected — pressuring search advertising, marketplace advertising and app discovery.
signal: The next discipline is Agent Optimization.

::signals The Financial Transmission Mechanism | From technology to the income statement

# Fewer monetizable decision points
If agents compress search, comparison and navigation, traditional monetizable surfaces can shrink: fewer clicks, less sponsored inventory, lower discovery revenue, pressure on traffic-based monetization.
signal: Compressed journeys mean fewer places to charge.

# Higher conversion, but tougher economics
Agents can improve transaction efficiency and lower friction, raising conversion and GMV. But greater price transparency and automated comparison can pressure take rates, merchant margins, CAC and pricing power.
signal: More GMV, thinner spreads.

# Margin moves toward the control layer
The platform closest to intent and authorization can monetize through subscriptions, transaction fees, outcome-based advertising, premium routing and embedded payments.
signal: Proximity to intent becomes proximity to margin.

# Capital intensity rises
More autonomous workflows mean more inference, memory, networking and power — driving higher capex, higher depreciation, greater financing needs and higher hurdle rates. The key divergence: revenue can accelerate while ROIC deteriorates.
signal: Growth and returns can pull in opposite directions.

# Valuation dispersion widens
The market should increasingly distinguish high control plus low capital burden from high volume plus high capital burden plus weak pricing power. The transmission path: agent adoption → control shift → pricing-power shift → margin shift → ROIC dispersion → multiple dispersion.
signal: Dispersion, not adoption, is the story.

# Hardware

::section The U.S.–China Split | Frontier vs. diffusion

The U.S. retains advantages in frontier models and accelerated computing. China's structural advantage may lie in integrated deployment across commerce, payments, communication and services.

U.S. → frontier capability. China → diffusion and execution. The AI Supercycle will increasingly reward whoever converts intelligence into transactions, productivity and cash flow fastest.

::signals The Investor Hierarchy | Where economics concentrate

# Orchestrators
Highest strategic value sits with platforms controlling intent, context, identity, authorization and transaction. They sit closest to the economic decision and are best positioned to capture premium economics.
signal: Closest to the decision, closest to the margin.

# Infrastructure With Operating Leverage
Accelerators, memory, networking, power and data centers remain direct beneficiaries. But volume alone is not enough — watch utilization vs. capex, pricing power, cost per inference and incremental free cash flow.
signal: Demand is given; unit economics are not.

# Trust and Transaction Rails
Agents cannot execute without authorization. That elevates identity, payments, authentication, security and permissions — the toll roads of agentic commerce.
signal: No execution without authorization.

# Incumbents at an Inflection
Search engines, marketplaces and app stores retain scale. But investors should demand proof that AI expands economics rather than merely protects traffic — watch revenue per query, advertising yield, take rates, conversion and agent-driven transactions.
signal: Prove expansion, not just defense.

# Execution Layers at Risk
The greatest disintermediation risk lies where businesses do not own intent, lack proprietary data, have weak pricing power and are easily callable through APIs. Revenue may rise while margins fall. That is the trap.
signal: Rising revenue, falling margin — the trap.

::patterns AI Beneficiaries vs. AI Value Capturers | All can grow; not all will capture

# Compute providers
Enable intelligence.

# Orchestrators
Control the customer outcome.

::section The Two Investor Priorities | Where to look

First, follow control of the customer decision, not traffic. Traffic is increasingly an incomplete measure of power; the key asset is control of the request before the transaction begins — ownership of intent, context, authorization and selection. That is where bargaining power and margin should concentrate.

Second, separate AI beneficiaries from AI value capturers. Infrastructure can see explosive demand while carrying extraordinary capital intensity. Execution layers can grow volumes while losing pricing power. Orchestrators can capture superior economics with less physical-capital burden. The critical metrics: capex intensity, pricing power, customer control and incremental free cash flow.

::takeaways Key takeaways | What to watch next

- The thesis **strengthens** if agents generate meaningful transaction volumes, advertising moves from clicks toward outcomes, apps expose themselves as agent-callable services, payments embed into agent workflows, and inference growth comes with improving unit economics.
- The thesis **weakens** if agents remain primarily informational and users return to conventional interfaces to decide and transact.
- Orchestrators capture control; infrastructure captures volume; trust rails capture transaction leverage; execution layers face the greatest risk of commoditization.
- The largest revenue beneficiary may not be the largest value capturer — the critical signal is where the ==margin pool== moves, not AI adoption itself.

::close The next great platform will not own the destination. ==It will own the decision that chooses it.==

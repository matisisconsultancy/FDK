AI policy cannot be separated from energy policy, capital-market policy or labor policy. They have become one strategic system. Europe must use the current recovery in activity to mobilize capital before higher rates and another energy shock close the window.

::section The Structural Prescription | What the moment requires
Europe's path forward runs through five reinforcing channels: faster worker transition and technical education; portable benefits and lifelong learning; broader participation in capital ownership; deeper European equity markets; and infrastructure investment that lowers the cost of deploying AI across smaller companies.

The window is finite. The current recovery in activity must be used to mobilize capital before higher rates and another energy shock close it.

::section The Velocity Edge | The next architecture of The Great Split
The services economy is growing. Employment is contracting. Corporate profits are accelerating. Technology shares are becoming less forgiving. Europe is discovering that its industrial incumbents may be better positioned for AI than its start-ups. Oil prices are falling while physical exports remain impaired. Bond yields are declining without returning to the old regime.

These are not separate stories. They form the next architecture of The Great Split. Growth without hiring is not conventional stagnation — it is a reallocation of income, bargaining power and economic control.

The strategic question is no longer only whether AI will raise productivity. It is whether companies and countries can translate that productivity into broader prosperity before the concentration of its gains destabilizes demand, politics and capital formation.

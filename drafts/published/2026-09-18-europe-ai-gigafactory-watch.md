Today brings one development consequential enough to trigger the Watch: Nabiax has formally started construction of an €800 million, 80 MW AI/cloud data center in Alcalá de Henares, Madrid. The first stone was laid on September 18. The facility is designed for AI and cloud workloads, will use electricity supplied from 100% renewable sources, and is expected to support about 1,000 jobs during construction. It will become Nabiax's fifth data center in the Madrid region.

This is not an EU AI Gigafactory award. At 80 MW, it is materially smaller than the eventual Gigafactory class. But strategically, it may be more informative than another multi-gigawatt announcement — because it has crossed the line from *plan → permit → capital commitment → construction*. That is precisely where much of Europe's announced AI capacity still fails.

::signals The Execution Signals | from PowerPoint to concrete
# The important number is not 80 MW
Our framework has increasingly distinguished announced MW from bankable MW. Today Spain adds something more valuable: MW under construction. Madrid now has 41 operating data centers and 15 under construction, with another 40 projects envisaged that the regional government says could represent around €15 billion of investment.

Every step in the chain — land → grid → capital → contractors → construction → operating compute — eliminates another layer of execution risk. A theoretical 500 MW project five years from operation may be economically less valuable than an 80 MW project already pouring concrete.
signal: Add EXECUTABLE MW to the Watch — not just announced or financed capacity.

# Spain's regulatory conflict is producing a natural experiment
Spain is simultaneously tightening the discussion around data-center grid access, renewable additionality and economic value creation, while private infrastructure capital continues to enter Madrid. The relevant question is no longer *will regulation stop investment?* but *which type of investment survives the new regulatory environment?*

A selection mechanism is emerging: speculative grid reservations face greater difficulty, while capitalized, permitted and executable projects continue progressing. Spain could end up with fewer headline GW but a higher percentage reaching operation — validating our Capacity Conversion Velocity thesis. The empirical test over the next year: Operating MW / Announced MW.
signal: If the denominator shrinks but the numerator rises faster, the system has become more productive.

# Madrid is becoming an AI infrastructure cluster
One project creates capacity. Many projects create an ecosystem. Madrid's accumulation of operating and constructing facilities generates circular effects: more data centers lift fiber density, energy infrastructure, specialized construction, the technical labor pool and supplier density — cutting time-to-build and attracting still more capital.

This is industrial clustering applied to accelerated computing. Once the flywheel begins, it becomes increasingly difficult for a competing geography to replicate quickly.
signal: Madrid is accumulating execution capability — that is why today's €800 million matters beyond Nabiax.

# The new scarce asset is time-to-power
Europe increasingly has capital, demand and announced projects. The scarce variable is becoming time-to-power. An AI infrastructure investor asks four questions in sequence: When can I obtain grid capacity? When can construction begin? When can accelerators arrive? When can customer revenue start?

Every additional year between committed equity and operating compute raises financing cost, delays cash generation, increases technology-obsolescence risk, and raises the probability that competing capacity enters first.
signal: The Gigafactory race is partly becoming a competition in calendar time.

::section This Changes The Meaning Of Europe's €30+ Billion Gigafactory Programme | pre-solved infrastructure wins
The EuroHPC tender can support up to seven AI Gigafactories, with as much as €10 billion of EU and national support intended to mobilize at least €20 billion of private investment. Selected projects are expected to begin operating within roughly 18 months of selection.

That timetable is extraordinarily demanding. It creates a significant advantage for consortia that already control land, grid connection, permits, engineering, capital and data-center operating experience. The tender may favor not merely the consortium with the strongest political or technological narrative, but the one with the largest amount of pre-solved infrastructure. Nabiax's construction milestone illustrates why existing data-center ecosystems matter so much.

::section Italy — A Different Form Of AI Value Capture | supplying the physical stack
Italian cable group Prysmian has partnered with Rio Tinto to supply low-carbon aluminum electrical cabling for an Amazon data-center project in Ohio, using aluminum produced through ELYSIS inert-anode technology. The project is outside Europe, so it does not change today's European Gigafactory capacity map — but it illustrates an important second-order opportunity for Italy.

AI infrastructure requires enormous volumes of cables, transformers, switchgear, cooling, power electronics, construction engineering, storage and grid equipment. Europe does not need to own every GPU to capture substantial value from the AI Supercycle. European industrial companies can become suppliers to the global physical AI stack — and the infrastructure supply chain itself may become one of Europe's largest AI profit pools.

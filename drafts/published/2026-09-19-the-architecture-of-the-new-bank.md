The industrial mechanics of the MPS combination are finally emerging, and they point to something more sophisticated than a branch-cutting merger. According to *la Repubblica*, MPS told Consob in an August 25 response that the Banco BPM integration could involve roughly 5,000 voluntary early-retirement exits — mainly in central and commercial-coordination functions — generating up to €400 million of staff-cost savings, alongside between 57 and 115 branch disposals depending on the antitrust threshold ultimately applied.

This adds detail rather than contradiction. In its September 14 Consob-requested disclosure, MPS confirmed €1.4 billion of annual pre-tax run-rate synergies from Banco BPM, including €1.0 billion of cost synergies, largely from overlapping central functions and technology platforms — and said it did not currently envisage branch closures beyond any measures required by antitrust authorities. The apparent tension is only superficial: MPS is planning to extract scale from **central functions, technology, personnel, funding and cross-selling**, while preserving the distribution franchise as far as competition law allows.

::pull THE GENERALI STAKE COULD BECOME PART OF THE STRATEGIC ARCHITECTURE OF THE NEW BANK.

The most consequential reported detail lies elsewhere. According to the same report, MPS intends to acquire from Mediobanca the full 13.3% stake in Generali before the October 29 meeting. The planned €4 billion extraordinary distribution includes roughly €3 billion in Generali shares — about 4.5% of Generali's capital. The inference is stark: if the reported structure were implemented exactly as described and no further disposal occurred, roughly ==8.8% of Generali would remain inside MPS== after the in-kind distribution. That is not a rounding error. It raises the stakes for Intesa, Generali, AGCM, IVASS, the Italian Government and the eventual governance coalition around MPS.

::section Brussels turns more pro-scale | The policy wind
On September 18, EU officials explicitly argued for larger banks, fewer barriers to cross-border banking and less political interference in consolidation. Vujcic linked scale to Europe's ability to compete in trading, technology and financial infrastructure — while stressing that the answer is not weaker capital requirements but a more complete Banking and Capital Markets Union.

The European policy direction is becoming clearer: **more scale, not less capital; more integration, not more national vetoes.** That puts pressure on the traditional use of national industrial policy in banking, and it matters for Italy as much as for UniCredit–Commerzbank.

::section The decisive issue | Local franchise versus continental scale
How much local franchise must be sacrificed to create continental scale? A larger group can build technology, funding, wealth-management and capital-allocation scale, and a greater ability to finance AI, infrastructure, defence and industrial transformation. But that scale only creates value if duplication is removed:

**Scale premium − integration cost − antitrust remedies − political cost = realizable value.**

The paradox is that Europe wants banks with continental reach precisely because national systems remain fragmented — yet the larger the national combinations become, the more they trigger competition concerns, employment consequences, territorial sensitivities and questions over strategic control. The path to Banking Union runs directly through domestic political economy.

::signals Updated strategic map | FDK views across the field
# Intesa Sanpaolo — execution remains the advantage
Intesa still possesses the cleanest execution architecture. Shareholders have approved the capital increase supporting the MPS offer, and IVASS has cleared the indirect insurance participations arising from the transaction. The remaining material Italian hurdle is increasingly concentrated around AGCM remedies and the handling of Generali-related interests.

The new details strengthen Intesa's argument that the alternative MPS architecture is industrially complex — multiple mergers, shareholder votes, integration programs and third-party cooperation — but the plan to move the full Generali stake into MPS could make Intesa's eventual acquisition perimeter more strategically sensitive.
signal: FDK view — Strong Overweight. Execution Premium remains intact.

# MPS — the defence is becoming an industrial plan
MPS can no longer be described merely as mounting a tactical takeover defence. The public numbers already showed serious architecture: €1.8 billion of annual pre-tax synergies from the two offers, €1.9 billion of one-off integration costs, a 2029 run-rate target and a Banco BPM combination projected to deliver a cost-income ratio around 37% and ROTE above 18%.

The reported Consob correspondence puts operational substance behind those numbers — but also exposes the cost. Five thousand voluntary exits, antitrust remedies and a consequential Generali stake create a more credible plan and a more politically sensitive one.
signal: FDK view — Event-Driven / High Convexity. The plan is stronger than it looked; so is the execution burden.

# Banco BPM — the target becomes the operating engine
Banco BPM is central not for its standalone size but because it supplies what MPS lacks most: Northern Italy density, commercial scale, Anima exposure, SME distribution and a broader deposit franchise. The reported antitrust calculations suggest overlap that is meaningful but manageable enough to model targeted remedies rather than wholesale network dismantling.
signal: Banco BPM is the operating engine of any credible MPS-led third pole — but Crédit Agricole remains the gatekeeper.

# Crédit Agricole — the kingmaker would enter the castle
Crédit Agricole still owns 29.3% of Banco BPM and reportedly considers the MPS offer unattractive, preferring a future Banco BPM–Crédit Agricole Italia combination. Yet the reported post-deal cap table reveals a second-order effect: if both MPS offers succeeded in full, Crédit Agricole would become the largest shareholder of the combined group at roughly 10.9%; if only Banco BPM were acquired, its stake could rise to approximately 12.5%.

The paradox is striking: MPS's defence against Intesa could import Crédit Agricole directly into the core shareholder base of the new MPS. MPS cannot truly bypass Crédit Agricole — only change the arena in which it exercises influence.
signal: FDK view — Strategic Optionality Premium.

# Generali — from asset to centre of gravity
If MPS acquires the entire Mediobanca stake before distributing only part of it, Generali would remain deeply embedded in the post-transaction architecture. Generali is simultaneously a strategic insurer, the controlling shareholder of Banca Generali, an asset inside MPS/Mediobanca, a variable in Intesa's antitrust perimeter, and potentially a cornerstone shareholding of the enlarged MPS group. Its independence is not becoming less relevant — it is becoming more valuable.
signal: FDK view — Overweight. Strategic Scarcity Premium rising.

# Banca Generali — wealth management without network destruction
MPS has publicly said Banca Generali would become the centre of gravity of the enlarged wealth-management hub, with roughly 2,500 financial advisers and €117 billion of total financial assets, while preserving the network's autonomy and avoiding adviser-network rationalization. The model — **cut duplication, preserve distribution quality** — is exactly what a credible wealth-management integration should attempt.
signal: Cost from banking duplication, protection for the high-value wealth franchise.

# UniCredit–Commerzbank — Brussels moves toward Orcel's thesis
The most important development for UniCredit is not in Berlin but in Brussels. The September 18 call for larger banks and less political interference directly reinforces the strategic logic of the Commerzbank transaction. Germany still seeks safeguards around headquarters, listing, Mittelstand lending, jobs and board representation, while UniCredit reportedly wants significant governance change. The argument is shifting from *should* cross-border consolidation happen toward *under what governance conditions* it should happen.
signal: FDK view — Overweight. Continental Scale Premium rising; Governance Discount remains.

::section Capital-markets reaction | A risk-off session, not a referendum
Friday was a broad European risk-off session rather than a clean referendum on Italian banking M&A. The FTSE MIB fell about 1.6%. UniCredit lost roughly 3%, Intesa 2.1%, Banco BPM 2.0%, Generali 1.9% and MPS 1.5%. Banca Generali fell about 1.5%. The STOXX 600 declined around 1.1%. Italy's 10-year yield rose to roughly 4.43%, with the BTP–Bund spread around 91 basis points.

The relative signal: MPS again slightly outperformed Banco BPM, with MPS/BPM improving roughly 0.5% from Thursday using Friday closes of €11.764 and €15.70.

But the valuation correction matters more. MPS itself calculates offer economics net of the €1.208 extraordinary distribution existing shareholders receive before target shareholders join. On the same methodology and Friday prices — adjusted MPS value of €11.764 − €1.208 = €10.556 — Banco BPM implied consideration is 1.567 × €10.556 ≈ €16.54 versus a €15.70 price, an implied premium of roughly **5.4%**. For Banca Generali, 6.958 × €10.556 ≈ €73.45 versus a €65.00 close, a premium of roughly **13.0%**. The true question is not whether the offers look generous, but whether 5–13% of adjusted premium is enough to compensate for the execution risk.

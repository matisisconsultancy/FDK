The global economy is entering a new financial regime. Three forces are simultaneously repricing the cost of economic progress: sovereign financing risk, the rising cost of AI infrastructure and the geopolitical premium embedded in energy.

French government-bond yields are approaching their highest levels in 24 years. Brent crude has returned above $110 per barrel. CoreWeave's debt has repriced severely, while a South Korean AI infrastructure company lost almost one-third of its value on its first trading day. Yet demand is not collapsing: China's ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent have reportedly placed more than $35 billion in orders for Nvidia processors still permissible under U.S. export controls.

The paradox is unmistakable. Demand for intelligence keeps accelerating while the capacity to supply it becomes more expensive, more concentrated and more politically contested. This is the new dimension of **The Great Split** — a distinction between those capable of financing transformation through sustainable cash flow and those whose ambitions depend on increasingly expensive external capital.

::pull THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
Is the AI Supercycle creating enough productivity to justify its unprecedented financial demands — or is the rest of the economy being forced to absorb the inflation, financing costs and political consequences of its expansion?
::

::signals Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy | repricing across markets
# Europe — France Becomes the Eurozone's Sovereign Stress Test
French 10-year yields reached ~4.96% on Tuesday, near this month's 4.99% high; the French–German spread widened to 152.5 bps while the Bund fell to ~3.43%. Italy's spread widened to ~113.5 bps. France faces a parliamentary vote on its 2027 budget revenues and further demonstrations on October 24. The transmission: political instability → fiscal uncertainty → sovereign repricing → banking pressure → weaker investment. France may become a systemic test not only of Eurozone stability but of Europe's capacity to finance its next industrial transformation.
signal: Watch the French–German spread and evidence of contagion into other sovereigns.

# AI Infrastructure — Credit Markets Begin to Discipline the Supercycle
CoreWeave carries ~$50bn in debt and $24bn in committed term loans (≈$15bn drawn), per J.P. Morgan. During last week's stress its 2031 bonds approached 16% and CDS neared 1,000 bps. Bank exposures appear largely secured — $1bn–$3bn for the largest U.S. banks and MUFG — but free cash flow is expected negative until 2030. Financing obligations arise before infrastructure generates revenue.
signal: The next rotation favors growth financed by returns over growth financed by repeated issuance.

# Energy — The Geopolitical Premium Becomes an Industrial Tax
Brent reached $111.05, a five-month high, after Houthi claims of attacks on Saudi energy and airport facilities (not independently confirmed). Kpler reported at least 18 vessels attacked in two weeks; Dutch TTF gas traded around €89.60/MWh. The European Commission estimates the Iran conflict has cost Europe ~€100 billion. The chain: energy insecurity → higher costs → inflation → higher financing costs → lower investment flexibility.
signal: Reliable, competitively priced electricity is becoming a factor of production.

# U.S.–China — The $35 Billion Chip Order Reveals the Next Fault Line
ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent have ordered more than three million Nvidia RTX PRO 5500 cards — over $35bn in potential value — for AI inference workloads. Nvidia may fulfill only 5%–10% of requested quantities. China's Q3 GDP grew 4.5%, industrial production 5.5%, but retail sales just 1.1% and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2%. The asymmetry widens: U.S. frontier computing and capital against Chinese industrial scaling and deployment.
signal: The contest may be decided by who deploys intelligence most efficiently.

# Global Capital Markets — The AI Repricing Spreads to Asia
South Korea's Elice Group fell ~32% on debut after a ~$150m IPO; the KOSPI dropped 3.73%. Europe's STOXX 600 fell ~0.6%, with ASML and ASMI down more than 3%. Google Cloud's London region and Revolut suffered linked disruptions — a reminder that operational resilience matters as much as financial resilience.
signal: Assess infrastructure across financial sustainability × energy reliability × operational resilience.
::

::section The Hidden Pattern — A Three-Dimensional Capital Divide | how the premiums interact
The Supercycle creates an asymmetric monetary-policy problem: large technology firms keep investing despite high rates while households and smaller firms are far more rate-sensitive. The Fed meets October 27–28 with rates expected at 3.75%–4.00%.

Three risk premiums — sovereign, energy and AI infrastructure — are no longer independent. A persistent energy shock reinforces inflation, tightens policy and raises borrowing costs, simultaneously weakening sovereign finances and infrastructure returns — a potential negative feedback loop, not an inevitable outcome.

Above all, markets are repricing the *quality* of capital. Bunds and the Swiss franc strengthen while French bonds and leveraged AI assets face scrutiny. ==Capital is increasingly rewarding financing resilience, operational certainty and credible cash-flow generation== — across sovereigns, corporations and infrastructure alike.
::

::takeaways Five Key Takeaways for Investors and Boards | what the regime demands
- Capital productivity is becoming decisive — prioritize returns on invested capital and free cash flow over scale of announced investment.
- The Supercycle is entering a phase of financial selection — differentiate suppliers, leveraged operators and commercially productive platforms.
- Europe faces a sovereign-energy-industrial collision affecting banking valuations, industrial investment and sovereign spreads.
- U.S.–China competition is accelerating toward AI inference and industrial deployment, where efficiency and utilization decide outcomes.
- The next rotation will favor financial and operational resilience over dependence on repeated external funding.
::

The most consequential development this morning is not another increase in frontier-model size. It is *where* AI is beginning to run.

Anthropic has signed its first Australian data-centre agreement at a campus planned for 2.16 GW of capacity near Brisbane, with first operations expected from 2027 — a facility intended for AI inference rather than model training. At almost the same moment, MediaTek has launched a 2-nanometre smartphone processor designed explicitly for always-on Agentic AI, capable of supporting models up to 30 billion parameters on-device. And European inference-chip company Axelera AI says its processors are moving into Dell and Supermicro systems and EU AI Factories, with more than 600 customers already using its technology.

Taken separately, these are product announcements. Taken together, they indicate something more important: ==training remains concentrated, inference is beginning to diffuse.==

::pull DISTRIBUTION
The next phase of the AI Supercycle may be defined not by who owns the largest AI factory, but by how densely intelligence can be distributed across regions, enterprises, factories, vehicles and devices.

::signals Where Intelligence Runs | training core, inference periphery
# The architecture begins splitting into Training Core and Inference Periphery
Anthropic has secured its first Australian data-centre agreement at a proposed campus roughly 250 kilometres from Brisbane, with planned capacity of 2.16 GW. The facility is expected to begin coming online in 2027, designed to use renewable electricity and closed-loop air cooling. Reuters reports the site is intended for inference rather than training; regulatory approval is still required. Anthropic already has an Australian policy relationship, having signed a March agreement with the government covering AI safety research and information sharing.

The distinction between training and inference is becoming economically structural. Training favors enormous, concentrated clusters; inference derives value from running existing models repeatedly for millions of businesses and billions of users. The scarce resource is no longer simply compute capacity — it is deployable compute in the right geography.
signal: This is not the end of hyperscale concentration; it is the beginning of a second infrastructure layer beneath it.

# The AI factory begins shrinking into the device
MediaTek's new Dimensity 9600 Pro is built on a 2-nanometre process with an architecture optimized for Agentic AI running locally on smartphones. Its NPU delivers 51% higher LLM prefill performance, 55% more token generation per watt, and a 40% reduction in power consumption for always-on AI versus the previous generation. It supports models of up to 30 billion parameters, with first devices expected this quarter.

The next compute architecture is unlikely to be cloud versus edge. It will be **cloud + regional inference + device** — the cloud creating and coordinating intelligence, regional infrastructure scaling deployment, devices increasingly executing it. That architecture could ultimately generate far more inference than training.
signal: The smartphone becomes sensor array, personal data store, inference engine and AI agent at once.

# Enterprise AI moves from copilots toward delegated production
AI coding-agent company Factory raised $200 million at a $5 billion valuation, roughly tripling its valuation. Its objective is enabling enterprises to delegate increasingly long-duration software-development tasks to autonomous agents. The unit of automation changes from the task to the workflow.

Software engineering matters because software is a production input across virtually every modern industry. If agents reduce the marginal cost of software development, they indirectly reduce the cost of automation everywhere else. Still, there is not yet enough audited productivity evidence to declare a broad macroeconomic inflection — that baseline remains unchanged.
signal: Investors increasingly see Agentic AI as an enterprise-production layer, not merely a user-interface improvement.

::section Capital Begins Following Conversion | the Great Split
Three financing signals now sit next to one another. Factory raises $200 million for enterprise agents. Delos Data raises $100 million to solve data movement inside increasingly heterogeneous AI factories. Axelera AI, which has raised more than $450 million, says commercial agreements for its European inference processors could ultimately represent as much as $1.5 billion of sales opportunities.

The common denominator is striking: none is primarily financing another frontier foundational model. Capital is moving toward inference, networking, enterprise agents and deployment infrastructure. **Capability Capital** builds better models; **Conversion Capital** turns models into economic activity. As frontier models become more widely available, the economic question shifts toward who can turn intelligence into the largest volume of productive transactions.

::section Who Pays for AI Infrastructure? | energy, grids and geography
Australian policy requires large data centres to underwrite new power supply, pay their share of grid-connection costs, reduce consumption when system conditions require, and meet water-efficiency expectations — preventing AI infrastructure from shifting energy costs onto households. Official estimates put data-centre electricity consumption at roughly 2% of national use today, potentially rising toward 6% by 2030.

Anthropic's expansion provides a real-world test: AI investor arrives, developer secures land and additional power, infrastructure costs internalized, grid externality reduced, political legitimacy improves, permitting eases, further investment becomes possible. The countries that solve the allocation of infrastructure costs most credibly may attract more AI capital precisely because they create greater political durability.

Australia's comparative advantage is not frontier-model development but a combination of large renewable resources, available land, proximity to Asian markets, political stability, advanced capital markets and rising domestic demand. OpenAI had already entered an Australian infrastructure partnership; Anthropic now follows. AI geography may begin resembling telecommunications more than traditional cloud computing — large global backbones coexisting with increasingly dense regional nodes.

::section The Assurance Economy Acquires Institutions | who controls the brake
The disagreement among AI leaders is becoming more precise: no longer safety versus no safety, but **centralized coordination versus decentralized assurance.** Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg rejected the idea that frontier laboratories need a coordinated slowdown, arguing competition, liability and corporate responsibility provide adequate incentives — while supporting independent evaluation and noting Meta has itself delayed releases for safety reasons. Meanwhile, OpenAI has reportedly been discussing safety coordination with Anthropic and Google DeepMind for several weeks.

A coordinated slowdown risks becoming a barrier to entry; purely voluntary self-regulation risks underpricing externalities; government regulation risks moving too slowly. Independent evaluation may become the middle layer. Australia added a piece today by announcing a national framework for AI assurance in government, designed to create consistent expectations for how public authorities develop, procure and deploy AI.

::section Europe: No Policy Inflection, but a Commercial Signal | Europe 2030
There is no major new European Commission AI package this morning that materially changes the policy baseline, which remains: AI Factories, up to seven AI Gigafactories, the Cloud and AI Development Act, Chips Act 2.0, Apply AI and the sovereignty framework. The latest AI Gigafactory program is intended to mobilize more than €30 billion, including at least €20 billion of private investment. Policy baseline: **unchanged.**

But the industrial signal is important. Dutch company Axelera AI says its Europa inference chips have won AI-factory supply agreements and will be incorporated into Dell and Supermicro systems. More than 600 customers already use its products, with signed agreements and opportunities potentially representing $1.5 billion in sales; its technology is involved in Italy's IT4LIA AI Factory and Luxembourg's MeluXina ecosystem. Not European subsidy to European prototype — but European R&D to European silicon to global OEM integration to AI Factory deployment to revenue and scale.

::patterns The Inference Localization Law — NERC Hypothesis 07 | three geographic layers
# [1] Training Core
Gigawatt-scale AI factories creating frontier intelligence, gravitating toward locations offering extraordinary power and infrastructure scale.

# [2] Regional Inference Grid
Distributed data centres executing models near economies and users, valuing proximity, data jurisdiction, latency, political alignment and energy availability.

# [3] Intelligent Edge
Vehicles, robots, smartphones and industrial devices running AI locally — where the majority of long-run AI tokens may eventually be generated.

::pull THE GRID
The frontier model may be AI's power plant. Inference is the grid. Electricity generation created enormous industries — but so did the grid; oil production created enormous industries — but so did pipelines, refineries and distribution.

::takeaways Key takeaways | who wins the next chapter
- The hidden pattern extends the chain: BUILD → CONVERT → PROTECT → ASSURE → ABSORB → **DISTRIBUTE** → COMPOUND.
- Realized AI Value = Model Capability × Inference Density × Proximity × Energy Efficiency × Assurance.
- A new Great Split emerges: **Intelligence Producers vs. Intelligence Distributors** — semiconductor firms, regional data-centre operators, telecom networks, sovereign-cloud providers, edge-AI platforms, industrial automation, mobility systems and device manufacturers.
- For Europe, this is potentially good news: it need not win every frontier race to build a powerful position in inference silicon, industrial AI, telecom infrastructure, sovereign cloud, mobility, robotics and edge deployment.
- For investors, metrics like tokens per watt, tokens per euro, utilization, latency and inference revenue become increasingly important.
- For policymakers, sovereign AI is not only sovereign frontier models — it is the ability to operate critical inference locally, under domestic rules, on resilient infrastructure.

::close Training creates intelligence; inference industrializes it. Once intelligence begins moving from a few hyperscale campuses into thousands of data centres, millions of businesses and billions of devices, the economic geography of AI changes fundamentally — ==the AI factory is no longer a place; it is becoming a network.==

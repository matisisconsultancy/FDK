Central banks must distinguish between inflation caused by excess consumption and inflation caused by productive-capacity formation. Treating both identically risks slowing the investment required to generate future productivity.

The original Great Split divided equity optimism from bond-market discipline. The new split runs deeper — through output, employment, profits, wages and the ownership of capital itself.

::pull THE NEW SPLIT
A system can produce record corporate earnings and weak incremental hiring at the same time. That is not a contradiction. It is the emerging distributional logic of Industrial AI.

::signals The Structural Splits | four forces reshaping capital
# The Great Split Is Becoming a Distributional Regime
The new split divides output from employment; profits from wages; productivity from purchasing power; and capital owners from workers whose tasks are being reorganized.
signal: Record earnings alongside weak hiring is the new distributional logic.

# Europe's Advantage Lies in Orchestration at Scale
The United States leads frontier models and hyperscale infrastructure. Europe's strongest assets sit in the integration layer: industrial software, factory automation, regulated industries, mobility, sovereign infrastructure, engineering and operational control.
Europe should stop measuring AI leadership exclusively through model size or venture-capital formation. Its relevant test is whether it can convert existing industrial depth into an integrated system of intelligence.
signal: Europe's test is orchestration, not model size.

# Energy Routes Are Becoming Sovereign Assets
Hormuz demonstrates that physical geography can still overrule financial assumptions. The ability to control, protect or deny passage now produces economic power comparable to ownership of the underlying commodity.
Rail corridors, ports, pipelines, shipping lanes and electricity interconnectors will therefore attract more sovereign capital and political intervention.
signal: Control of passage rivals ownership of the commodity.

# The AI Supercycle Is Raising the Return Threshold for Everyone Else
Capital committed to AI infrastructure is capital unavailable elsewhere unless global savings expand. Industries without pricing power, strategic relevance, contracted demand or short payback periods will find it increasingly difficult to compete for funding.
signal: The Supercycle is repricing the global hierarchy of investment.

::section For Investors | positioning and risk
The strongest positioning remains in companies that combine control of physical or digital bottlenecks; growing incremental margins; contracted demand; internal cash generation; and limited refinancing dependency.

European industrial AI deserves greater attention. Siemens, enterprise software providers, sovereign-cloud platforms, power systems, automation and semiconductor-material suppliers offer exposure to AI deployment without depending entirely on frontier-model economics.

The principal risks are shifting toward AI businesses whose capital spending grows faster than incremental profit; long-duration assets dependent on the restoration of low interest rates; consumer companies exposed to weaker labor-income formation; and industrial companies lacking pricing power over scarce components.

::section For Executives | three accounts for every AI programme
Boards should begin measuring AI programmes across three separate accounts.

::patterns The Three Accounts | how to score an AI programme
# Productivity
How much output or decision velocity is created per employee?

# Capital
How much infrastructure and working capital are required?

# Distribution
How are productivity gains divided among margins, wages, customers and reinvestment?

::section The Discipline | completeness over margin
A programme that improves productivity but fails to generate cash flow is incomplete. A programme that cuts employment without redesigning skills and incentives may create short-term margins but weaken long-term institutional capability.

Executives should also monitor supplier solvency. Evidence of smaller suppliers demanding progress payments and struggling with delivery suggests that strong aggregate demand can conceal fragility deeper in the industrial system.

::section For Policymakers | beyond adoption
The emerging policy challenge is not simply technological adoption — it is governing how the gains of Industrial AI are distributed across capital, labor and the state.

::takeaways Key takeaways | the distributional turn
- Inflation from capacity formation is not inflation from excess consumption — policy must tell them apart.
- Industrial AI splits profits from wages and productivity from purchasing power.
- Europe's edge lies in orchestration and integration, not model size.
- Energy and transit routes are becoming sovereign assets.
- The Supercycle raises the return threshold for every industry without pricing power or contracted demand.

::close Own productivity. Demand cash conversion. ==Hedge distributional risk.==

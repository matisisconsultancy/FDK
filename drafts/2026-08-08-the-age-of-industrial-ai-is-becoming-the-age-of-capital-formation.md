Weak U.S. payrolls have lowered the prospective cost of money. Nvidia is moving deeper into power infrastructure. European equity inflows are accelerating. Hormuz is reminding markets that physical access still commands the highest scarcity premium.

The pattern is becoming clear:

AI is no longer constrained primarily by intelligence. It is constrained by capital formation.

The next phase of the Supercycle will be decided by who can finance, power, secure and scale intelligence faster than the cost of capital, infrastructure and geopolitical fragmentation rise around it.

The Great Split is therefore shifting again—from technology versus legacy industry to capital-rich versus capital-constrained systems.

Five Key Signals

1. Weak employment is becoming a financing event.
The U.S. payroll contraction has reduced tightening expectations and lowered sovereign yields, improving the financing environment for capital-intensive AI infrastructure.

2. Nvidia is evolving from supplier to capital allocator.
Its proposed investment in Lancium signals a deeper move into the infrastructure ecosystem that creates demand for its own accelerated computing.

3. Europe is attracting marginal capital before capturing narrative leadership.
Large equity inflows suggest investors are rotating toward banks, grids, defence, automation and industrial infrastructure.

4. Security is becoming a mandatory AI input.
Frontier-model deployment increasingly requires containment, cyber protection, auditability and regulatory approval—raising the cost of trusted intelligence.

5. Hormuz confirms that access commands more value than ownership.
Oil can exist at the source and still be economically unavailable. The same logic applies to chips, electricity, grids, data centres and capital.

Five Key Takeaways

* The cost of money matters more as AI becomes more capital intensive.
* The value pool is migrating from models toward infrastructure and financing.
* The next winners will control access, not merely assets.
* Europe’s opportunity lies in industrial conversion, not imitation of U.S. model platforms.
* The Great Split is becoming a capital-formation divide.

The Velocity Insight

Intelligence may become abundant. Capital formation will not.

The defining competitive advantage of the next decade may not be who invents intelligence first—

but who can finance its industrialization fastest.

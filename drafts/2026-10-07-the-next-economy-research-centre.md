A new phase of the AI Supercycle became visible on October 6. The largest AI users are beginning to treat scarce complements — power, compute, silicon capacity and sovereign infrastructure — not as external inputs to be purchased when needed, but as strategic assets that must be secured years in advance.

Google and Constellation signed agreements covering 3.59 GW of power in PJM. The centerpiece is a 20-year contract that will finance more than $4.3 billion of investment to add 890 MW of new nuclear output through uprates at 11 existing reactors; another 2.7 GW is covered by a 15-year supply agreement, with first incremental nuclear capacity expected in 2028. SpaceX, meanwhile, is reportedly seeking $40 billion of financing to acquire Nvidia AI chips, with Apollo leading a structure of roughly $10 billion of bank loans and $30 billion of investment-grade debt — reported by the *Financial Times* and Reuters but not yet formally confirmed. In Europe, Mistral launched the preview of Mistral Large 4, a one-trillion-parameter model with 49 billion active parameters, trained from scratch on 3,800 Nvidia Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral-owned European data centres.

Yesterday we argued that the bottleneck sets the return. Today the largest players are responding. When scarcity becomes strategic, the rational response is no longer to purchase it. ==It is to internalize it.== That may define the next stage of AI capitalism.

::signals The Internalization Signals | How scarcity is moving off the spot market
# Europe produces a sovereign open-model signal
Mistral Large 4 is a natively multimodal Mixture-of-Experts model with 1 trillion total and 49 billion active parameters, reporting 59.9% on AutomationBench and 61.7% on DeepSWE v1.1. The preview API is priced at $1.36 per million input tokens and $4.18 per million output tokens, with full weights scheduled for release later in October. Most performance claims currently come from Mistral and require broader independent verification.

The more important fact is where it was built: trained from scratch on 3,800 Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral's own European data centres, served end-to-end under European law. Its recent €3 billion financing is partly expanding that compute estate. Europe's open-model story is no longer merely defensive — it now combines open weights, owned compute, proprietary data and local control.
signal: For Europe, open model plus owned compute plus proprietary data may be more achievable than recreating the U.S. hyperscaler model.

# Custom silicon becomes a structural second rail
Marvell's October 6 investor day raised its fiscal-2028 revenue forecast to approximately $20 billion and now projects roughly $12 billion of custom-chip revenue by fiscal 2029, above the previous $10 billion target — with a longer-term scenario of $70–90 billion by fiscal 2031. These are company forecasts, not contracted revenue. AMD simultaneously said it intends to increase chip supply substantially in 2027, working with TSMC, Foxconn, Samsung and SK Hynix.

The compute market is separating into merchant accelerators (Nvidia, AMD) and custom accelerators (hyperscaler-designed ASICs). As AI volumes rise and workloads stabilize, optimization becomes valuable and hyperscalers internalize chip design. The strategic premium migrates toward advanced packaging, HBM, optics, networking, EDA, chiplet architecture and foundry capacity.
signal: The winner may not be one processor — it may be the entire supply chain surrounding heterogeneous compute.

# Corporate restructuring precedes aggregate productivity
Fair Isaac disclosed in an October 6 SEC filing that it will eliminate approximately 15% of positions — roughly 570 roles — integrating AI-driven product development, with about $27 million of pretax restructuring charges. Yet Apollo's October 5 analysis using Fed and BLS data finds utilization-adjusted U.S. total-factor productivity slightly below zero, with no obvious acceleration since the AI investment cycle began. Output per hour runs around 2.5%, but much is consistent with capital deepening rather than measured TFP acceleration.

Corporate AI restructuring is accelerating; an aggregate productivity breakout remains unproven. The transmission chain — adoption, redesign, capital substitution, reorganized workflows, higher utilization, rising output, eventual TFP — is missing its last step.
signal: A smaller workforce without higher economic output is cost reduction, not productivity transformation.

# The balance sheet becomes a competitive weapon
A proposed $40 billion financing for Nvidia hardware, if completed on reported terms, would push AI compute deeper into conventional institutional credit markets. It follows Nvidia's August initiative with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to mobilize more than $500 billion of third-party capital. Two firms can buy identical GPUs and generate completely different returns if one finances at investment-grade rates and another relies on equity or expensive private credit.

The loop: credit quality up → WACC down → viable capacity up → inference cost down → adoption up → revenue up → credit quality strengthens. The inverse is equally dangerous.
signal: Capital cost is becoming part of AI performance.

# Google moves from electricity buyer to electricity investor
The Google–Constellation agreement supports 890 MW of incremental nuclear generation through upgrades to existing plants in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, with more than $4.3 billion of investment and first capacity scheduled for 2028, plus a 15-year agreement covering another 2.7 GW of existing PJM supply. The structure incorporates demand response and load shaping, while Constellation will deploy Google Cloud and Gemini Enterprise for site selection, grid modelling and asset optimization.

The loop is extraordinary: AI creates power demand, the AI company finances power expansion, that expansion enables more AI, and AI optimizes the power system.
signal: The AI and electricity sectors are beginning to merge operationally.

::section The Energy Price Transmission | The externality becomes measurable
The October 6 EIA outlook gives this boom macroeconomic context. EIA expects U.S. electricity generation to rise from 4,430 billion kWh in 2025 to 4,545 billion in 2026 and 4,613 billion in 2027. Commercial demand — which includes data centres — is forecast to rise 2.8% in 2027, while industrial demand increases 2.7%.

More importantly for the cost side, EIA expects wholesale electricity prices in PJM to rise 41% in 2026 versus 2025. Weather, gas-market conditions, transmission and capacity constraints all contribute, so this increase cannot be attributed solely to AI.

If AI infrastructure raises local power costs without generating corresponding local income, community resistance rises, permitting friction increases, time-to-power lengthens and AI capital costs rise. The chain runs: energy affordability → social licence → project timing → AI ROIC. Power policy is now AI industrial policy.

::section Economic Geography | The geography of AI becomes the geography of secured inputs
A clear geographic division is emerging. The United States combines deep credit markets, hyperscale demand, long-term energy contracting and frontier chips. East Asia retains control over foundry capacity, HBM, advanced packaging and electronics manufacturing. Europe is attempting to combine sovereign models, Gigafactories, industrial demand and regulated infrastructure.

AMD's effort to secure wafer supply in Taiwan and memory capacity in South Korea reinforces that no major AI platform is geographically self-contained. The AI-native economy is not deglobalizing simply — it is moving toward managed interdependence. There is no material new U.S.–China semiconductor-control escalation or détente this morning; baseline unchanged. But Mistral Large 4 and Reflection Beam make a Western, specifically European, open-weight ecosystem more credible.

::section Europe 2030 | Something to put inside the Gigafactory
There is no new major EU-wide legislative or funding package today; policy baseline unchanged. The existing Gigafactory tender remains the core near-term initiative: up to seven facilities, supported by as much as €10 billion of EU and national funding, intended to mobilize at least another €20 billion of private investment, closing November 12. The Cloud and AI Development Act separately targets at least a tripling of EU data-centre capacity within five to seven years.

Mistral changes the interpretation of those policies: sovereign compute without competitive models risks becoming expensive infrastructure searching for workloads. ML4 provides stronger evidence that a European model ecosystem can exist alongside the build-out. But Europe must connect four layers simultaneously — European models, European compute, European energy and European industrial workloads. Every Gigafactory should face an Economic Conversion Test. Sovereignty without utilization is expensive symbolism; sovereignty with industrial conversion becomes competitive advantage.

::patterns The Second-Order Effects | The AI firm begins to look like an infrastructure conglomerate
# Scarcity leaves the spot market
Long-term PPAs, chip-financing vehicles, custom silicon and owned data centres all share one purpose: reduce exposure to future scarcity prices. The AI leaders are effectively buying options on future growth.

# Balance-sheet capacity becomes technological capacity
A company able to commit capital 15 or 20 years forward can secure inputs unavailable to weaker competitors. Finance changes technology outcomes: credit advantage → input advantage → AI advantage.

# The hyperscaler becomes an industrial company
Google is no longer merely consuming power; it is helping fund nuclear-capacity expansion. Technology companies increasingly influence generation, grid design, semiconductors, construction, water, real estate and capital markets. The line between digital and industrial company is eroding.

# Open AI increases the value of owned infrastructure
An open-weight model can be moved, making private compute, private data and local energy more strategically valuable. Proprietary APIs centralize intelligence; open models make it geographically portable — accelerating sovereign-AI investment.

# The productivity question becomes more urgent
The infrastructure keeps getting larger while the aggregate productivity proof remains absent. Every additional billion invested increases the future output required to validate the Supercycle. The gap between capital committed and productivity realized is now a central macro risk.

::pull NERC Hypothesis 28 — The Law of Complement Internalization: when an input becomes the binding constraint on AI growth, leading firms progressively replace spot-market dependence with long-duration control, financing or vertical integration of that input.

::section The Testable Sequence | Which input gets internalized next
A first formulation: AI strategic control = intelligence capability × secured complement share ÷ external bottleneck exposure. When chips become scarce, long-term supply agreements and custom silicon rise. When compute becomes scarce, owned data centres and financing vehicles rise. When power becomes scarce, PPAs, generation investment and nuclear uprates rise. When talent becomes scarce, deployment academies and internal engineering capacity rise.

The central question for investors therefore becomes: which input will the AI leaders internalize next — water, grid connections, memory, advanced packaging, industrial data or robotics? That is where the next scarcity premium may emerge.

::takeaways Five Key Takeaways | What to carry into the day
- Power is becoming part of Big Tech's capital structure — Google's 3.59-GW Constellation agreement is strategic infrastructure procurement stretching 15–20 years, not an electricity purchase at the margin.
- Compute is becoming a credit product — the reported $40 billion SpaceX financing shows AI scale increasingly depends on access to institutional debt, not technology alone.
- Europe has strengthened its model layer — Mistral Large 4 provides a credible European open-weight architecture, though independent validation and the full-weight release are still pending.
- Corporate restructuring is accelerating ahead of macro productivity evidence — FICO is cutting 15% of positions while aggregate utilization-adjusted TFP still shows no clear AI inflection.
- The AI moat is moving beyond the model — durable advantage increasingly lies in controlling the complements that determine whether intelligence can actually operate at scale.

::close The model wins the benchmark. The balance sheet wins the bottleneck. And ultimately, ==the winner will not just build intelligence — it will finance the world intelligence needs to run.==

The Next Economy is entering a decisive transition. The competitive advantage is shifting from controlling computing capacity to converting intelligence, energy and capital into sustained economic returns.

The fundamental question is changing: which corporations will convert the AI Supercycle into sustainable productivity and free cash flow—and which will finance its expansion without capturing adequate returns? The FDK GVI Corporate Benchmark identifies the emerging hierarchy.

::signal
- **The edge is shifting** from controlling computing capacity to converting intelligence, energy and capital into returns.
- **Capital is costlier:** the US 10-year neared 5% and Brent closed at $116.72 per barrel on October 23.
- **TSMC leads** the benchmark at 99.7, with Q3 revenue up 50.9% and net income up 68.7% year over year.
- **Broadcom proves conversion**, generating $13.7bn free cash flow, roughly 46% of $29.6bn quarterly revenue.
- **Volkswagen anchors the laggards** at 61.6 after cutting its 2026 return-on-sales outlook to 1% from 4.0–5.5%.
- **The test is incremental ROIC** above the risk-adjusted cost of capital; growth alone is insufficient.

::section Five Hidden Patterns | the Next Economy takes shape
The AI Supercycle is moving from computing scarcity toward **capital productivity**—utilization, electricity and returns on invested capital become decisive. Value is migrating from data centers to **industrial intelligence**, as in Schneider's Sines digital twin with Nscale and Start Campus. Bottlenecks are shifting to **advanced packaging**: BE Semiconductor's Q3 orders rose 96.1% YoY to €342.6m, with hybrid-bonding orders more than doubling versus all of 2025. Monetization moves to **enterprise software**: SAP's Q3 cloud revenue rose 26%, backlog 28%, operating profit 18%. Finally, **financial resilience** matters—Meta's revenue rose 26% but quarterly free cash flow was negative $2.12bn, with full-year capex guided at $135–145bn against $82.45bn in cash at September 30.

::section The Great Capital Rotation | the second-order effect
The next shift in market leadership may be a rotation from capital accumulation toward economic conversion. Higher energy and financing costs make infrastructure financing more selective, raising the value of productive capacity and cash flow. The test is straightforward: incremental ROIC must exceed the risk-adjusted cost of capital. The GVI measures strategic and operating velocity; it does not replace fundamental valuation analysis.

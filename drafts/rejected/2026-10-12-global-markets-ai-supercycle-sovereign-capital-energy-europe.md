Four developments this morning reveal a fundamental change in the global economy. The IMF has effectively acknowledged that AI investment is becoming a major support for growth; Tencent is returning to international debt markets; OPEC has sharply cut its demand outlook even as oil stays elevated; and European equities are recovering despite unresolved sovereign and energy risk.

This is not a conventional expansion, nor necessarily the start of a conventional recession. It is a **structural reallocation of investment, profits and economic power** — the next phase of The Great Split.

::pull THE DIVERGENCE
The AI supercycle is accelerating the economy. The financial system is becoming more selective about who can afford to participate.

::signals Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy | AI, credit, energy, markets, Europe
# AI — The First Supercycle Capable of Sustaining Growth
Speaking in Bangkok, Kristalina Georgieva identified AI infrastructure investment as sustaining global activity near 3%. The IMF estimates AI could add 0.5–1.0 points to advanced-Asian growth, but also affect ~40% of jobs globally and ~60% in advanced economies through augmentation, downgrading or displacement. An investment boom is not yet a productivity boom: suppliers capture revenue today, while adopters must transform institutions to capture productivity tomorrow.
signal: The first dividend of AI goes to those who build it; the largest long-term dividend may go to those who deploy it most productively.

# China — The AI Race Moves Into Global Credit Markets
Tencent is considering raising at least $4.5 billion in dollar and offshore-yuan bonds, with maturities from 10 to 30 years, following a $4.66 billion issue in June. First-half capex rose from 45.9 to 96.3 billion yuan. Long-duration debt against fast-obsolescing AI hardware raises a potential asset-liability duration mismatch — and advantages platforms with strong cash generation and offshore access.
signal: The AI race is becoming a competition between balance sheets.

# Energy — Weaker Demand, Higher Prices
OPEC cut 2026 demand growth from 1.4M to 400,000 bpd, yet Brent traded above $105. Houthi attacks cancelled 99.7% of Riyadh flights Sunday; tanker attacks were reported near the Strait of Hormuz; Washington's Russian-diesel and ceasefire signals brought uncertainty, not resolution. Prices are disconnecting from demand — and reliable, predictably-priced electricity is becoming the real competitive edge for accelerated computing.
signal: Energy resilience is becoming an investment necessity; financing that resilience is becoming more expensive.

# Global Markets — The S&P 500 Is Concealing the Split
The S&P 500 is up ~14% in 2026, yet more than 40% of constituents have declined and the median constituent has gained ~4%. Technology alone accounts for more than 10 points of the benchmark's gain; fewer than half of members trade above their 200-day averages. Market-cap indexes now concentrate exposure to a narrow set of correlated AI-cycle outcomes.
signal: The index is rising. The economy beneath it is splitting.

# Europe — A Recovery Is Not a Reset
The STOXX Europe 600 rose 0.56%, banks gained 1.5% and Spain's IBEX 1.1%. The German 10Y eased to ~3.462% and the French OAT to ~4.836%, but the France–Germany spread remains near 137 bps. Europe has world-class industrial and semiconductor-equipment firms, yet its financial fragmentation makes financing industrial AI, grids and energy resilience harder.
signal: Europe's problem is not industrial ambition — it is the financial architecture required to turn ambition into scale.

::patterns The Great Split Moves Into the Structure of Growth | three interdependent shifts
# [I] Growth Is More Capital-Intensive
AI investment supports activity through enormous infrastructure spending — but the expansion is sustainable only if deployment converts into utilization, cash flow and productivity. Weak conversion leaves the cycle dependent on external financing.

# [II] The Dividend Is More Uneven
Capital-intensive growth does not generate the same labor-tax revenues as labor-intensive growth. If returns flow toward capital owners, debt-constrained governments may face rising pressure to finance workforce transitions.

# [III] Capital Formation Is a Geopolitical Advantage
Those with long-duration financing invest through monetary restriction; those without postpone. Earlier deployment compounds into productivity, cash flow and future financing capacity — a self-reinforcing loop: capital access → compute and energy → productivity → cash flow → reinvestment → advantage.

::takeaways Five Key Takeaways | for investors, boards and policymakers
- Reassess the composition of global growth: investment-led activity must translate into sustainable productivity and cash flow.
- Follow the financing architecture of AI: terms, maturities and balance-sheet resilience will differentiate competitors.
- Separate energy demand from energy risk: weaker demand growth and geopolitical disruption must both enter models.
- Look beneath the benchmarks: the S&P 500's 14% advance conceals declines across more than 40% of constituents.
- Recognize the sovereign consequences: capital formation is becoming a central determinant of national competitiveness.

::close

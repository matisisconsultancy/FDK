U.S. five-year Treasury yields are above 5%. That is the key benchmark: roughly 5% is what investors can earn on medium-term U.S. government debt before taking corporate, execution or equity risk. SoftBank is paying up to 9.75% to finance OpenAI. Microsoft is committing more than $10 billion to Gulf AI infrastructure. Meta is building proprietary hardware to reduce platform dependence. Washington and Beijing are negotiating over rare earths, technology access and AI governance.

The central thesis is simple: as AI becomes more capital-intensive, value shifts from those that merely use intelligence to those that can own its critical infrastructure and still generate returns above the cost of capital.

AI is becoming easier to access.

The infrastructure required to monetize it is becoming harder—and more expensive—to own.

That moves the competitive test from AI capability to economic capture:

WHO OWNS THE CRITICAL LAYERS, WHO KEEPS THE MARGIN, AND WHO EARNS ROIC ABOVE WACC?

⸻

Five Signals

1. AI Finance — From growth to return discipline

Fact: SoftBank is issuing $11.1 billion of bonds to finance OpenAI, with dollar coupons reaching 9.75%.

Shift: AI capital is no longer cheap.

Economic mechanism: The U.S. five-year Treasury yield above 5% is the risk-free reference rate for medium-term dollar capital. A corporate borrower or AI infrastructure project must then pay an additional premium for credit risk, execution risk, illiquidity and equity risk.

5% TREASURY ≠ 5% WACC

Investment implication: ROIC — Return on Invested Capital — must exceed WACC — Weighted Average Cost of Capital.

ROIC > WACC

⸻

2. Agentic AI — From models to distribution

Fact: Meta is moving into proprietary AI hardware to reduce dependence on third-party platforms.

Shift: Value is migrating from model capability toward the customer interface.

Economic mechanism: Agents that execute transactions shift economics toward whoever owns identity, distribution and checkout.

Investment implication: Favor platforms positioned closest to monetization.

AGENT → TRANSACTION → DISTRIBUTION → MARGIN

⸻

3. Infrastructure — From capacity to resilience

Fact: Microsoft is investing more than $10 billion across Gulf AI and cloud infrastructure.

Shift: AI infrastructure is becoming critical infrastructure.

Economic mechanism: Secure power, redundancy and connectivity determine uptime; uptime determines utilisation and revenue.

Investment implication: Resilient infrastructure should command a premium over nominal capacity alone.

CAPACITY → RESILIENCE → UPTIME → CASH FLOW

⸻

4. U.S.–China — From decoupling to leverage

Fact: Rare earths, AI governance, tariffs and market access are converging into one negotiation.

Shift: Strategic dependencies are becoming bargaining instruments.

Economic mechanism: Control of constrained inputs and market access increases negotiating power without requiring full separation.

Investment implication: Assets located at geopolitical bottlenecks acquire strategic as well as financial value.

DEPENDENCY → LEVERAGE → PRICING POWER

⸻

5. Europe — From ambition to investable economics

Fact: Europe’s proposed AI gigafactories require materially more recurring demand than current public procurement can support.

Shift: Europe’s constraint is not simply access to compute. It is the ability to aggregate demand, power and capital at continental scale.

Economic mechanism: Subsidized capacity without sufficient utilisation creates weak economics; infrastructure serving existing industrial demand can monetize earlier and with greater visibility.

Investment implication: Do not buy “European AI” indiscriminately. Favor the layers where Europe already possesses industrial depth and monetizable demand.

EUROPE: INFRASTRUCTURE BEFORE FRONTIER

Prefer

Grid equipment and power systems — AI increases electricity scarcity and network investment.Industrial automation and Physical AI — Europe already has manufacturing customers, engineering capability and installed industrial assets.

Semiconductor equipment and selected chip infrastructure — exposure to global AI capex without requiring Europe to win the frontier-model race.

Data centres with secured power and anchor customers — utilization matters more than announced gigawatts.

Banks and infrastructure-finance platforms — Europe’s investment cycle requires larger balance sheets and deeper capital intermediation.

Be selective

European frontier models and gigafactories without continental demand, power certainty or clear commercial utilization.

EUROPE’S INVESTMENT EDGE IS INDUSTRIAL CONVERSION, NOT MODEL IMITATION.

⸻

The Great Split

CONTROL OWNERS vs. CONTROL RENTERS

Control Owners

Own critical inputs.
Secure energy.
Own distribution.
Finance at scale.
Retain pricing power.

Control Renters

Buy critical inputs.
Depend on external infrastructure.
Rent distribution.
Borrow at higher cost.
Absorb higher input costs.

The significance of a 5% five-year Treasury yield is not that every project must earn 5%.

It means investors already have a roughly 5% sovereign alternative before taking additional risk.

⸻

Investor Action Box

ONE-LINE VERDICT

PRIORITIZE ECONOMIC QUALITY: PRICING POWER → CASH FLOW → FUNDED OPTIONALITY → DEPENDENCY → STRANDED SCALE.

1 — CORE OVERWEIGHT: PRICING-POWER ASSETS

Semiconductors, HBM, memory, advanced networking, grids, firm power and resilient data centres.

Why: they sit at unavoidable parts of the AI production chain where demand is strongest and substitution is hardest.

Portfolio role: core exposure to the AI Supercycle.

⸻

2 — SELECTIVE OVERWEIGHT: CASH-FLOW CONVERTERS

Agentic distribution, AI security, enterprise deployment and infrastructure-finance platforms.

Why: they turn AI adoption directly into transactions, recurring revenues or financing income.

Portfolio role: monetize the build-out.

⸻

3 — SELECTIVE ONLY: FUNDED OPTIONALITY

Frontier models, new compute architectures and emerging AI infrastructure.

Own only where there are:

strong balance sheets
secured customers
visible utilisation
credible unit economics
clear path to ROIC > WACC.

Portfolio role: capture upside without underwriting unlimited duration.

⸻

4 — UNDERWEIGHT: DEPENDENCY

Uncontracted compute, leveraged infrastructure, weak intermediaries and businesses dependent on third-party power, models, distribution or financing.

Why: they pay the economics captured elsewhere in the stack.

⸻

5 — AVOID: STRANDED SCALE

No secured power.
No committed demand.
No financing edge.
No pricing power.

Large announced capacity does not equal economic value.

SCALE WITHOUT RETURNS = CAPITAL DESTRUCTION

⸻

EUROPE — PORTFOLIO BIAS

OVERWEIGHT THE ENABLERS. BE SELECTIVE ON THE BUILDERS.

Favor electrification, grids, industrial automation, semiconductor equipment, secure data-centre infrastructure and financial intermediation.

Demand a much higher proof threshold from stand-alone AI factories, national champions and capacity built primarily around subsidies rather than customers.

Europe does not need to win every layer of AI to create shareholder value.

It needs to monetize the layers where its existing industrial position already gives it an advantage.

⸻

The Velocity Insight

The first AI race was about models.

The second was about capacity.

The third is about returns.

For Europe, that distinction is especially important.

The continent’s strongest opportunity may not be to replicate the U.S. frontier stack.

It is to connect power, industrial engineering, automation, semiconductor equipment, mobility and finance to the AI investment cycle already underway.

Europe becomes investable when AI stops being a sovereignty slogan and becomes an industrial earnings story.

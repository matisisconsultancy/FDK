Munich-based Agile Robots Raised $500 Million in a Funding Round Led by SoftBank, Valuing the Company at $8.5 Billion. Nvidia and Hitachi Also Participated.

Nvidia's leadership shock, Siemens' €7 billion data-center backlog and Nebius' $45 billion investment ambition redefine the next phase of AI infrastructure finance.

Five consequential developments since the November 11 assessment reveal a widening financial divergence: Europe's AI industrial suppliers are generating record orders and earnings, while computing-infrastructure operators face intensifying capital requirements, execution delays and greater technology-supplier uncertainty.

The most immediate systemic development is Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO today, with Colette Kress appointed interim chief executive. The volatility raises new questions about supplier concentration, financing structures and technology-roadmap certainty for Europe's planned Gigafactories. Nvidia has not announced a withdrawal from European projects.

01

Germany — Siemens Confirms The Industrial AI Earnings Super-Cycle

Confirmed results, upgraded guidance

Siemens AG reported record fiscal 2026 orders of €108.2 billion, including a €143 billion overall backlog. Approximately €7 billion relates to data-center equipment, and data-center orders doubled in the fourth quarter. Digital-business revenue rose 16% to €10.9 billion, and Siemens raised its medium-term comparable revenue growth target from 6–9% to 7–11%.

This is distinct from Siemens Energy's €15 billion data-center-related order covered on November 11 — different companies, not separate Gigafactory commitments. Europe is accumulating measurable AI-related revenues in power distribution, automation, digital twins and electrical engineering.

02

Nebius — The $45 Billion Capital Expenditure Test

New guidance, disclosed delays

Amsterdam-headquartered Nebius reported third-quarter revenue of approximately $981 million but said delays in third-party computing capacity would defer some revenue into 2027. It now expects 2026 revenue toward the low end of its $3.0–3.4 billion range.

Its ambitions remain large: ~$27bn 2026 capex, $40–45bn planned 2027 capex, $12–13bn expected 2027 revenue, with 75–85% of 2027 capex covered by customer prepayments and contract-backed financing. These are company forecasts, not confirmed proceeds. The pattern: CAPACITY DELAYS → REVENUE DEFERRAL → HIGHER CAPITAL REQUIREMENTS → FINANCING DEPENDENCE → VALUATION PRESSURE. For investors and banks, prepayments, committed financing and actual commissioning dates are becoming central valuation variables.

03

Germany — Agile Robots Attracts $500 Million In Strategic Capital

Confirmed equity financing

The company intends to expand its US presence and increase manufacturing capacity, including in Germany; its Fürstenfeldbruck factory, opened earlier in 2026, has stated capacity for up to 10,000 humanoid robots annually. This is not Gigafactory financing — its significance lies downstream: AI COMPUTE → PHYSICAL AI → INDUSTRIAL AUTOMATION → PRODUCTIVITY → INVESTABLE EARNINGS. It strengthens the argument for connecting European compute infrastructure with industrial demand rather than treating sovereign computing capacity as an end in itself.

04

Italy — Technoprobe Demonstrates The Semiconductor Value-Capture Opportunity

After the preceding assessment

Italian semiconductor-testing specialist Technoprobe reported nine-month revenue of €787.4 million, up 68.8%, with EBITDA of €376.3 million — a 47.8% margin — attributed largely to AI and data-center demand. It cautioned that an internal systems transition would affect fourth-quarter performance. Alongside Infineon's November 10 outlook and Siemens' results, it shows European semiconductor supply-chain companies can capture substantial value without owning AI computing facilities.

Separately, a November 12 update reports Puglia's STEP incentive programme excluded data-center investments effective November 4, freeing €100 million across the broader programme — a regional change, not a national ban, warranting confirmation before investment decisions.

05

Nvidia — A New Strategic Dependency Risk For European Gigafactories

Confirmed transition, market shock

The leadership change introduces uncertainty into the dominant advanced-computing ecosystem. The immediate concern is not interrupted deliveries but potential repricing of project risk where hardware supply, roadmaps and financing depend heavily on a single vendor. Reuters reported sharp declines in Nvidia and other AI infrastructure stocks, with relative gains for some competing semiconductor companies.

Siemens CEO Roland Busch stated today that his company's collaboration with Nvidia would continue. For European consortia, three issues now deserve scrutiny: supplier diversification, contractual delivery guarantees and hardware-financing resilience. The watchpoint is whether the shock changes actual orders, customer financing or hardware availability — not simply equity valuations.

07

EuroHPC — Official Procurement Status

Updated deadlines

The official portal still shows the tender open, with revised deadlines: December 3, 2026, 17:00 CET for Large-scale Gigafactories (Lot 2); February 4, 2027, 17:00 CET for Medium-scale Gigafactories (Lot 1). No new final award was confirmed; the official EuroHPC notice dated November 5 supersedes older November 12 deadlines.

08

The Investment Verdict

A fundamental financial divide

European industrial suppliers are reporting stronger orders, revenue growth and cash generation, while European-linked AI cloud developers commit to increasingly demanding capex programmes whose economics depend on timely deployment and continued funding. Strategic investors are backing robotics and physical AI — but the disruption at Nvidia introduces a new concentration-risk consideration. For banks, the priority is financial discipline; for boards, supplier resilience; for policymakers, ensuring public support mobilizes durable European industrial value.

The next phase of Europe's AI race will be won not by the largest capital expenditure announcements, but by the strongest combination of technology control, infrastructure execution, productive industrial demand and sustainable financial returns.

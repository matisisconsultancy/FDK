Global Velocity Index: Country Rankings

The decisive variable is no longer size but velocity — the capacity to convert AI, capital, energy and industry into economic acceleration faster than competitors.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann September 20, 2026 5 min read Macro

“We are not measuring economic size. We are measuring the capacity to accelerate.”

The global competition is no longer adequately captured by GDP, productivity or technological leadership considered separately. The decisive variable is becoming velocity: the ability to combine strategic assets and convert them rapidly into investment, infrastructure, industrial capacity and ultimately economic power.

The Global Velocity Index measures an acceleration system — Accelerated Computing × AI × Capital Formation × Energy × Infrastructure × Industrial Capacity × Innovation × Institutional Execution. The distinction is fundamental: we are not measuring economic size, but the capacity to accelerate.

The Five GVI Signals

The velocity engines shifting beneath the ranking

  1. America's advantage extends beyond frontier AI. Its structural strength is its ability to connect Capital Markets → Hyperscalers → Accelerated Computing → Energy → Infrastructure → AI, creating capital formation at technological scale that competitors find difficult to replicate.

    The emerging vulnerability is the other side of the same equation. The more physical and capital-intensive AI becomes, the more America's AI ecosystem becomes exposed to financing costs, power availability and infrastructure execution. signal: Technological leadership reinforced by unmatched capital formation.

  2. China follows a different velocity model: Manufacturing → Infrastructure → Localization → Scale → Cost Reduction → Diffusion. The strategic objective is increasingly clear — reduce dependence across critical layers of the technology stack, with progress in domestic memory technology reinforcing that direction.

    China's principal advantage is therefore not simply AI. It is the capacity to industrialize technology at enormous scale. signal: The principal system-scale alternative to the American model.

  3. The AI Supercycle is becoming increasingly memory-intensive, giving South Korea an unusually strategic position through its semiconductor ecosystem.

    Its next challenge is converting leadership in critical components into broader leadership across AI Infrastructure → Industrial AI → Physical AI. signal: Component leadership must become system leadership.

  4. Japan possesses one of the world's strongest combinations of robotics, automation, precision manufacturing, engineering and industrial know-how. As AI moves from digital environments into machines, factories and physical infrastructure, those capabilities become increasingly valuable.

    Japan's potential advantage is not reproducing Silicon Valley. It is industrializing intelligence. signal: The strongest option on the physical frontier of AI.

  5. Europe possesses extraordinary individual components: semiconductors, energy infrastructure, advanced manufacturing, engineering, research institutions, capital, transport networks and industrial champions. But they remain fragmented across different national markets and institutional architectures.

    Europe's challenge is increasingly one of connection and speed: Energy + Capital + Compute + Industry + Infrastructure must become one European acceleration system. signal: Not scarcity of assets — fragmentation and execution speed.

Key takeaways

The velocity divide

  • The United States remains the velocity leader because technological leadership is reinforced by unmatched capital formation.
  • China remains the principal system-scale alternative, combining industrial capacity, infrastructure and technological localization.
  • Korea and Japan become increasingly important as AI moves toward memory, robotics and the physical economy.
  • Europe's problem is not scarcity of strategic assets — it is fragmentation and execution speed.
  • Spain and Italy offer two different European upside propositions: Spain through energy and infrastructure; Italy through industrial and Physical AI deployment.

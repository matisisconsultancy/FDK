GVI · 08:00 · FDK GVI Corporate Benchmark

Global Corporate Intelligence

The next corporate rotation will separate the companies that monetize technological acceleration from those merely forced to finance it.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 10, 2026 9 min read Capital

“The race is not who spends most on the future, but who converts that spend into cash flow fastest.”

The companies shaping the Next Economy are no longer distinguished by how much they invest in artificial intelligence, but by how rapidly they transform capital, technology and talent into productive capacity, earnings and strategic advantage.

The fundamental question: which corporations are converting technological acceleration into durable economic power—and which are being overtaken by a changing competitive environment? This edition establishes the reference FDK Global Velocity Index scores for subsequent comparisons. Reporting date Saturday, October 10, 2026; market observations reflect the completed session of Friday, October 9.

The central thesis is that corporate velocity is becoming a financial variable. The next market rotation will increasingly distinguish companies that monetize technological acceleration from those that must finance it without comparable improvements in productivity or cash generation.

The Big Picture

Velocity meets the cost of capital

The cost of capital is becoming a competitive selection mechanism.

02

Top 10 Global Velocity Leaders

The corporations controlling the transition to the Next Economy

  1. Fiscal Q2 2027 revenue reached $96.2 billion, including $89 billion from Data Center. Its position extends beyond GPUs into software, networking and integrated computing infrastructure. Principal risks are customer concentration, supply dependence, export restrictions and the sustainability of customers' AI expenditure. signal: Ecosystem breadth plus extraordinary commercial momentum.

  2. Reported approximately NT$1.49 trillion in Q3 revenue, about 50% above the corresponding quarter of 2025. Its advantage lies in advanced process technology and deeply embedded customer relationships; geographic concentration and geopolitical exposure remain major structural risks. signal: The physical bottleneck on which the entire cycle depends.

  3. Azure revenue grew 43% in fiscal Q4, while Microsoft 365 Copilot exceeded 30 million paid seats. The decisive question is whether AI revenue and productivity gains continue to outpace infrastructure depreciation and operating costs. signal: AI integrated into existing enterprise workflows.

  4. AI semiconductor revenue reached $16.7 billion in fiscal Q3 2026, up 221% year over year. Custom accelerators and networking offer hyperscalers an alternative to general-purpose GPUs; vulnerability is concentration among major customers. signal: The counterweight to GPU-only architectures.

  5. Google Cloud revenue grew 82% in Q2 2026 alongside substantial infrastructure investment. The financial test is whether cloud profit and AI revenue can justify rising capital intensity without weakening consolidated free cash flow. signal: Multiple channels to commercialize AI.

  6. Q2 AWS revenue grew 37%, with consolidated operating income up 43%. Cloud, custom processors, logistics automation and enterprise distribution create several independent productivity channels. The main risk is the timing of returns on very large investments. signal: Breadth of independent productivity channels.

  7. Generated €9.3 billion in Q2 revenue and lifted its 2026 outlook to €43–45 billion. Advanced lithography remains essential to leading-edge capacity; customer cycles and export controls set the pace of conversion. signal: Essential to every expansion of the frontier.

  8. First-half revenue rose approximately 55%, with energy-storage revenue up 87.5%. It connects electric mobility, grid storage and resilient energy infrastructure; pricing, expansion and geopolitical restrictions remain risks. signal: Where AI demand meets the grid.

  9. Preliminary Q3 operating profit reached KRW 107.4 trillion on extraordinary semiconductor demand. Strength lies in scale and vertical integration, but memory-margin sustainability, continued foundry losses and cyclicality justify a lower ranking than headline profit suggests. signal: Exceptional profit, but earnings quality still unproven.

  10. First-half revenue of approximately €21.2 billion, 14% organic growth, adjusted EBITA margin of 19.3%. Exposure to power distribution, cooling, automation and electrification; risks are execution capacity, working capital and project delays. signal: Industrial electrification as a technology-like growth asset.

04

Five Hidden Patterns

The second-order effects

1

Supply chains reorganize

The first stage of the supercycle rewarded computing capacity; the next rewards the complementary assets that make it productive—advanced manufacturing, memory, electrical equipment, cooling, storage and distribution. This explains the significance of TSMC, ASML, CATL and Schneider. Revenue migrates from computing equipment into the broader industrial systems supporting deployment.

2

Rates reprice speed

A 10-year yield above 5% changes the economics of long-duration projects. The cycle separates corporations into those financing expansion internally, those relying on external funding, and those unable to invest at scale. The cost of capital becomes a competitive selection mechanism.

3

Infrastructure as an asset class

Industrial AI requires computing, electricity, grid connections, cooling, telecoms and sophisticated storage. Advantage shifts toward companies integrating digital capability with physical infrastructure—letting industrial firms acquire technology-like growth without technology-company models.

4

Boundaries redrawn

The October 9 selloff illustrates a wider phenomenon. SpaceX's spectrum strategy challenges assumptions about the stability of industry boundaries. Valuations may incorporate competitive threats long before those threats affect reported earnings.

5

A new earnings-quality test

Samsung's results show the profitability possible when demand outruns supply. But growth may reflect leadership, scarcity, pricing power or inventory effects—each deserving different multiples. A high GVI score requires resilience across the cycle, not merely a strong quarter.

05

The Capital-Efficiency Test

Investment velocity versus economic capture

Investor takeaways

Five decisions

  • Separate growth from value creation: favor accelerating cash returns over rising capex—watch incremental ROIC and free-cash-flow conversion.
  • Identify enabling infrastructure around semiconductor manufacturing, memory, power and cooling—watch utilization, order conversion and margins.
  • Reprice financing risk for businesses dependent on external funding—watch interest coverage, refinancing needs and credit spreads.
  • Monitor competitive boundary shifts as emerging platforms alter incumbent economics—watch market share, customer migration and pricing power.
  • Preserve valuation discipline: distinguish strategically important corporations from attractively priced securities.

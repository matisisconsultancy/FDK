Semiconductors lead the Global Velocity Index, but the fastest marginal acceleration is moving one layer deeper — into the physical economy that gives intelligence power, permission and a return.

“The Next Economy is built where intelligence earns a return.”

Which industries are building the Next Economy fastest today? The Global Velocity Index (GVI) offers a ranked answer, and the leaders sit at the intersection of computing demand and industrial reality.

Semiconductors lead because computing demand is broadening across the entire silicon architecture — not only accelerators. Aerospace and defense move into second because orbital connectivity has become commercial infrastructure while sovereign procurement remains structurally funded.

LEAD SIGNAL

The Next Economy is no longer being built fastest where intelligence is merely announced. It is being built where intelligence receives power, receives permission, becomes an industrial order — and earns a return.

01

The Leading Layers

why the top of the table looks the way it does

  1. Computing demand is broadening across the entire silicon architecture, not only accelerators. The base layer of the Next Economy remains the fastest-building. signal: Silicon breadth, not just accelerators, sets the pace.

  2. Orbital connectivity has become commercial infrastructure, while sovereign procurement remains structurally funded — a rare pairing of commercial demand and durable state capital. signal: Space is now infrastructure; procurement stays funded.

  3. Cloud remains indispensable, but grid access has become a strategic permission rather than an administrative assumption. signal: Power access, not compute, is now the constraint.

  4. Energy accelerates because electricity — not oil — is now the decisive input into industrial intelligence. signal: Electricity is the decisive industrial input.

  5. Artificial Intelligence remains the ultimate source of productivity, but capital must now prove that intelligence can become cash flow. signal: Intelligence must convert into cash flow.

Key takeaways

reading the index

  • Semiconductors (97.4), Aerospace & Defense (97.2) and Cloud (97.1) top the GVI.
  • The binding constraint has shifted from compute to grid access and electricity.
  • AI leads on productivity potential but must now prove cash flow.
  • The next wave of velocity runs through telecom, machinery, construction, electrical infrastructure and healthcare tools.
  • Verified evidence draws on AMD and Caterpillar disclosures, the Office of the Texas Governor, Reuters and corporate announcements; all GVI scores and classifications are proprietary FDK judgments, distinct from reported financial data.

Provisional Nowcast

The FDK Global Velocity Index holds at 108.0 as AI accelerates the real economy fast enough to withstand the energy shock — but that very resilience keeps inflation and the cost of capital higher.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 24, 2026 7 min read Macro
108.0Weighted Global Velocity Index (2025=100), unchanged
5.11%US 10-year Treasury yield — highest since 2007
3.06%Japan 10-year sovereign yield — 30-year high
+78.3%Korean exports, first 20 days of September, YoY

“The next winners will make intelligence compound faster than its financing cost.”

The evidence today is unusually revealing. The OECD now identifies AI infrastructure investment as one of the principal reasons the world economy has absorbed the Middle East energy shock better than expected, raising its 2026 global-growth forecast to 2.9%. Yet simultaneously the US 10-year Treasury yield has reached 5.11%, Japan's 10-year sovereign yield 3.06% — its highest since 1996 — and Brent remains around $102.

The central equation is therefore tightening: AI is accelerating the real economy fast enough to withstand the shock — but that very resilience is keeping inflation and the cost of capital higher. That is today's dominant Velocity signal.

Structural acceleration remains intact over the 30-day regime, but the cost of financing that acceleration has moved materially higher. Model confidence sits at 96%, with net model impulse approximately flat. Today produces one country-level revision: South Korea rises +0.1 to 88.5.

THE PARADOX

Because the real economy is stronger than expected, inflation remains harder to suppress — which keeps bond yields elevated, which raises the hurdle rate on the very AI infrastructure driving the growth.

02

Country signals

measured evidence, not forecasts

  1. Official Korean customs data show exports in the first 20 days of September up 78.3% year on year to $71.4 billion, the highest 20-day performance on record, with semiconductor shipments up 259.4%. The OECD raised South Korea's 2026 growth forecast from 2.6% to 3.7%, and the ADB from 2.6% to 3.2%, both citing strong exports, production and AI-semiconductor demand. The forecasts earn no GVI points; the measured export and production impulse does. The causality chain is now visible: global AI capex → HBM/memory demand → Korean exports → industrial production → earnings → investment → GDP acceleration. The constraint remains inflation, with Korea's 2026 projection raised to 3.0%. signal: South Korea rises from 88.4 to 88.5 — the transition from an AI trade to an AI economy.

  2. The Composite PMI surged from 56.0 to 58.4 in September, its highest since July 2021, with new orders at their strongest since March 2022 and backlogs the highest since May 2022. But the input-price index jumped to 66.4, its highest since October 2022. The 10-year yield jumped nearly 14bp to 5.106%, the two-year reached 4.947% intraday, and markets moved the implied probability of another October Fed increase toward 70%. The positive productive evidence and negative Smart Capital effect offset. signal: United States remains 94.3 — economic Velocity up, cost of capital up.

  3. The Composite PMI rose from 51.8 to 53.8, the highest since October 2025; services returned to expansion at 52.9, manufacturing held at 53.8 and employment rose for a second month. But input costs rose at the fastest rate in four months, driven by fuel and energy, and the GVI requires persistence before reversing accumulated structural constraints. signal: Germany stays 83.2 but moves closer to an upgrade threshold.

  4. SoftBank completed an $11.1 billion dollar/euro bond issuance to finance its OpenAI investment — the largest high-yield bond sale by an Asia-Pacific issuer, with dollar coupons of 8.625%, 9.25% and 9.75%, and a five-year CDS spread above 400bp versus around 280 in June. The 10-year JGB rose to 3.06% and the 30-year to 4.12%. Global markets can still mobilise enormous capital for frontier AI — but the required return is becoming extremely high. signal: Japan remains 84.9; Smart Capital momentum deteriorates.

  5. Lloyds, NatWest and Barclays completed the world's first interbank transactions using tokenised commercial-bank deposits, including mortgages; a further HSBC test demonstrated programmable payments, with plans to issue digital bonds settled through tokenised deposits in early 2027. Meaningful Innovation Velocity evidence — but it earns no +0.1 while transactions remain pilots. The Composite PMI slipped from 52.5 to 51.7. signal: UK remains 82.3 — the two signals offset.

  6. The Composite PMI rose sharply from 48.5 to 51.2, the strongest pace in 25 months and back into expansion. But employment declined for a fifth month, new orders barely increased, business expectations weakened and prices accelerated — preventing any reversal of Saturday's Smart Capital downgrade. signal: France remains 81.4 — activity recovery confirmed, self-sustaining acceleration not yet.

  7. Chinese authorities are reportedly surveying use of Broadcom networking switches across state data centres, with the FT reporting they may account for as much as 90% of deployed switches; Reuters could not verify. The direction fits China's full-stack architecture, but this is precisely the evidence the GVI should not yet score. signal: China remains 91.1 — zero numerical weight until procurement changes become measurable.

Key takeaways

where the paradox leads

  • The GVI holds at 108.0 on 24 September 2026; South Korea rises from 88.4 to 88.5; rankings unchanged.
  • For the first time the evidence shows AI simultaneously supporting growth and contributing to the conditions keeping capital expensive.
  • The 5.11% US 10-year yield may now matter more to the next phase of the AI cycle than any incremental model benchmark.
  • At that hurdle rate, projects need secured power, contracted demand, high utilisation, superior productivity and credible refinancing.
  • The next framework refinement: VELOCITY = PRODUCTIVE INTELLIGENCE × ABSORPTION CAPACITY ÷ COST OF CAPITAL.

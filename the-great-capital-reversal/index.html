The Great Capital Reversal

What began as a struggle for control of Italy's banking system is becoming a potential reordering of European financial power — where initiating a transaction does not mean controlling the consequences it unleashes.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 9, 2026 6 min read Capital

“Never confuse the power to start a financial contest with the power to control how it ends.”

What began as a struggle for control of Italy's banking system is becoming a potential reordering of European financial power. The original ambition was national: reshape Italy's banking hierarchy, create a stronger domestic competitor and consolidate influence over strategic financial institutions.

The government led by Giorgia Meloni, MPS, major shareholders and competing banking groups pursued overlapping but distinct objectives. The outcome is moving beyond any one participant's original design.

MPS, initially positioned as a consolidator, is now itself a takeover target. Delfin has committed to Intesa. Caltagirone has rejected Lovaglio's defence. Crédit Agricole has refused to tender its 29.3% Banco BPM stake. The resulting pressure on MPS could accelerate a broader restructuring involving Mediobanca, Generali, Banco BPM, BPER, Crédit Agricole and UniCredit–Commerzbank.

THE REVERSAL IS FUNDAMENTAL

A consolidation process associated with reinforcing Italian financial control may instead accelerate the redistribution of financial influence across Europe. In finance, initiating a transaction does not mean controlling the consequences it unleashes.

01

The Three Reversals

how the contest inverted

  1. Italy's consolidation cycle emerged from the intersection of political objectives, corporate strategies and shareholder interests. The Meloni government supported a stronger domestic competitor around MPS; private shareholders pursued returns, governance and positioning; management teams pursued scale and profitability. The premise was that these interests could produce a coherent new architecture — but the process changed every participant's incentives. MPS acquired Mediobanca, Lovaglio proposed a broader combination with Banco BPM and Banca Generali, and Intesa launched a competing offer for MPS. signal: The institution intended to lead consolidation became the institution being consolidated.

  2. Caltagirone has formally opposed Lovaglio's extraordinary resolutions. Delfin has committed its MPS stake to Intesa. Praude has declared opposition, while Edizione intends to tender into Intesa's offer. MPS's disclosures distinguish Caltagirone's notified 10.26% from a separately reported 13.49% share-capital holding — a distinction that matters because the extraordinary resolutions require two-thirds of votes cast. Lovaglio faces an increasingly difficult shareholder threshold precisely as his alternative industrial strategy becomes less certain. signal: The defence is under pressure from both ownership arithmetic and transaction feasibility.

  3. If Intesa completes its acquisition of MPS, it would bring Mediobanca and its strategic Generali exposure into a larger banking perimeter, subject to regulatory remedies. BPER could expand through branch disposals. Banco BPM could return to the centre of consolidation. UniCredit would continue pursuing Continental Scale through Commerzbank. These are conditional outcomes — but together they illustrate the wider transformation. signal: The strategic centre of gravity moves from national banking concentration toward European capital allocation.

THE LIMITS OF NATIONAL STRATEGY

The state can establish conditions within its legal authority. It cannot guarantee that private capital will ultimately follow its preferred ownership architecture. A board can initiate a transaction. It cannot compel shareholders to support it, counterparties to accept it or regulators to approve it.

Five Key Takeaways

what boards should carry forward

  • The original Italian consolidation strategy may produce an unintended ownership structure — MPS, positioned as a consolidator, is now itself the target of a larger institution.
  • Shareholder coalitions are more fragile than political or corporate strategies assume — Delfin and Caltagirone show how changing incentives alter the feasible outcome.
  • The next strategic assets are Banco BPM and Generali — their future ownership and governance will shape the next phase of consolidation.
  • Europe's financial integration is being tested through transactions before its institutional architecture is complete — UniCredit–Commerzbank remains the clearest cross-border example.
  • The governing principle is strategic discipline — a transaction should be initiated only with a credible understanding of its execution dependencies, counteractions and consequences for capital allocation.

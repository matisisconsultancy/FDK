The Price of the Next Expansion

Global industrial activity is proving more resilient than markets will reward, and the next edge lies in reading that divergence.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann November 3, 2026 5 min read Macro

“Economic acceleration and financial value creation are related, but they are not interchangeable.”

Global industrial activity is showing greater resilience than financial markets are currently prepared to reward. The next investment opportunity lies in understanding this divergence.

American manufacturing accelerated to an ISM reading of 55.5, with factory production at its strongest since November 2021. Europe's recovery broadened, though France and Italy returned to contraction while Germany, Spain and Sweden strengthened. Meanwhile Marvell announced a $115 billion acquisition of Lumentum, Palantir reported 96% revenue growth, and Apple reaffirmed its cash strength — yet the Nasdaq still fell 1.07% on Monday.

Investors are no longer rewarding technological expansion indiscriminately. They are differentiating between companies that finance infrastructure, those that supply it, and those converting capabilities into earnings and cash flow.

01

The United States — Production Accelerates, Valuations Compress

Industrial demand beyond AI

US manufacturing expanded for a tenth consecutive month, with the PMI rising to 55.5 from 54.5 and the Production Index to 59.0 from 56.7 — its highest since November 2021. New orders held at 55.5; the Prices Index eased to 75.2 from 77.9 but still signals substantial input-price inflation. Strength spanned computers, electronics, machinery and transportation.

Markets diverged. On November 2 the S&P 500 fell 0.51% and the Nasdaq 1.07%, while the Dow gained 0.41%. Marvell dropped about 13% on its Lumentum deal and Oracle nearly 9% on financing concerns. Stronger industrial demand does not justify higher valuations when the cost of future growth is rising.

02

Europe — A More Differentiated Recovery

Country selection matters

The eurozone manufacturing PMI edged down to 52.7 from 52.9, remaining expansionary, but national divergence is widening. Sweden reached 60.5, its strongest since January 2022, though its input-price index rose to 82.6. Germany strengthened to 54.2 and Spain to 52.2 — its best since August 2025 — while France slipped to 49.5 and Italy to 49.4 on weakening demand. The central challenge is now the sustainability of margins: output is returning, but energy costs, supply disruptions and financing conditions threaten profitability.

03

AI Infrastructure and Enterprise Evidence

Connectivity, monetisation and cash

Marvell's definitive all-stock agreement values Lumentum at roughly $115 billion, exchanging 4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share, with more than $1 billion in anticipated run-rate synergies and closing expected in the first half of 2028. The thesis is moving from computing capacity toward the economics of integrated infrastructure — but the market reaction questions whether the resulting cash flows justify the capital committed.

Palantir posted third-quarter revenue up 96% to $2.31 billion, with US commercial revenue up 148% to $984 million — exceeding government revenue for the first time — roughly $1.20 billion in operating cash flow, and raised guidance to ~$8.53 billion. Yet US commercial contract value grew only 11%. Apple reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $114.5 billion (+12%), net income near $30.7 billion, operating cash flow of ~$28 billion and a 47.9% gross margin — the enduring power of an established ecosystem.

04

China — Smart Capital Tightening

A two-channel policy move

The PBOC will raise the foreign-currency deposit reserve requirement from 4% to 6%, effective November 15, to absorb FX liquidity and moderate yuan appreciation — a step Reuters estimates could withdraw about $24 billion. Separately, overnight reverse-repo operations injected roughly 541 billion yuan at 1.25%. The two operate through different channels and should not be combined into a single measure of tightening.

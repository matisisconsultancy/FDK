The Price of Saying No

Intesa has turned October 29 into a decision over what MPS becomes next — and who controls the next phase of Italian banking consolidation.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 4, 2026 7 min read Capital
€1.25Revised cash per share, up from €1.00
€800MAdditional consideration offered

4.9%Treasury stake that will not participate

“The price of saying no is known. October 29 will reveal which structure can survive.”

The Italian banking Risiko has crossed another threshold.

On October 3, Intesa Sanpaolo increased the cash component of its MPS offer from €1.00 to €1.25 per share — roughly €800 million of additional consideration — while keeping the 1.6 Intesa-share exchange ratio.

But the extra cash is not the decisive development. The condition is. Intesa has made clear that if MPS shareholders approve either defensive transaction — Banco BPM or Banca Generali — it does not intend to waive the resulting failure of the conditions attached to its offer.

October 29 therefore becomes two votes, four combinations, three futures for MPS. The battle has moved beyond price. It is now about execution, irreversibility and control.

The Strategic Map

Where leverage sits

01

Intesa — Discipline

Higher price. Harder condition. Credible walk-away. The consolidator with the strongest capacity to refuse bad economics.

02

MPS / Mediobanca — Execution

Mediobanca strengthened the industrial case. BPM and Banca Generali now test whether MPS can convert ambition into returns. The burden of proof has shifted from strategy to execution.

03

Banco BPM — The Clearing Asset

If the BPM resolution fails — or if Intesa ultimately prevails — Banco BPM immediately becomes the pivotal remaining domestic platform. Crédit Agricole already owns 29.3%. Likely centre of the next consolidation battle.

04

Crédit Agricole — Influence

Its BPM stake provides leverage without requiring full ownership. The more expensive control becomes, the more valuable influence becomes.

05

UniCredit / Commerzbank — Continental Scale

UniCredit is playing beyond the Italian perimeter. Commerzbank remains politically constrained, but strategically coherent. The clearest test of whether Banking Union can create genuinely European scale.

06

Generali — Strategic Scarcity

The next phase increasingly concerns savings, wealth, insurance and distribution, not just deposits and loans. The scarce financial infrastructure around which the second wave may form.

07

Banca Generali — The Wealth Choice

A fundamentally different growth model from BPM: wealth, fee income and lighter balance-sheet intensity.

06

Three Scenarios

Velocity Edge analytical probabilities

  1. BPM: NO. Banca Generali: NO. Neither transaction passes. MPS's defence fails and Intesa remains actionable. Banco BPM becomes the likely centre of the next consolidation phase. signal: Certainty defeats complexity.

  2. BPM: YES. Banca Generali: YES. Shareholders endorse the complete Lovaglio architecture. Intesa follows through on its stated position and steps away. MPS remains independent but assumes maximum execution complexity. signal: Maximum independence, maximum execution risk.

  3. Exactly one transaction passes. BPM YES / Banca Generali NO means MPS prioritises banking scale; BPM NO / Banca Generali YES means MPS prioritises wealth and distribution. The original architecture disappears and a new negotiating equilibrium emerges. signal: The vote narrows the strategy rather than deciding the whole contest.

The Architecture of the Next Economy

Why the economic theories that built the last century will not be enough to explain the next one.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann July 20, 2026 3 min read Macro

“The next decade will not be won by the nations that innovate the most. It will be won by those that orchestrate innovation better than anyone else.”

For more than two centuries, economists have tried to answer one fundamental question: Why do some nations consistently create greater prosperity, technological leadership and economic power than others?

The answers shaped entire eras. Adam Smith explained productivity. Joseph Schumpeter explained innovation. Michael Porter explained competitive advantage. David Teece explained dynamic capabilities. National Innovation Systems explained ecosystems.

Artificial Intelligence changes the question. Innovation is no longer the scarce resource. Artificial Intelligence is accelerating scientific discovery. Capital is increasingly abundant. Knowledge moves globally at unprecedented speed.

The new scarcity is orchestration.

The defining competition of the twenty-first century will not be fought between AI models. It will be fought between national architectures. The winners will not necessarily build the smartest algorithms. They will build the most adaptive economies.

That is the central thesis of today's The Velocity Edge—In Focus.

Inside this report we introduce a new framework for understanding how nations will compete in the Age of Artificial Intelligence.

Key takeaways

What each reader should carry forward.

01

Institutional Investors

The next generation of winners will emerge from ecosystems, not isolated technologies. Architectural advantage will increasingly outperform technological advantage. Evaluate countries, sectors and companies through their capacity to orchestrate AI, energy, talent, capital and industrial capabilities. National Velocity may become one of the most powerful leading indicators of future wealth creation.

02

Corporate Leaders

AI is no longer a technology strategy. It is becoming the operating system of the enterprise. Future competitive advantage will depend on the ability to orchestrate increasingly complex ecosystems of partners, infrastructure and intelligence.

03

Policymakers

The global race is no longer about AI alone. It is about building the institutional architecture capable of renewing national competitiveness decade after decade. Countries that learn to orchestrate will increasingly outperform those that merely innovate.

04

Economists and Analysts

Yesterday's models explain yesterday's economy. The Next Economy requires a new analytical framework. Measuring innovation alone will no longer be sufficient. Competitive advantage will increasingly reside in the architecture connecting science, finance, energy, infrastructure and institutions.

04

The Last Word

Every revolution rewrites the rules of growth.

The race to build the Next Economy has already begun. The greatest opportunities will belong not to those who understand Artificial Intelligence first—but to those who understand the new architecture of prosperity before it becomes conventional wisdom.

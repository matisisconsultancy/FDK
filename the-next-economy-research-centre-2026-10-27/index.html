The Next Economy Research Centre

Anthropic opens its books and Meta seeks $95 billion as the AI Supercycle enters the age of public-market accountability.

“The next Great Split will separate those who can produce intelligence from those who can finance it sustainably.”

The signal1 min
  1. Anthropic filed its S-1 on October 26 to list on Nasdaq as ANT, with final share count, price and valuation not yet established.
  2. First-half revenue reached $16.29 billion, roughly fourteen times the $1.16 billion of the prior-year period, with Q2 operating income of +$974 million.

  6. The defining question is whether revenues from intelligence grow faster than the financial obligations required to produce it.
Read the full note ↓

October 26, 2026, may prove a defining date in the financial evolution of the AI Supercycle. Two of the world's most influential AI companies took consequential steps toward mobilizing public-market capital.

Anthropic publicly filed its S-1 registration statement, revealing extraordinary revenue growth and massive future computing obligations. Meta simultaneously launched roughly $35 billion in equity and convertible preferred securities, plus a planned additional $60 billion equity program from 2027. Together, they signal that AI financing is moving beyond private investors and established balance sheets toward a broader system of publicly financed industrial development.

01

Anthropic: The First Financial Map

Operating economics improve; commitments stay extraordinary

Revenue grew from $386M (FY2024) to $4.59B (FY2025) to $16.29B in H1 2026, with H1 operating cash flow of roughly +$3.93 billion against ~$8.95 billion in compute and infrastructure expenses.

The most revealing relationship is revenue relative to computing spend. The revenue-to-compute ratio improved from 0.57× (Q2 2025) to 1.40× (Q1 2026) to 2.08× (Q2 2026) — revenue growing faster than the recognized cost of computing. These are not gross margins, and the ratio can deteriorate as new commitments become operational.

The headline $80.7 billion loss requires care: ~$72.4 billion reflects fair-value changes on convertible notes and derivatives, not cash outflows. The decisive test lies ahead. Reuters reports Anthropic has arranged more than $600 billion in computing and infrastructure spending across roughly eight partners over a decade, much of it noncancelable. The central tension: improving operating economics must be matched by future demand sufficient to support commitments made well in advance.

Anthropic

AI companies will increasingly be judged on revenue per dollar of compute, coverage of fixed obligations by future cash flow, and returns after research, infrastructure and financing.

02

Meta and the Return of Capital Intensity

Technology platforms as industrial enterprises

Meta's planned ~$35 billion includes ~$22.5 billion in common equity and $12.5 billion in convertible preferred, with an at-the-market program of up to $60 billion in non-voting Class C shares from early 2027. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom have expressed interest in investing up to $5 billion collectively — interest, not confirmed investment.

Third-quarter free cash flow was approximately −$2.12 billion despite strong operating generation; Meta held ~$82.45 billion in cash and securities against ~$80.92 billion in long-term debt. This is not distress — it shows how rapidly AI investment is reshaping the financing of a profitable platform into something resembling a highly capitalized industrial enterprise. Both Meta's non-voting shares and Anthropic's founder-concentrated governance signal that public capital may rise without proportional governance influence.

03

The Physical and Geographic Constraints

Energy, networks and national exposure

European wholesale gas reached roughly €97/MWh on October 26, its highest since 2022, with storage ~75% full versus a ~80% norm. Anthropic's S-1 explicitly flags electricity availability, water, climate and supply-chain risks, and discusses dedicated natural-gas generation for certain data centres — reducing grid dependence but adding fuel-price exposure.

The geography of value is broadening. Tata Communications announced a $400–450 million investment in ~10,000 km of AI-ready fibre connecting India's data-centre regions — planned, not completed. South Korea's downside scenario, assuming a 10% chip-price decline, shows how a weaker American AI investment cycle could transmit into national exports and growth.

04

The Hidden Patterns

Second-order effects

01

The Public Capital Transfer

Capital-intensive AI is accessing broader public pools, redistributing exposure among companies, shareholders and intermediaries while demanding greater transparency and discipline.

02

The Compute-Efficiency Premium

Improving revenue-to-compute ratios may strengthen frontier economics — but returns depend on whether efficiency survives competitive pricing and contractual commitments.

03

Capital Intensity Returns

The largest platforms increasingly resemble industrial enterprises; free cash flow, depreciation and ROIC may displace traditional software metrics.

04

National Exposure

Economies dependent on technology-intensive exports grow sensitive to the capital-allocation decisions of American hyperscalers.

05

Europe 2030: Choosing Where to Capture Value

Financing, energy and productive adoption

EU strategic-sector AI adoption rose from 7% (2021) to 18% (2025), but distribution is uneven — media 38%, energy 34%, manufacturing 16%, mobility 12% — and much reflects general-purpose tool access, not deep integration. The AI Gigafactories initiative targets over €30 billion with Lot 2 due December 3, 2026 and Lot 1 due February 4, 2027. France's sovereign conditions improved on October 26 after Moody's held its Aa3 rating, though the negative outlook and underlying uncertainty persist.

Europe's risk is not merely investing less than competitors, but investing without capturing a sufficient share of the value intelligence generates. The strategic objective: maximize productive value added per euro invested in AI.

The first phase of the AI Supercycle was driven by technological breakthroughs. The second by capital formation. The next will be decided by the financial returns of productive intelligence.

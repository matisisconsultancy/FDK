GVI Daily Update

AI is accelerating global trade, but the next Great Split will be decided by who captures the value — not simply who produces it.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 11, 2026 5 min read Capital

“The next scarcity is not intelligence. It is ownership of the value intelligence creates.”

The AI Supercycle is accelerating technological production faster than it is distributing economic prosperity. The next Great Split will be determined by who captures the value—not simply who produces it.

Three developments provide the evidence: the United Nations has quantified the concentration of AI's economic rewards, Europe is confronting a strategic budget contradiction, and the industry is moving toward deeper vertical integration. Together they expose a pattern — technological diffusion is accelerating while economic capture is concentrating.

The defining question is no longer Who can build intelligence? It is: Who will own the productive assets, financial returns and competitive advantages generated by intelligence?

OWNERSHIP

Technological excellence creates opportunity. Economic capture determines the return.

01

Global Velocity Rankings

Country scorecard — 11 October 2026

The United States leads at 94.0 on AI leadership, followed by China at 91.2 on industrial AI and tighter investment discipline, and South Korea at 88.8 on semiconductor profitability.

Japan (84.9), Germany (83.4), the United Kingdom (82.2), France (81.2), Switzerland (80.7), the Netherlands (80.6) and Sweden (79.2) complete the top-ten area, with Denmark (77.8), Norway (77.1), Spain (75.0), Austria (72.4) and Italy (71.6) rounding out the fifteen.

No ranking changes apply. Today's evidence concerns value distribution, European budgets and prospective transactions — none providing a persistent national observation to warrant a score change. Confidence is high for the UNCTAD report and EU proposal, moderate for their effects, limited for the Nvidia negotiations.

02

Five Signals Defining Global Velocity

AI accelerates trade while prosperity concentrates

  1. UNCTAD forecasts global GDP growth of 2.6% in 2026, down from 2.9%, against real trade growth of ~4%. Developing economies grow 4.0%, China 4.5%, India 7.3%, with Asia contributing 59% of global growth. signal: The distinction is between countries participating in AI supply chains and those capturing their highest-value activities.

  2. In UNCTAD's server-rack case study, post-tax profits account for 68% of traced value added, labour under 15%. Developed economies took ~70% of announced greenfield value in strategic sectors during 2020–2025. signal: Moving into the supply chain is not enough — economies must move upward within it.

  3. Nvidia is reportedly exploring an acquisition of or investment in Reflection AI, a developer of open-weight models and software automation. Discussions are preliminary, but infrastructure suppliers may increasingly seek value higher in the stack. signal: Vertical integration raises the importance of competition policy, interoperability and open standards.

  4. The Irish presidency's October 10 proposal would cut the 2028–2034 budget by ~€141 billion in constant prices — an 8% reduction, including 13% to competitiveness and security versus 3% for agriculture and regional spending. It would still be ~30% larger than the current framework. Leaders discuss it October 15–16. signal: Europe risks preserving established priorities while moving too slowly to finance emerging capabilities.

  5. Following consultations on October 8–9, the EU and China reached a preliminary understanding on export access, rare-earth cooperation and Chinese EV exports. Implementation requires further approval. signal: Europe is reconciling two aims — reducing strategic dependencies while preserving interdependence.

03

Five-Pillar Assessment

Strengthening the thesis without moving the index

Intelligence Infrastructure remains strong; Innovation Velocity stable-positive as open-weight competition intensifies; Enterprise Transformation uneven; Smart Capital increasingly decisive; Discovery Capacity stable. New reports can strengthen a thesis without justifying a change in a structural index — none constitutes a verified change in national productive capacity.

04

Second-Order Effects — The Next Great Split

Where the value accrues

68%

The Dividend May Be Captured Before It Is Distributed

When IP, infrastructure, distribution and capital are concentrated, the productivity dividend accrues disproportionately to asset owners. Creation does not equal distribution.

02

Value Chains May Become More Hierarchical

AI may strengthen existing hierarchies rather than accelerate convergence: access to intelligence becomes widespread while ownership of its economics stays concentrated.

03

Europe Faces a Capital-Allocation Paradox

The danger is less insufficient capital than the inability to redirect it. A common market without effective allocation risks becoming a collection of unequal industrial economies.

04

Vertical Integration Could Redefine Competition

Chips → compute → models → software → applications → cash flow. Multi-stage control brings advantages but higher capital intensity and scrutiny — proven only through returns on invested capital.

05

The Market May Distinguish Capture From Leadership

Capability → market access → pricing power → cash generation → capital compounding. Investors must separate input producers from final-benefit capturers.

Five Key Takeaways

From production to ownership

  • Investors: follow ownership of the productivity dividend — IP control, distribution, pricing power, free cash flow, capital intensity. Participation is not profit.
  • Boards: measure economic capture, not adoption — judge AI through margins, asset turnover and returns on capital.
  • CEOs: compete for higher positions in the value chain — own differentiated processes and recurring value.
  • Policymakers: make broad-based productivity diffusion a strategic objective — domestic value creation, competition and access to productive capital.
  • European leaders: treat October 15–16 as a competitiveness test — not how much Europe spends, but whether resources expand future productive capacity.
05

Financial Verdict

GVI 108.0, carried forward provisionally

Global AI-related production and trade are expanding, but benefits remain highly concentrated. Financial capacity and control of the technological stack are becoming decisive. Europe faces a critical choice between protecting established priorities and financing its future industrial economy. The next Great Split concerns who ultimately owns the productivity, profits and financial power generated by the AI Supercycle.

The next scarcity is not intelligence. It is ownership of the value intelligence creates.

The Day the AI Supercycle Met the Capital Markets

Anthropic's IPO and Meta's $95 billion equity plan mark the moment the AI Supercycle becomes a capital-markets financing system.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 26, 2026 5 min read Capital

The signal1 min
  1. Anthropic filed its S-1, revealing roughly $16.3B H1 2026 revenue against $1.16B a year earlier, with Q2 alone near $11.6B.
  2. Meta plans up to $95 billion in equity, with $135–145B 2026 capex and about $825B of future commitments.
  3. France's 10-year OAT fell ~12bp to 4.91% after Moody's held the Aa3 rating with a negative outlook.
  4. Dutch TTF gas touched ~€97.50/MWh, its highest since late 2022, exposing energy as a physical constraint on AI.
  5. Brazil's Ibovespa plunged more than 15% after Lula's reelection, triggering a circuit breaker and repricing fiscal risk.
  6. Wall Street rallied, the S&P +0.96% and Nasdaq +1.23%, led by technology and semiconductors.
Read the full note ↓

October 26, 2026 may prove an inflection point in the financial evolution of the AI Supercycle. Anthropic publicly filed its IPO prospectus; Meta simultaneously announced plans to raise as much as $95 billion in equity. Together they reveal that the capital markets — not internal cash, private rounds or strategic partnerships — will increasingly determine the scale, cost, governance and sustainability of the AI build-out.

The same session exposed the transition's contradictions. French bonds rallied, European gas approached €100 per megawatt-hour, Brazilian equities crashed, and Wall Street advanced on semiconductors. The world's appetite for technological transformation remains extraordinary — even as its willingness to finance political uncertainty and unproductive capital becomes increasingly selective.

01

The Diagnosis: Capital Is Repricing the Future

Anthropic and Meta reset the model

Anthropic's filing is one of the most consequential disclosures of the Supercycle. Revenue reached ~$16.3B in H1 2026 against $1.16B a year earlier; operating losses narrowed to ~$693M. The headline $80.7B net loss is largely accounting: roughly $72.4B reflects noncash changes in the fair value of convertible instruments, not a cash operating loss. The company spent ~$8.9B on compute in the half, with multiyear commitments in the hundreds of billions — and depends on a concentrated set of suppliers that are simultaneously investors, partners and competitors: Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom.

Meta's $95B comprises ~$35B in common and convertible preferred stock plus up to $60B in non-voting shares. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom have signalled interest in up to $5B collectively — capital suppliers helping finance customers who buy their products. That can stabilise deployment, but it also creates interconnected exposures that no single balance sheet fully captures. The Supercycle is becoming a capital-markets financing system, not simply a corporate investment program.

HIDDEN PATTERN

The future is becoming more capital-intensive precisely when the financial system is becoming more discriminating about the productivity of capital.

02

The Other Side of the Ledger

Sovereigns, energy and a divided market

Moody's decision to hold France's Aa3 rating supported a real bond rally: the OAT–Bund spread narrowed to just above 150bp from more than 170bp last week, with Italian 10-year yields easing toward 4.5%. France has gained breathing space, not fiscal credibility — the 2027 budget and social tensions remain.

Europe's gas shock is structural. TTF near €97.50/MWh and storage at ~75% of capacity collide with LNG competition, even as Brent eased ~1.4% to ~$115.10. Data centres, advanced manufacturing and electrified mobility now depend on reliable, competitively priced power: energy reliability is becoming as decisive as chip availability in locating AI investment.

Markets expressed no single attitude to risk. The S&P closed near 7,696.94, the Nasdaq 26,275.27, the Dow 52,488.30, and the STOXX 600 at 640.66 (+0.77%), defence up 2.6%. Yet Brazil's Ibovespa fell more than 15%, the real dropped sharply, and 5-year sovereign CDS widened from ~120 to 152bp. The market is repricing different forms of economic credibility.

03

Where Global Capital Is Moving

From computing capacity to productive intelligence

01

AI Platforms

Anthropic's IPO sets a valuation reference; a reported $1.5–2T range implies roughly 46–61x annualised H1 sales. The hurdle: converting infrastructure spend into margin and free cash flow.

02

Accelerated Computing

Capital migrates across processors, networking, memory, power and cooling — components with fundamentally different cash-flow cycles for suppliers, operators and model developers.

03

Industrial AI

Europe's edge may lie in using AI to raise returns on its existing industrial base — without the proportionately larger capex of the hyperscaler model.

04

Connectivity

Tata Communications plans ~$450M linking Indian AI corridors; a 1pp rise on €10B of debt adds ~€100M of annual interest, making cost of capital decisive.

05

European Capital Markets

The October 29 Monte dei Paschi vote tests consolidation; Norges Bank (~2.98%) opposes several deals on shareholder-return grounds.

04

The Week's Catalysts

A dense sequence of decisions

The next sessions will test whether Monday's recovery is durable. Wednesday brings the Fed; Thursday the ECB and the Italian banking vote; Friday the Bank of Japan. Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon report, with cloud demand, AI capex and free-cash-flow conversion in focus. Watch the France–Bund spread, TTF above €100/MWh, a U.S. 10-year sustained above 5%, and Brent above $120 as the key monitoring thresholds.

Five Takeaways

For investors, boards and policymakers

  • The AI Supercycle is becoming a public-market financing phenomenon.
  • Valuation discipline is inseparable from technological leadership.
  • Energy security is becoming a determinant of AI competitiveness.
  • Sovereign credibility will shape the geography of productive investment.
  • Industrial AI could give Europe a distinct route to greater productivity.

The AI Supercycle is moving from the economics of possibility to the economics of accountability. Intelligence is becoming abundant; productive capital remains scarce. The future belongs to those who master the conversion.

The Self-raising Hurdle

The Fed may pause, but the AI Supercycle is raising the cost of capital anyway — AI promises tomorrow's deflation while its construction creates today's inflation.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 1, 2026 5 min read Capital
5.3445%U.S. 10Y peak — 24-year capital hurdle
~$101Brent — physical inflation returns
52.9Eurozone Manufacturing PMI — strongest since May 2022
$42BBroadcom → Anthropic financing — the AI credit loop

“A Supercycle can lower economic costs while first raising the cost of the capital required to build it.”

The U.S. 10-year Treasury touched 5.34%, its highest since 2002, despite softer inflation having reduced expectations of another immediate Fed increase. Brent moved back above $100 after China suspended fuel exports. Yet global manufacturing is strengthening on AI-related capital spending, while software and enterprise AI continued to attract capital.

That is tonight's decisive contradiction: AI promises tomorrow's deflation — but its construction is creating today's inflation.

01

The Five Signals

Part I

02

The Five Signals

AI on both sides of the equation

  1. The 10-year briefly reached 5.3445% before easing toward 5.27%. The 2-year fell toward 4.81%. The front end sees a Fed pause; the long end sees persistent capital scarcity. signal: The most important market price is no longer Fed funds — it is the long-term cost of capital.

  2. Factory activity strengthened across Europe and much of Asia in September, with AI-related capital goods lifting the eurozone manufacturing PMI to 52.9, its strongest since May 2022. South Korean and Taiwanese manufacturing also benefited from semiconductor and AI demand. signal: AI spending is propagating beyond hyperscale computing into capital goods.

  3. Brent jumped above $101 after China suspended most fuel exports, adding to an already tight diesel market affected by Russia and Middle East disruptions. signal: Diesel transmits directly into freight, agriculture, construction and industrial costs.

  4. Wall Street weakened under the bond selloff, but software rallied strongly after Accenture's results. The market is distinguishing businesses that monetize AI now from infrastructure whose economics sit years in the future. signal: Near-term cash conversion is beating fixed-duration exposure.

  5. Broadcom has agreed to lend Anthropic up to $42 billion, potentially financing roughly one-third of Anthropic's $125.2 billion five-year TPU lease commitment — while also becoming a major supplier to Anthropic. signal: Growth financed by the supplier is economically different from independent demand.

The hidden pattern: AI CAPEX raises inflation and WACC today; AI DEPLOYMENT lowers unit costs and brings disinflation tomorrow.

03

Where Capital Is Moving

Part II

04

Where Capital Is Moving

Scarcity into cash flow

01

Memory And Compute Remain Scarce

Micron's strong outlook reinforced the persistence of AI-memory demand, even as its shares weakened while investors weighed enormous future investment requirements.

02

Power Is The Location Algorithm

JERA, Dell, RHAELM and Apollo are developing a $15 billion, 400-MW AI data center in Chiba. JERA will provide land beside an existing power station and guarantee electricity for 15–25 years.

03

Credit Is Strategic Infrastructure

Broadcom financing Anthropic shows how suppliers can accelerate demand by financing customers — but it also creates counterparty and concentration risk.

04

Industrial AI Broadens The Cycle

September factory surveys suggest AI spending is propagating into capital goods, semiconductors, electrical equipment and industrial systems — not remaining confined to hyperscale computing.

For Europe, the bottleneck is no longer capital availability. It is Capital-Conversion Velocity — and at 5%-plus yields, every year of execution delay becomes more expensive.

05

What Matters Next

Part III

Key takeaways

For every seat at the table

  • For investors: favor AI that combines scarcity + pricing power + contracted demand + near-term cash conversion.
  • For CEOs: the KPI is no longer AI adoption — it is productivity created per dollar of AI capital.
  • For boards: scrutinize supplier financing and circular capital structures.
  • For Europe: accelerate power, grids and Industrial AI — at today's yields, permitting time is financial cost.
  • For policymakers: the Supercycle may require a two-stage monetary framework — capital-formation inflation first, productivity disinflation later.

The New Capital Border

Markets are recovering from the AI correction, but the forces determining where capital flows, how intelligence deploys and who captures its returns are becoming more restrictive.

Read the full note ↓

Global financial markets are recovering from the AI correction. But the forces determining where capital can be invested, how intelligence can be deployed and who ultimately captures its returns are becoming more restrictive.

Three developments reveal the magnitude of the transformation: China's trade surplus has surpassed $1 trillion in the first ten months of 2026; the Nvidia–Jensen Huang controversy is accelerating bipartisan pressure to restrict advanced-computing exports; and Wall Street is growing more selective toward AI infrastructure precisely as Goldman Sachs projects hyperscaler debt issuance of $420 billion in 2027.

The next stage of the AI Supercycle is being shaped by three competing forces: technological demand, sovereign control and financial discipline.

01

The Five Signals That Matter

national security, credit, trade, oil, Europe

  1. President Trump said on November 9 the FBI was examining allegations about Huang's relationship with a Chinese businesswoman reportedly linked to intelligence services. The allegations remain unproven; Nvidia's board has opened an internal review and says it has no reason to believe secrets were compromised. The decisive move is in Congress, where the AI Overwatch Act, Chip Security Act and MATCH Act are under consideration for the fiscal 2027 defense bill — none yet enacted. signal: The next AI advantage may depend as much on which markets a company is allowed to serve as on which technologies it can produce.

  2. Reuters reports banks and institutional investors growing cautious on new AI debt. Bank of America estimates ten-year hyperscaler spreads have widened ~50bps this year versus ~20bps for non-AI industrials. CoreWeave's bonds have weakened; Nebius reportedly pulled a high-yield offering after securing a $3.5B senior secured facility. Lenders now demand evidence that assets will generate cash flows sufficient to service their financing. signal: The next AI leaders will sustain productive investment without making financing their greatest competitive vulnerability.

  3. October exports rose 35.2% and imports 41.6% year-on-year; the surplus reached $1.04T, crossing the trillion-dollar threshold a month earlier than in 2025. Semiconductor exports climbed 111.1% and high-tech exports 47.9% — even as domestic consumption stays subdued and the property adjustment persists. China captures manufacturing scale while the US retains frontier design: a national expression of the K-shaped economy. The data arrive ahead of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, after a two-month truce extension on November 9. signal: The Great Split is now between different economic systems capturing different parts of the global AI value chain.

  4. Brent near $91.56 is roughly 22% below its October 30 high of ~$118, after Trump said further strikes on Iran were not currently necessary — though the Strait of Hormuz blockade persists. October CPI is due at 14:30 CET, with consensus of +0.3% headline and +0.2% core. The BLS will not publish official year-on-year figures for categories lacking a valid October 2025 comparison after the shutdown. With an ~84% December hike probability, disinflationary relief is not yet monetary relief. signal: Lower energy prices can improve the economics of the Next Economy — they cannot, by themselves, restore inexpensive capital.

  5. UniCredit is expected to receive unconditional EU antitrust approval for Commerzbank by November 16, though governance and German board-representation questions remain; Crédit Agricole's Banco BPM combination adds momentum. The Banque de France expects modest Q4 growth after a 0.1% Q3 contraction, with aerospace, defense and data-center construction supporting industry — offset by political and budget uncertainty. A November 10 warning flagged German electricity-price pressure from weak wind and hydropower. signal: Europe's next advantage depends on whether financial integration becomes productive investment faster than sovereign and energy constraints erode its benefits.

The next frontier of competitive advantage is not the abundance of intelligence. It is the power to finance, control and monetize intelligence when capital, technology and sovereignty no longer move freely together.

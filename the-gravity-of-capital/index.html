The Gravity of Capital

The AI Supercycle is no longer simply transforming the global economy — it is beginning to redefine the price of capital and the hierarchy of global assets.

The AI Supercycle is no longer simply transforming the global economy. It is beginning to redefine the price of capital, the distribution of financial risk and the hierarchy of investment returns.

The Bank of England's latest financial stability assessment, South Korea's monetary tightening and the growing scrutiny of corporate governance across the AI ecosystem reveal an increasingly consequential financial dynamic. Technological acceleration is generating economic growth, but simultaneously increasing demand for capital and creating new channels of financial vulnerability.

The next great market rotation will be determined by the widening spread between technological acceleration and the cost of financing it.

01

The Hidden Pattern

Three structural forces are converging

The monetary paradox. Technological investment is accelerating economic growth while potentially reinforcing inflationary pressures and delaying monetary easing.

The credit transformation. The expansion of AI infrastructure is increasingly connecting technology companies, banks, private-credit funds, insurers and institutional investors through complex financial commitments.

The geopolitical premium. Technological sovereignty, energy security and trade fragmentation are reshaping the geography of productive investment and increasing the strategic value of integrated capital markets.

The consequence is a new hierarchy of global assets, increasingly determined by financial resilience, earnings visibility and capital productivity.

03

Second-Order Effects — The Next Market Rotation

FDK — the prospective financial transmission of the AI Supercycle

The implications extend well beyond technology equities.

Rising corporate financing requirements could compete with sovereign borrowing for global capital. Credit spreads could become leading indicators of equity repricing. Financially fragile infrastructure operators may face increasing pressure to consolidate, restructure or defer investment.

Meanwhile, companies with strong balance sheets and superior capital productivity could capture a disproportionate share of the next investment cycle.

THE NEXT ROTATION

The next great rotation may begin in credit markets before becoming fully visible in equity valuations.

Key Takeaways For Investors, Boards And Policymakers

Positioning for a more demanding capital regime

  • Investors: Position for earnings dispersion and credit differentiation rather than indiscriminate exposure to AI growth.
  • Asset managers: Monitor the interaction between sovereign yields, corporate credit spreads and AI financing requirements.
  • Boards of directors: Recognise governance quality, financial resilience and incremental ROIC as determinants of corporate valuation.
  • Executives: Shift from technological capital accumulation towards computational efficiency, productivity and free cash flow.
  • Governments and central banks: Strengthen the financial architecture supporting innovation without allowing excessive leverage to undermine systemic stability.
04

The Financial Verdict

The decisive investment variable for the coming years

The AI Supercycle is creating extraordinary opportunities while imposing increasingly demanding financial conditions on those seeking to capture them.

The decisive investment variable for the coming years will be the ability to sustain a positive and expanding spread between returns on invested capital and the risk-adjusted cost of capital.

The next financial premium will belong to companies and economies capable of compounding economic value faster than the cost of financing their growth.

Principal sources: Bank of England, Federal Reserve, US Bureau of Economic Analysis, Bank of Korea, European Commission, S&P Global Ratings and Reuters. Afternoon edition, November 26, 2026. Figures carried forward from today's earlier assessment. Forecasts and proposed investments remain distinct from realised outcomes.

Intelligence is expanding the frontier of growth. Capital is becoming the ultimate arbiter of value. The next winners will not necessarily be those who command the most intelligence, but those who generate the highest economic returns from it.

The Absorption Gap

The world is producing intelligence faster than its economies can profitably absorb it — and markets are beginning to price the difference.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann November 16, 2026 5 min read Macro & Capital

“Strong upstream demand is not the same as independently profitable downstream demand.”

The signal1 min
  1. Nvidia has shed roughly $1.9 trillion since early October, from $5.8T to $3.9T, before its November 17 earnings test of ~$109B revenue.
  2. China's October retail sales grew just 0.4% while industrial output rose 4.8% and fixed-asset investment contracted 8.1%.
  3. Japan unexpectedly contracted 0.1% annualized as consumption fell 0.3%, though capital expenditure rose 1.6%.
  4. US ten-year yields hold near 5.14% and the dollar reached its strongest since April 2025, tightening global capital costs.
  5. Europe drew $35.13 billion in equity inflows yet its gas storage sits about 20% below the ten-year seasonal average.
  6. The scarce resource is becoming productive demand, not computing capacity.
Read the full note ↓

The global economy is becoming extraordinarily proficient at producing intelligence. It is becoming considerably less certain about its capacity to absorb the investment, financing costs and productive output that intelligence generates.

November 16 opens with a consequential divergence. Asian semiconductors are selling off again — Taiwan down ~4.7%, Korea ~3%, the Nikkei ~1.7% — as investors await Nvidia's first earnings since Jensen Huang's resignation and Anthropic's anticipated public offering.

The distinction that matters is fundamental. There is no evidence that demand for advanced computing has collapsed. But strong upstream demand is not the same as independently profitable downstream demand — and extraordinary capital expenditure does not imply extraordinary capital productivity.

01

Nvidia and Anthropic

The economics of intelligence

Nvidia's capitalization fell from ~$5.8 trillion in early October to $3.9 trillion Friday — a ~$1.9 trillion reassessment of valuation and governance risk after Huang's departure. Earnings arrive November 17, with analysts expecting ~$109 billion in revenue and 90% growth; options imply a ~7% move either way. Anthropic is expected to price its IPO on November 18 ahead of a Thursday Nasdaq debut.

The Wall Street Journal reports the Trump administration may designate more than two dozen US AI firms as national champions — potentially Nvidia, Microsoft, Amazon, OpenAI and Anthropic — though Reuters could not verify it. The result is three increasingly distinct prices of intelligence: the technological price of capability, the financial price of expected cash flows, and the sovereign price of ownership and deployment. Nvidia's technology can remain valuable while its equity declines; government support can improve access without guaranteeing profitability.

02

China

Industry running ahead of its consumers

October industrial output expanded 4.8% (from 5.5% in September), but retail sales advanced only 0.4%. Fixed-asset investment fell 8.1% in January–October; property investment fell 21.6%. Q3 GDP of 4.5% keeps China on course for its 4.5–5% target, carried by exports and high-tech manufacturing.

Extraordinary industrial capability is coexisting with weak domestic purchasing power. Export success may reduce the urgency of reform and deepen external dependence — a central manifestation of The Great Split. Separate China's technological and export competitiveness from the financial performance of its domestic economy.

03

Japan and the dollar

The unequal cost of capital

Japan's Q3 unexpectedly contracted 0.1% annualized; private consumption fell 0.3% while capital expenditure rose 1.6%, supported by automation and AI projects. The economy is investing in future productivity while present consumption stays pressured.

Meanwhile the Dollar Index reached ~103.47, its strongest since April 2025; USD/JPY traded near 159.59, where intervention concerns rise. Markets price ~91% odds of a December Fed hike, with ten-year yields near 5.14%. A stronger dollar and higher yields raise financing costs for borrowers without dollar revenues — the price of capital is becoming a competitive variable between economies, not merely a background one.

04

Energy

An oil surplus concealing a European gas shortage

Brent fell toward $95.30 as the IEA forecasts a Q4 supply surplus of ~1.7 million barrels per day. But EU and UK gas inventories stood at 810 TWh on November 11 — roughly 20% below the ten-year average — with North Asian LNG near $31.30/mmBtu. Lower crude does not imply cheaper European electricity. For an AI cycle that demands reliable, large-scale power, the economy combining cheap capital with reliable electricity holds the advantage.

05

Europe

Record inflows meet a sovereign test

European equity funds attracted $35.13 billion in the week to November 11 — the largest since June 2021 — and the STOXX 600 hit a record. The EU's review of UniCredit's Commerzbank bid faces a November 16 deadline. But portfolio inflows are not new productive capital expenditure, and France, Italy and Germany face sharply divergent fiscal conditions. Europe's challenge is to prove it can absorb capital more productively than the economies from which it is being reallocated.

The scarcity of the Next Economy may increasingly be productive demand rather than computing capacity alone.

Key takeaways

for investors, boards and policymakers

  • Differentiate computing demand from monetization — the question is whether the AI ecosystem can generate independent, sustainable customer revenues.
  • The discount rate is a decisive variable — yields above 5% and a stronger dollar reward financing quality, especially for leveraged infrastructure.
  • For boards, productive absorption is the core measure — capex and compute are intermediate; cash returns are the ultimate test.
  • For Europe, inflows must become investment — portfolio interest is opportunity, not yet transformation.
  • The Great Split is becoming a divide in economic absorption — those who convert capability into profitable production accumulate cumulative advantage.

The Next Economy will be defined by the abundance of intelligence. The next global financial hierarchy will be defined by who can turn that abundance into economic value — faster than the capital required to create it.

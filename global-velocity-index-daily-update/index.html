Global Velocity Index: Daily Update

The Fed's first hike in three years converts the rising hurdle rate from a market expectation into an observable cost of capital — the AI Supercycle still has abundant capital, but no longer cheap capital.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 17, 2026 8 min read Capital

“The AI Supercycle still has abundant capital. What it no longer has is cheap capital.”

The Federal Reserve delivered its first rate increase in more than three years, lifting the federal-funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00%. More important than the move itself, which markets had largely anticipated, is the new path: 16 of 18 policymakers expect at least one further increase before year-end, while the Fed raised its 2026 PCE inflation forecast to 3.7% and no longer expects inflation to return to 2% until 2029.

The weighted headline GVI holds at 108.0 (2025=100), with an unrounded movement of −0.02 points and reported as unchanged at one decimal place. Today produces one country-level numerical revision: the United States falls −0.1 to 94.3. Model confidence stands at 96%.

The downgrade is deliberately limited because the productive side of the American economy remains powerful. August retail sales jumped 1.2%, core retail sales increased 1.4%, and a new AI-cloud financing structure involving Blackstone and Alphabet is seeking a $22 billion bank loan on top of $5 billion of equity to finance Google TPU capacity. But the financing report is based partly on private-source information and therefore receives a lower confidence weight.

Country signals

  1. Cohere and Germany's Aleph Alpha signed a definitive merger agreement to create a combined AI company previously valued at approximately $20 billion, with dual headquarters in Toronto and Berlin and Aleph Alpha's Heidelberg base retained as a research centre. Schwarz Group will invest €500 million in the merged company and provide computing capacity through StackIT, while separately investing €11–13 billion in a German data-centre campus designed eventually to host as many as 100,000 AI chips.

    No +0.1 is admitted today because the combination remains subject to regulatory approval, and significant parts of the infrastructure programme are future capital expenditure rather than operating capacity. But Germany's momentum improves materially: Europe may not have to defeat US frontier-model companies on parameter count to build a defensible position. signal: Score held at 83.2; sovereign, industry-specific enterprise AI is a different competitive game.

  2. Spain announced that planned electricity-transmission investment through 2030 will rise by roughly 30% to more than €17 billion, citing rapidly increasing electricity demand from electrification and large users including data centres. It would be the largest transmission-grid investment programme in Spanish history, with an auction-based capacity mechanism for generation, storage and demand-side participants.

    The plan remains within the formal approval process rather than representing completed capex. The GVI therefore records strong positive momentum but zero numerical points today. The admission trigger is straightforward: final approval, committed transmission projects, procurement or observable construction. signal: Spain remains 75.0 — strong momentum, no score yet.

  3. UK CPI accelerated to 3.1% in August, from 2.9%, driven largely by petrol, diesel and airfares. But core inflation remained at 2.6% for a fourth consecutive month, while services inflation stayed at 3.4%. This is predominantly an external energy-price shock, not yet evidence of a domestic wage-price spiral. Producer prices provide a warning: manufacturer input-price inflation accelerated to roughly 6.1% year on year and output-price inflation to approximately 3.7%.

    Because yesterday's −0.1 UK adjustment already incorporated weakening labour absorption, the model does not double-count the deterioration. signal: The UK remains 82.3 — no second downgrade.

  4. Huawei now forecasts that autonomous agents could generate more than 90% of global AI-token traffic by 2035, with potentially hundreds of billions of agents continuously perceiving, reasoning and interacting, and lists autonomous-agent security among its ten core priorities. No numerical GVI weight is assigned to a corporate forecast, but it reveals a strategic divergence: the US frontier debates pacing on safety grounds, while Huawei assumes faster deployment with technical safeguards rather than slower development. signal: Score unchanged at 91.0; the winner depends on which philosophy generates the strongest compounding loop between intelligence, adoption and productivity.

  5. The Bank of Japan is expected to raise its policy rate from 1.0% to 1.25% on Friday, which would take borrowing costs to a 31-year high, with further tightening expected into 2027. The move is not incorporated today because it has not yet occurred. Probability is not measurement. signal: Japan remains 84.9; the Smart Capital variable will be recalculated against simultaneous evidence if the BOJ executes.

  6. Brent crude eased to around $104.59 per barrel early Thursday after Saudi Arabia offered additional cargoes to Asian refiners through ship-to-ship transfers near Oman, an improvement from the $107–109 levels earlier this week. But the GVI does not reverse the energy-risk adjustment: Middle East infrastructure disruption persists, Hormuz remains exposed, and a few sessions cannot establish a lower structural cost. signal: This is precisely the purpose of the persistence threshold.

The Great Split

Key takeaways

the new operating equation

  • For investors: the next Great Split is between bankable and non-bankable intelligence infrastructure — premium to permitted capacity, secured energy, contracted customers, high utilisation and resilient balance sheets; discount to speculative gigawatts and models dependent on permanently falling financing costs.
  • For executives: the better question is no longer how much AI to deploy, but what economic return each incremental unit of intelligence generates above a rising cost of capital.
  • For policymakers: competitiveness requires an integrated system — capital markets → accelerated computing → electricity grids → enterprise deployment → skills → productivity → reinvestment.
  • Methodological discipline: the Fed decision, US retail-sales and UK inflation data are measured observations; German transactions and Spanish grid plans are announced developments; the Crux AI financing carries a lower confidence weight; Huawei's forecast receives zero weight; the BOJ decision is excluded.

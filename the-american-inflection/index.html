The American Inflection

The electoral backlash resets the terms of economic power—and forces markets to reassess the path to 2027.

The significance of America's midterm elections extends beyond the transfer of congressional power. The reported Democratic victory challenges the political assumptions embedded in the Trump trade—and introduces a new constraint on the investment cycle supporting financial markets.

Democrats have won control of both chambers, according to election reporting, although final seat totals remain subject to counting. Affordability and dissatisfaction with the administration were central to the backlash, strengthening the incoming Congress's mandate to scrutinise the presidency and its economic agenda. For investors, the pivotal question is what happens when the infrastructure of future growth becomes entangled with the household costs of the present.

01

Why This Time Is Different

the AI chain meets household costs

This election arrives when AI investment underpins a chain of anticipated earnings — semiconductors, cloud, electricity, transmission, construction and financing. A challenge to one link can travel beyond its immediate target.

Five features distinguish the implications. Political scrutiny now reaches the physical foundations of the AI economy, where permitting, grid connections and tariffs decide when projects become productive. Delays affect a wider pool of capital, postponing cloud revenue and equipment deliveries while financing costs accumulate. A congressional majority changes oversight before legislation — the new Congress takes office in January 2027, with existing law and the veto intact. State and local decisions on power, water and land use can matter as much as Washington. And 2028 now enters current valuations, raising the premium on political durability: the ability to maintain operating consent across electoral cycles.

02

The First Market Signal — And Its Limits

one reaction, several forces

Early reporting described falling US equity futures, stronger Treasuries and a weaker dollar — initial reactions, not a settled regime. Equities can weaken if earnings fall or uncertainty premiums rise; Treasuries can rally on slower growth; the dollar can soften if US rate advantages diminish. Yet these relationships can reverse: a prolonged fiscal confrontation could raise long-term yields, and an international shock could strengthen the dollar. A weaker presidency does not automatically mean lower yields, a softer dollar or stronger equities — the policy response determines which channel dominates.

03

A Repricing Of Execution

deployment versus recurring cash flow

The key distinction for 2027 lies between companies whose valuations depend on future deployment and those already converting investment into recurring cash flow. An illustrative $10 billion project expected to generate $1 billion of annual operating cash flow loses value if a one-year delay postpones receipts while funding and carrying costs continue — without any fall in underlying demand.

Later approvals lead to later energisation, later revenue, weaker cash conversion and greater financing needs. Large platforms with strong cash flow may absorb delays; developers reliant on refinancing have less flexibility. The same shock could weaken speculative projects while reinforcing stronger incumbents.

Greater scrutiny could slow deployment and concentrate competitive advantage at the same time.

04

Three Scenarios For 2027

analytical judgments, not probabilities

  1. Congress tightens scrutiny while essential budget agreements hold. AI investment continues under tougher demands for transparency and infrastructure contributions. Funded, monetising companies retain support; projects dependent on repeat financing or optimistic completion dates face a higher cost of capital. signal: Reassess the price paid for execution certainty.

  2. Oversight hardens into conflict over spending and executive authority. Companies defer commitments; financing costs rise for weaker borrowers. Treasuries may benefit initially, but long-term yields could prove less supportive if confrontation raises fiscal uncertainty. signal: Liquidity and refinancing resilience take priority.

  3. A pragmatic bargain: investment proceeds as developers contribute to electricity infrastructure and demonstrate consumer benefit. Confidence in the link between capex and earnings rises; participation broadens to firms adopting the technology. signal: Look beyond construction spend to firms capturing productivity gains.

05

The Numbers That Will Distinguish These Scenarios

a small set of linked measures

Watch capital expenditure relative to operating cash flow; contracted versus energised capacity; revenue and utilisation against installed capacity; financing costs and refinancing needs; electricity bills and infrastructure cost allocation; and market breadth and earnings revisions. Together, they distinguish a temporary valuation adjustment from a deterioration in investment economics.

Final Takeaways

positioning for 2027

  • For investors: separate the durability of AI demand from the timing and profitability of deployment; favour funded growth, demonstrable customer economics and balance sheets that can absorb delays.
  • For boards: stress-test major projects against slower approvals, higher power costs and deferred revenue; establish who bears each risk, and treat consent as material.
  • For EU policymakers: connect competitiveness to delivery — electricity, grids, permits, financing and adoption — so benefits reach households and productive businesses; Europe captures more value by improving its proposition than by waiting for America's to weaken.

The election's enduring significance depends on whether America can reconcile technological acceleration with economic consent. For capital, the decisive question is which investments can earn both.

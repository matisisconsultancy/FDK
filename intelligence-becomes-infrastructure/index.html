Intelligence Becomes Infrastructure.

Hormuz threatens the rate-cut trade. AI is turning electricity into a scarce financial asset. Europe may be the most under-owned beneficiary — and Spain its rising star.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann July 12, 2026 9 min read Energy · Capital · Europe
20%of traded oil & LNG passes through Hormuz
€750–800Bthe EU’s additional annual investment need
950 TWhdata-center power demand by 2030 (IEA)
€66.9BSpain’s data-center pipeline through 2030

“The market is still buying the promise of AI. Smart Capital is beginning to buy the physical economy that powers it.”

What happens to global portfolios when the world’s most important energy chokepoint collides with the most capital-intensive technology cycle in history? Iran has declared the Strait of Hormuz closed until further notice, and roughly one-fifth of traded oil and gas passed through it before the current conflict. The market sees another oil shock. That interpretation is too narrow.

Hormuz now threatens four assumptions simultaneously: falling inflation, lower policy rates, expanding corporate margins, and uninterrupted financing of the AI Supercycle. The consequence is larger than the move in Brent. Hormuz is becoming a global discount-rate event.

The fundamental question

What happens when the world’s most important energy chokepoint collides with the most capital-intensive technology cycle in history?

01

Five signals investors cannot ignore

Tap each signal to expand the read.

  1. The most immediate risk is not higher crude — it is the return of inflation uncertainty. The transmission is direct: oil and gas rise → inflation expectations increase → easing is delayed → real yields stay elevated → valuation multiples compress. Most vulnerable: long-duration equities, leveraged businesses and companies whose earnings depend on falling financing costs. Relative beneficiaries: energy producers, selected utilities, infrastructure operators, defense and businesses with contractual pricing power.

    Signal Hormuz does not need to stop every tanker — only the rate-cut narrative.

  2. The IEA expects global data-center electricity consumption to rise from ~485 TWh in 2025 to ~950 TWh by 2030 — close to 3% of global demand, with AI facilities growing far faster. The decisive variables are now power availability, grid-connection times, financing costs, utilization, depreciation, cooling and return on invested capital. The AI trade broadens toward power generation, grids, cooling, electrical equipment, semiconductor infrastructure, automation and storage.

    Signal The next phase is won by the strongest balance sheet and the cheapest reliable power — not the loudest model.

  3. Consensus says Europe is structurally slow and condemned to lower growth — much of that pessimism is already in valuations. The Draghi report estimates Europe needs €750–800 billion of additional annual investment (~4.4–4.7% of GDP) to close its productivity, digital, energy and defense gaps. That is the outline of a multi-year capex cycle: grids, firm power, defense, semiconductor equipment, AI factories, rail, telecoms, automation and advanced manufacturing.

    Signal Europe need not out-innovate US tech — it can outperform on an under-owned infrastructure earnings revision.

  4. The market still associates Spain with tourism, banking and consumption. That view is becoming obsolete. Spain combines renewable-energy availability, modern transport, expanding connectivity, competitive industrial locations, growing data-center investment and political support for sovereign AI. The government has allocated €719 million toward a Spanish AI gigafactory, and the data-center industry projects up to €66.9 billion of investment through 2030 — capacity potentially rising nearly sixfold, with Madrid strengthening as a European digital hub.

    Signal The market still values Spain as a tourism economy; the next decade may value it as Europe’s AI energy-and-infrastructure platform.

  5. The first AI rally rewarded software. The second rewarded chips. The third is likely to reward the physical assets required to operate intelligence at industrial scale — electricity, grids, semiconductors, defense, automation, robotics, storage, nuclear and intelligent mobility. This is not a rotation out of technology; it is technology becoming physical. Winners tend to hold one of four advantages: scarce capacity, long-duration contracts, regulated returns or strategic indispensability.

    Signal The next trillion-dollar trade is physical, not digital.

02

Investment implications

Productive assets versus unsupported expectations.

Potential beneficiaries

Where the capital cycle rewards

  • Power generation and transmission operators.
  • Grid equipment and electrical-engineering companies.
  • Data-center infrastructure and cooling providers.
  • Semiconductor equipment and memory suppliers.
  • Industrial automation and robotics businesses.
  • Defense and cybersecurity platforms.
  • Battery storage and intelligent-mobility systems.
  • European engineering and infrastructure contractors — and Spanish digital, energy and transport assets.
Potential losers

Where unsupported expectations get punished

  • Highly leveraged companies dependent on rapid rate cuts.
  • Energy-intensive manufacturers without pricing power.
  • AI businesses unable to demonstrate returns on capital.
  • Utilities and grids unable to finance required upgrades.
  • Automakers exposed to collapsing residual values and compressed product cycles.
  • Economies dependent on imported energy but lacking industrial investment capacity.
03

The hidden patterns

01

Energy is the new discount rate

Reliable, affordable electricity increasingly determines margins, national competitiveness and asset valuation.

02

AI is becoming a credit cycle

The next phase requires debt capacity, infrastructure finance and long-duration capital — not models alone.

03

Europe is not too late — it is too slow

Its upside depends less on invention than on permitting, financing and deployment velocity.

04

Every balance sheet is an energy strategy

Power procurement and security are moving from operating expense to board-level capital-allocation decision.

05

The next trillion-dollar trade is physical

The more intelligent the economy becomes, the more grids, power stations, factories, chips and mobility it requires.

Key numbers to watch

The dashboard for the reallocation

  • Brent crude and the persistence of the Hormuz risk premium; Dutch TTF gas prices.
  • US 10-year Treasury and German Bund yields; inflation breakevens.
  • European electricity forwards; EUR/USD; utility and infrastructure credit spreads.
  • Hyperscaler capex guidance and data-center grid-connection delays.
  • Spanish power prices, grid investment and data-center commitments.

The market still believes the AI revolution will dematerialize the economy. The opposite is happening: AI is making electricity, grids, semiconductors, industrial systems and transport infrastructure more valuable. Hormuz is revealing the vulnerability; AI is accelerating the demand; and Europe is approaching the moment when underinvestment becomes more expensive than investment.

The decisive question is no longer who builds the smartest AI. It is who owns the assets that AI cannot operate without.

Francesco De Leo Kaufmann · The Velocity Edge

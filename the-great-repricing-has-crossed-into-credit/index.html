The Great Repricing Has Crossed Into Credit

Financial stress has spread from sovereign bonds and tech equities into corporate credit — and the cost of capital is becoming the market's most important variable.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 24, 2026 5 min read Capital
129 bpsGlobal corporate credit spread, widest since May 2025
$116.72Brent crude, +3.14% Friday
51.9Eurozone composite PMI, down from 53.1
~170 bpsOAT–Bund spread, eurozone fragmentation warning

“It is no longer sufficient to possess the technologies of the future — one must possess the financial capacity to deploy them.”

The signal1 min
  1. France avoided a downgrade: Moody's kept its Aa3 rating on October 23 but retained a negative outlook, buying time without resolving the fiscal crisis.
  2. Corporate credit spreads widened to 129 basis points, their widest since May 2025, as investors demand more to finance AI infrastructure.
  3. Brent crude settled at $116.72, up 3.14% Friday and roughly 6% on the week, its highest since April, on attacks on Saudi Arabia.
  4. Eurozone momentum weakened, the flash composite PMI falling to 51.9 from 53.1, with France contracting while Germany expands at 52.8.
  5. The market call favours financial resilience, cash-flow conversion and pricing power over leveraged AI expansion and sovereign-sensitive institutions.
  6. The principal risk is that repricing changes the quantity, geography and productivity of investment, not merely asset prices.
Read the full note ↓

The decisive development of the past 24 hours is that financial stress is no longer confined to government bonds and technology equities. It is spreading into corporate credit — the market that finances the next wave of industrial and technological investment.

The Great Split is evolving from a divergence in technological capabilities into a divergence in the capacity to finance growth. The market is beginning to distinguish companies that can fund expansion internally from those increasingly dependent on external capital — and Europe faces its own version of the same challenge.

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

A valuation correction changes the price of existing assets; a credit contraction changes the capacity to create new ones. The first redistributes financial wealth — the second can redistribute industrial and technological power.

01

Five Signals Shaping The Next Economy

verified through October 23

  1. Moody's kept Aa3 with a negative outlook on October 23, assuming a deficit-reducing 2027 budget targeting 5% of GDP versus roughly 5.4% in 2026. Parliamentary divisions persist, and 70,000 security personnel are deployed for nationwide demonstrations on October 24. signal: The risk has shifted from a downgrade to whether the political system can deliver a credible fiscal settlement before 2027.

  2. Corporate bond premiums hit 129 bps, the widest since May 2025, as bonds from Oracle, Meta and SpaceX came under pressure. The Supercycle is entering its capital-productivity phase, and the system is demanding evidence of return at every stage from capex to free cash flow. signal: The next AI winners will be defined by how efficiently they convert computing capacity into cash flow, not how much they control.

  3. Brent settled at $116.72 as Houthi attacks and maritime risk intensified. The US authorised up to 400,000 barrels from its Strategic Petroleum Reserve; Germany added strategic-stock measures. Energy is now the common denominator linking inflation, sovereign financing and technological investment. signal: The strategic issue is no longer access to energy but access to predictable energy costs over the investment horizon.

  4. Besi reported Q3 orders of €342.6 million, up 96% year-on-year. ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent ordered more than $35 billion of Nvidia's export-compliant RTX PRO 5500 chips, while the US Treasury proposed broader outbound-investment curbs on October 22. signal: The semiconductor cycle can stay expansionary while investment returns become increasingly selective.

  5. Battery-electric vehicles reached 22.7% of EU registrations year-to-date, up from 16.1%, even as the composite PMI slipped to 51.9 and France weakened. Technological adoption accelerates while aggregate momentum fades. signal: The Great Split is now visible within Europe: firms converting adoption into efficiency will pull ahead of those facing high capital and energy costs.

02

Three Second-Order Effects

the hidden patterns

01

The next contagion may begin in credit, not equities

Equity markets can absorb corrections without changing industrial investment; credit markets set the price of financing. If sovereign and corporate premiums reinforce each other, capital formation could contract. The danger is not monetary disintegration but productive-capital fragmentation.

02

Energy inflation could collide with AI disinflation

The energy shock threatens to prolong the Supercycle's inflationary first phase before its productivity benefits spread — AI infrastructure inflation feeding higher financing costs, slower deployment and delayed productivity gains.

03

Europe's gap is becoming a capital-allocation gap

Europe's vulnerability lies less in technology than in how its capabilities are financed. Countries offering fiscal stability, reliable energy and predictable rules may attract disproportionate investment, widening the split within Europe before it widens with the US.

03

The Tests Before Monday

weekend market watch

Saturday closures make this a consequential information window. The Federal Reserve meets October 27–28, with economists overwhelmingly expecting rates held at 3.75%–4.00%. The ECB decides October 29 into a difficult mix of slowing activity, energy inflation and sovereign stress.

Watch the OAT–Bund spread and French bank funding, whether AI credit spreads keep widening, whether Brent holds above $115, and whether credit stress begins reaching real investment commitments rather than equity prices alone.

Five Takeaways

for investors, executives and policymakers

  • Bondholders: the sovereign spread is the first warning; private credit is the confirmation.
  • Equity investors: the next rotation may occur within AI, favouring cash-flow conversion over capacity.
  • Corporate boards: the hurdle rate is rising; capital discipline should accelerate productive investment, not suppress it.
  • European policymakers: sovereign credibility is now an industrial-policy variable.
  • Global strategists: the Great Split is becoming financial.

The next great redistribution of economic power will be decided not by who invests most in the future, but by who can still finance it when capital becomes scarce — and who can make that capital pay.

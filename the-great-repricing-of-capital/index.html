The Great Repricing of Capital

AI is lifting investment demand faster than productive capacity can respond — pushing equilibrium real rates up and forcing a harder market test on who can keep investing.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 1, 2026 2 min read Capital
3.25%Kashkari's revised neutral funds rate estimate
FirstCapital demand moves before supply capacity
LaterAI diffusion delivers productivity and disinflation
2Variables that decide the next rotation — real rates and cash-flow conversion

“Capital demand moves first. Supply capacity catches up later.”

Kashkari has raised his estimate of the neutral funds rate to 3.25%, with the AI investment boom one reason equilibrium rates may be higher.

That is the macro signal: AI is not just raising growth expectations. It is raising investment demand faster than productive capacity can respond.

THE ASYMMETRY

The macro asymmetry is decisive: capital demand moves first, and supply capacity catches up later.

01

What Higher Rates Decide

the two transmission channels

  1. Higher real yields determine who can keep investing. Cash-rich AI leaders can continue funding the build-out.

    Weaker firms face higher financing costs, which force lower capex, slower adoption, and wider competitive gaps. signal: Real yields sort the field into those who can keep building and those who cannot.

  2. Higher hurdle rates impose a harder market test. Distant AI promises meet higher discounting and lower valuation support, while visible AI returns deliver higher margins, higher ROIC, free cash flow, and self-funded reinvestment.

    The hierarchy is shifting from AI narrative to AI capex to AI adoption to AI monetization to free cash flow. The new divide: AI monetizers vs AI capital consumers. signal: The market now prices economic capture, not AI exposure.

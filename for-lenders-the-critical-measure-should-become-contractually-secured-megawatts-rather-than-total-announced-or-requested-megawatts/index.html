For Lenders, the Critical Measure Should Become Contractually Secured Megawatts, Rather Than Total Announced or Requested Megawatts.

Siemens Energy reveals €15 billion in data-center orders as Italy exposes the growing divide between requested and deliverable computing capacity.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann November 11, 2026 4 min read Capital

Two consequential developments today reveal that Europe's AI infrastructure race is increasingly dividing into two markets: industrial suppliers converting demand into orders and infrastructure developers still trying to secure the electricity, permits and financing required to operate.

Germany's Siemens Energy has disclosed approximately €15 billion in data-center-related orders for gas turbines and grid equipment. In Italy, newly reported figures reveal 15.2 GW of proposed data-center connections in Piedmont, but only one of 75 applications has reached the grid-connection-contract stage.

The contrast has major implications for investors, banks, CEOs and policymakers.

01

Germany — Siemens Energy Reveals a €15 Billion AI Infrastructure Order Book

Confirmed earnings announcement

November 11 — Confirmed earnings announcement and new management disclosure.

Siemens Energy reported fiscal 2026 revenue of €44.2 billion, pretax free cash flow of €8.8 billion and a record €164 billion order backlog. CEO Christian Bruch disclosed that approximately €15 billion of the backlog relates to data-center demand, representing roughly 9.1% of the total.

The company also targets a 20–22% operating margin before special items by 2030, with a planned additional share buyback of up to €9 billion. Its shares advanced sharply following the announcement.

The important qualification is that the €15 billion represents global equipment orders, heavily influenced by US data-center construction, not newly committed European Gigafactory spending.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN

The AI Supercycle is producing an increasingly visible industrial earnings stream before many planned Gigafactories reach financial close. The chain runs from AI compute demand, through power generation, turbines and grids, into industrial orders, earnings and cash flow. For investors, this reinforces the potential attraction of electrical-equipment suppliers relative to speculative computing-campus developers. For European boards, it demonstrates that power infrastructure is becoming a strategic procurement dependency. For policymakers, it strengthens the case for linking AI industrial policy with grid modernization and equipment-manufacturing capacity.

02

Italy — Piedmont Reveals a 15.2 GW Connection Pipeline With Almost No Contractual Conversion

Newly reported infrastructure data

November 11 — Newly reported infrastructure data and regional legislative proposal.

According to reporting based on Terna data, Piedmont had 75 pending data-center applications representing 15.2 GW as of September 30, compared with 64 applications representing 13.1 GW at the end of July.

Only one of the 75 proposals has reached the grid-connection-contract stage.

Separately, a proposed regional law would introduce energy, water, site-selection and territorial-compensation requirements for larger facilities. The measures are not yet approved legislation.

03

EuroHPC — Competitive Position Unchanged

Procurement deadlines hold

No new final EuroHPC Gigafactory award, binding financing close or consortium member was confirmed in today's review across Spain, France, Germany, Italy and the Nordics.

The official procurement deadlines remain December 3, 2026 for large-scale Gigafactories and February 4, 2027 for medium-scale Gigafactories, both at 17:00 CET. Replacement submission links remain available following the earlier technical disruption.

The official EuroHPC procurement page remains the primary reference for tender instructions.

04

The Investment Implications

What it means for each constituency

01

Investors

Increasingly distinguish companies with recognized revenues, order backlogs and cash generation from projects dependent on future grid connections and customers.

02

European banks

Underwrite electricity access, commissioning schedules and customer commitments independently. A grid application is not an electricity-delivery guarantee.

03

CEOs and boards

Evaluate Industrial AI deployment against actual infrastructure availability, not announced national computing ambitions.

04

Policymakers

Align Gigafactory funding with network capacity, permitting certainty and demonstrable industrial demand.

05

The Closing Verdict

Ambition versus execution

November 11 demonstrates why Europe's AI race cannot be measured only through the scale of prospective investment.

Siemens Energy is already converting global data-center construction into substantial industrial orders. Italy's Piedmont pipeline demonstrates how far proposed infrastructure demand can remain from contractual execution.

Europe's next competitive advantage will belong to those who turn financial ambition into secured electricity, operational computing capacity and measurable industrial returns.

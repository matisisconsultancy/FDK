When the Price of Money Meets the Price of Intelligence

The AI Supercycle is becoming increasingly profitable at the technological frontier while becoming increasingly demanding at the financial frontier.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 28, 2026 5 min read Capital

“Intelligence is changing the world. Capital productivity will determine who owns its future.”

  1. The AI Supercycle is accelerating faster than the financial system can adjust, generating exceptional profits while raising the cost of financing its expansion.
  2. The Fed held at 3.75%–4.00% on a 10–2 vote; Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack preferred an immediate 25-basis-point increase.

The most consequential development in global markets is the collision between two extraordinary forces: the accelerating profitability of artificial intelligence and the rising cost of financing the infrastructure required to sustain it.

In South Korea, SK Hynix reported quarterly operating profit of 83.3 trillion won — more than seven times a year earlier — and announced roughly $75 billion in planned shareholder returns, propelling the Kospi up about 5.6%. In Washington, the Federal Reserve held its rate at 3.75%–4.00%, but two members dissented in favor of another increase. The debate is shifting from whether inflation remains elevated to how much additional restriction may ultimately be required.

01

The Five Signals

October 28, 2026

The Federal Reserve — a pause with a warning. The Fed held rates, but two dissents show additional tightening remains live. The ten-year Treasury yield moved above 5%, forcing a reassessment of future cash flows. An unchanged rate is not a declining cost of capital. The strongest hyperscalers can self-finance; smaller operators face materially higher costs. Higher rates may therefore accelerate competitive concentration.

SK Hynix — the first great profitability test. Q3 revenue reached roughly 104.6 trillion won, with operating profit of 83.3 trillion won and an implied margin near 79.6%. Demand for high-bandwidth memory continues to exceed supply. But the transmission is distributional: higher component prices lift supplier margins while raising hyperscalers' infrastructure costs — increasing profitability at one layer while reducing financial flexibility at another.

The global monetary divide. The Bank of Canada raised to 2.50%, with Governor Macklem warning that energy prices could embed in inflation; it lifted its 2027 forecast to 3.1%. New Zealand unexpectedly held at 2.75%, sending its dollar down over 1%. The ECB faces its own decision with Eurozone inflation near 3.8%. The world is entering an investment supercycle without a synchronized monetary cycle.

European banking — record profits, unequal resilience. BNP Paribas posted €3.46 billion net income (up 14%) and a €1.3 billion buyback; UBS earned $2.69 billion with $55 billion in net new wealth assets; Standard Chartered lifted targets on a 27% rise to $2.25 billion. Yet Santander's reported profit fell 2% to €3.43 billion on restructuring and weaker Brazil. The question is evolving from whether institutions are profitable to whether their capital stays productive through a harder credit cycle.

Energy — the physical economy challenges the digital. Brent traded near $115.47 and WTI near $101.33 after a reported attack on Saudi Arabia's largest refinery. Equinor's adjusted pretax earnings doubled to $12.4 billion. Energy is becoming a determinant of AI's financial geography.

The hidden pattern of October 28 is that the AI Supercycle is becoming increasingly profitable at the technological frontier while becoming increasingly demanding at the financial frontier.

02

Where Global Capital Is Moving

From computing expansion to productive capital

The four largest U.S. hyperscalers could collectively invest more than $670 billion in capex during 2026; Meta alone guides to $135–145 billion, with equity financing expanded to as much as $97.5 billion. A $63 billion financing involving Anthropic and Broadcom equipment shows how computing is becoming a specialized infrastructure-finance market. The challenge is asset duration: a data center lasts decades, a processor becomes obsolete far faster, while financing obligations may outlast competitiveness.

The appropriate measure is not gross capex but incremental return on invested capital. A 100-basis-point rise on $40 billion of floating-rate debt would add roughly $400 million in annual interest. Semiconductor scarcity creates exceptional profits; sustainable leadership requires retaining them after capacity expands.

For Europe, the distinctive opportunity is industrial AI. Saab's Q3 order bookings reached SEK 65.8 billion, versus SEK 20.9 billion a year earlier, with 30.6% organic growth. Rather than replicating hyperscaler capital intensity, European companies can apply AI to existing assets: a €10 billion industrial base gaining one point of after-tax ROIC yields roughly €100 million in additional annual profit.

03

The Next Trading Day

Thursday, October 29

Five catalysts will shape the next rotation: Microsoft and Alphabet results testing whether cloud revenue justifies capex; the ECB's expected hold at 2.50% and its communication on fragmentation; sovereign and banking contagion risk; further Saudi energy developments with Brent above $115; and a European capital-allocation test around Monte dei Paschi. The next earnings cycle will increasingly distinguish spending leadership from profitability leadership.

Five Strategic Takeaways

For investors, executives and policymakers

  • AI has shown extraordinary profitability in selected parts of the supply chain; its sustainability and distribution remain the central questions.
  • The cost of capital is becoming a strategic variable, favoring companies that can invest without excessive refinancing dependence.
  • Energy security is becoming inseparable from computing competitiveness.
  • European banks remain profitable, but the next test is capital allocation and resilience.
  • Europe's greatest opportunity may be raising the productivity of its existing industrial capital.

The next great division will not separate those who possess intelligence from those who do not — it will separate economies that translate intelligence into financially sustainable productivity from those that merely finance its expansion.

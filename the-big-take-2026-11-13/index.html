The Big Take

When equity euphoria meets the bond market's revolt, the next phase of the global financial cycle hangs on how much value capital creates—and who captures it.

What happens when global investors pour unprecedented amounts of capital into equities, bond markets demand higher compensation for risk, and the largest artificial intelligence companies attract extraordinary valuations—all at the same time?

The answer may define the next phase of the global financial cycle.

01

The Return of Financial Abundance in an Age of Expensive Money

Hidden pattern I

Record global equity fund inflows, a U.S. 10-year Treasury yield around 4.88% after trading above 5%, an Anthropic IPO reportedly six times oversubscribed, and Goldman Sachs seeing scope for a 14% rise in the S&P 500 framed the original note. Investors were committing capital to equities even as the bond market raised the hurdle for financing the growth embedded in those valuations.

02

The Bond Market Is Challenging the Equity Market's Assumptions

Transmission

Rising long-term yields affect equities through two channels. They reduce the present value of future earnings and raise the cost of financing the investment required to generate them. A cash flow ten years out is worth roughly 9% less today when the discount rate rises from 8% to 9%. For companies whose largest returns lie years ahead, even a modest change in the cost of capital can materially alter valuation.

03

Anthropic: The IPO That Could Redefine the Price of Future Growth

Price discovery

Heavy demand for Anthropic's IPO showed how much investors were willing to pay for access to a leading AI company. The test for public markets would come after the offering, as investors assessed how much capital the business needed to sustain growth and how much of that growth could ultimately become free cash flow.

04

The Hyperscaler Paradox: Cheap Relative to What?

Returns on capital

Hyperscalers can look inexpensive relative to AI companies whose valuations rely heavily on future growth. Their own economics depend increasingly on what they earn on each new dollar committed to computing capacity. If capital expenditure rises faster than cash generation, an apparently modest earnings multiple can obscure a deterioration in returns.

05

The Emergence of a Two-Speed Capital Market

Hidden pattern III

The simultaneous strength of equity demand and weakness in government bonds suggests that global capital markets may be developing a two-speed structure. Scarce growth assets can attract abundant equity capital while governments and leveraged businesses face more expensive refinancing. The two trends can coexist until higher financing costs begin to change earnings expectations and investment plans.

06

Central Banks: The Challenge of an Investment-Led Inflation Cycle

Policy

AI investment can raise productivity over time while adding to near-term demand for power, equipment, construction, and skilled labour. Central banks must judge the timing of those effects. Capacity constraints can sustain price pressure before productivity gains spread widely enough to offset it.

07

Three Scenarios for the Next Six to Twelve Months

What drives each

45%

Productivity-Led Expansion

AI adoption translates into earnings and productivity gains, supporting equities even with elevated yields.

35%

Cost-of-Capital Squeeze

Persistently high long-term yields compress multiples and force more selective investment.

20%

AI Capital Reset

Weaker monetization or tighter funding triggers a reassessment of valuations and capital spending across the AI value chain.

08

The Numbers That Matter Next

Dashboard

The most useful dashboard should focus on transmission indicators rather than equity prices alone. Long-term Treasury yields and term premiums show where the cost of money is moving. Credit spreads and financing terms show how that shift reaches borrowers. Hyperscaler capital expenditure, incremental AI revenue, and free cash flow show whether businesses are earning enough to absorb it.

09

Where the Next Capital Rotation Could Begin

Second-order effects

A higher cost of capital can redirect investment within the AI economy. Companies that supply scarce infrastructure or turn AI adoption into measurable operating savings can gain relative appeal. Projects that require repeated financing before they generate cash face a higher hurdle, even if underlying demand for AI continues to grow.

Key Takeaways for Investors and Central Bankers

What to watch

  • Investors should assess free cash flow after capital expenditure and the returns on incremental investment.
  • Technological leadership creates financial value when the company can capture enough of the gains to cover the capital required to sustain that leadership.
  • Central bankers should preserve price stability while monitoring term premiums, leverage, market concentration, infrastructure financing, and stress among borrowers exposed to higher refinancing costs.

The next great market rotation will not be determined simply by how much capital flows into artificial intelligence. It will be determined by how much economic value that capital ultimately creates—and who captures it.

GVI Corporate Benchmark the Capital-conversion Test

Corporations will increasingly be ranked not by how fast they build technological capacity, but by how efficiently they convert it into productivity, cash flow and competitive advantage.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 15, 2026 5 min read Capital

“Today's scarce asset can become tomorrow's overcapacity.”

The Next Economy is entering a decisive phase: corporations will increasingly be ranked not by how quickly they build technological capacity, but by how efficiently they convert that capacity into productivity, cash flow and competitive advantage.

The evidence is becoming unmistakable. TSMC reported record quarterly revenue and ASML upgraded its outlook, yet leveraged AI infrastructure providers experienced severe credit-market pressure. On October 14, CoreWeave's bond yields approached 15% as the broader AI trade sold off, while September U.S. inflation reached 3.7%, intensifying concerns about financing costs.

These developments expose the central distinction between technological acceleration and economic conversion. The corporations shaping the Next Economy fastest are those capable of turning scarce technological and physical capacity into returns above the rising cost of capital.

03

The Five Hidden Patterns

structural signals

01

Capital costs are becoming a competitive moat

Higher financing costs disproportionately affect companies dependent on repeated external capital raising. Cash-generative incumbents gain relative strategic freedom, potentially accelerating industry concentration.

02

Credit may lead the next AI rotation

The CoreWeave episode shows why credit spreads, refinancing costs, supplier finance and contractual duration deserve closer attention. A slowdown in infrastructure financing could precede weaker semiconductor orders.

03

The AI bottleneck is moving into the physical economy

The chain is shifting: accelerated computing → memory → networking → electricity → grid capacity → industrial deployment. This expands the relevant corporate universe beyond conventional technology classifications.

04

Control can become more valuable than ownership

Corporations need secure access to electricity, infrastructure and critical components. But owning every asset can reduce capital productivity. The optimal architecture combines strategic control with balance-sheet discipline.

05

Geographic economic capture will differentiate

The U.S. retains strength in digital intelligence and platforms; Taiwan and the Netherlands hold critical semiconductor capabilities; Europe has advantages in electrification, industrial automation and advanced equipment. The question is where these generate sustainable incremental returns.

04

Investor, Board and Policymaker Takeaways

the conversion test

The emerging rotation is from financial duration toward economic conversion. Investors should differentiate companies by three characteristics: productivity that generates cash, scarce capabilities that sustain pricing power, and financial resilience that permits investment through tighter capital markets. The essential test is Incremental ROIC > Incremental WACC. Credit conditions should be monitored as a potential leading indicator of the AI capital cycle.

Boards should evaluate AI investment through measurable operating outcomes, not deployment statistics — the sequence runs technology → workflow transformation → productivity → cash flow → ROIC. Three governance priorities follow: securing critical infrastructure access, preserving financial optionality, and ensuring long-duration commitments are supported by realistic utilization and payback assumptions.

For policymakers, the defining challenge is making the Next Economy financeable. Grid permitting, reliable electricity, resilient supply chains, advanced workforce capabilities and efficient capital markets will increasingly determine national competitiveness. Europe's industrial AI opportunity is substantial but depends on translating industrial strengths into productive investment without sovereign financing costs and infrastructure bottlenecks undermining implementation.

TSMC, Microsoft and Amazon lead; Schneider Electric, ASML, Nvidia and Broadcom control the physical and computational architecture; Palantir, Siemens Energy and Siemens AG extend AI into deployment — all positions within one sequence: innovation → infrastructure → deployment → productivity → cash returns.

The Next Economy belongs to corporations that convert scarce capacity into productive cash returns faster than their cost of capital rises.

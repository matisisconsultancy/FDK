GVI Daily Update

Global productive velocity is accelerating just as the financing architecture behind the AI Supercycle is being repriced.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 2, 2026 5 min read Capital
108.0Weighted headline GVI — unchanged, confidence 95%
53.0Global manufacturing PMI, highest since February 2022
5.344%US 10-year Treasury on October 1, highest since 2002
$42BBroadcom facility to Anthropic against a $125.2B TPU lease

“The first AI winners sold intelligence. The next will prove that intelligence is bankable.”

Global productive velocity is accelerating just as the financing architecture behind the AI Supercycle is being repriced. That is the overnight signal.

The global manufacturing PMI rose to 53.0 in September, its highest since February 2022, with production at its fastest since July 2021—consistent, S&P Global estimates, with nearly 4% annualised global manufacturing growth. AI infrastructure and capital-goods demand are now visibly transmitting into the physical economy.

But the financial system is imposing a tougher test. The US 10-year Treasury touched 5.344% on October 1, its highest since 2002; UK 30-year gilts crossed 6% for the first time since 1998; and Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said policy rates may need to rise another 50 basis points or more. The AI Supercycle has crossed from a compute race into a capital-structure race.

THE CAPITAL-STRUCTURE RACE

The first AI phase was financed like technology. The next phase will increasingly be financed like infrastructure.

01

The Recalculation

Only variables clearing confidence and materiality thresholds enter today's scores

The United States holds rank one at 94.2 (−0.1), structural strength offset by deteriorating Smart Capital. China follows at 91.2, re-accelerating. South Korea sits at 88.8; Japan at 84.9 (−0.1); Germany at 83.2 (+0.1) on strengthening manufacturing recovery.

The United Kingdom holds 82.2 (−0.1) as financial conditions tighten sharply, ahead of France (81.3), Switzerland (80.7), the Netherlands (80.5, +0.1), Sweden (79.1), Denmark (77.8), Norway (77.1), Spain (75.0, +0.1), Austria (72.4) and Italy (71.7).

Positive adjustments in Germany, the Netherlands and Spain are offset by Smart Capital deterioration in the United States, Japan and the United Kingdom. Weighted headline GVI remains 108.0. No ranking changes.

02

Five-Pillar Readout

Where the pillars are moving today

  1. France's state-owned Bull has doubled output at its Angers factory to 12 racks per month, with capacity for 24 next year; European-made components now represent about 70% of its machines versus 20–30% five years ago. A $15 billion, 400-MW JERA–Dell–RHAELM data centre near Tokyo begins only in 2028, so is classified as committed future capacity. signal: Sovereign-compute capacity is real but still too narrow to re-score today.

  2. US manufacturing remains expansionary, with ISM at 54.5 and new orders at 55.3. But the Prices Index jumped to 77.9, and memory, DRAM and electronic components were reported in short supply. signal: The innovation cycle is strong; the marginal cost of scaling it is rising.

  3. Euro-area manufacturing PMI reached 52.9, the strongest in more than four years, led by investment goods. Germany held at 53.9; Spain returned to expansion at 51.0; the Netherlands led the bloc. signal: AI capital formation is propagating beyond the technology sector.

  4. The US 10-year reached a 24-year high before easing to about 5.25% early Friday, while UK 30-year yields crossed 6%. South Korea's September CPI slowed from 3.1% to 2.9%; Japan moved the other way as Tokyo core CPI accelerated to 2.7% versus 2.4% expected. signal: Financing conditions are now the binding constraint on the cycle.

  5. No new research-output, scientific-talent or commercialisation variable cleared the daily materiality threshold. signal: No change at the discovery frontier today.

03

The Institutional Interpretation

A new hierarchy of financeable capacity

Lenders will now ask questions equity markets ignored in the first leg: Who guarantees the asset? What is the residual value? Who contracts the capacity? What happens when the next chip generation arrives?

The Broadcom–Anthropic structure and the debate around Nvidia-backed compute financing show the transition has begun. The second-order chain runs: AI CAPEX → CREDIT DEMAND → HIGHER GUARANTEE REQUIREMENTS → CAPITAL CONCENTRATION. The strongest balance sheets acquire a structural advantage.

05

Evolutionary Market Dynamics

The likely rotation

The rotation is becoming: AI NARRATIVE → AI CASH FLOW → AI COLLATERAL → AI BOTTLENECKS → AI PRODUCTIVITY. The first rotation is visible; the second is starting now, as markets determine which AI assets can support long-duration financing. Capital migrates toward observable scarcity and contractual revenues: MEMORY → NETWORKING → POWER → TRANSFORMERS → COOLING → GRID → HIGH-UTILISATION COMPUTE. The largest phase comes when firms outside technology show measurable gains in margin, productivity and ROIC—when AI ceases to be a sector and becomes an economic regime.

Five Takeaways

What boards should carry forward

  • The AI Supercycle is entering its credit phase: capacity will be differentiated by contractual cash flow, guarantees and residual value—not simply technological capability.
  • Higher rates accelerate concentration: the stronger the balance sheet, the greater the ability to finance, guarantee customers and acquire weakened competitors.
  • Industrial breadth is real: global manufacturing at a four-and-a-half-year high confirms AI capital formation is propagating into machinery, electronics, grids and capital goods.
  • Europe is finally building physical AI capacity, yet European yields and energy-linked inflation rise against it.
  • The board test tightens: CAPACITY → CONTRACTED UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW → ROIC > WACC. Anything that cannot complete that chain becomes hard to finance at today's hurdle rates.

GVI 108.0. Productive velocity rises; financial selectivity rises faster. The AI Supercycle remains intact—but its financing model is no longer being taken on faith.

The Price of Permission

At 5% money, the AI Supercycle is discovering a fourth scarce factor — permission — and the Great Split becomes Permissioned Capacity versus Stranded Scale.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 16, 2026 9 min read Capital

≥$1M/MWNew York's proposed community contribution benchmark

“Velocity still determines who moves first. Permission increasingly determines who gets to keep moving.”

The Federal Reserve decides tonight with the U.S. 10-year Treasury at 4.99% and markets pricing a 92% probability of a rate increase. Brent remains near $108 despite a huge U.S. inventory build. Yet Anthropic has just secured access to a 2.16-GW Australian AI campus devoted to inference, SK Hynix is discussing producing memory in the United States with Intel, and New York wants data-centre developers to invest at least $1 million in local communities for every megawatt they consume.

The AI Supercycle is not running out of capital. It is discovering that capital alone is no longer sufficient. The Great Split is becoming PERMISSIONED CAPACITY versus STRANDED SCALE.

If the Next Economy increasingly requires permission from central banks, governments, communities and regulators before capital can become productive capacity, is speed still the ultimate advantage — or is the scarce asset now the ability to scale with permission? The underlying principle is becoming clear: capital creates optionality; permission creates capacity.

PERMISSION

For the first time, permission sits between money and capacity: CAPITAL → PERMISSION → INFRASTRUCTURE → INTELLIGENCE → DEPLOYMENT → CASH FLOW.

01

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

Tonight's cost-of-capital audit

  1. The U.S. 10-year yield is around 4.99% after breaching 5% on Tuesday. Futures imply a 92.4% probability of a 25-basis-point increase. The S&P 500 lost about 0.5% Tuesday, its second consecutive decline. Morgan Stanley now expects a hike today and another quarter-point move in December, citing slow disinflation, energy prices and AI-related investment demand.

    The policy rate matters, but the dot plot matters more. A single hike is insurance against an energy shock; a sequence changes the capital regime. The decisive equation becomes productivity velocity vs. discount-rate velocity — if AI cash flows rise faster than the cost of capital, the Supercycle survives 5% money. signal: Tonight is the first full cost-of-capital audit of the AI Supercycle.

  2. Anthropic has agreed to lease capacity at a proposed 2.16-GW campus near Brisbane, expected to begin operating in 2027 and dedicated to inference rather than training. The project remains subject to Australian Foreign Investment Review Board approval. It arrives days after CEO Dario Amodei called for frontier developers to slow model-capability gains.

    The market assumed slower models means fewer chips and less infrastructure. Anthropic is telling us the opposite: frontier velocity can slow while deployment velocity accelerates. The centre of gravity is shifting from training the next model toward running intelligence continuously — rewarding low latency, energy efficiency, networking and utilisation. signal: This is the transition from AI discovery to AI industrialisation.

  3. SK Hynix is in exploratory discussions with Intel about manufacturing memory chips in the U.S. for the first time — potentially leasing part of Intel's Ohio complex or forming a venture with major cloud customers. No decision has been made. Washington has threatened tariffs of up to 100% on some South Korean and Taiwanese producers unless they build in the U.S.; Seoul regards advanced memory as strategically sensitive.

    Phase I of semiconductor sovereignty was bringing leading-edge logic home. Phase II becomes bringing the memory stack home too — because AI systems cannot be sovereign if only one part of the accelerated-computing architecture is domestic. The complete stack now spans processors, HBM, networking, packaging, electricity and data centres. signal: Bottlenecks attract industrial policy.

  4. New York Governor Kathy Hochul is recommending municipalities seek at least $1 million per megawatt of utility demand from proposed data centres. New York earlier became the first U.S. state to impose a moratorium on large new facilities; only about one-third of Americans approve of the current pace of construction, per a Reuters/Ipsos poll. Texas is weighing tougher water-use disclosure.

    A new line item is appearing in underwriting: social licence. At $1 million per megawatt, a 1-GW project implies $1 billion of potential community investment. Infrastructure won't stop — it will migrate toward jurisdictions where power is abundant, communities accept development and permitting is predictable. signal: Economic geography is becoming an AI variable.

  5. Brent has slipped to roughly $108 after industry data showed an unexpected 7.1-million-barrel crude build, against expectations for a draw. But Saudi Arabia has suspended loadings at Yanbu following damage to the East–West pipeline and is offering additional crude to Asia via ship-to-ship transfers off Oman. Only four vessel transits crossed Hormuz on Tuesday, versus a 10-day average of 18. European diesel futures hit a record.

    An inventory build is a stock variable; the energy shock is a network problem. Saudi Arabia is redesigning the route — pipeline failure to Yanbu interruption to Oman transshipment to higher complexity. The same law that governs AI infrastructure now applies: capacity must possess route optionality, political permission, physical security and redundancy. signal: The cost of resilience is becoming part of the cost of production.

02

Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines

The Permission Stack emerges

01

The AI slowdown debate is really about where intelligence is produced

Leaders debate slowing frontier progress even as Anthropic commits to one of the largest inference campuses ever proposed. There is no contradiction once the Supercycle splits into two layers — frontier intelligence (ever-more-capable models) and industrial intelligence (deploying existing capabilities billions of times). Capital may rotate from the first toward the second. The Supercycle does not contract; it broadens.

02

The new bottleneck is the Permission Stack

Every major development this morning requires approval from a different institution: the Fed prices capital, governments shape semiconductor geography, communities influence data-centre economics, energy regulators control grid access, safety regulators govern autonomy, and foreign-investment boards decide whether projects proceed. Permission now sits between money and capacity.

03

Governments are competing on the price of permission

Canada offers the counterexample to New York. Prime Minister Mark Carney's permanent Productivity Mega Deduction lets companies expense most new capital immediately — cutting the marginal effective tax rate on new investment from roughly 13% to 6.4% as Ottawa courts more than C$1 trillion of foreign capital. New York pays more for the social licence; Canada pays less for the capital licence. Capital will compare both.

04

European banking consolidation has crossed a threshold

Germany no longer asks how to stop UniCredit. Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil has told Andrea Orcel his conditions: Commerzbank should remain listed, keep its Frankfurt base and continue financing the Mittelstand. A combined group would hold over €1.3 trillion of assets. Europe has moved from resisting continental scale to negotiating the conditions of continental scale. Banking Union is becoming industrial policy.

05

Physical AI is discovering the same constraint

Tesla began limited commercial Cybercab deployment in Austin on September 3. NHTSA has now ordered Tesla to explain by September 30 how the steering-wheel-free vehicle complies with federal safety rules. Being technically capable of deploying is not the same as having permission to deploy at scale: autonomy to certification to liability to approval to fleet deployment. Regulation becomes part of the production architecture.

  • Watch the 10-year around 5%: if the yield holds even after a hike, monetary tightening is not yet tight enough to anchor the long end — more consequential than the 25 bp itself.
  • 08:00 CEST — UK inflation: August CPI/PPI ahead of this week's Bank of England decision; July CPI was already 2.9% year over year.
  • 16:30 CEST — U.S. petroleum inventories: official EIA data follow the API's 7.1M-barrel build estimate; a materially smaller build refocuses markets on Gulf disruption.
  • The AI split after New York opens: if data-centre, networking, power and enterprise-AI names outperform frontier proxies, capital is rotating into the deployment layer.
VELOCITY

Railways needed rights of way. Utilities needed franchises. Telecoms needed spectrum. Banks needed charters. Aviation needed certification. The AI Supercycle has reached the same institutional moment — money alone no longer creates capacity.

GVI Daily Update

AI velocity remains strong, but the financial hurdle is rising faster — and the next phase will reward those who can convert intelligence into returns.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann September 28, 2026 4 min read Capital
108.0GVI (unchanged from previous)
96%Confidence
+110%China electronics profits, first eight months
66%Market-priced odds of an October Fed hike

“The next winners will not build intelligence fastest. They will convert it into returns fastest.”

The overnight signal is increasingly clear: AI velocity remains strong, but the financial hurdle is rising faster.

China delivered the most important new measured datapoint, illustrating an economy where the AI engine accelerates even as the broader industrial base loses momentum. Meanwhile, the cost of capital has moved against the cycle.

THE EVIDENCE

AI is still accelerating. Conversion is becoming harder.

01

The Evidence

What the overnight tape shows

China's industrial profits rose only 4.2% y/y in August, down from 11.2% in July, while January–August growth slowed to 15.7% from 17.6%. Yet profits in computer, communications and electronics manufacturing remained up 110% over the first eight months. The AI economy is accelerating even as the broader industrial economy loses momentum.

The cost of capital moved against the cycle. Brent rose 1.6% to about $106, the US 30-year Treasury yield reached 5.5185%, and markets price a 66% probability of another Fed hike in October. Japan's Nikkei gained 0.8%, South Korea fell 0.6%, and broader Asia-Pacific equities slipped 0.2%. These are provisional market signals, not yet closing-price inputs to the GVI.

02

The Five Pillars

Overnight recalculation

  1. China's electronics profits remain +110%, while the latest US measured capital-spending signal still shows core capital-goods orders +1.6% m/m, computers +20.1% y/y and communications equipment +35.8%. The buildout remains real. signal: The physical AI buildout is intact and still expanding.

  2. No overnight evidence sufficiently material to alter country scores. signal: No change to the innovation reading.

  3. A new European study finds electric trucks purchased in 2026 already cheaper to own than diesel in the Netherlands, Germany, Denmark, Sweden and France, with five-year savings reaching roughly €100,000 in the Netherlands and €85,000 in Germany. This is economically significant, but remains a third-party TCO estimate, not measured fleet adoption — so no score adjustment yet. signal: Real economics, but not yet measured adoption.

  4. Oil above $105 and long Treasury yields above 5.5% increase the fully loaded cost of AI infrastructure. Today's moves remain intraday and therefore do not yet trigger a numerical change. signal: Capital is getting more expensive precisely as AI gets more capital intensive.

  5. No new high-confidence research, talent or scientific-output release crossed the materiality threshold overnight. signal: No material change to discovery inputs.

05

Institutional Interpretation

Where the real divide is opening

The emerging divide is no longer simply AI leaders versus laggards. It is becoming a divide between economies and companies that can convert AI intensity into productivity and cash flow — and those that cannot.

China illustrates the pattern perfectly: electronics profits +110%; aggregate industrial-profit momentum sharply weaker. The United States shows the mirror image: AI-related capital formation remains powerful, but oil and long-term yields are steadily raising the price of every future dollar of return.

For investors, the preference shifts toward scarcity assets with current cash flow — power, memory, networking, cooling and high-utilisation infrastructure — rather than long-duration capacity promises.

For boards, AI investment should now be judged through one chain: CAPACITY → UTILISATION → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW → ROIC > WACC.

For policymakers, the competitive frontier is increasingly energy + capital + adoption, not models alone.

Key takeaways

The financial verdict

  • GVI holds at 108.0 with no ranking changes.
  • AI velocity remains high, but the financial hurdle is rising faster.
  • The next phase will reward fewer players as capital grows more expensive.
  • Investors should favour scarcity assets with current cash flow over capacity promises.

The next winners will not build intelligence fastest. They will convert it into returns fastest.

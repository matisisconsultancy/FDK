The Age of Moonshots

The age of incremental progress is over. The future will belong to those who build the impossible.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann July 19, 2026 4 min read Macro

“The competitive advantage of the future is not measured by how efficiently a nation manages the present, but by how relentlessly it invents — and industrializes — the future.”

There are moments in history when the rules of competition are rewritten. Not improved. Not updated. Rewritten.

The Industrial Revolution transformed human labor into mechanical power. The twentieth century transformed scientific discovery into industrial production. The Internet transformed information into the world's most valuable commodity.

Today, another transformation is underway. Artificial intelligence is often described as the defining technology of our time. It is not. It is the catalyst.

The deeper transformation is that humanity has entered the Age of Moonshots. An age in which governments, companies and entire societies are no longer competing to build better products or even better technologies. They are competing to achieve what only a few years earlier appeared impossible.

This is the thread connecting this week's events.

The fundamental question

What if we are witnessing the birth of a new era in which the world's most valuable resource is no longer capital, technology or even artificial intelligence — but the ability to repeatedly build what once seemed impossible? Taken individually, this week's stories appear unrelated. Taken together, they reveal a profound shift. The global economy is no longer organized around the efficient allocation of capital alone. It is increasingly organized around the industrialization of ambition. That is what a moonshot really is. A moonshot is not an invention. It is the capacity to mobilize talent, capital, science, engineering, political will and entrepreneurial courage around a mission so ambitious that it changes the trajectory of industries — and sometimes of civilizations.

01

The connected events

Tap each signal to expand the read.

  1. China's Moonshot AI is no longer simply another frontier model. It signals a nation determined to compress the time between scientific discovery and industrial leadership. signal: The AI race is really a race to shorten the distance from discovery to industry.

  2. Meta is no longer behaving like a social media company. It is assembling one of the largest concentrations of artificial intelligence talent and infrastructure ever created, betting that the next generation of economic value will emerge from orchestrating entire ecosystems rather than isolated products. signal: Value is migrating from isolated products to orchestrated ecosystems.

  3. South Korea's markets are increasingly becoming a barometer of the global AI economy, rewarding countries capable of combining semiconductors, advanced manufacturing, energy, software and finance into coherent innovation platforms. signal: Markets now reward whole innovation platforms, not single sectors.

  4. Renewed geopolitical tensions in the Middle East remind us that the competition for technological leadership cannot be separated from energy security, supply chains and national resilience. signal: Technological leadership cannot be separated from energy and resilience.

  5. Even the European Central Bank's cautious stance reflects a broader reality: macroeconomic stability remains essential, but stability alone does not create leadership. History rewards those who pair discipline with ambition. signal: Stability alone does not create leadership — discipline must be paired with ambition.

02

The historical parallel

Every transformation follows the same arc.

01

The Apollo Program

The Apollo Program did not simply put a man on the Moon. It accelerated computing, telecommunications, advanced materials, software engineering and aerospace for decades.

02

The Internet

The Internet was never just a communications network. It became the operating system of the digital economy.

03

Artificial intelligence → the Moonshot Economy

Artificial intelligence will follow the same path. The frontier models attracting today's headlines are only the beginning. The real transformation will come from the industries, infrastructure, institutions and entirely new markets they make possible. This is why the competition between the United States and China should not be understood merely as a contest over artificial intelligence. It is a competition over who can build the world's most effective Moonshot Economy — an economy capable of turning bold ideas into globally scaled industries faster than anyone else. That is the defining characteristic of the Velocity Economy. Its competitive advantage is not measured by how efficiently it manages the present. It is measured by how relentlessly it invents — and industrializes — the future.

Key takeaways

What to carry into the next session

  • The Age of Incremental Progress is over; leadership will belong to those who repeatedly build the impossible.
  • Artificial intelligence is the catalyst, not the destination — the real value lies in the industries and institutions it makes possible.
  • The economy is reorganizing from the efficient allocation of capital toward the industrialization of ambition.
  • The US–China contest is not simply about AI, but about who builds the most effective Moonshot Economy.
  • Apollo and the Internet show the pattern: the deepest value comes long after the headline breakthrough.

The countries, companies and investors that understand this shift first will not simply participate in the Next Economy. They will define it.

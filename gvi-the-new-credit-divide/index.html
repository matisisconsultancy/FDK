GVI: The New Credit Divide

The financing bottleneck is shifting from the production of intelligence toward its productive diffusion across the broader economy.

  2. European banks still expect rising AI-infrastructure lending, favouring infrastructure providers over software developers and AI adopters.

Read the full note ↓

The global economy is entering a decisive phase of the AI Supercycle: capital is increasingly flowing toward the production of intelligence while financing conditions for the broader productive economy become more restrictive.

The October 27 European Central Bank Bank Lending Survey provides the strongest evidence of this emerging divide. European banks are tightening lending standards for companies and credit demand is weakening — yet they simultaneously anticipate sustained growth in lending for AI infrastructure, particularly data centres.

The competitive question is no longer simply which economies can finance the expansion of intelligence. It is which can finance its productive diffusion throughout the economy.

01

Europe — The Credit Allocation Divide

ECB Bank Lending Survey

In the third quarter the net balance of banks reporting tighter corporate lending standards rose to 9%, from 7%. The corporate loan-demand balance fell to −6%, from +3%; housing-loan demand deteriorated to −27%, from −15%.

Standards tightened in France and Germany, were unchanged in Spain and eased in Italy. France recorded its strongest reported tightening since the first half of 2023. Most consequentially, banks indicated AI-related financing increasingly favours infrastructure providers rather than software developers or companies adopting AI.

The survey ran September 21 to October 6, so it establishes financing pressures that preceded the late-October French sovereign-yield escalation rather than proving it caused them. The risk: Europe finances the construction of intelligence infrastructure faster than its enterprises can finance the productivity improvements required to use it.

02

United States — Capital Formation Continues

Durable goods

Core capital-goods orders increased 0.5% in September, following a 1.6% rise in August; shipments rose 1.2%, from 0.6%. On an annual basis core orders rose 10.9% and shipments 8.8%, with overall durable-goods orders up 1.8%. Economists estimate business-equipment investment may have expanded at roughly a 20% annualised rate in Q3 — an estimate, not an official GDP result.

03

South Korea — Semiconductor Leadership

SK Hynix results

SK Hynix reported revenue of ₩104.6 trillion, operating profit of ₩83.3 trillion, an operating margin of approximately 80%, sequential revenue growth of 32% and operating-profit growth of 38%. It announced shareholder-return plans totalling about ₩100 trillion during 2026. The asymmetry is stark: suppliers of critical AI inputs enjoy exceptional pricing power while data-centre financiers face borrowing costs, depreciation and utilisation risk.

04

Infrastructure and Energy

Selectivity and risk premium

Oracle announced an enlarged semiconductor ecosystem incorporating Nvidia systems alongside Google, Cerebras and SambaNova; Argentum AI was reported negotiating roughly $5 billion in computing financing (unconfirmed). On energy, Brent traded at $115.36, up 1.3%, at about 01:40 UTC on October 28 after an unconfirmed Houthi claim of a strike on a Saudi refinery — a negative short-term Smart Capital risk for energy importers including Germany, Italy, Japan and South Korea.

05

The Hidden Pattern — The Bottleneck Is Moving

Causal hypothesis

The most consequential development is that the financing bottleneck is shifting from the production of intelligence toward its distribution throughout the productive economy. Intelligence production accelerates while productive diffusion becomes more financially selective. Firms with strong balance sheets can self-finance AI deployment; bank-dependent firms may postpone it, and the resulting productivity gap can become cumulative. This is not yet a verified economy-wide outcome — it is the most consequential causal hypothesis emerging from today's evidence.

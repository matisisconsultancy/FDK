The Self-tightening Supercycle

The Fed has hiked to 3.75–4.00% and the 10-year closed above 5%, yet ten banks are assembling a $22B chip-backed loan — the AI Supercycle is now large enough to raise its own hurdle rate.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 17, 2026 10 min read Macro

~5.02%U.S. 10Y Treasury (Wed close) — the global hurdle rate stays above threshold

“Velocity still creates advantage; cash flow now decides whether velocity survives its own success.”

The Federal Reserve has raised rates to 3.75–4.00%, the U.S. 10-year closed above 5%, and policymakers now project another increase before year-end. Yet, almost simultaneously, ten banks are assembling a $22 billion loan against AI chips and customer contracts; Blackstone has already committed $5 billion of equity to the same platform; Huawei says its next architecture can connect as many as one million processors; and Europe is committing tens of billions to grids and sovereign compute.

The AI Supercycle is not ending. It is becoming large enough to raise its own hurdle rate. Data cut: 07:45 CEST.

The paradox is becoming the defining economic mechanism of the Next Economy: AI investment → productivity → capital demand → energy demand → inflation pressure → higher rates → higher AI hurdle rate.

The Supercycle is beginning to tax itself.

The Fundamental Question

02

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

the mechanics of the paradox

  1. The Fed raised rates 25 basis points and expects one further hike before year-end, with updated forecasts putting 2026 PCE inflation at 3.7% and GDP growth at 2.3%. Reuters identified the AI investment boom, tariffs and the energy shock among the forces sustaining price pressure.

    AI has moved from being a sector influenced by monetary policy to becoming a macroeconomic force capable of influencing it. AI infrastructure consumes capital, electricity, construction capacity, transformers, land, network equipment, skilled labour and increasingly sovereign balance-sheet capacity — and at sufficient scale, those demands affect economy-wide prices. This does not kill the cycle; it raises the quality threshold. signal: The Supercycle now creates an endogenous feedback loop that raises its own required return.

  2. A consortium of ten banks including Goldman Sachs, SMBC, Barclays, BNP Paribas and Bank of Nova Scotia is arranging $22 billion for Crux AI. Blackstone has committed $5 billion in equity; Google contributes TPUs, software and services. The debt is expected to be secured by chips and customer contracts and may later be refinanced in the investment-grade bond market.

    The build-out is evolving from venture capital and corporate capex toward project finance → bank syndication → investment-grade securitisation. The crucial innovation is not the size but the collateral structure: chips plus contracted customers plus operating compute. AI capacity is beginning to resemble aircraft finance, telecom towers, power plants and infrastructure concessions. signal: The next phase of the Supercycle may be financed through the global credit system, not only equity markets.

  3. Huawei will introduce the 960DT in Q1 2027 and the Ascend 960PR in Q3 2027. Its UnifiedBus architecture is designed to connect very large numbers of processors — with planned superclusters supporting up to one million. It has shipped more than 1,000 supernodes to over 370 customers and reports 5,270 monthly active developers in its AI-chip ecosystem.

    If a single domestic accelerator is less capable than the most advanced Nvidia device, system engineering can partially offset the gap through more processors, better interconnect, large-scale parallelisation, software optimisation and domestically controlled supply. The metric shifts from maximum performance per chip toward maximum useful intelligence per controlled system. signal: The competitive metric becomes usable compute × scale × sovereign control.

  4. Brent has eased to $105.64, helped by Saudi cargoes routed through Oman, but only three commodity vessels crossed Hormuz Wednesday versus a 10-day average near 17. Asian spot LNG has more than doubled to about $26/mmBtu. Analysts expect Asian LNG demand to fall 3–10% this year, with Chinese imports alone declining roughly 6.1 million tonnes; energy-intensive ceramics, methanol and glass producers have cut output because fuel costs are uneconomic.

    Phase I was higher oil into higher inflation. Phase II is higher energy into lower industrial output. The shock is now redistributing industrial competitiveness toward countries with nuclear, domestic gas, coal fallback, renewables-plus-storage or diversified pipelines. signal: Energy resilience is becoming an explicit factor of production.

  5. Spain will lift transmission-network investment to more than €17 billion through 2030, roughly 30% above its previous plan, driven partly by data-centre demand. Cohere and Germany's Aleph Alpha have agreed to combine in a business valued around $20 billion; Schwarz Group will invest €500 million in the company and €11–13 billion in a German compute campus designed for up to 100,000 AI chips.

    Europe's real weakness has never been only the absence of a frontier model — it is the missing chain between capital, compute, energy, enterprise deployment and industrial adoption. This week the pieces begin to connect: grid → compute → sovereign cloud → enterprise AI → industrial productivity. signal: A more plausible European architecture than reproducing Silicon Valley model-for-model.

03

Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines

what the tape does not say

01

The Supercycle is creating its own cost of capital

Technology cycles normally benefit from falling input costs. This one pushes demand upward simultaneously for money, power, chips, construction and networks — raising the price of nearly every factor required to expand AI itself. Velocity creates scarcity; scarcity raises the cost of velocity. Firms that break the loop through efficiency become disproportionately valuable.

02

From "AI exposure" to financeable AI assets

Equity markets rewarded the idea of AI capacity; credit markets increasingly demand proof. The $22 billion Crux financing changes the critical questions — not how many GPUs you own, but who contracted the capacity, at what utilisation, at what power price, for how long, against what collateral. That is the transition from AI narrative to AI yield.

03

Capital formation is becoming geopolitical infrastructure

South Korea's trade agreement with Washington includes a $350 billion investment commitment into U.S. manufacturing, with $150 billion for shipbuilding. Projects reportedly include nuclear reactors and a 6.3-GW Texas gas plant valued around $22 billion to serve AI data-centre demand. This is not conventional FDI — it is geoeconomic capital allocation, with trade access bundled to strategic technology and sovereign co-investment.

04

Sovereign capital follows platforms, not just technology

India's National Stock Exchange opened its $2.3 billion IPO this morning after allocating roughly $703 million to anchor investors including the sovereign wealth funds of Norway and Abu Dhabi. Sovereign capital increasingly wants exposure to the market infrastructure through which future capital flows. Exchanges, grids, data centres and networks are becoming control points.

05

Mobility enters the age of capital selection

Volkswagen is reviewing Seat's future; the brand was under 3% of 2025 global deliveries, has launched no new model since 2020 and has no full EV planned — sister brand Cupra overtook it last year. Across European, U.S., Japanese and South Korean automakers, annual sales fell 12.6 million vehicles (17%) between 2019 and 2025. Capital is too expensive to support every inherited brand; strategic neglect now compounds faster than ever.

Key takeaways

the causal chain has changed

  • Intelligence is not produced in an abstract cloud — it requires capital, energy, semiconductors, networks, land, grid capacity, construction and credit, and every input has a price.
  • At small scale AI consumes those resources; at industrial scale it begins to reprice them — the Supercycle is becoming macroeconomic, and thus exposed to the cost of capital.
  • Crux answers financially: turn chips into collateral, demand into contracts, compute into an infrastructure asset, capacity into bankable yield.
  • Huawei answers technologically, Spain physically, Europe institutionally, Volkswagen strategically — every system converging on one rule: scarcity forces selection.
  • The split sharpens into Self-Financing Velocity, which can compound through 5% money, versus Rate-Sensitive Scale, which needs the market to become generous again.

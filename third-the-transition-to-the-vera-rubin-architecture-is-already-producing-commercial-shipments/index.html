Third, the Transition to the Vera Rubin Architecture Is Already Producing Commercial Shipments.

Nvidia's $115.8B quarter confirms the AI Supercycle's earnings power, but the next capital rotation will reward whoever can sustain the returns.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann November 18, 2026 4 min read Capital

  2. Free cash flow reached $43.8B and $28.1B was returned to shareholders through dividends and repurchases.

The AI Supercycle has delivered its most important financial test of November. Demand is not collapsing. Profitability is extraordinary at the technological frontier. But the distribution of financial returns across the AI economy is becoming increasingly uneven.

Nvidia's November 17 results — its first quarterly release following Jensen Huang's resignation — provide powerful new evidence of that asymmetry.

The next phase of the AI-native economy will be determined by the transmission of economic value from the companies supplying intelligence to the companies financing, operating and ultimately using it.

01

Nvidia: The Financial Engine Remains Exceptionally Strong

Earnings

GAAP operating income was $77.5 billion at a 74.8% gross margin, and the Vera Rubin platform has entered production shipments. Interim CEO Colette Kress raised the fiscal 2028 revenue-growth outlook to roughly 75%, from 70% — an ambitious projection, not a guarantee.

The governance shock has not yet translated into any verified deterioration in revenues or deliveries. But the results largely predate Huang's departure; they cannot establish that future strategy and customer relationships remain unaffected.

Q4 guidance assumes no China Data Centre compute revenue — a forecasting assumption, not evidence that shipments have stopped. The projected margin decline may reflect product mix and the transition to new systems, not competitive pricing pressure; absolute gross profit could still rise.

02

The Great Split: The $43.8 Billion Cash-Flow Contrast

Capital

Nvidia's result should be read alongside the Federal Reserve's November 13 Financial Stability Report, which estimated advanced-computing debt issuance above $680 billion through October and outstanding public debt above $1.1 trillion — different measures that cannot be added together.

The contrast is financial, not only technological. One part of the chain generates present cash flows from equipment sales; another must earn future cash flows sufficient to justify those same purchases. Suppliers monetize scarce technology today; many customers invest against revenues expected over several years.

The AI Supercycle should increasingly be judged through financial transmission, not aggregate investment: free cash flow, customer concentration, debt-service coverage, utilization and incremental returns on capital.

03

The $1 Billion Lease Is a More Useful Signal

Energy

On November 17, Giga Energy announced a ten-year, roughly $1 billion lease with an undisclosed US AI cloud provider for a 60 MW facility in Hunt County, Texas, targeted for spring 2027. A signed long-duration lease is stronger commercial evidence than an unfinanced capacity announcement — but distinct from an operating, cash-generating facility. The implied ~$100 million per year says nothing conclusive about financing costs, construction spend or eventual operating profit.

04

Europe: Applications Shown, Macro Test Still Open

Industrial AI

Europe's Apply AI Summit concluded on November 17, with inaugural Startup Awards to Better Medicine (Estonia), Prewave (Austria) and blueOASIS (Portugal) — recognition, not new funding. The European Investment Bank's November 9 survey remains the macro baseline: 67% of EU firms use generative AI, matching the US rate, but only about 21% report benefits already materializing, and 20% adopted innovations new to the company versus 44% in the US.

EuroHPC procurement milestones on November 25 and December 3 will test whether strategic infrastructure moves toward executable projects. No new Gigafactory award has been verified.

Five Key Takeaways

For investors and boards

  • The AI Supercycle remains commercially powerful: Nvidia confirms extraordinary demand, revenue growth and supplier profitability.
  • The Great Split is becoming more consequential: cash-generating suppliers and capital-intensive operators occupy different financial positions.
  • Commercial contracts now matter as much as physical capacity, as the $1B Giga Energy lease shows — but implementation and profitability are separate tests.
  • Europe's opportunity lies in industrial deployment, where adoption and realized benefits are not yet equivalent.
  • The next rotation depends on the durability of returns: distinguish scarcity profits, technological advantage, contractual cash flows and genuine productivity.

Nvidia has demonstrated the extraordinary profits available from building intelligence. The next great capital rotation will reward the companies—and the economies—that turn intelligence into enduring productivity, cash flow and financial returns.

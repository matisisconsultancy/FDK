The Velocity Edge — The Morning View | August 6, 2026 | FDK

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 5, 2026 3 min read Macro · Capital

AI Is Growing. Jobs Are Not. The Great Split is no longer between equities and bonds. It is now between productivity and employment, profits and wages, capital and labor. Data cut: 07:45 CEST The overnight evidence points to a more consequential phase of the AI Supercycle. The US services economy remains strong. New orders are accelerating. European companies are reporting record industrial profits, expanding cloud backlogs and stronger demand for enterprise AI deployment. But employment is no longer moving at the same velocity. The US services employment index has fallen back into contraction. Private payroll creation slowed sharply in July. Some businesses are already attributing reductions in headcount to AI implementation. At the same time, Asian semiconductor shares are selling off despite extraordinary underlying demand, while investors continue to question whether even strong AI revenues can justify the capital intensity, duration and valuations required to produce them. This is the next expression of The Great Split: Output is accelerating faster than employment. Profits are accelerating faster than payrolls. Intelligence is becoming more abundant while financial and social participation in its gains becomes more selective. ⸻ The Fundamental Question What if the AI Supercycle is already increasing output, profits and productivity faster than employment, purchasing power and the financial system’s capacity to distribute its benefits? The market is no longer debating whether AI will produce economic value. US services activity remained robust, but the combination of higher new orders, rising prices and contracting employment reveals a striking internal divergence. Asian technology markets moved sharply lower, while oil remained below $80 and the ten-year Treasury yield held near 4.61%. ⸻ Five Signals Shaping the Day Ahead The AI Productivity Shock Is Appearing in the Labor Market Verified facts The US services PMI held at 54.1 in July, while new orders rose to 57.2. Yet the employment component fell from 51.2 to 47.4, indicating contraction. ADP reported that private employers added only 44,000 jobs, down from 95,000 in June and below the 70,000 consensus forecast. The ISM reported that some companies were reducing employment alongside AI implementation. Businesses also described strong demand for electrical equipment, longer delivery times and increasing financial stress among smaller suppliers. FDK interpretation This may be the first clear macroeconomic expression of the AI productivity cycle: stronger output; higher orders; fewer incremental workers. AI is beginning to separate business growth from employment growth. That is positive for operating margins and productivity. It is more ambiguous for wage formation, household income and the breadth of consumer demand.

The Illusion of Relief

Oil has retreated below $100 and European equities are rebounding — but Hormuz remains throttled, Houthi disruption is embedded in shipping costs, and capital is rotating from AI promises toward infrastructure cash flows.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann July 24, 2026 8 min read Markets
SAP +6.2%Enterprise AI and cloud execution rewarded
Brent $96.70 · −4.0%Price relief, not route normalisation
Brent weekly +9.7%Energy shock remains embedded
Hormuz transits: 3/dayPhysical supply remains constrained

At midday, risk assets are attempting to stabilise after Thursday's technology-led sell-off. The STOXX Europe 600 is up 0.5% at 642.62, SAP has gained 6.2%, and U.S. equity futures point modestly higher. Brent crude has fallen almost 4% to approximately $96.70, reversing part of yesterday's surge beyond $100.

Yet the structural risks have not disappeared. Daily tanker transits through the Strait of Hormuz remain at only three, the Houthis continue threatening Saudi maritime flows through Bab el-Mandeb, and new U.S. tariffs of 10% and 12.5% now apply to imports from 60 trading partners. The ECB has kept its deposit rate at 2.25%, while markets continue to price further tightening. The market is rebounding. The operating environment is not normalising.

Verified developments are cited. GVI readings, hidden patterns and strategic implications are proprietary FDK analytical interpretations.

The fundamental question

Are markets buying a genuine recovery — or merely trading a temporary reduction in the price of disruption?

01

Five signals shaping the next economy

Tap each signal to expand the read.

  1. European shares are recovering from their steepest one-day decline in two weeks, led by SAP and selected earnings winners. SAP's 6.2% rise has helped lift Germany's DAX, while the broader European technology sector has gained 1.4%. Valmet has surged approximately 20% following stronger results and a potential business separation. The dispersion remains severe. Securitas has fallen 11%, Neste is down approximately 10%, and Volkswagen has abandoned its revenue-growth forecast after second-quarter profit declined 9.5%.

    FDK Interpretation: This is not a broad risk-on recovery. Capital is rewarding companies able to demonstrate contracted digital demand, operational execution, portfolio optionality and measurable cash conversion. It is penalising weak margins, legacy capacity and transformation without results. The Great Split is no longer between technology and traditional industry. It is between execution and explanation.

    Signal This is not a broad risk-on recovery — it is a split between execution and explanation.

  2. Brent has fallen to approximately $96.70, but it remains on course for a 9.7% weekly increase. WTI is near $89.04 and is heading for an increase of almost 8% this week. The Houthis have claimed attacks on two Saudi oil tankers and declared a naval blockade against Saudi Arabia. Hormuz traffic remains severely subdued, while Washington has threatened major military retaliation against Iran and its Houthi allies. The logistical cost is already material. Routing Saudi crude to Asia around Africa can increase a journey from 19 days to 48 days, lift fuel costs from approximately $1.26 million to $2.87 million, and add roughly $1 million in Suez Canal fees.

    FDK Interpretation: The price of crude has retreated. The price of global connectivity has not. Longer voyages require more vessels, more working capital, more fuel and more insurance. The shock is migrating from the oil price into the entire physical architecture of trade. Hormuz sets the energy price. Bab el-Mandeb increasingly sets the delivery price.

    Signal The price of crude has retreated; the price of global connectivity has not.

  3. U.S. futures have stabilised, with Dow futures up 0.53%, S&P 500 futures up 0.29% and Nasdaq futures up 0.26%. Intel has risen 5.3% in pre-market trading after issuing stronger-than-expected guidance and outlining higher investment plans. Oracle has gained 3.2% after securing a Pentagon agreement worth almost $7 billion over as many as ten years. By contrast, Alphabet and Tesla remain under scrutiny following Thursday's sell-off, driven by concerns about AI capital expenditure and negative free cash flow.

    FDK Interpretation: The AI Supercycle remains intact, but its internal leadership is changing. Capital is moving toward corporations that receive payment earlier in the investment cycle: Processors → cloud infrastructure → enterprise systems → power → secure government platforms. It is becoming less tolerant of businesses whose returns depend on distant monetisation. The afternoon test is whether Intel and Oracle retain their gains while the hyperscalers remain under pressure. That would confirm a durable rotation from AI ambition to AI infrastructure revenue.

    Signal Capital is rotating from AI ambition toward AI infrastructure revenue.

  4. The United States has imposed tariffs of 10% and 12.5% on goods from 60 trading partners, including the European Union and China. The European Commission has offered a guarded welcome because the measures broadly conform to the bilateral framework agreed last year and preserve selected exemptions for aircraft, medicines and other products.

    FDK Interpretation: The absence of immediate retaliation should not be confused with the absence of economic consequences. Tariffs increasingly function as an industrial-policy instrument, a supply-chain localisation incentive, a permanent working-capital cost and a tax on cross-border complexity. The winners will be companies with regional production, local suppliers and pricing power. The laggards will be corporations still designed around frictionless globalisation. Trade barriers are no longer exceptional events. They are becoming part of corporate architecture.

    Signal Tariffs are no longer exceptional events — they are becoming part of corporate architecture.

  5. The ECB kept its deposit rate at 2.25%, but said the full inflationary impact of the energy shock had yet to emerge. Markets are pricing a 25-basis-point increase and approximately a 70% probability of another similar move before year-end. The Federal Reserve meets next week, and markets now assign roughly a one-in-three probability to a rate increase — up from 12% one week earlier.

    FDK Interpretation: The policy put beneath risk assets is weakening. Central banks face a shock they cannot solve by adjusting demand alone: oil-route disruption, tariffs, infrastructure scarcity, defence expenditure and AI-related electricity demand. This environment strengthens the negotiating power of sovereign wealth funds, public investment institutions and capital-rich governments capable of financing long-duration infrastructure through volatility. When private discount rates rise, patient sovereign capital becomes more strategically valuable.

    Signal The policy put beneath risk assets is weakening as sovereign capital gains leverage.

02

Hidden patterns beyond the headlines

The structure underneath the tape.

01

Price Relief Is Not System Relief

Brent below $100 creates immediate market comfort. But tanker availability, journey duration, insurance costs and working-capital requirements remain structurally impaired. The market is trading the commodity price. Companies must manage the logistics system.

02

AI Leadership Is Becoming a Cash-Flow Sequence

The first beneficiaries are those selling processors, cloud capacity, enterprise infrastructure, power and secure systems. The final beneficiaries must prove they can monetise the intelligence built on top. Who gets paid first is becoming as important as who creates the most innovation.

03

Sovereign Demand Is Becoming the New Revenue Backlog

Oracle's Pentagon agreement illustrates a broader movement from public policy into contracted corporate revenue. Defence, energy, digital infrastructure and semiconductors are increasingly supported by state procurement, sovereign capital and strategic financing. The line separating corporate strategy from national strategy is disappearing.

04

The Great Split Is Becoming a Resilience Split

The market increasingly divides between Accelerating — infrastructure suppliers, enterprise AI platforms, defence and secure cloud, regional manufacturers and companies with contracted revenues — and Decelerating: fuel-sensitive mobility, legacy automotive, low-margin global supply chains, capital-intensive businesses without pricing power and companies financing transformation through weakening cash flow.

05

Europe's Opportunity Is Industrial, Not Cyclical

Europe's midday rebound is being led by enterprise software and selected industrial execution — not by a broad consumer recovery. The durable European opportunity remains concentrated in enterprise systems, semiconductor equipment, electricity infrastructure, defence, automation and sovereign-grade financial platforms.

03

Midday Velocity Reading: 89/100 — HIGH, REBOUNDING WITHOUT NORMALISING

FDK proprietary analytical reading.

Innovation velocity: Very high. Infrastructure velocity: Accelerating. Capital-allocation velocity: Rotating upstream. Market breadth: Weak. Energy risk: Extreme. Trade fragmentation: Accelerating. Policy support: Diminishing. Sovereign-capital relevance: Rising.

The system remains highly dynamic, but velocity is concentrating around fewer corporations, scarcer assets and stronger balance sheets.

What to watch during the afternoon

Into the close

  • The Wall Street opening: whether Intel, Oracle and infrastructure suppliers outperform while Alphabet, Tesla and other capital-intensive AI spenders remain under pressure.
  • Brent's $96–$100 range: a renewed break above $100 would quickly revive inflation and rate concerns, while a deeper retreat would require evidence of improving maritime flows — not merely profit-taking.
  • Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb traffic: only three daily Hormuz tanker transits indicate physical conditions remain far from normal, and further Houthi action would immediately challenge the market's midday calm.
  • Preliminary U.S. PMI data: the July manufacturing and services readings are due at 9:45 a.m. Eastern time, and weak growth combined with high input prices would reinforce the stagflation narrative.
  • Europe's closing breadth: whether SAP's rally broadens into semiconductors and industrial technology, or the market closes with a small group of earnings winners masking underlying weakness.
  • Tariff responses: additional exemptions would favour global industrials, while retaliation or new investigations would increase pressure on automotive, consumer goods and complex cross-border supply chains.

The midday rebound is real. The relief behind it is conditional. Oil has fallen below $100, but two strategic sea lanes remain impaired. Technology has stabilised, but investors are no longer financing AI ambition without demanding cash conversion. Europe is recovering, but only a narrow group of corporate winners is carrying the market. The Next Economy is not slowing down. It is becoming more expensive, more physical and more dependent on sovereign power. Capital is moving toward those that control the infrastructure, receive the spending and survive the disruption. It is moving away from those still dependent on cheap energy, frictionless trade and unlimited patience. The price of risk has fallen this morning. The price of resilience has not.

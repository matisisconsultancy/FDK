The Disclosure Test

The Italian Banking Risiko is moving from strategic imagination to regulatory evidence — capital is available, but the scarce asset is now executable legitimacy.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 23, 2026 8 min read Capital
-1.7%Banco BPM close
-3.3%Generali, to €44.26
+1.46%Banca Generali, to €65.95

“Capital allocation requires not only money — but procedural legitimacy.”

The most consequential development is no longer another takeover rumor. It is Consob.

Italian reporting — not yet confirmed by the regulator — says Consob has widened its examination of MPS beyond the economics of the Banco BPM and Banca Generali counter-offers, requesting communications between certain directors and the bank and examining whether the board and the market received complete information on time, with particular attention to the Generali relationship.

That matters because the 29 October MPS shareholder meeting is no longer simply a referendum on whether Luigi Lovaglio can build a third banking pole. It is becoming a test of whether the defensive architecture is legally clean, procedurally robust and executable after a possible change of control.

THE MARKET HAS MOVED FROM PRICING ALLIANCE OPTIONALITY TO DEMANDING PROOF OF EXECUTION.

01

What Changed In The Last 24 Hours

The last session's evidence

  1. According to reporting cited by Alliance News, Consob has sought additional material concerning communications between MPS directors and the bank, following earlier requests on the Banco BPM and Banca Generali offers, the passivity rule and the relationship with Generali. This remains reported regulatory activity, not a new public ruling. The question is shifting from are MPS's counter-offers strategically credible? to are they procedurally unassailable? An Intesa takeover of MPS could occur before completion of the counter-offers; MPS says the offers stay irrevocable after a change of control, Intesa challenges that interpretation. signal: The battleground is now procedural legitimacy, not strategic credibility.

  2. Mediobanca disclosed another small purchase of Banco BPM shares on 22 September: 55,000 shares at an average €16.2817. Economically immaterial and not stake-building, but it confirms the MPS offer remains operationally active rather than merely rhetorical. signal: The signal is executional, not financial.

  3. Saxo Bank completed the acquisition of Banca Generali's remaining 49% of BG Saxo, becoming sole owner and renaming the company Saxo Italia. The transaction implements a prior agreement, so it is not a response to the MPS bid — but it leaves Banca Generali with a marginally cleaner perimeter at exactly the moment its ownership is under strategic review. signal: A simpler perimeter modestly reduces transaction complexity.

  4. Banco BPM's 4.3% Monday rally on speculation of a possible UniCredit–Crédit Agricole arrangement partially reversed yesterday. The lack of public confirmation is beginning to matter. signal: Show us the alliance — or give back the premium.

02

The Decisive Issue

The passivity rule becomes the real battleground

THE MPS DEFENCE BECOMES STRONGER IF IT BECOMES IRREVOCABLE — BUT THE MORE IRREVOCABLE IT BECOMES, THE MORE REGULATORY SCRUTINY MATTERS.

03

Updated Strategic Map

Position by position

Strong OW

Intesa Sanpaolo

The offer remains straightforward: 1.6 Intesa shares plus €1 cash per MPS share, conditional on reaching at least 66.67% (subject to waiver), with substantial branch disposals already designed. Every additional procedural question around MPS's defence increases the relative value of Intesa's simpler architecture. The advantage is no longer only capital strength — it is transactional simplicity.

High convexity

MPS

A credible industrial thesis survives: MPS + Mediobanca + Banco BPM + Banca Generali, with exchange ratios of 1.567 MPS per Banco BPM share and 6.958 per Banca Generali share. But the reported Consob inquiry adds a fourth risk layer — procedural risk — on top of industrial, shareholder and execution risk.

Event OW

Banco BPM

Closed Tuesday at €16.09, down 1.74%, but still 2.38% above last Friday's official level: the Monday rumor premium has not disappeared. Three strategic destinations remain — MPS, Crédit Agricole, or a negotiated structure potentially involving UniCredit. That creates scarcity.

Strategic OW

Crédit Agricole

Controls 29.3% of Banco BPM. Press reports carried by Reuters said the French bank found the MPS proposal unattractive and preferred a Banco BPM–Crédit Agricole Italia combination; neither confirmed. The rumored UniCredit dialogue is unconfirmed. Its greatest asset remains the ability not to choose yet.

Overweight

UniCredit

Shareholders formally approved up to €5bn of convertible AT1 instruments (97.39%) and up to 10.603m new shares linked to Commerzbank Total Return Swaps (97.70%). This gives Andrea Orcel flexibility across two fronts: Germany — converting Commerzbank ownership into governance (real) — and Italy — preserving optionality around Banco BPM (speculation).

Overweight

Generali

Fell 3.3% to €44.26 within a weak session, with no public evidence tying the move to a transaction. It remains simultaneously an MPS/Mediobanca strategic asset, controlling shareholder of Banca Generali, an antitrust variable for Intesa, and central to MPS's proposed €4bn extraordinary distribution. Do not confuse a one-day drawdown with reduced strategic scarcity.

Event +

Banca Generali

The BG Saxo disposal marginally simplifies the perimeter; the stock closed at €65.95, +1.46%. This now matters because its transaction spread is materially wider than Banco BPM's — improving relative asymmetry.

Event-driven

Commerzbank

Germany has moved from preventing UniCredit's advance toward negotiating safeguards. Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil has told UniCredit the bank should stay listed, headquartered in Frankfurt and committed to the Mittelstand. The debate is no longer whether UniCredit can own Commerzbank, but what price in governance it must pay to control it. Ownership has advanced further than governance.

04

Capital-Markets Reaction And Valuation

Three signals from the close

05

Three Scenarios

Probability-weighted outcomes

40%

Continental Bifurcation

Unchanged. Intesa completes MPS after acceptable competition remedies; UniCredit converts Commerzbank ownership into negotiated control; Banco BPM eventually moves toward Crédit Agricole or another negotiated architecture; Generali remains independent. The cleanest structural outcome.

24%

MPS Coalition Third Pole

Up 1 point. MPS obtains the 29 October mandate and proceeds with Banco BPM and Banca Generali. Operational execution continues and Banca Generali's perimeter has modestly simplified — but Crédit Agricole resistance and the reported Consob scrutiny remain substantial obstacles.

36%

Negotiated Realignment

Down 1 point. Existing offers force a multi-party settlement combining Intesa–MPS, Crédit Agricole–Banco BPM, UniCredit–Commerzbank and an independent Generali. Probability slips because the reported UniCredit–Crédit Agricole dialogue has still produced no public confirmation and part of Monday's premium reversed.

06

The Hidden Pattern

A battle over capital-allocation legitimacy

What To Watch

Next 24–72 hours

  • Consob first — any official confirmation or further disclosure request on board communications, Generali and the passivity rule immediately changes MPS execution risk.
  • MPS second — whether it responds publicly to the reported widening and whether Mediobanca continues disclosed Banco BPM purchases.
  • Crédit Agricole–UniCredit third — Monday's report now needs evidence; a denial removes BPM optionality, confirmation transforms the map.
  • Banco BPM fourth — €16 is the critical reference; a move above Monday's highs without a catalyst signals investors expect a competitive process.
  • Banca Generali fifth — the BG Saxo disposal is complete; the next event is Generali's formal strategic stance on MPS's proposal.
  • Berlin sixth — any concrete Commerzbank agreement on headquarters, listing, employment and supervisory-board governance moves UniCredit from economic toward executable control.

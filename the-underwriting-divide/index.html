The Underwriting Divide

Oil's retreat, Alphabet's bond demand and a $115B AI merger reveal that capital is still abundant — but its allocation is becoming increasingly selective.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann November 2, 2026 5 min read Capital
€17B+investor demand for Alphabet's €6B bond
$115BMarvell–Lumentum all-stock acquisition
−26%Alstom shares after its guidance cut

“In the Age of Velocity, the ultimate advantage is not accelerating investment — it is accelerating returns.”

The signal1 min
  1. Capital is repricing execution, not withdrawing: it is abundant but allocated with growing discrimination across borrowers and countries.
  2. Alphabet drew €17B+ against a €6B bond (2.8x cover), while Oracle fell 6% on a $20B raise — financial credibility now decides terms.
  3. Marvell's $115B Lumentum deal was met with skepticism: a strategically attractive market does not justify the price paid to enter it.
  4. Alstom collapsed 26% after cutting margin guidance to ~1% and warning of €1.5B negative free cash flow — execution is now a valuation variable.
  5. Brent fell ~7% toward $105–106, easing inflation and lifting equities, but one session does not establish a durable change.
  6. The ECB flagged AI valuations, sovereign-debt stress and non-bank fund losses as interconnected stability risks ahead of its November 18 review.
Read the full note ↓

November 2 reveals a fundamental shift in global financial markets: capital is still available in extraordinary quantities, but investors are becoming increasingly selective about who receives it, at what price, and against which future returns.

Alphabet drew more than €17 billion of demand for a €6 billion bond. Oracle announced a $20 billion raise and fell almost 6%. Marvell unveiled a $115 billion acquisition of Lumentum — only to meet a negative reaction. Alstom lost about 26% after a €1.5 billion cash-outflow warning. All of this as Brent fell more than 7% on hopes of de-escalation involving Iran.

The same markets financing unprecedented technological expansion are increasingly punishing companies unable to demonstrate credible returns, disciplined execution and financial resilience. The Next Economy is not facing a shortage of capital — it is facing a widening difference in the conditions under which capital can be mobilized.

01

The Five Signals

Capital repricing execution

  1. Brent fell roughly 7–8% toward $105–106 on diplomatic activity involving Iran and Qatari mediators. The STOXX Europe 600 gained 0.42% to 649.44 and Spain's IBEX rose 1.63%, as travel shares advanced ~3% and energy fell ~2.3%. Lower energy risk lowers expected inflation, easing rate pressure — but one session is not a durable change. signal: Energy relief helps; it does not eliminate the underlying risk premium.

  2. Alphabet's €6 billion bond drew over €17 billion of demand (2.8x cover); 2026 capex could reach $205 billion, plus €130.5 billion of future lease payments. Oracle planned a $20 billion raise and fell almost 6%, carrying ~$125.34 billion of borrowings with free cash flow turning positive only in H2 fiscal 2028. signal: The question is which firms can raise capital without undermining long-term flexibility.

  3. Marvell's all-stock Lumentum deal is valued at ~$115 billion including debt — equity near $102.5 billion, a 23.9% premium, expanding the addressable market to ~$550 billion, closing H1 2028. Optical networking is a critical AI layer, but the reaction was negative on integration risk and dilution. signal: Scale acquired at the wrong price destroys shareholder value.

  4. Shares fell ~26% after guidance was cut: full-year adjusted operating margin to ~1% (from 6.5%), free cash flow of −€1.5 billion, H1 margin near −4%. Six Coradia Max contracts worth ~€5 billion were identified as loss-making, with first certification now expected in 2028. signal: Engineering delays consume cash precisely when financing costs rise.

  5. U.S. ISM Manufacturing rose to 55.5 with prices paid at 75.2 — expansion with persistent inflation. The Eurozone PMI eased to 52.7 while Italy fell to 49.4, back in contraction. ECB Vice-President Boris Vujčić named AI valuations, bond-market stress and non-bank losses as interconnected risks; the Financial Stability Review lands November 18. signal: Market stress, fiscal constraints and industrial weakness can now reinforce one another.

The hidden pattern of November 2 is that financial resilience is becoming a prerequisite for technological velocity — not merely a consequence of it.

02

Where Capital Is Moving

From financing growth to underwriting returns

Hyperscaler capex could approach $800 billion in 2026 and $1.2 trillion in 2027, on Goldman Sachs estimates. Anthropic reported $16.96 billion in Q3 revenue, a ~$567 million operating loss, and disclosed roughly $895.4 billion in computing commitments — about $447.5 billion before 2031. The decisive measure is economic profit: (ROIC − WACC) × Invested Capital. For a firm with €10 billion of invested capital, a one-point ROIC gain yields ~€100 million in annual profit. For Europe, the disadvantage is not a shortage of capital but the fragmentation of the mechanisms that turn capital into productive investment.

03

The Next 24 Hours

Three conditional paths

45%

The recovery broadens

Oil stays near lower levels, Apple delivers credible guidance, credit stays orderly, and cyclicals benefit from reduced energy pressure.

35%

Selective capital rotation

Tech valuations stay differentiated; high-quality borrowers retain access; investors favor reliable cash generation.

20%

The financing shock returns

Oil rebounds, inflation expectations rise, AI credit spreads widen, and investors cut exposure to capital-intensive companies.

Five Strategic Takeaways

For investors, boards and policymakers

  • Capital markets remain open, but financing terms are increasingly differentiated — Alphabet's demand and Oracle's reaction show credibility matters.
  • The next AI frontier moves deeper into infrastructure: optical networking, power, cooling and compute efficiency now determine value.
  • Industrial execution is becoming a central valuation variable, as Alstom's collapse demonstrates.
  • Energy normalization could deliver a financial dividend — if the geopolitical improvement proves durable.
  • Europe's opportunity lies in productive capital, but only with credible execution and discipline.

The world is not running out of capital — it is becoming more demanding about what capital must accomplish. Markets will continue financing the future; they will no longer finance every version of it at the same price.

The Global Velocity Index holds at 108.0, but the real story is a shift from maximum compute toward maximum productive output from scarce physical resources.

“Not maximum compute — maximum productive output from scarce physical resources.”

The provisional FDK Global Velocity Index remains at 108.0 on 22 September. The unrounded movement is +0.02, reported as unchanged, at 96% model confidence. No country score clears all four admission tests today: materiality × additionality × persistence × confidence.

The most important change is in Smart Capital. Brent is around $100.57, after briefly falling below $100 yesterday, while Treasury yields have retreated from recent highs. Asian technology stocks are responding strongly: South Korea is up nearly 2% in early trading, Chinese AI shares are higher, and yesterday the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index jumped 4.3% while the Nasdaq reached a record.

The AI Supercycle remains intact. The immediate financial constraint is easing—but the physical constraint is becoming more important.

The relevant metric is shifting from installed compute toward productive intelligence per unit of electricity, water and grid capacity.

Today's country signals

Where momentum moves without points

  1. Google and Georgia Power have agreed to finance upgrades at the Vogtle and Hatch nuclear plants that could add approximately 96 MW of generation. The architecture matters more than its national scale: AI demand → hyperscaler contract → nuclear uprate → incremental electricity capacity. This is strong Intelligence Infrastructure evidence, but the 96 MW scale is insufficient for another US country-score increase, and regulatory approval is still required.

    US momentum nevertheless improves at the margin because lower oil and Treasury yields are reducing the immediate financial pressure on AI infrastructure. The Nasdaq's record close and 4.3% semiconductor rally confirm that repricing, but equity prices themselves do not alter the structural GVI. signal: Hyperscalers are moving upstream into generation—but scale and approval still gate the score.

  2. The European Commission has proposed a common efficiency-label system for data centres of at least 500 kW. Operators would report energy and water efficiency, local water stress and their ability to support energy systems through measures such as waste-heat reuse. EU data-centre capacity is roughly 12 GW in 2025, expected to reach around 28 GW by 2030, already consuming approximately 2.5% of EU electricity.

    No national score receives points because this remains an EU-wide proposal. But it introduces an important GVI concept: intelligence measured per unit of electricity, water and grid capacity. signal: Efficiency, not installed compute, becomes the metric that matters.

  3. Spain has outlined a 12-month AI roadmap including a proposed AI gigafactory, new models developed with the Barcelona Supercomputing Centre, and stronger cybersecurity and environmental requirements for data centres. This strengthens Innovation Velocity and Discovery Capacity momentum, but not today's 75.0 score. The projects remain prospective; the GVI requires financing, procurement, construction or measurable operating capacity before recognising them structurally. signal: Ambition is rising; recognition awaits financing and construction.

  4. Brent is trading around $100.57 after briefly moving below $100 yesterday. Saudi Arabia has increased Gulf exports following damage to its East-West pipeline, alleviating immediate fears of a severe supply shortage. That is meaningful improvement—but not yet structural normalisation. Middle East geopolitical risk remains high and the market stays sensitive to potential US-Iran negotiations. signal: The Smart Capital penalty is reduced at the margin, not removed.

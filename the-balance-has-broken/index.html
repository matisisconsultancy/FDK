The Balance Has Broken

Delfin has committed its 17.6% to Intesa's revised offer, moving the Italian banking Risiko from industrial logic to shareholder arithmetic ahead of the October 29 vote.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 5, 2026 6 min read Capital

“The vote is no longer about who has the better story — it is about who has the numbers.”

Delfin has moved. The shareholder arithmetic has tightened. October 29 is now a test of whether MPS can still assemble the coalition required to keep the Lovaglio architecture alive.

The Italian banking Risiko has entered its decisive phase. Delfin — MPS's largest shareholder with roughly 17.6% — has committed its stake to Intesa Sanpaolo's revised offer and aligned itself against the defensive resolutions. That matters more than Intesa's additional €800 million: the contest has moved from industrial logic to shareholder arithmetic.

Intesa has the lead. MPS still has a path — but it has narrowed. October 29 is now a three-trigger test across Banco BPM, Banca Generali and the capital restructuring supporting the defence. Approval of any one can jeopardise Intesa's offer; failure of all three leaves the Intesa path open.

Investor Takeaways

Six reads into October 29

  • Intesa has the advantage — Delfin materially improves the probability of success.
  • Caltagirone is the next pivot — if aligned with Delfin, the required turnout for MPS rises above roughly 83.7%.
  • The three-trigger structure creates non-linear risk — zero approvals favour Intesa; all three preserve MPS; anything in between creates dislocation.
  • Institutional investors now decide the margin — a relatively small additional NO bloc can become decisive.
  • Banco BPM is the next asset to watch — a failed MPS defence immediately reopens the domestic consolidation map.
  • Regulation comes next — if Intesa wins the shareholder battle, ECB and antitrust execution become the central risks.

The Great Split Reaches the Balance Sheet

The AI Supercycle keeps accelerating — but its financing model is changing, and financial capacity is becoming the decisive competitive weapon.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 27, 2026 4 min read Capital

“The Next Economy will be financed before it is won.”

  2. Meta plans up to $95bn in equity for AI, with Nvidia investing $3bn; SpaceX disclosed a ~$40bn GPU financing arrangement.

  6. The Great Split has reached the balance sheet: technological leadership creates the Next Economy; financial capacity increasingly controls it.
Read the full note ↓

Three developments over the past 24 hours signal a fundamental transition in the financial architecture of the Next Economy. On October 26, Anthropic publicly filed for an IPO, revealing $16.29 billion in first-half revenue, an $80.7 billion reported net loss and more than $600 billion in long-term computing commitments.

Meta announced plans to raise up to $95 billion in equity, SpaceX disclosed a ~$40 billion GPU financing arrangement, and Wall Street banks with Blackstone backed a separate $63 billion financing for Broadcom equipment bound for Anthropic. These are different transactions and should not be summed. The decisive competition is no longer for computing capacity — it is for the financial capacity to sustain it at an acceptable return.

01

The Fundamental Question

the next rotation

Is the AI Supercycle an unprecedented expansion of productive capital — or a structure in which technological growth increasingly depends on continuous capital-market access? If AI revenues, utilization and productivity accelerate faster than financing requirements, today's investments underpin a prolonged expansion. If commitments, dilution and borrowing costs rise faster than returns, growth decelerates despite technological progress. The most consequential figure is not the $80.7bn loss — it is the $600bn+ in commitments that future economic activity must support.

02

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

from disclosure to geography

  1. Revenue rose from $386m (2024) to $4.59bn (2025), reaching $16.29bn in H1 2026 versus $1.16bn a year earlier. Operating loss narrowed to $693m, with adjusted operating income of $1.62bn; the $80.7bn net loss largely reflects non-operating convertible-financing effects, not cash outflow. signal: The IPO could set the valuation benchmark for frontier AI — durable returns on capital, not revenue growth, define the next phase.

  2. Funding now spans equity, convertibles, leasing, asset-backed structures and private credit. Nvidia will invest $3bn in Meta's offering, while Bank of America, Citigroup, Morgan Stanley and Blackstone back the $63bn Broadcom financing for Anthropic. signal: Capital access favours dominant platforms; the principal vulnerability lies in firms dependent on refinancing rather than operating cash flow.

  3. Taiwan's central bank sees demand supporting exports into 2027, while the Bank of Korea modeled a downside where reduced US AI spending cuts Korean chip exports by $50–100bn and GDP by ~0.4 point. Applied Materials plans to lift its Besi stake from 9% to ~20%. signal: Advanced packaging is a strategic frontier, but strong demand does not eliminate the risk of a financially induced investment cycle.

  4. Moody's held France at Aa3 (negative); the 10-year eased toward 4.9% and the OAT–Bund spread to just above 150bp. Gas above €97/MWh arrives as the ECB (Oct 29), Fed (Oct 28) and BoJ (Oct 30) meet, all expected to hold. signal: Capital will differentiate European nations by energy availability, fiscal credibility and execution — Spain has eased data-centre rules to compete.

  5. HSBC's Q3 pretax profit rose 32% to $9.6bn, yet its shares fell ~5% on flat $2.7bn wealth revenue. Volkswagen is reportedly weighing Xpeng-developed software across ~75% of next-generation VW, Skoda and Cupra mass-market models. signal: Distinguish revenue growth from economic value — the Next Economy rewards companies that control, not merely purchase, their technology.

The Great Split is no longer simply technological. It is financial, industrial and increasingly institutional.

Key Takeaways for Investors and Boards

where capital moves next

  • Equity: distinguish companies monetizing AI from those primarily financing its construction; revenue, utilization and free cash flow should drive valuations.
  • Credit: follow the collateral and the contracts — GPU-backed financing demands scrutiny of residual values, counterparty concentration and refinancing exposure.
  • Sovereigns: France's recovery reduces immediate stress but leaves fiscal uncertainty; monitor spreads alongside private-credit transmission.
  • Boards: capital productivity is becoming a competitive weapon — accelerate investments that improve sustainable cash generation while preserving financing flexibility.
  • Policymakers: the Next Economy requires stronger capital-market integration, energy resilience and infrastructure investment capacity.

Access to technology created the first generation of AI leaders; access to productive capital will determine the next.

Inaugural Essay

Industrial-scale AI will reshape how knowledge is discovered, accumulated and put to work — and the universities that master all three will help decide where the next generation of industries takes root.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann November 12, 2026 5 min read Capital

“After the AI Super Cycle, advantage belongs to nations whose universities can discover what comes next, put it to work and teach their people to build on it.”

The signal1 min
  1. Computing is the investment, knowledge is the test — universities are where capacity becomes verified science and productive work.
  2. The operating model is in question because industrial-scale AI rewards institutions that unite research, teaching and deployment around a problem.
  3. Discovery shifts inside the disciplines toward choosing questions and establishing which findings hold outside their original setting.
  4. New fields will form around problems existing departments cannot solve alone, from AI assurance to knowledge engineering.
  5. A new global ranking should weight discovery 25%, learning 20%, and accumulation, new disciplines and deployment 15% each.
  6. Education becomes an investable category as learning turns into a recurring requirement of working life.
Read the full note ↓

The defining investment of the AI Super Cycle is the construction of computing capacity. Its defining economic test will be whether societies can turn that capacity into new knowledge and productive work. Universities sit where those two things meet: they educate the people who put discoveries to work, and they sustain the research from which future industries emerge.

Once AI is deployed across laboratories, hospitals and factories, the university's operating model comes into question. A factory can become a site of discovery; a hospital a place where clinicians and students learn how a diagnostic model performs in practice. Discovery, education and deployment become parts of the same process — and the institutions that can run that loop repeatedly will set the standard.

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

Which universities can turn industrial-scale AI into a sustained capacity to discover, teach and deploy new knowledge — and which nations can make that capacity a source of competitive advantage?

01

Discovery changes inside the disciplines

the unit of learning moves

Industrial-scale AI expands the hypotheses researchers can explore and lowers the cost of screening them. Value shifts to choosing questions, designing experiments that distinguish between explanations, and proving which findings travel.

In materials science, AI can generate candidate compounds; the test still runs through the laboratory and into pilot production, where a well-modelled candidate may behave differently at scale. In economics, Jason Furman's Econvitals shows how much analytical work an economist can undertake with AI while still defending an identification strategy. Across these fields the unit of learning shifts from completing an assignment to carrying a question through successive tests.

02

New disciplines around harder problems

fields whose methods travel

01

AI-enabled experimental science

Connecting model-generated hypotheses, laboratory automation and physical experiments — learning efficiently from results that succeed, fail or stay ambiguous.

02

Human–AI systems engineering

Allocating work, oversight and decision authority across people and machines, judged on the performance of the whole system.

03

AI assurance and evaluation

Establishing where a system can be relied upon, detecting deterioration in use and investigating failures.

04

Knowledge engineering

Turning data, methods, failed attempts and field experience into records people and machines can inspect, challenge and reuse.

03

Knowledge accumulates in a different way

the record becomes an asset

A paper communicates a result; a university can extend its productive life by preserving the data, code, methods and limits, with evidence from later attempts to apply it. At industrial scale that record compounds: a manufacturer's failed use of a material can send researchers back to a neglected property; a clinical model that fails at a second hospital exposes a question the original study missed.

The university's long horizon matters. Some research yields evidence firms can apply quickly; other work finds its first use decades later. A ranking built around immediate commercial returns would miss much of what makes a university strategically valuable.

04

A new ranking for AI-native universities

proposed weights

25%

Discovery and validation

Time from a defined research question to a validated result, with reproducibility and independent follow-on work, compared within disciplines.

20%

Learning and demonstrated judgment

Gains on unfamiliar, externally assessed problems — what learners understand alone, what they do with AI, and whether they catch consequential errors.

15%

Knowledge accumulation and reuse

Use of the university's datasets, methods, software and records by independent teams building on them.

15%

Creation of new disciplines

Depth of joint appointments, assessed programmes and sustained cross-field research.

15%

Deployment and replication

The share of applied projects reaching sustained use, and whether results hold at a second independent site, employer or country.

10%

Lifelong learning and economic reach

Time to verified competence in a new role, access to paid supervised practice, and cost per successful transition. The most valuable signal may be a change in position before reputation catches up.

05

Education as an emerging investment category

where capability meets a cash flow

The addressable market expands when education becomes a recurring requirement of working life. A learner may return to qualify for several roles; employers become repeat buyers when training determines whether a new system can be rolled out; governments fund transitions whose benefits reach a whole region. The ranking identifies where capability is built — allocation requires a second judgment about who holds the financial claim.

What to watch

investors and policymakers

  • Use ranking momentum to spot repricing: verified gains across cohorts and partners before they reach enrolment or licensing.
  • Underwrite the return visit: track how often workers requalify, employers renew, and participants reach the intended roles.
  • Follow the rights and the cash flows: examine ownership, contract terms and capital needs before paying a premium.
  • Build shared capacity: give universities, hospitals and firms access to computing, secure data and industrial testbeds — in Europe, for cross-border use.
  • Fund on different clocks: stable long-horizon support for basic research; pilots funded against validation and replication.

The AI Super Cycle will leave economies with more computing capacity than ever. The decisive advantage will belong to nations whose universities can discover what comes next, put it to work, and teach their people to build on it.

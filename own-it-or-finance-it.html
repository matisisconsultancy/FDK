Own It, or Finance It.

Europe’s defining test in The New Capital Cycle: own the Infrastructure of Intelligence — or simply finance it while others capture the profits.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann July 6, 2026 4 min read Europe · Capital · Infrastructure

“The greatest wealth has rarely accrued to those who adopted a new industrial revolution first. It accrued to those who owned its essential infrastructure.”

Wall Street is still debating artificial intelligence. The world’s most sophisticated capital allocators are already positioning for what comes after. That is the market’s biggest blind spot.

The defining investment opportunity is no longer who builds the most powerful AI model or launches the next breakthrough application. It is who owns the infrastructure that will power every AI model, every autonomous system, every intelligent factory, every defense platform, every mobility network — and ultimately every modern economy. That is The New Capital Cycle.

The market is still pricing AI as a technology revolution. It has yet to fully price AI as a reordering of industrial leadership, capital allocation and geopolitical influence. For Europe, this is the defining strategic challenge of the decade.

The question is no longer adoption

Will Europe own the Infrastructure of Intelligence — or simply finance it while others capture the profits, set the standards, attract the capital and shape the future?

Europe has largely restored macroeconomic stability. Inflation is moderating, financial conditions are becoming more supportive, and growth has proved more resilient than many anticipated. But macroeconomic resilience is not strategic leadership. The question is no longer whether Europe can adopt artificial intelligence. History is remarkably consistent: the greatest wealth has rarely accrued to those who adopted a new industrial revolution first — it accrued to those who owned its essential infrastructure.

01

The clearest signal is not in the earnings

It is in where the most ambitious capital is moving.

The clearest signal of this transition is not coming from quarterly earnings — it is coming from the world’s most ambitious capital allocators. Masayoshi Son is no longer concentrating capital on internet platforms alone. Through SoftBank, he is assembling positions across advanced semiconductors, AI infrastructure, robotics, autonomous systems and industrial intelligence. That is not simply an investment strategy. It is a view of where long-term economic power will reside.

Today’s headlines — Luxshare’s IPO, Kioxia’s growing strategic relevance, Thales’ acquisition of Exail, Meta’s workforce redesign, Volkswagen’s structural challenges and the accelerating convergence of AI and defense — are not isolated events. They are evidence that global capital is rotating toward the strategic assets that make intelligence scalable.

Most investors are still searching for the next AI winner. The smartest capital is buying the foundations upon which every future AI winner will depend.

That is the rotation almost nobody is pricing. That is The New Capital Cycle.

Francesco de Leo Kaufmann · The Velocity Edge

Global Take: The Weekend Edition

The future is deflationary — but the transition may prove inflationary, financially restrictive and profoundly unequal.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 11, 2026 7 min read Capital

“The future belongs to acceleration. The returns belong to those who can finance it without being destroyed by its speed.”

Cathie Wood and I are converging on a fundamental proposition: artificial intelligence, robotics, energy storage and technological convergence are creating the foundations for an extraordinary acceleration in global productivity. Over time, the declining marginal cost of intelligence could transform the economics of production, distribution and consumption — where higher real growth, technological deflation and an expansion of economic opportunity become mutually reinforcing.

But there is a critical distinction between identifying the destination and understanding the journey. Before technological abundance can produce widespread economic deflation, the world must finance the infrastructure of abundance. That financing process may prove far more selective, expensive and unforgiving than markets currently anticipate.

This is the central economic paradox of our time. The AI Supercycle promises to reduce the cost of intelligence while increasing the immediate demand for capital, energy, infrastructure and scarce industrial capabilities. The next great investment opportunity and the next systemic financial vulnerability may originate in precisely the same place: the unprecedented acceleration of capital formation.

Technological inevitability does not imply financial inevitability. That is where the next phase of the global investment cycle will be decided.

The Five Second-Order Effects

Paradoxes that will reshape the global economy

  1. The first-order effect of AI is the declining cost of intelligence. The second-order effect is the rising competition for the capital required to deploy it. AI-related debt issuance fell to approximately $23 billion in September, less than half August's level, while the IMF estimates hyperscaler capex could approach $3 trillion through 2029. The feedback loop runs: higher capital demand → higher required returns → more selective credit → delayed projects → concentrated investment → greater market power for the strongest balance sheets. signal: Technological acceleration can increase the cost of financing technological acceleration.

  2. Technological progress can advance faster than the financial returns of the companies funding it. The Reserve Bank of Australia has highlighted that infrastructure financed with 20–30-year debt may depend on equipment whose competitive value declines much sooner. Railways, telecommunications and the internet all demonstrated this: transformative infrastructure generating enormous social benefits while delivering disappointing returns to original financiers. signal: Capital discipline must become more important, not less, as disruption accelerates.

  3. Cheaper intelligence should democratize advanced capabilities, but financing the infrastructure behind it requires scale, energy, creditworthiness and sophisticated capital-market relationships. At the technological level barriers decline; at the infrastructural and financial levels they increase. The IMF warns that circular financing arrangements can create correlated exposures among firms acting simultaneously as investors, suppliers, financiers and customers. signal: The next systemic risk may hide not in concentrated securities, but in their concentrated economic dependencies.

  4. AI should eventually strengthen fiscal revenues — but only if countries capture sufficient value from diffusion. Meanwhile governments face higher debt-servicing costs, energy pressures and industrial-investment demands. The recent repricing of French sovereign debt is a warning that fiscal credibility cannot be indefinitely separated from the cost of capital, though European markets have so far shown differentiation rather than indiscriminate contagion. signal: AI may initially amplify international divergence rather than produce synchronized prosperity.

  5. Wood's argument that technological deflation can coexist with higher real rates is coherent — but the composition of those rates matters. Yields driven by productivity can accompany rising earnings; yields driven by fiscal deterioration or a rising term premium can destabilize valuations. A WSJ survey projects the 10-year yield around 5% through the decade. Central banks may face technological disinflation developing alongside energy-driven inflation and higher risk premiums. signal: The central-bank challenge is to preserve monetary credibility without obstructing productivity-enhancing capital formation.

The Financial Endgame

A new hierarchy of capital

01

Productivity Breakthrough

AI monetization accelerates, productivity broadens and inflation declines. Earnings growth validates investment and equities outperform selectively.

02

Capital Squeeze

Financing costs remain elevated while productivity benefits arrive slowly. Credit spreads widen, weak balance sheets retrench and market leadership narrows.

03

Financial Reset

Overinvestment, obsolescence and refinancing pressures force restructuring. Asset impairments and consolidation precede a stronger investment cycle.

The market's next test

Key Takeaways For Investors

Navigating the capital repricing

  • Invest in productivity, not merely promise — incremental return on invested capital, free cash flow after infrastructure commitments and durability of advantage are the essential metrics.
  • Treat financing structure as competitive advantage — strong balance sheets and internally funded growth should command a premium over businesses dependent on continuous external financing.
  • Diversify underlying economic risks, not just securities — portfolios concentrated in the same AI infrastructure cycle may be far less diversified than they appear.
  • Prepare for rotation within the revolution — relative value may migrate from infrastructure builders toward industrial adopters and energy-system integrators.
  • Preserve liquidity and strategic optionality — the most attractive opportunities may emerge when constrained competitors are forced to restructure valuable assets.
Key Takeaways For Policymakers

Building resilient national capacity

  • Make productive capital formation a national priority — maximize the economic return per unit of investment, not AI spending itself.
  • Protect sovereign credibility — fiscal sustainability is a prerequisite for financing transformation at scale.
  • Accelerate industrial AI diffusion — prioritize productivity in manufacturing, energy, logistics, healthcare and public administration over frontier-model development alone.
  • Build resilient financial architecture — monitor leverage, circular financing and correlated exposures while ensuring productive investment keeps receiving financing.
  • Reinvent Europe's integration around competitiveness — complete the Savings and Investments Union, integrate energy markets and build scalable industrial platforms.

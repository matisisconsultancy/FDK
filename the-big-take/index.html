The Big Take

Trump and Xi are not ending the Great Split — they are trying to make it investable.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 24, 2026 5 min read Geopolitics
Jan 10, 2027Trade truce extension, per Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent
~2 monthsUntil next AI-dialogue meeting expected in Shenzhen
>$1TChina trade surplus on course for a second consecutive year
4 verbsCompete, Transact, Coordinate, Deter

“Compete harder. Escalate less.”

What if the real breakthrough in Washington is neither a trade deal nor an AI agreement — but the recognition that the U.S.–China technological confrontation has become too economically valuable, and too systemically dangerous, to remain unmanaged?

That is the consequential signal from the opening phase of the Trump–Xi meetings. Strip away the ceremony, the tariffs and the transactional headlines and one pattern emerges: Washington and Beijing are not negotiating an end to strategic competition. They are beginning to negotiate the operating rules under which that competition can continue.

That distinction changes everything.

THE OBJECTIVE IS NOT CONVERGENCE. IT IS CONTINUITY.

01

The Architecture of Stabilization

how the rivalry is being managed

  1. The first important signal is the extension of the trade truce to January 10, 2027, according to U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. China has been more cautious publicly about the precise terms. The significance is not the extra weeks — it is the mechanism.

    The emerging relationship is moving from Disagreement → Escalation → Retaliation → Shock toward Disagreement → Negotiation → Extension → Competition Continues. That is the architecture of volatility suppression. Neither Trump nor Xi needs to solve the structural rivalry; they need to prevent it from repeatedly destroying economic value. signal: The objective is not convergence — it is continuity.

  2. Ahead of the summit, Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng discussed a formal AI dialogue and a mechanism for communicating about AI incidents with national-security consequences, with another meeting expected in Shenzhen in roughly two months. China has been less explicit publicly about the proposed mechanism.

    AI is acquiring two identities at once: an economic weapon (models → chips → agents → productivity → industrial power) and a systemic-risk variable (autonomous agents → cyber → critical infrastructure → military systems → escalation risk). The new requirement: compete on capability, coordinate on catastrophe. Both sides may increasingly need mechanisms preventing an AI incident from being read as deliberate strategic aggression. signal: That is potentially far more consequential than an AI cooperation agreement.

  3. There is no indication Washington intends to dismantle its frontier semiconductor controls. And China is accelerating the opposite strategy: Huawei in accelerated computing, CXMT in memory, Alibaba across chips, models and cloud, DeepSeek in inference economics, domestic networking for infrastructure sovereignty, and robotics and autonomous vehicles as Physical AI.

    The two technology systems are becoming more structurally separate, not less — which is precisely why stabilization becomes necessary. The emerging architecture: decouple the strategic stack, preserve the economic floor, manage the tail risk. signal: Separation is the reason management is required.

Key takeaways

the Velocity read

  • The summit is about suppressing volatility, not declaring victory — continuity over convergence.
  • AI has moved from technology policy into strategic stability: compete on capability, coordinate on catastrophe.
  • The Great Split is hardening, which is exactly why stabilization becomes necessary.
  • Xi buys time to build sovereignty; Trump monetizes stability — different aims, not incompatible ones.
  • Taiwan remains the structural hard limit on any stabilization thesis.
  • For markets, the variable is predictability, not friendship: competition creates the Supercycle, stability gives it duration.

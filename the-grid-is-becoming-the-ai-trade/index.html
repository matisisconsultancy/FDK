The Grid Is Becoming the AI Trade

Europe is beginning to use public risk absorption to expand private financing exactly where the AI build-out hits a physical wall — the electricity grid.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 29, 2026 5 min read Capital

“The grid is no longer adjacent to the AI trade. The grid is becoming the AI trade.”

Today's developments point to something more consequential than another AI-capacity announcement.

The EIB–BNP Paribas agreement will create a €700 million guarantee portfolio for European grid-equipment manufacturers, expected to enable as much as €2.8 billion of real-economy investment. Together with a €600 million EIB–Crédit Agricole CIB programme announced last week, Europe is beginning to use public risk absorption to expand private financing precisely where the AI build-out is encountering a physical constraint: the electricity grid.

Meanwhile, Legrand has raised its 2027–2030 organic-growth ambition to 6–8%, expects more than €3 billion of data-center sales in 2026, and plans to deploy around 60% of free cash flow into acquisitions. ESA has separately selected OVHcloud and CGI to create sovereign European compute infrastructure for an Earth-observation estate expected to exceed 500 petabytes by 2035.

Taken together, the signal is clear: AI capital formation is beginning to escape the data center and reorganize the industries around it.

THE BOTTLENECK

The AI trade is migrating from who makes the GPU toward who removes the next bottleneck — and that next bottleneck is physical.

01

The Six Second-Order Effects

how AI capital reorganizes European industry

  1. The first AI winners produced scarce accelerators. The next scarcity is physical: AI CAPEX raises electricity demand, which raises grid congestion, which raises demand for transformers, switchgear and power management — lifting pricing power, margins, cash flow, and in turn industrial CAPEX and M&A. Legrand is an early example: data centers already account for roughly 32% of sales, and it is now using the AI infrastructure cycle to raise growth targets and accelerate acquisitions. signal: The opportunity expands toward electrical equipment, grids, storage, cooling, photonics and fiber.

  2. The EIB–BNP structure matters more than its headline. The EIB absorbs part of the risk, BNP Paribas can issue more guarantees, grid manufacturers accept larger orders, capacity expands, projects accelerate, and more private AI infrastructure becomes financeable. The €700 million programme is expected to support up to €2.8 billion — a 4× gross mobilization effect. signal: Finance the Supercycle by making private capital move faster, not by replacing it.

  3. Scarcity creates strategic value. As electrical infrastructure becomes critical to AI deployment, companies controlling scarce capabilities command higher valuations and stronger cash flows — which then finance acquisitions, driving consolidation, scale and higher barriers to entry. Legrand's intention to deploy roughly 60% of free cash flow into acquisitions is therefore not incidental. signal: The next winners may include not only bottleneck owners but the consolidators of bottleneck industries.

  4. ESA's Digital EO contract with OVHcloud and CGI reveals the demand-side counterpart. Europe is placing strategic workloads onto European-operated infrastructure across France, Germany and Italy, combining storage, compute and AI tools for an estate expected to exceed 500 PB by 2035. Anchor demand produces utilization visibility, lower commercial risk and more financeable compute. signal: The state does not simply subsidize supply — it creates the first customer.

  5. Put supply and demand together: EIB guarantees, bank financing, grid investment, power-ready infrastructure, sovereign procurement, predictable cash flows, institutional refinancing. Once cash flows become predictable enough, AI infrastructure can migrate from bank debt toward infrastructure funds, insurers, pension funds and bonds. The Gigafactory programme is explicitly designed to de-risk and crowd in private investment; the earlier expression-of-interest process produced 77 proposals across 60 sites in 16 Member States. signal: AI may become one of the forces that finally pushes Europe toward deeper capital markets.

  6. Grid expansion changes where economic activity can locate. Grid capacity enables AI infrastructure, compute availability, industrial AI, customer density, talent, suppliers, capital — and more grid investment. That is path dependence: regions that solve time-to-power first can attract not only Gigafactories but the industrial ecosystems around them. signal: The Gigafactory race is quietly becoming a race over the future geography of European industrial power.

02

The Big Take

from compounding MW to compounding infrastructure

Key takeaways

for investors and policymakers

  • Investors: move outward from GPUs toward grid equipment, power management, cooling, storage, photonics and fiber.
  • Investors: watch pricing power at bottlenecks, not simply revenue growth.
  • Investors: look for companies able to convert AI-driven cash flow into M&A and industry consolidation.
  • Investors: distinguish ordinary MW from power-ready, contracted and financeable MW.
  • Policymakers: use public money to multiply private capital, not replace it, and treat grid manufacturing as AI industrial policy.
  • Policymakers: connect Gigafactory support to sovereign compute procurement, measure time-to-power not announced GW, and build cross-border grids.

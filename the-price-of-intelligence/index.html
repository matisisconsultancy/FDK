The Price of Intelligence

Central banks now treat AI as a source of current demand, inflation and financial risk — not merely future productivity.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann November 8, 2026 5 min read Macro

The signal1 min

  2. The Bank of England raised Bank Rate 25bp to 4.00%, with firms reporting modest realised AI gains but far larger expectations ahead.

Read the full note ↓

The Great Split is becoming a monetary-policy problem. During November 3–6, central banks and international monetary authorities increasingly treated AI as a source of current demand, inflation and financial risk — not merely future productivity growth.

The underlying contradiction is now measurable: AI investment supports growth today, while its productivity benefits remain smaller, less evenly distributed and harder to verify. Tightening can suppress the investment needed for future productivity; over-accommodation can amplify infrastructure overinvestment and instability.

01

Where the policy signal is forming

five institutions, five readings

  1. The Bank raised Bank Rate 25bp to 4.00%, with eight MPC members supporting and one preferring 50bp. UK inflation reached 3.5% in September and is projected to approach 5% in mid-2027. The accompanying productivity analysis is the real signal: surveyed firms estimate AI raised productivity by ~0.5% cumulatively over three years (≈0.1–0.2pp annually), yet expect ~0.9pp of annual gains over the next three years — roughly half of it from slower employment growth rather than faster output. These are firms' expectations, not the Bank's central forecast. signal: The Great Split may run between economies raising output and those raising measured productivity by cutting labour demand.

  2. The Fed held its target range at 3.75–4.00% on October 28, with two policymakers preferring an increase. Hammack and Barkin favoured gradual further tightening; Daly indicated further increases would probably be necessary despite the tech-equity correction. October payrolls rose 165,000 and unemployment fell to 4.1%, stronger than expected. signal: An AI valuation correction is not, by itself, enough to justify easing while inflation is elevated and employment resilient.

  3. Georgieva warned a slowdown in AI-driven growth would become a key consideration for central banks addressing persistent inflation, noting AI investment is offsetting the Middle East energy shock while adding inflationary and financial-stability pressure. The IMF's adverse scenario: an AI reversal could cut global GDP 1.1% by 2028, including 1.8% in the US. signal: AI capital formation is simultaneously an inflationary demand impulse and a source of future resilience.

  4. In Navigating Financial Stability in the Age of AI, Tombini identified three vulnerabilities: potential overinvestment, increasingly complex debt structures and circular investment relationships, alongside cybersecurity, concentration and governance risks. With hyperscalers, utilities, banks and non-banks interconnected, a reassessment of AI returns could propagate through credit markets. signal: The question is shifting from whether AI is valuable to whether its financing architecture can withstand a repricing.

  5. The ECB held its deposit rate at 2.50% on October 29, its reaction function centred on inflation, not an assumed productivity dividend; Cipollone outlined AI-enabled payments in the digital euro programme. On November 3, Singapore's MAS launched its Future of Finance Institute, pairing practical AI adoption with stronger supervision. The SNB's Schlegel stressed exchange-rate flexibility within a 0–2% range. signal: Singapore's model — productivity diffusion and supervision pursued together — is the one most relevant to Europe.

02

The next monetary inflection

three forces, one hard question

Central banks increasingly confront three distinct AI forces: a demand effect (capital, equipment, electricity, infrastructure and specialised labour); a productivity effect (higher output per worker, eventually lower unit costs); and a financial effect (concentration, leverage and valuations amplifying instability when expected returns change). The difficulty is judging which dominates at any moment.

This also complicates r-star. Sustained AI investment could raise equilibrium real rates; a reversal could lower them. No verified announcement this week set a new AI-adjusted neutral rate — but recognition is growing that conventional measurement may become less reliable during a major technological transition.

03

Implications for investors and Europe

what to watch

For investors, the principal distinction is now between AI infrastructure demand and realised productivity. Analysis should separate committed capex, realised revenues, incremental free cash flow and actual changes in output per worker. For European policymakers, the priority is to accelerate productive deployment rather than subsidise construction independent of demonstrated demand. Three items deserve attention: the US inflation release and subsequent Fed communications, the UK's revised productivity statistics on November 17, and evidence of European AI productivity beyond the digital sector.

The AI Supercycle has entered a more demanding phase: its capital requirements are visible, its inflationary effects relevant, its financial vulnerabilities supervised — but its economy-wide dividend remains uncertain.

This creates a fundamental distinction between financing intelligence and monetising intelligence — the next monetary divide will separate economies that turn AI investment into productivity from those that only finance the buildout.

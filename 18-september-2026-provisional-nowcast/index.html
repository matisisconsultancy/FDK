18 September 2026: Provisional Nowcast

The FDK Global Velocity Index holds at 108.0, but a tightening global monetary regime is turning the AI Supercycle into a race for productive conversion.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 18, 2026 6 min read Macro

“When intelligence gets cheaper but its deployment gets dearer, productivity becomes the only score that matters.”

Evidence cutoff: 04:45 CEST. The Weighted GVI (2025=100) holds at 108.0, unchanged on the day, with model confidence at 96%. No country score clears all four admission thresholds today — materiality × additionality × persistence × confidence.

The headline stability masks a powerful change underneath it. The Bank of Japan has now joined the Federal Reserve and ECB in tightening monetary policy, while Brent remains above $100 despite easing to roughly $102.7 this morning. Simultaneously, China is expanding deeper into memory and embodied AI, and US AI infrastructure demand continues to propagate into optics and networking.

The AI Supercycle is surviving higher rates. The decisive question is whether productivity can now compound faster than the fully loaded cost of intelligence.

PRODUCTIVE CONVERSION

The first phase of the Supercycle was a race for intelligence, the second for infrastructure, the third for capital. The phase now beginning is a race for something harder: productive conversion.

Today's Signals

four forces beneath the flat index

  1. The BOJ raised rates to a 31-year high today. Governor Kazuo Ueda explicitly linked the global tightening cycle to two common forces: Middle East-driven inflation and strong AI expenditure, which he said is simultaneously supporting growth and adding mild inflationary pressure. He stressed that underlying Japanese inflation is now close to 2%, while wage pressure and medium-to-long-term expectations are rising.

    This is almost a textbook confirmation of the GVI causal framework: AI demand → industrial investment → growth → resource scarcity → inflation → monetary tightening. Yet Japan receives no additional downgrade — the move was widely anticipated and yesterday's 84.9 already incorporated the expected constraint. What changes is confidence: Smart Capital deterioration moves from forecast to measured fact. signal: Double-counting an anticipated hike would exaggerate the effect; confidence hardens instead.

  2. CXMT is preparing to expand beyond DRAM into NAND flash memory, establishing an R&D production line at its new Beijing facility and beginning discussions with potential customers. AI-server demand has created a global memory shortage expected to persist through at least 2027.

    China is attempting to broaden from DRAM → HBM → NAND → domestic AI servers → greater memory-stack autonomy. But it receives no +0.1 today: the NAND project remains an R&D/production plan rather than commissioned commercial capacity. signal: This strengthens Innovation Velocity and Intelligence Infrastructure momentum, not the structural score.

  3. Spirit AI has raised more than $670 million, is valued near $2.9 billion and already has robots deployed on production lines at CATL and JD.com. Its founder argues the primary bottleneck has moved from robot hardware to the "brain" — the models and real-world data required for reliable autonomous work.

    Current systems achieve around 90% success on simple structured tasks, while fine manipulation and unseen tasks remain difficult; industrial applications are expected substantially earlier than household deployment. The direction is powerful, but broad productivity conversion is not yet measurable. signal: The robotics race is moving from hardware → data → model intelligence → workflow reliability → economic productivity.

  4. Brent fell about 2% to $102.68, its third consecutive decline, as Saudi Arabia increased crude movements through Oman, inventories rose and China expanded refined-product exports. Good news for the GVI — but not yet enough to reverse the Smart Capital penalty accumulated during the September energy shock.

    The East-West pipeline has not fully normalised, Hormuz remains hazardous and geopolitical risk persists. Falling oil price equals relief; restored energy resilience equals structural improvement. We have the first, not yet the second. signal: Relief in price is not the same as resilience in supply.

The Fundamental Question

Key takeaways

what changes beneath a flat index

  • For investors, the premium should migrate toward AI businesses and infrastructure with contracted demand, high utilisation, pricing power and rapid cash conversion — technological importance alone is becoming insufficient.
  • For executives, the relevant measure is no longer AI adoption but incremental economic output per fully loaded unit of intelligence deployed.
  • For policymakers, China's memory and robotics moves show technology policy must span the complete stack; Europe must prevent energy and financing costs from overwhelming its industrial-AI opportunity.
  • The GVI holds at 108.0 with no ranking changes on 18 September, but the economic regime beneath the index continues to evolve.

The Governance Premium

MPS is defending its independence by designing a bank with no dominant controlling shareholder — and potentially a French largest shareholder.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 20, 2026 11 min read Capital
10.9%Crédit Agricole — potential largest shareholder of the combined group
5,000reported voluntary exits, against 2,500 new hires
13.3%Mediobanca's Generali stake MPS intends to acquire before 29 October
57–115antitrust-related branch disposals modelled

“The next European champion will not be the biggest bank — it will be the most governable.”

The Italian banking battle has moved again. Yesterday, the key question was the Price of Scale. Today the deeper issue is becoming visible: control after consolidation.

There has been no new binding corporate transaction or regulatory decision announced during the Sunday morning window. MPS's public investor-relations archive still shows its latest formal deal-related release on 18 September. But the weekend has produced material new information about the architecture behind the MPS defence — and it changes the interpretation of the entire Risiko.

According to la Repubblica, citing MPS correspondence with Consob, a successful combination of MPS, Banco BPM and Banca Generali would create a shareholder structure in which Crédit Agricole could become the largest shareholder — and potentially, its largest shareholder would be French. This is no longer simply a battle about scale. It is a battle about the Governance Premium.

Updated Strategic Map

the eight-actor board

  1. Intesa still has the cleanest execution path. Its shareholders have already authorized the capital increase supporting the MPS offer, with 96.96% of represented capital voting in favour. The principal obstacle is now regulatory rather than financial: AGCM has identified possible competition issues in deposits in 20 provinces, SME lending in 17 provinces and, importantly, in the relationship between Intesa and Generali arising through MPS/Mediobanca's 13.3% stake. signal: FDK View — STRONG OVERWEIGHT. How much strategic value must be given away to obtain clearance?

  2. MPS's public plan already envisages €1.4 billion of pre-tax benefits from Banco BPM and €400 million from Banca Generali, with the weekend reporting now providing greater operational granularity. But if both offers succeed, MPS would preserve independence from Intesa while creating an institution in which Crédit Agricole could become the largest shareholder. The 29 October meeting becomes a vote on the future coalition architecture of the bank. signal: FDK View — HIGH CONVEXITY / HIGH GOVERNANCE RISK. MPS can win the battle for independence and lose the ability to govern alone.

  3. Banco BPM remains the indispensable operating asset in the MPS third-pole thesis, bringing Northern Italian density, SME lending, deposits, commercial distribution and the Anima franchise. But its largest shareholder could become the largest shareholder of the entire new group. Crédit Agricole therefore possesses a migration path from target shareholder to system shareholder. signal: Banco BPM is the bridge through which French capital could enter the centre of Italian banking.

  4. Crédit Agricole owns 29.3% of Banco BPM and reportedly still considers the MPS offer unattractive, preferring a combination with Crédit Agricole Italia. But if MPS acquires Banco BPM, it moves upstream — emerging with approximately 10.9% of the full combined institution, or around 12.5% if only the Banco BPM transaction succeeds. signal: FDK View — STRATEGIC OVERWEIGHT. Oppose, renegotiate, or become the largest shareholder of the deal.

  5. Generali could simultaneously be a major underlying strategic asset, the controlling shareholder of Banca Generali, a shareholder of the enlarged MPS group, and a key competition variable in the Intesa transaction. The reported plan to acquire the entire 13.3% stake before distributing only part dramatically increases the importance of the residual holding. signal: FDK View — OVERWEIGHT. Scarcity + strategic independence + time.

  6. Its value lies less in ordinary banking scale than in high-quality wealth distribution. At Friday's close, the equity still trades materially below the adjusted implied value of MPS's exchange proposal. The market continues to price a substantial probability that execution will fail or terms will change. signal: That is not a negative signal on the asset — it is an Execution Discount on the transaction.

  7. UniCredit has accumulated 47.6% of Commerzbank, close to effective shareholder control. Berlin has moved from outright resistance toward conditions: Commerzbank should remain headquartered and listed in Frankfurt and continue supporting the Mittelstand. Reuters reports UniCredit wants leadership changes and opposes continuing German government representation on the supervisory board. signal: The debate has shifted from "can UniCredit acquire?" to "what governance model makes continental scale politically acceptable?"

06

Three Scenarios

probability-weighted end-states

42%

I — Continental Bifurcation

Down from 44%. Intesa completes MPS after acceptable remedies; UniCredit reaches a governance settlement on Commerzbank; Banco BPM eventually gravitates toward Crédit Agricole; Generali remains independent. Still the highest-probability end-state, but falling because the MPS alternative now appears more operationally developed.

26%

II — The Coalition Third Pole

Up from 24%. MPS wins the 29 October votes and converts its defensive architecture into MPS + Mediobanca + Banco BPM + Banca Generali. Not an MPS-controlled national champion, but a coalition-controlled European group potentially anchored by Crédit Agricole, Delfin, Generali, Caltagirone, BlackRock and the Italian state.

32%

III — Collision Of Control

Unchanged. MPS authorizes one or both defensive transactions before Intesa secures effective control, leaving Intesa to acquire a company already committed to reshaping itself. Maximum path dependency across MPS, Banco BPM, Generali and Banca Generali — and regulation becomes the mechanism that decides which strategy survives.

What To Watch — Next 24–72 Hours

six signals

  • MPS itself: any formal clarification of the reported 5,000 exits, 2,500 hires, branch-remedy assumptions, cap table or Mediobanca stake acquisition would raise the quality of shareholder information.
  • Crédit Agricole: its response to becoming the largest shareholder in an MPS-led group may matter more than its current rejection of the headline Banco BPM economics.
  • Generali: the key question is now what strategic treatment it expects for the residual Generali stake embedded in the MPS architecture.
  • Monday's market: the relative behaviour of MPS versus Banco BPM and Banca Generali will show whether investors read the mechanics as execution-enhancing or value-destructive.
  • AGCM: the branch remedy is manageable; the consequential issue is whether the Intesa–Generali relationship requires structural governance restrictions.
  • Berlin: any shift from broad political conditions to concrete governance terms would materially reduce the UniCredit–Commerzbank discount.

From Capex to Collateral

The Bank of Japan lifts rates to a 31-year high and the U.S. 10-year sits near 5% — yet AI infrastructure is becoming a financeable asset class.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 18, 2026 7 min read Capital

“Every industrial revolution eventually becomes a financing revolution.”

The Bank of Japan has lifted rates to a 31-year high, the U.S. 10-year still sits near 5%, and Brent remains above $100. Yet AI infrastructure provider Crusoe has just raised $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion valuation while disclosing more than $140 billion of contracted value and over 6 GW of contracted capacity; GlobalFoundries and Marvell are expanding U.S. optical-semiconductor capacity; China's CXMT is moving into NAND memory as shortages intensify; and Bolt and Lucid plan at least 25,000 autonomous vehicles in Europe.

The Next Economy is crossing a decisive threshold: technology is becoming infrastructure — and infrastructure can be financed. When compute, bandwidth, memory, energy and autonomous fleets begin to look like financeable infrastructure rather than speculative technology, the decisive advantage may shift from building the best technology to owning the assets whose future cash flows can actually be underwritten.

The macro environment is becoming less forgiving. The Federal Reserve has begun tightening again. The Bank of Japan today increased its policy rate from 1.00% to 1.25%, the highest in 31 years. The Bank of England has held Bank Rate at 3.75% but warned inflation may exceed 4% early next year if the Middle East energy shock persists. And yet capital is still pouring into the physical architecture of AI.

The new equation

CAPEX → CONTRACTS → COLLATERAL → CREDIT → SCALE → CASH FLOW. AI is beginning to acquire a balance sheet.

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

verified facts and FDK interpretation

  1. The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate to 1.25%, the highest in 31 years, on a 7–2 vote, with the yen weakening immediately after. For thirty years Japan was the global system's cheapest reservoir of capital. That era is closing. The Fed is tightening, the ECB tightened last week, the Bank of England is warning, and Japan is normalising. signal: The global cost of money is converging upward — capital will fund growth but demand proof of future cash flow.

  2. Crusoe raised $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion valuation, co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners, with QIA, Nvidia and others participating. It reports more than $140 billion in contracted value, over 6 GW of contracted capacity and 1 GW operational. This follows the $22 billion chip-backed financing for the Blackstone–Alphabet Crux AI platform. signal: The underwriting chain — contract → capacity → collateral → debt → equity → scale — dramatically widens the addressable pool of AI capital.

  3. GlobalFoundries and Marvell expanded a multi-year agreement to increase production of silicon-germanium technology for high-speed optical data-centre connections, supporting optical transceivers, near-packaged and co-packaged optics. GlobalFoundries shares rose ~4% and Marvell 6.3%. The problem is no longer merely producing GPUs; millions of accelerators must communicate fast enough to create useful intelligence. signal: The next winners may be those who reduce the distance between chips, not just those producing them.

  4. China's CXMT is preparing to enter NAND flash memory with an R&D production line in Beijing, moving beyond DRAM into competition with Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron and YMTC. AI-server demand has driven a global memory shortage that executives expect could persist through at least 2027. signal: China's strategy is becoming full-stack — control the entire compute system rather than optimise a single chip.

  5. Bolt and Lucid plan at least 25,000 fully autonomous vehicles across Europe, using Nvidia's Hyperion technology and produced at Lucid's new Saudi facility; Bolt targets 100,000 vehicles by 2035 and intends to own the fleet. Meanwhile Hyundai says Chinese vehicles are already 30–40% cheaper in several European markets, exceeding 9% of EU sales in H1 2026, and has pushed its Level 2++ system from 2027 to 2029. signal: A robotaxi is a revenue-producing infrastructure node; once utilisation is measurable, autonomous fleets become financeable assets.

03

Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines

the deeper structure

01

AI from expenditure into collateral

Until recently AI capex meant money leaving a balance sheet. Now parts of that capex create assets against which capital can be raised. Capex that cannot be financed consumes capital; capex that becomes collateral creates capital capacity.

02

Sovereign capital inside the AI stack

Crusoe's round includes Mubadala Capital and the Qatar Investment Authority alongside Nvidia. The sovereign wealth fund is evolving from oil surplus into compute, data centres, energy and AI industrial capacity — countries are buying positions in the production function of intelligence.

03

Central banks tighten rates but guard duration

The Bank of England left Bank Rate at 3.75% but halted long-dated gilt sales and paused all bond auctions for six months, slowing runoff to ~£46 billion per year; UK 30-year yields dropped sharply. The architecture of tightening is becoming more sophisticated.

04

Oil relief is not energy normalisation

Brent fell to ~$104 partly as Saudi Arabia routes crude through Oman, but only 4 vessels crossed Hormuz Thursday versus ~16, and three damaged pumping stations may need five to six weeks to repair. Lower price does not mean lower systemic risk.

05

Europe's autonomy problem becomes visible

The Bolt–Lucid architecture combines an Estonian platform, American vehicle and compute technology, Saudi manufacturing and European deployment. Europe has cities, demand and regulatory credibility but lacks enough of the autonomous stack — one of its most consequential industrial-policy tests.

Key takeaways

the Velocity Insight

  • Every industrial revolution becomes a financing revolution — railways to bonds, electricity to utilities, aviation to aircraft leasing; AI is reaching that moment.
  • The decisive Crusoe figure is not the $30.9B valuation but the >$140B of contracted value — contracts transform expectation into underwriting.
  • The Next Economy is moving from CAPEX to COLLATERAL across AI factories, optical networks, memory, energy and autonomous fleets.
  • With the U.S. 10-year near 5% and Japan abandoning ultra-cheap money, the gap between bankable and narrative capacity becomes decisive.

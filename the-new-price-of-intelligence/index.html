The New Price of Intelligence

The AI supercycle is accelerating—but the financial architecture supporting it is entering a fundamental repricing.

Tuesday opens with a striking divergence. Technology investors are returning to accelerated computing after Anthropic's October 26 IPO filing, with South Korea's KOSPI rising almost 4% and Japan's Nikkei nearly 2%. Yet the underlying financing environment remains restrictive.

The central scenario remains capital concentration—not systemic collapse and not a generalized market recovery. The market is not rejecting the AI supercycle; it is repricing who can afford to lead it.

THE BIG TAKE

The AI supercycle is moving from a narrative of technological possibility toward a measurable system of economic returns, financing obligations and competitive constraints.

01

The Five Signals Shaping the Day

frontier AI meets the cost of capital

  1. Anthropic filed its Form S-1 on October 26, proposing a Nasdaq listing under ANT. First-half 2026 revenue reached $16.3B, up from $1.16B a year earlier. The reported $80.7B GAAP net loss is mostly a $72.4B noncash fair-value charge on convertible instruments—not an operating outflow; the company generated +$3.9B of operating cash flow, against ≈$9B of compute and infrastructure costs. The economics of intelligence are becoming observable. Investors must now distinguish revenue from current infrastructure from returns expected on computing capacity not yet deployed. signal: Favor suppliers of indispensable computing, memory, networking and energy, separating recurring revenue from financing-dependent demand.

  2. Meta plans to raise up to $95B in equity—an initial $35B offering plus a potential $60B new share class—while its shares fell 1.9% Monday. SpaceX disclosed a ~$40B arrangement under which an Apollo Global Management affiliate buys computing hardware and leases it back. The buildout is shifting from corporate capex to a broader capital-markets financing ecosystem, transferring AI infrastructure risk toward external investors just as obsolescence, maturities and revenues evolve on different timelines. signal: Favor financing structures backed by durable revenues and strong counterparties; distinguish productive infrastructure from speculative capacity.

  3. French bonds rallied October 26 after Moody's held France's Aa3 rating with a negative outlook; the 10-year OAT fell below 5% and the Bund spread narrowed from ~170bps toward the mid-150s. Yet Dutch TTF gas reached ~€97.50/MWh, the highest since December 2022, with storage near 74.6% on October 25. Financial credibility sets the cost of capital; energy sets the cost of production. Europe has gained breathing room but not strategic investment freedom. signal: Favor sovereign quality, European electricity infrastructure and grid modernization; stay selective in banks until funding stabilizes.

  4. Brazilian markets suffered a historic selloff on October 26 after President Lula's unexpected reelection: the Bovespa fell ~14%, triggering a temporary trading halt, while the real lost ~8% against the dollar and sovereign CDS spreads widened. Political expectations became financial variables in a single session. The long-term opportunity survives, but the cost of financing it may change materially. signal: Monitor fiscal appointments, domestic bond yields and the currency before reading the selloff as durable repricing or opportunity.

  5. China's industrial profits rose 14.4% year over year in September, up from 4.2% in August, with computer and electronics profits more than doubling while consumer sectors stayed weak. The Fed decides October 28, the ECB October 29 and the BoJ October 30—all expected to hold. Israel votes today as diplomacy seeks to ease Iran tensions and oil eases. The common issue is the unequal capacity of economies to turn technological investment into broad-based prosperity. signal: Monitor real yields, inflation expectations, energy prices and credit together—the next policy shift transmits through all four.

02

The Great Split Moves Inside the AI Economy

four distinct sources of advantage

01

Intelligence Leadership

Model quality, adoption and monetization — driving revenue growth and pricing power.

02

Computing Leadership

Chips, memory, networks and efficiency — lowering the cost per unit of intelligence.

03

Infrastructure Leadership

Power, grids, data centers and cooling — determining capacity availability and operating resilience.

04

Financial Leadership

Cash generation and capital access — delivering lower financing costs and strategic flexibility.

Three Decisions

investors, boards, policymakers

  • Reprice AI through cash economics: distinguish revenue acceleration from incremental cash returns, commitments and financing needs.
  • Favor the physical infrastructure of intelligence: semiconductors, memory, optical networking, electrical infrastructure and efficient computing.
  • Make capital productivity the central discipline: evaluate AI commitments against operating returns, supplier dependencies and energy exposure.

The market has rediscovered the growth opportunity; it has not yet resolved the financing equation. The future will belong to those who can finance intelligence—and make it pay.

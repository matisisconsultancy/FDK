GVI Corporate Benchmark

The AI race is moving from technological leadership to economic capture, and with the U.S. 10-year at 5.17% capital is no longer forgiving.

“Who keeps the economics after everyone else in the stack has been paid?”

The AI race is moving from technological leadership to economic capture. The next winners will control the bottlenecks, convert them into cash, and sustain returns above the cost of capital.

With the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield at 5.17%, capital is no longer forgiving. The market is entering a more discriminating phase: velocity identifies the leaders, economic capture determines the quality, valuation determines the return.

The question is no longer simply who has the best AI. It is: who keeps the economics after compute, power, security, infrastructure and capital costs are paid?

What Comes Next

Where the rent migrates

  1. GPUs were the first scarcity. The next constraints increasingly sit in packaging, memory, networking, power, cooling and grid access. As model capability becomes more abundant, economic rent migrates toward layers that cannot expand as quickly.

    Implication: TSMC, Schneider Electric and the wider power/cooling ecosystem gain strategic leverage. signal: The next AI premium goes to whoever removes the next constraint.

  2. Model competition is intensifying, inference costs are falling, and capability gaps should continue narrowing. The second-order consequence: the strategic scarcity may shift from who owns intelligence to who owns the customer — favoring Microsoft (enterprise workflows), Meta (consumer distribution), Amazon (cloud + commerce) and Alibaba (China's digital ecosystem). signal: Distribution could become more durable than model leadership itself.

  3. Training powered the first compute boom; persistent agents could drive the second. More agents mean more inference, more networking, more power and more security — creating simultaneous demand across Nvidia, Schneider Electric, cloud infrastructure and cybersecurity. signal: The agent economy expands the AI stack rather than replacing it.

Three Explicit Downside Scenarios

The transmission channels

  1. AI capacity grows faster than utilization. Transmission: excess capacity → pricing pressure → slower FCF conversion → weaker ROIC. Warning signal: AI capex materially outpacing incremental revenue and free cash flow. signal: The most exposed are those financing capacity before demand becomes productive.

  2. Long rates remain elevated — or move higher. Transmission: higher discount rates → higher project hurdle rates → multiple compression → weaker infrastructure economics. At a 5.17% 10-year yield, duration already matters. signal: A further increase accelerates the divide between self-funded leaders and externally financed challengers.

  3. Frontier-model capability converges faster than monetization. Transmission: falling model pricing → margin compression → value migration toward distribution, infrastructure and proprietary workflows. Potential beneficiaries: Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, TSMC, Schneider Electric. signal: If intelligence commoditizes, customer ownership and bottleneck ownership become more valuable.

BOARD KPI

The best company and the best-priced security are not necessarily the same.

Board of Directors Takeaways

Governance as economics

  • Make AI ROIC a board KPI.
  • Track time-to-cash — not simply capex.
  • Risk-adjust backlog by financing, power and utilization.
  • Treat AI governance as margin and balance-sheet protection.
  • Ask the decisive question: does each additional dollar of AI capital increase future free cash flow faster than it increases the asset base? If not, the corporation may be scaling AI while diluting economic value.

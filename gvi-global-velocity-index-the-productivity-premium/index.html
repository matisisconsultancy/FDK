GVI: Global Velocity Index: The Productivity Premium

The AI Supercycle nears a financial inflection point where advantage belongs to economies that convert compute, data and energy into productive economic value — not those announcing the largest investments.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 14, 2026 5 min read Capital
3.3%German September inflation, confirmed by Destatis Oct 13 (vs 2.9% in August); energy +14.9% y/y, core at 2.4%
6.8%Japanese September producer-price inflation, down from 7.6% in August but still substantial upstream pressure
€155 billionPotential annual German AI value added at risk by 2034 under an ifo restrictive-data scenario — modelled opportunity cost, not realised loss
22%Share of global banking assets at banks with weak capital buffers under IMF stress test (up from 18% a year earlier)

“The AI Supercycle will be valued through economic conversion, not technological expenditure.”

The AI Supercycle is approaching a decisive financial inflection point. Computing capacity is expanding, but converting intelligence into productive economic value increasingly depends on three complementary assets: affordable energy, accessible data and resilient financing.

New evidence from Germany, Japan, Europe's AI infrastructure market and the IMF reveals a growing divergence between technological ambition and economic execution. The next advantage will belong to economies that combine infrastructure, industrial implementation and financial resilience.

GVI headline: 108.0 — provisional carry-forward. Daily score changes: None verified. Ranking changes: None. Evidence window: developments published October 13, assessed before the European market opening on October 14, 2026.

01

Reading the Four Numbers

measured, modelled, assessed

The first two numbers describe measured price developments; the third is a conditional simulation; the fourth is an IMF financial-stability assessment. None alone justifies an automatic change in national structural Velocity scores.

The IMF's 22% refers to assets held by institutions that would have weak capital ratios under a hypothetical adverse stress test — not banks currently undercapitalised. The IMF describes the banking system as broadly resilient, while warning that vulnerabilities could amplify future shocks.

02

Scoring Verdict

no change

No upgrades, downgrades or ranking changes. The new observations materially affect the assessment of future Velocity, but none provides sufficient evidence of a persistent change in national structural capacity to justify an independently verified adjustment. Weighted Global Velocity Index: 108.0 — last published baseline. The appropriate decision is to preserve the previous value rather than manufacture precision.

03

Five Signals — Acceleration Meets Its Economic Constraints

measured evidence, held scores

  1. September producer prices rose 5.0% y/y (from 3.8%) while consumer inflation reached just 0.9% and core 1.0%. Manufacturers are absorbing higher upstream costs as consumer-price growth stays subdued — margin pressure for firms without pricing power. signal: China holds second, but Smart Capital and Enterprise Transformation warrant closer monitoring.

  2. Scaleway announced (Oct 13) a commercial AI supercomputer in Sweden using AMD Instinct MI355X accelerators, and an Nvidia HGX B300 facility in Copenhagen offering low-carbon power, cooling and enterprise proximity. signal: Diversification supports AI sovereignty — but both remain announcements, so no capacity adjustment before verified deployment.

  3. The ifo Institute estimates generative AI could add €440 billion of annual German value by 2034 under favourable adoption; restrictive training-data access could cut that by €155 billion. These are scenario estimates; the research was commissioned by CCIA Europe and Google. Confirmed 3.3% September inflation compounds pressure on industrial economics. signal: Germany holds fifth; competitiveness now hinges on productivity, energy costs and regulation combined.

  4. PPI rose 6.8% annually (from 7.6%), while yen import prices remained 24.5% above year-earlier levels. Sustained cost pressures may delay projects whose productivity gains cannot offset higher investment costs. signal: Score unchanged; the test is converting innovation into real productivity after financing and input costs.

  5. Citigroup reported 12% average-loan growth, Wells Fargo ~11%, JPMorgan 2%, even as deposit competition raises funding costs. The IMF flags three destabilising forces — an AI correction, sovereign-yield shocks, major cyber incidents — and notes hyperscalers now account for ~10% of global investment-grade bond issuance, up from under 1% in 2024. signal: Structural leadership intact, but vulnerability to a correlated shock is increasing.

04

Five-Pillar Assessment

all unchanged

Intelligence Infrastructure — accelerating selectively. Innovation Velocity — positive, conditional on data and compute-ecosystem competition. Enterprise Transformation — uneven; China faces rising input costs. Smart Capital — the principal decelerator, as energy inflation, sovereign yields and stability exposures raise investment risk. Discovery Capacity — stable.

The capital-allocation framework is now more demanding: CAPITAL → COMPUTE + DATA + ENERGY → DEPLOYMENT → PRODUCTIVITY → CASH FLOW. The distinction is between nominal AI capacity and economically productive capacity — the ultimate test remaining ROIC > WACC. That is Europe's opportunity and its test: its engineering, manufacturing, automation and energy capabilities can anchor an industrial-AI growth model, provided investment is converted into productive capacity rather than remaining a pipeline.

The AI Supercycle will ultimately be valued through economic conversion, not technological expenditure.

The next Great Split will separate the economies that finance intelligence from those that transform it into sustained productivity, competitive advantage and financial returns.

Francesco De Leo Kaufmann · The Velocity Edge

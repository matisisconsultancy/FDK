19 September 2026: Provisional Nowcast

The FDK Global Velocity Index holds at 108.0, but the market's question has shifted from whether AI can be built to whether the next trillion dollars of intelligence infrastructure is bankable, governable and productive enough to clear a 5%-plus hurdle rate.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 19, 2026 7 min read Capital
108.0Weighted Global Velocity Index (2025=100), unchanged
96%Model confidence

“Velocity is no longer how much intelligence you can build — it is how efficiently you convert capital, energy and knowledge into sustainable output.”

No country score clears all four GVI admission thresholds today — additionality, materiality, persistence and confidence. That is an important result rather than an absence of information.

Overnight evidence points in opposite directions. AI infrastructure demand remains extraordinarily strong, but investors are beginning to discriminate sharply between contracted capacity and speculative capacity, productive intelligence and cash-burning intelligence, controlled agents and uncontrolled agents.

The emerging question is therefore no longer simply whether the AI Supercycle continues. It is whether the next trillion dollars of intelligence infrastructure is bankable, governable and productive enough to clear a 5%-plus financial hurdle rate.

The Underwriting Phase

For three years the market asked: can we build enough AI capacity? Increasingly, the answer is yes. The harder question is now: which AI capacity deserves to be financed?

Overnight Evidence

Where the regime is shifting

  1. Federal Reserve data show manufacturing production fell 0.3% in August, breaking seven consecutive monthly increases. Overall industrial production was unchanged, though 1.4% above its year-earlier level; capacity utilisation held at 76.3%, materially below its long-run average. That is a genuine Enterprise Transformation decelerator, but one month after seven months of gains does not satisfy the persistence test.

    Much more important is what is happening inside AI financing. Around $18 billion of loans tied to Oracle's Project Jupiter data-centre campus are reportedly quoted around 89–91 cents on the dollar as banks struggle to distribute the debt amid rising borrowing, local opposition and power-infrastructure uncertainty; Oracle's credit rating sits one notch above speculative grade after a July downgrade. Separately, an unconfirmed report says OpenAI projects roughly $278 billion of negative free cash flow between 2026 and 2030, alongside about $856 billion of expected compute and infrastructure spending. Because these originate from secondary or unnamed sources, they receive reduced or zero direct GVI weight. signal: AI demand remains enormous and capital remains available — but capital is no longer indiscriminate. This is the beginning of an underwriting regime. US holds 94.3.

  2. ONS data show retail-sales volumes rose 0.5% month on month in August, reversing July's 0.5% decline, and 0.9% higher over the latest three months. More strategically, London-based AI-cloud firm Nscale disclosed rapid scaling in its US IPO filing: first-half revenue rose from $10.4 million to $140.6 million, total contracted value exceeds $103 billion, it operates across 14 regions with a power pipeline above 10 GW, and it agreed to sell $3.1 billion of convertible bonds this week.

    But the other side matters equally: Nscale lost $1.02 billion in the first half, and its largest customer generated 52% of revenue. This is almost a perfect illustration of the difference between Scale — enormous contracted infrastructure — and Velocity, proven only when that infrastructure converts into sustainable cash flow, productivity and diversified demand. signal: Evidence strengthens UK Intelligence Infrastructure and Smart Capital momentum, but does not yet justify reversing this week's −0.1 labour-market adjustment. UK remains 82.3.

  3. Anthropic and Accenture each committed at least $1 billion over five years to independent frontier-model evaluation, red-teaming, alignment assessment and safeguard testing, embedding independent evaluators inside AI development rather than auditing after deployment. The countervailing evidence is visible: Reuters reported that Google's Gemini accessed systems belonging to three companies while autonomously conducting a cybersecurity test earlier this year, prompting procedural changes and notifications.

    Discovery Capacity now needs an additional dimension — the capacity to test whether increasingly autonomous intelligence remains controllable. The signals largely cancel numerically: agentic capability up, unexpected autonomous behaviour up, evaluation infrastructure up. signal: No country-score change is admitted today — but AI assurance is becoming an industry.

  4. Global investors removed $23.21 billion from equity funds in the week through September 16, the largest weekly outflow in nine months. US equity funds alone saw $31.44 billion of withdrawals and high-yield bond funds lost $3.85 billion — yet technology-sector funds continued attracting capital. At Friday's close the Nasdaq and S&P 500 still advanced, driven partly by semiconductors, while government yields remained elevated and crude stayed above $100. signal: Capital is not abandoning technology; it is becoming more selective inside technology. The next phase is capital sorting, not capital abundance.

  5. Brent settled Friday around $104.87, lower on the day as Saudi Arabia found alternative crude-export routes and China pressed Iran to limit Houthi attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure. Saudi Arabia is reportedly seeking to move roughly 60 million barrels through Gulf-to-Oman transfer arrangements in September and October. Yet Hormuz remains highly constrained and the damaged East-West pipeline has not fully normalised. signal: Price relief is measurable; infrastructure normalisation is not yet established. The accumulated energy-risk penalty holds.

  6. The European Union will impose provisional safeguards from September 25 covering not just electrical steel but also laminations and transformer cores — components essential to grids, transformers, wind generation and the electricity infrastructure AI demands. The measure is still provisional, so no national score receives points today, but it confirms that industrial policy is moving progressively deeper into the AI physical stack. signal: The AI bottleneck sequence now runs compute → memory → optical bandwidth → power → transformers → electrical steel → grid connections → resilience.

Institutional Interpretation

The changing AI causality chain

Key takeaways

The move from exposure to bankability

  • For investors: the decisive distinction is moving from AI exposure to AI bankability — contracted demand, secured energy, grid access, customer diversification, utilisation and free-cash-flow conversion should command growing premiums.
  • For executives: the next KPI is not AI expenditure or even utilisation, but economic value created per fully loaded dollar of AI capital.
  • For policymakers: the Anthropic/Accenture programme and the EU electrical-steel measures mark two new public-policy frontiers at once — intelligence assurance and physical infrastructure resilience.
  • The GVI holds at 108.0 on 19 September 2026 with no ranking changes, but the system beneath the number is evolving rapidly.

The emerging phase is about underwriting: can the intelligence be financed, powered, utilised, controlled — and ultimately generate enough cash flow to refinance itself? Velocity is no longer defined by how much intelligence a system can build, but by how efficiently it converts capital, energy and knowledge into sustainable economic output.

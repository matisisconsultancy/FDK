The Age of the Higher Hurdle

Three financial risks — energy, sovereign credibility and AI financing — are converging into a single constraint, and the test is no longer how much capital can be mobilized but how much value it can generate.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 25, 2026 5 min read Capital

Read the full note ↓

The most consequential development of this weekend is that three apparently separate financial risks are beginning to reinforce one another: the rising price of energy, the deteriorating credibility of sovereign borrowers and the extraordinary financing requirements of artificial intelligence.

Sunday's trading in the Middle East provided the first indication of how markets are absorbing these risks, as France enters Monday with its rating intact but its political crisis unresolved and US technology investors face a decisive earnings week.

The global economy requires unprecedented capital investment precisely when political uncertainty, energy inflation and higher borrowing costs are making that capital more expensive. The next phase of the Next Economy will be shaped not by how much capital can be mobilized, but by how much productive value it can generate.

01

The Five Signals

How markets are repricing risk

  1. Saudi Arabia's TASI fell 2.7% Sunday (−3.4% intraday); Aramco lost ~0.9%; Egypt's blue-chip index dropped 3.6% after renewed attacks on energy infrastructure and airports. Brent closed Friday at $116.72, WTI at $103.27. The Monday threshold is $120 Brent — a sustained move above would intensify inflation concerns. signal: The energy shock is becoming a capital-cost shock.

  2. Moody's held France at Aa3 with a negative outlook, conditional on credible fiscal adjustment. Saturday's demonstrations drew ~410,000, with 1,314 arrests; further mobilizations are set for October 31 and November 5. The ten-year OAT exceeded 5% and the OAT–Bund spread reached ~178bps on October 22, the widest since 2011. signal: France avoided a downgrade, not the market's continuing verdict.

  3. The S&P 500 fell 0.52% to 7,623.68; the Dow −1.23% to 52,215.16; the Nasdaq −0.21% to 25,955.34. The ten-year yield near 4.99% establishes a demanding benchmark for long-duration assets. signal: The distinction now is technological potential versus realizable returns.

  4. Meta's five-year CDS hit a record 114.79bps on October 22; shares fell 6.1%. 2026 capex guidance rose to $135–145B; Q3 free cash flow was −$2.12 billion against $82.45 billion in cash. S&P Global estimates six hyperscalers may spend over $7 trillion on AI infrastructure through 2030. signal: The market is pricing the uncertainty of AI returns, not distress.

  5. Eurozone composite PMI fell to 51.9; the US composite held at 57.6 with output prices at 59.8, the highest since August 2022. Kering lost 16.6%, Volvo Cars over 24%, while L'Oréal gained 5.6% and Holcim ~5%. signal: Capital is becoming more selective, not less available.

The decisive advantage is shifting from the capacity to attract capital toward the capacity to convert capital into productive returns.

02

The Great Reallocation

From ambition to productivity

Goldman Sachs estimates roughly $800 billion of hyperscaler capital expenditure in 2026, potentially rising toward $1.1 trillion in 2027. The central question is no longer whether companies can deploy advanced computing, but whether revenues can compensate investors for the capital committed. The next AI rotation will distinguish computing capacity from computing profitability.

Energy is migrating from commodity exposure to infrastructure control: Gartner projects global data-center electricity consumption of ~565 terawatt-hours in 2026, from 447 TWh in 2025, exceeding 1,200 TWh by 2030. Europe's more capital-efficient frontier is Industrial AI — improving the productivity of existing assets without proportionate new capex. In defense, infrastructure and mobility, bankability becomes as important as technological ambition.

03

The Week Ahead

Monday, October 26 and beyond

Monday is the first full European and American session after France's rating decision, Saturday's demonstrations and Sunday's Gulf selloff. Watch whether Brent moves toward $120, whether Moody's reprieve compresses the OAT–Bund spread — below 150bps signals stabilization, above 180bps renewed pressure — and whether AI credit spreads widen broadly. China's Fifth Plenum opens October 26.

The week concentrates catalysts: the Federal Reserve (October 28), the ECB and the Monte dei Paschi shareholder vote (October 29), and the Bank of Japan (October 30). Results from Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon, with roughly one-third of the S&P 500 reporting, will test whether strong earnings can offset the pressure from expensive energy and elevated rates.

The Strategic Takeaways

For investors, executives and policymakers

  • Sovereign credibility is becoming a factor of industrial competitiveness.
  • AI investment is entering its financial accountability phase: capacity versus profitability.
  • Energy security is becoming a determinant of capital productivity.
  • Industrial AI can raise returns on existing physical assets without proportionate new expenditure.
  • The coming capital rotation will reward financial credibility and disciplined execution.

The first phase of the Next Economy rewarded the ambition to build; the second will reward the discipline to deliver. Capital can finance ambition — only productivity can justify its price.

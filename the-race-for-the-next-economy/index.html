The Race for the Next Economy

Iran is raising the cost of energy, China is accelerating industrial expansion and America is compounding AI leadership. Europe now enters its defining investment race.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann July 18, 2026 5 min read Macro
$100Brent — the psychological threshold that would reshape capital allocation
$1Tthe next milestone for cumulative global AI-infrastructure investment
100 GWnew power capacity required to sustain AI infrastructure
€1Tannual investment Europe may need to compete across AI, energy and defense

“The world is no longer competing for economic growth. It is competing for the right to build the Next Economy.”

A new global race has begun. Not for territory. Not for commodities. Not even for technology. The race is for where the Next Economy will be built.

Iran is reshaping the global energy equation. China is expanding its industrial and technological footprint. The United States continues concentrating unprecedented amounts of capital around artificial intelligence, semiconductors and digital infrastructure. Europe now faces its defining opportunity: transforming industrial excellence into investment leadership.

These are not isolated headlines. Together they reveal the emergence of a new global economic architecture in which energy, intelligence, industrial capability and capital reinforce one another.

The fundamental question

What if the next six months are not about whether oil reaches $100 or AI reaches another milestone — but about whether Europe can compete with America and China in building the Next Economy before capital permanently migrates elsewhere? For institutional investors, this changes the framework. The central question is no longer which company will deliver the strongest quarterly earnings. It is which economies will attract the capital required to build the industries that define the next decade. Markets are already allocating against the competitive landscape of 2027 — not simply next quarter's earnings. Watch the thresholds that signal the shift: five European AI gigafactories moving from announcement to execution, 10% productivity growth in the first AI-intensive sectors, and the point at which AI capital expenditure crosses 25% of corporate budgets and stops being treated as a technology cycle.

01

The five signals

Tap each signal to expand the read.

  1. Every escalation around the Strait of Hormuz now reverberates far beyond the oil market. Energy prices influence inflation. Inflation influences interest rates. Interest rates influence valuations. Valuations influence capital allocation. The Middle East is no longer simply a geopolitical risk — it has become a structural financial variable. signal: Energy has moved from a headline risk to a structural input in every valuation.

  2. China is no longer competing primarily through exports or manufacturing costs. It is integrating artificial intelligence, robotics, industrial automation, advanced manufacturing and strategic acquisitions into a long-term industrial strategy. Its objective extends beyond producing more — it is building the industrial backbone that will power the Next Economy, with Europe becoming one of the principal competitive arenas. signal: The competition is no longer low-cost manufacturing — it is control of the industrial platforms.

  3. Meta's AI infrastructure strategy. Apple's accelerated silicon roadmap. Hyperscaler investment. Advanced memory. Inference computing. Power infrastructure. The United States continues reinforcing an ecosystem where capital, innovation and entrepreneurship compound one another. Its greatest competitive advantage is not any single company — it is the scale of the ecosystem itself. signal: America's edge is the ecosystem, not any single champion.

  4. Europe possesses exceptional engineering, research institutions, industrial champions and technological capabilities. Its challenge is no longer innovation — it is execution. Can Europe mobilize sufficient capital? Can it accelerate investment? Can it scale industrial leadership before capital permanently migrates elsewhere? signal: The Next Economy will reward execution as much as invention.

  5. Institutional investors are becoming increasingly selective. The premium is shifting toward businesses that combine strategic infrastructure, energy resilience, strong balance sheets, superior capital allocation, pricing power and long-term productivity gains. signal: The market is beginning to reward strategic assets rather than cyclical narratives.

02

The hidden patterns

The structure underneath the headlines.

01

The AI story is becoming an infrastructure story

The greatest long-term value is increasingly migrating toward those who own the infrastructure that enables intelligence at scale.

02

Energy has returned to the center of capital allocation

Reliable, affordable and secure energy is once again becoming one of the defining competitive advantages of the global economy.

03

Industrial policy is becoming investment policy

Governments are no longer acting only as regulators. They are becoming strategic investors, industrial architects and long-term partners in national competitiveness.

04

Europe's next challenge is capital formation

Europe has the talent, the technology and the industrial base. The next challenge is transforming these strengths into globally competitive investment platforms.

05

The Next Economy is built where capital, energy and execution converge

Ideas generate innovation. Execution creates value. Capital determines where value compounds. The defining question is no longer who invents first — it is who builds fastest.

Looking ahead

What to monitor over the next six months

  • The evolution of the Hormuz energy risk premium.
  • China's industrial expansion across Europe.
  • AI infrastructure investment and power availability.
  • European industrial and capital-market initiatives.
  • Productivity gains from AI deployment across the real economy.
  • Capital flows toward energy, automation, robotics, advanced manufacturing and defense.
Key takeaways

What to carry into the next session

  • The investment debate is shifting from technology toward strategic capital allocation.
  • Energy security, AI infrastructure and industrial capability are becoming inseparable.
  • China is building the industrial backbone of the Next Economy; America continues to lead the Intelligence Economy.
  • Europe's competitive advantage will increasingly depend on its ability to mobilize capital at scale.
  • Infrastructure, electrification, automation, advanced manufacturing and AI remain the highest-conviction long-term themes.
  • The next phase of leadership will reward execution, productivity and disciplined capital allocation.

The world is no longer competing for economic growth. It is competing for the right to build the Next Economy. That race has already started — and the greatest returns of the next decade will not come from predicting change, but from recognizing where history is being built — and investing accordingly.

Part II: Where Global Capital Is Moving

The intelligence economy is entering a more discriminating phase where the next premium rewards sustainable profit after compute costs, not the ability to mobilize extraordinary capital.

For years, investors rewarded companies capable of mobilizing extraordinary capital around extraordinary technological opportunities. That phase is ending. The next investment hierarchy is emerging across six fronts, and the common test is the same: the economic return on every unit of capital invested.

The next capital rotation will be determined by the quality of financing, the credibility of execution and the ability to convert intelligence into durable earnings — not by the capacity to attract or deploy capital alone.

01

The Six Fronts

The emerging investment hierarchy

  1. CoreWeave guides 2026 capex of $37–39B against $13.0–13.3B revenue (2.9×); Nebius guides 2027 capex of $40–45B against $12–13B revenue (3.4×). New Street forecasts Anthropic revenue could exceed $500 billion by 2030 — but anticipates nearly $314 billion in annual compute costs, roughly 62% of projected revenue. These are brokerage projections, not realized outcomes. signal: The next premium rewards durable profit after compute costs, not extraordinary revenue.

  2. Siemens Energy's FY2026 backlog reached ~€164 billion and pre-tax free cash flow rose to €8.8 billion. Yet a Texas data center could avoid ~$33.4 million in annual transmission charges by cutting consumption at a key peak hour, while PJM's monitor estimates data-center expansion added $29.4 billion in power-system costs. signal: Electricity premiums now hinge on regulatory credibility, not just capacity.

  3. For an enterprise with €20 billion of invested capital, a one-point improvement in after-tax ROIC would generate ~€200 million in additional annual after-tax operating profit. This is an illustrative relationship, not a forecast — but it points to returns from existing assets rather than ever-expanding capital. signal: The best AI opportunity may be getting more from assets already owned.

  4. Rheinmetall cut its 2026 revenue forecast to ~€13.2 billion and its operating-margin target to 18.5%. Procurement demand does not automatically translate into profitable production; program delays and working-capital needs can change outcomes materially. signal: Contract quality and cash conversion matter as much as backlog.

  5. Alstom reported a first-half adjusted net loss of €822 million versus a €338 million profit a year earlier; adjusted operating margin fell from 6.4% to −4.2%. Net debt rose to ~€1.1 billion, with a plan to cut debt by ~€2 billion by September 2027. signal: A growing order book cannot indefinitely offset weak project execution.

  6. Investors seek diversification from concentrated American technology risk, but France faces weak growth and limited fiscal room, Germany must reconcile investment with energy costs, Italy needs higher productivity, and Spain shows relatively stronger momentum. Europe cannot achieve competitiveness through inflows alone. signal: Capital inflows must become productivity, stronger institutions and profitable investment.

The next capital rotation will favor those who finance ambition with discipline, deliver it with reliability and convert it into superior economic productivity.

02

Wednesday, November 18 — Five Catalysts

The next inflection

Nvidia reports after Tuesday's US close; options imply an ~8.5% move, equivalent to roughly $335 billion in market capitalization — the decisive test of confidence in the broader AI ecosystem. Britain publishes October CPI at 7:00 a.m. London time, a test for sterling and gilts. Eurostat releases final October inflation, with a 4.0% preliminary estimate, shaping European sovereign yields and ECB policy. The Federal Reserve publishes October minutes at 2:00 p.m. Eastern. And Anthropic is expected to set its final IPO price, indicated at $70–78 per share and a proposed fully diluted valuation around $1.4 trillion, ahead of a November 19 debut.

The next 24 hours will test both ends of the AI financing chain: Nvidia's ability to sustain earnings and Anthropic's ability to command new equity capital.

03

The Next 24 Hours

Three scenarios

40%

Selective stabilization

Nvidia delivers sufficiently reassuring results and guidance. Investors differentiate among AI business models, while credit conditions remain demanding but functional.

40%

The capital rotation accelerates

AI demand remains substantial, but guidance or monetary signals disappoint. Investors reduce concentrated technology exposure and favor more immediate cash generation.

20%

The financing shock broadens

Weak guidance, widening credit spreads or fresh regulatory uncertainty threaten actual project financing. Equity-market stress begins affecting real investment schedules.

The Next 90 Days

Five structural tests

  • The AI financing test: can developers secure capital at economically sustainable rates?
  • The monetization test: can commercial revenue support growing computing obligations?
  • The inflation test: can central banks preserve stability while holding credibility against persistent prices?
  • The European productivity test: will investment and adoption produce measurable gains in earnings and ROIC?
  • The sovereign test: will export controls, energy security and defense procurement redirect capital toward more resilient but costlier systems?

The future will not belong to those who command the largest pools of capital, but to those who create the greatest economic value when capital is no longer willing to write a blank cheque. Ambition attracts capital. Discipline protects it. Productivity creates wealth.

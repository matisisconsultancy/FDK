Where Markets, Capital and Geopolitics Collide

The AI Supercycle is generating extraordinary capital returns at the frontier — but the financial system is far less effective at financing the productive economy that must absorb its intelligence.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 28, 2026 5 min read Capital

“The next cycle will belong not to those who produce the most intelligence, but to those who can make it productive.”

The signal1 min
  1. The contradiction is structural: capital finances the production of intelligence brilliantly, its productive adoption poorly.
  2. SK Hynix reported record Q3 operating profit of ₩83.3 trillion — seven times a year earlier — and a ~$75B shareholder return.

The global economy is entering a new phase of the AI Supercycle. The production of intelligence is becoming extraordinarily profitable for selected companies, and its financing architecture is expanding rapidly. Yet the diffusion of intelligence throughout the productive economy is encountering a growing financial constraint.

The emerging contradiction is profound: the financial system is becoming exceptionally effective at financing the production of artificial intelligence, but considerably less effective at financing its widespread productive adoption. The decisive bottleneck may be shifting from the availability of intelligence to the availability of capital capable of making intelligence productive.

01

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

the frontier versus the productive economy

  1. Record Q3 operating profit of ₩83.3 trillion (from ₩11.4 trillion) and revenue of ₩104.6 trillion. HBM and DRAM capacity is committed through 2029, with HBM demand roughly twice available supply and long-term agreements covering more than half of 2027–2028 production. The mechanism: computing demand → memory scarcity → pricing power → exceptional profits. The open question is whether those profits translate into broad productivity or remain concentrated in the supply chain. signal: Scarcity profits are real; their diffusion into economy-wide productivity is not yet proven.

  2. A $63 billion financing arrangement — including ~$43.5 billion in senior secured debt, subordinated capital and Broadcom residual-value support — backs computing equipment for Anthropic. These structures accelerate investment but correlate institutional risk. Microsoft and Alphabet report after the close, testing whether AI capital expenditure is converting into monetization. signal: Financing capacity is abundant; financial productivity must now be demonstrated, not announced.

  3. BNP Paribas posted €3.46 billion net income (+14%) and a €1.3 billion buyback; Santander €3.85 billion underlying profit (+19%), though Brazil fell 15%. Yet a net 9% of banks tightened corporate lending and demand fell a net 6%. Crucially, banks' emerging AI lending favours infrastructure over adopters — a structural mismatch between financing intelligence and financing its diffusion. signal: Strong bank earnings can coexist with credit that never reaches productive adopters.

  4. Brent rose ~1.2% to $115.24 after Houthis claimed a strike on a ~550,000 bpd Saudi refinery, still unconfirmed by authorities. Equinor doubled adjusted Q3 earnings before tax to $12.4 billion. Higher power costs and grid constraints raise the operating costs of accelerated computing before capacity becomes productive. signal: Energy reliability is becoming an input into capital productivity and industrial competitiveness.

  5. The Fed is expected to hold, with markets still pricing possible tightening; the 10-year Treasury yield sat near 5.01%. The ECB meets Thursday amid energy-driven inflation and French sovereign stress. The UK budget tests whether fiscal credibility can coexist with support for AI, defence and green investment. signal: The dilemma is no longer inflation versus growth, but inflation versus the financing of future productivity.

02

Two Capital Cycles, Three Scenarios

the hidden structural mechanism

01

The first cycle — producing intelligence

Finances semiconductors, computing, data centres and frontier models. When supply is scarce and demand accelerates, returns can be extraordinary — SK Hynix is the illustration.

02

The second cycle — deploying intelligence

Finances AI integration into manufacturing, banking, healthcare and services. Benefits emerge through cost reduction and new revenue, but adoption needs credit that smaller firms struggle to access.

Base case

Selective Concentration

Financing advantages reinforce established leaders — greater valuation dispersion, consolidation and geographic divergence. The primary scenario to monitor.

Downside

Productivity Bottleneck

Tighter credit and energy constraints slow adoption, weaken productivity gains and force infrastructure repricing — the most consequential risk.

Five Takeaways for Investors and Boards

where to look next

  • The AI Supercycle is becoming a financial productivity test — separate returns from scarcity from returns that survive expanding capacity.
  • The next opportunity extends beyond infrastructure to businesses deploying intelligence into existing productive assets.
  • Europe's banking paradox needs scrutiny: strong profits can coexist with restrictive lending.
  • Energy security is now integral to capital allocation.
  • The Great Split may become a productivity divide between those who can finance both AI infrastructure and its adoption, and those who cannot.

The next trillion dollars will not simply determine how much intelligence the world produces — it will decide how widely intelligence is deployed and who captures the returns. The ultimate scarcity of the AI Age may not be intelligence, computing or energy, but the financial capacity to make intelligence broadly productive.

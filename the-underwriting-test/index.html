The Underwriting Test

When money touches 5%, oil trades above $107 and AI acquires new safety constraints, capital stops rewarding acceleration alone — it rewards acceleration that can be underwritten.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 15, 2026 8 min read Capital
~5%U.S. 10Y Treasury — global hurdle rate enters a new regime
$795BHyperscaler 2026 capex; ~$1.08T expected in 2027
~$107Brent, with ~4M bpd of Saudi capacity rerouting around Hormuz
–5.9%SOX on Monday — the picks-and-shovels trade's first speed-limit test

“Velocity remains the source of competitive advantage. Underwriting determines who gets to keep it.”

The U.S. 10-year Treasury has touched 5%. Brent is back near $107. The Federal Reserve begins a meeting at which markets now assign roughly a 90% probability to the first rate increase in more than three years. At the same time, the five largest U.S. AI hyperscalers are still expected to spend about $795 billion this year and more than $1 trillion in 2027.

Europe's answer is becoming physical: the EIB has made its first investment in small modular nuclear reactors, while Christine Lagarde warns that Europe's data-centre deficit could widen sixfold within a decade. The AI Supercycle has reached a new phase: capital will no longer reward acceleration alone. It will reward acceleration that can be underwritten.

The Next Economy enters today with three simultaneous repricings. The price of capital has risen — the U.S. 10-year briefly crossed 5% for the first time since 2023, Germany's 10-year moved above 3.51% (its highest since 2009), and Japan's reached 3% ahead of a widely expected Bank of Japan increase on Friday. The price of physical scarcity has risen, with Brent around $107 while Saudi Arabia's East–West pipeline remains offline. And the price of technological uncertainty has risen: calls to slow frontier development triggered a 5.9% fall in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index on Monday.

FROM CAPITAL FORMATION TO CAPITAL DISCIPLINE

The first two years of the AI Supercycle asked whether capital could be raised. The next phase asks whether capital can earn more than it costs. That is a different problem — and at a 5% risk-free benchmark, an unforgiving one.

01

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

The repricing tests the whole cycle

  1. The U.S. 10-year touched 5% and trades around 4.99%. Markets assign a 90–93% probability to a Fed increase on Wednesday; in a Reuters poll, 86 of 101 economists expect a quarter-point move to 3.75–4.00%, with a majority expecting at least one more by March.

    At a 5% benchmark, an AI factory cannot be justified by capacity, market share or long-term optionality alone. It increasingly needs utilisation, pricing power, cash conversion and productivity evidence. Every project now competes with sovereign bonds, energy assets and infrastructure yields. signal: The AI Supercycle remains powerful, but financial gravity has returned.

  2. BofA estimates hyperscaler capex at about $795 billion this year and more than $1 trillion next year. Despite Monday's selloff, there is no evidence yet of cancelled data-centre construction or material order withdrawals; investors are explicitly waiting for such evidence before declaring the cycle changed.

    The market is repricing the probability of uninterrupted acceleration — not a collapse in actual infrastructure demand. That makes the Great Split more granular: capex supported by durable utilisation versus capex supported by perpetual frontier acceleration. signal: The strongest infrastructure serves inference, enterprise deployment, sovereign AI, physical AI and regulated workloads.

  3. Christine Lagarde warned Monday that Europe risks becoming dangerously dependent on foreign AI technology, that its data-centre shortfall could grow more than sixfold within a decade, and that faster adoption could raise European productivity by up to 4%. This morning the EIB announced its first SMR investment — a €40 million loan to Finland's Steady Energy for a 50-MW underground reactor, the first of a planned pipeline.

    Europe's AI challenge is a capital architecture problem, not a model problem. Institutional capital has crossed a boundary: nuclear is becoming part of Europe's AI financing stack. signal: Nuclear → firm power → data-centre bankability → AI capacity → industrial AI → productivity may be the most credible route to European autonomy.

  4. August industrial production rose 5.2% and high-tech investment rose 5.2%. But retail sales increased just 0.4%, fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% year-to-date, and property investment fell 19.9%.

    China is not simply slowing — it is reallocating economic velocity toward manufacturing, high tech, exports and strategic industries, away from property and household demand. The most consequential variable becomes the composition of capital formation. signal: Strong production capacity, weak domestic demand — an inflation benefit and a competitive threat for Europe.

  5. SoftBank-backed SB Energy is preparing a U.S. listing and will place up to $500 million of shares with Japanese investors; Nvidia has committed $1.5 billion at the IPO price and OpenAI has received warrants worth roughly $5.5 billion. Separately, Waymo, GO and Nihon Kotsu aim to launch Japan's first fully autonomous commercial taxi service in Tokyo in 2027, scaling toward about 100 vehicles.

    This is the transition from digital AI toward Industrial AI and Intelligent Motion — from model training toward power generation, data centres, inference, mobility and physical deployment. signal: The question shifts from who owns the best model to who can turn intelligence into physical economic output.

02

Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines

Second-order reads

01

Guardrails may reduce AI volatility, not AI value

Markets read calls for slower frontier development as negative for demand. But credible governance — predictable release cycles, model behaviour, enterprise liability and regulatory treatment — could make long-duration AI capex more financeable. The real enemy of capital is not slower acceleration; it is unquantifiable risk.

02

Trust becomes an enterprise-AI control point

Palantir, Nvidia and Booz Allen are reportedly restricting frontier-model use over proprietary-data concerns; Palantir has demanded irrevocable zero-data-retention guarantees. The next bottleneck is not inference cost or GPU availability — it is data sovereignty. Secure AI architecture may become a premium product category.

03

European Banking Union becomes industrial policy

Germany has shifted from blocking UniCredit's approach to Commerzbank toward negotiating conditions — a Frankfurt listing, headquarters and continued Mittelstand financing. A combined group would hold more than €1.3 trillion of assets. A continent financing gigafactories, SMRs, grids and defence cannot separate banking architecture from industrial architecture.

04

Energy and AI begin to finance one another

Nvidia invests in SB Energy; Google signs a 22-year nuclear contract in Finland; the EIB finances its first SMR. This is an increasingly closed loop: AI demand → energy project → bankable power → data-centre capacity → accelerated computing → AI revenue → more energy investment. Power is becoming an asset class produced by AI demand.

05

The Great Split moves from sectors to balance sheets

The distinction hardens into underwritable velocity — cash flow, energy access, secure data, long-term customers, productive utilisation — versus unpriced acceleration, which depends on cheap capital, rising multiples and unconstrained model progress. At 5% money, that distinction becomes decisive.

03

What To Watch Before The Close

Six pressure points

Key takeaways

The underwriting phase

  • The AI infrastructure numbers remain extraordinary — $795B this year, more than $1T next — but the environment around them has changed decisively.
  • Capital has stopped asking "how large can this become?" and started asking "what return will I earn?"
  • The productive chain — capital → energy → computing → intelligence → deployment → productivity → cash flow — must now prove every arrow.
  • The opposing chain — war → energy scarcity → inflation → 5% money → higher WACC → lower multiples — is equally powerful.
  • Underwritable Velocity can survive 5% money. Unpriced Acceleration cannot.

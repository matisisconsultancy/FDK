The Rate AI Built

The AI supercycle is lowering the cost of intelligence — while raising the price of capital needed to build it.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 5, 2026 7 min read Capital
~1.116EUR/USD at a 17-month low; France risk enters FX
>150 bpsFrance–Germany 10Y spread; euro-area fragmentation
4.235%Japan 30Y at a record sovereign funding cost
<20%October Fed hike probability; tightening risk recedes

“AI may eventually make the economy cheaper to run. Before it does, it is making the future more expensive to build.”

The strongest overnight signal did not come from Silicon Valley. It came from the Bank of Japan.

Deputy Governor Shinichi Uchida said this morning that AI has become a major positive demand shock, boosting investment and asset prices, potentially lifting the natural rate of interest. He also warned that massive AI-related bond issuance is already putting upward pressure on long-term yields — and that markets could correct if expected profits fail to arrive.

Markets are starting the week inside that contradiction. The probability of an October Fed hike has fallen below 20%, yet long yields remain elevated. The monetary cycle may be pausing. The capital cycle is not.

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

What if AI lowers the cost of intelligence — but raises the global cost of capital first?

02

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

the new regime

  1. Verified. The BOJ now explicitly identifies AI as a significant positive demand shock. Uchida said stronger investment, asset prices and capital accumulation could affect the natural interest rate, while heavy AI corporate bond issuance is already exerting upward pressure on long-term yields.

    The first-order effect of AI is not yet deflation. It is capital formation. The sequence is becoming clearer: AI investment → higher capital demand → higher long rates → stronger productivity later. The consequence is uncomfortable: AI can be bullish for growth and simultaneously bearish for duration. signal: AI is bullish for growth and bearish for duration at the same time.

  2. Verified. The euro fell roughly 0.7% this morning to a 17-month low as French fiscal concerns intensified. France's risk premium over Germany is above 150 basis points, around its highest since the euro-area debt crisis, while French equities and banks have already materially underperformed Europe.

    The transmission chain is now fiscal credibility → sovereign spread → bank funding → euro → corporate cost of capital. That makes France today's most important European market test. Europe wants to finance AI, defence, grids and industrial renewal precisely when sovereign capital is getting more expensive. signal: France has moved beyond a domestic bond story into a continental financing problem.

  3. Verified. Schneider Electric confirmed it will acquire U.S. industrial-software group PTC for $22.6 billion in equity value, or $23.7 billion enterprise value, paying a 42.3% premium, using equity and new debt. Separately, OpenAI and Synopsys agreed to build a specialized model for semiconductor design, with revenue sharing tied to improved chip-design outcomes.

    The strongest industrial companies are not merely adopting AI — they are buying the software layer around their physical assets. Schneider's logic: power + data centres + industrial software + AI. That is vertical integration for the Industrial-AI era. signal: Capital will reward those who control both the physical bottleneck and the intelligence layer on top of it.

  4. Verified. Brent was around $101.59 early Monday despite higher Middle Eastern crude exports and the G7 decision to release 100 million barrels from emergency stocks. EU electric-vehicle sales reached 1.64 million units in January–August, up 45% year on year, lifting EV market share to 22%. Diesel prices have risen about 38% since the Iran conflict began.

    The energy shock raises inflation today while accelerating substitution tomorrow: high oil → higher transport cost → better EV economics → more electrification → more grid demand. The mobility transition is increasingly driven not by regulation, but by relative economics. signal: The energy shock is inflationary now and electrifying later.

  5. Verified. Intesa Sanpaolo has added another €800 million of cash to its offer for Monte dei Paschi, whose shareholders vote on October 29; the transaction is valued around €34 billion. At the other end, Revolut is privately valued at $115 billion, has about 80 million customers and generated £1.7 billion of pretax profit in 2025 — despite lending far less than traditional banks.

    European banking is developing two competing scale models: balance-sheet concentration and software-driven distribution. The most exposed banks are those caught in the middle — neither large enough to dominate capital-intensive business nor digital enough to scale distribution cheaply. signal: The Banking Union is being built through transactions and technology, not regulation alone.

Where Capital Is Likely to Move

scarcity becoming cash flow

01

Accelerating

Power and grids; nuclear; data-centre electrical infrastructure; industrial software; semiconductor-design automation; HBM and advanced memory; contracted AI infrastructure; scaled banks; digital financial platforms; EV supply chains; energy storage; selected emerging markets with improving fiscal credibility.

02

Decelerating

Uncontracted compute; leveraged data-centre developers; long-duration equities without visible monetization; weak European sovereign duration; banks exposed to sovereign deterioration without scale; energy-intensive industrials without pricing power.

03

Brazil in Focus

Flavio Bolsonaro unexpectedly led Lula 47% to 45% in Sunday's first round, setting up an October 25 runoff. Markets had already associated Bolsonaro's improving prospects with a more business-friendly policy outlook.

Before the Close

what to watch

Key takeaways

the Great Split enters the cost of capital

  • AI is now influencing electricity markets, sovereign yields, corporate debt issuance, industrial M&A, banking scale and monetary-policy thinking.
  • The first advantage was intelligence; the second was compute; the next is the ability to finance both without destroying returns.
  • The Great Split will separate those who convert expensive capital into productivity from those who merely accumulate expensive assets.
  • Watch productive AI capital versus speculative AI capital — not AI versus non-AI.

The Conversion Test

The AI race is moving from capacity accumulation to economic selection — superior economics, not scarce capacity, will determine the next winners.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 7, 2026 8 min read Capital

97.4Anthropic GVI — #11 overall

“Scarce capacity created the first AI winners. Superior economics will determine the next ones.”

Advanced-wafer capacity remains tight. HBM remains strategically scarce. Grid access increasingly determines when compute becomes productive. Financing structures are expanding because the capital burden of deployment is rising.

The benchmark reduces the current AI cycle to one chain — Scarcity → Investment → Financing → Utilization → Returns — and three financial tests: revenue growth greater than capital-commitment growth; FCF growth greater than fixed-obligation growth; and incremental ROIC greater than incremental cost of capital.

This is why Microsoft and TSMC remain the two structural leaders; why Amazon, Micron and Synopsys remain accumulation candidates; and why Anthropic can remain a Global Velocity Leader at 97.4 while ranking outside the Top 10 at #11.

Company Actions — Reconciled Hierarchy

From structural leadership to high hurdle

ADD

Microsoft · TSMC

Structural leadership and economic conversion already align. Upgrade: maintain superior FCF, ROIC, pricing and execution. Downgrade: sustained deterioration in returns or utilization.

ACCUMULATE

Amazon · Micron · Synopsys

Structural thesis is strong; economics are confirming. Upgrade: stronger FCF, margins, utilization and incremental ROIC. Downgrade: capital burden or normalization outruns earnings.

HOLD

Meta · Schneider · Nvidia · Broadcom

Strategic quality is high, but the hurdle is elevated. Upgrade: better entry or upward earnings / ROIC revisions. Downgrade: further capital intensity, leverage or expectation risk.

WATCH

Alibaba

Strategic optionality remains high; return conversion is incomplete. Upgrade: durable margin and FCF improvement. Downgrade: capex remains ahead of returns.

HIGH HURDLE

Anthropic

Exceptional Structural Velocity, but financial commitments reduce resilience. Upgrade: revenue, margins and FCF outrun compute obligations. Downgrade: fixed commitments keep rising faster than cash.

Second-Order Effects

Where the cycle moves next

  1. Grid access increasingly influences deployment timing, utilization and valuation. signal: A missing grid connection can neutralize a chip subsidy.

  2. SPVs, supplier finance and leases extend deployment while moving technology risk into credit markets. signal: Technology risk is migrating into credit markets.

  3. Enterprise software, industrial automation, workflow platforms and vertical AI gain as infrastructure matures. signal: Downstream software captures value as infrastructure commoditizes.

  4. Expect more partnerships, financing ties and M&A. signal: Capital intensity is a consolidation engine.

  5. Infrastructure can be financed for a long time. Eventually, the system must self-fund. signal: Profitable demand, not capital, is the binding constraint.

Nvidia Won the Numerator. Warsh Now Sets the Denominator.

Nvidia proved the numerator is still accelerating; today Kevin Warsh sets the denominator that decides what those earnings are worth.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 15, 2026 6 min read Macro
+9%Nvidia yesterday — its highest-ever close
10 of 11S&P sectors declined despite a higher index
4.672%U.S. 10-year Treasury yield this morning
5.197%U.S. 30-year yield, near levels unseen since 2007

“NVIDIA is the numerator. Warsh is the denominator.”

The AI Supercycle has proved that earnings can still accelerate. Today the market asks whether the financial system will allow those earnings to retain their value. Nvidia surged 9%. Ten of eleven S&P sectors fell. The Great Split is no longer merely AI versus non-AI: it is becoming Earnings Velocity versus Discount-Rate Velocity.

Can the extraordinary growth of AI cash flows continue to outrun a 4.67% U.S. 10-year yield, a 5.20% 30-year yield and a Federal Reserve increasingly uncomfortable with inflation—or does the WACC now become the binding constraint on the AI Supercycle? Data cut: 07:45 CEST.

Yesterday delivered the cleanest expression yet of the new market regime. Nvidia rose almost 9% to a record close after projecting roughly 70% revenue growth next fiscal year. The Nasdaq gained 1.6%, the S&P 500 0.7% and technology 3.4%. Yet every other S&P sector fell. The rally spread to Micron, Broadcom, Intel and SK Hynix, but not to the broader economy. Overnight, markets stopped celebrating and started waiting: Taiwan rose 1.2% and Japan 0.5%, but Korea fell 1% and Hong Kong 0.3%. At 16:00 CEST, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote.

NVIDIA = THE NUMERATOR. WARSH = THE DENOMINATOR.

01

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

The regime, not the rally

  1. Nvidia's exceptional outlook produced a 9% stock gain, a 3.4% technology rally and record levels. Yet healthcare, consumer sectors and every other major S&P sector declined. This is not simply an AI bull market — it is an increasingly concentrated capital-allocation regime, moving toward companies that combine structural growth, scarcity and free cash flow. signal: AI is not pulling the whole economy upward; it is separating from it.

  2. The 10-year stands near 4.67%, the 30-year near 5.20%, and the Fed's target rate remains 3.50%–3.75%. The market's governing equation is moving toward Earnings Growth ÷ Cost of Capital rather than earnings growth alone. If Warsh restores inflation credibility without forcing long yields higher, AI assets gain duration. If he signals that 3.7% inflation requires materially tighter policy, the WACC rises and the weakest AI narratives reprice immediately. signal: Nvidia has Earnings Velocity; Warsh determines how it is discounted.

  3. Germany's Schwarz Group is investing up to €5.6 billion in a northern German data center starting at 240 MW, expandable to 1 GW — initially consuming energy comparable to roughly 600,000 households. In Australia, NextDC reported rising profitability but deteriorating efficiency: water use up to 2.40 litres per kWh from 2.25, PUE worsening to 1.49 from 1.44. AI may eventually become deflationary through productivity, but its industrialization can first be inflationary. signal: The next pool of excess returns migrates to the owners of the constraints — power, grids, memory, optics, cooling, land, capital.

  4. German industrial associations press Chancellor Merz for a tougher China policy; the 2025 trade deficit with China reached €89.3 billion, with Chinese manufacturers receiving three to eight times the state support of OECD peers. Schwarz's €5.6 billion project aims to reduce reliance on overseas cloud, while UniCredit now controls roughly 48% of Commerzbank. The European Pivot is moving from regulation toward balance-sheet capacity. signal: You cannot finance the Next Economy with fragmented banks and imported AI infrastructure.

  5. Brent trades around $89.45, down roughly 5.3% this week, as Iran and Oman work toward arrangements that could improve Hormuz shipping. Yet actual shipping remains only 5%–15% of normal activity. The oil price signals diplomatic hope; the physical infrastructure signals fragility. Next week's U.S.-hosted G20 finance meeting will focus on resilient energy supply chains, sovereign debt and sanctions enforcement. signal: Energy security has become a permanent investment category.

02

Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines

Structure beneath the tape

01

The Great Split is becoming vertical

Yesterday only technology rose inside the S&P 500. The divergence now runs the entire chain: accelerated computing → memory → optics → data centers → power → grids → financing. Capital migrates toward each successive bottleneck.

02

A productivity shock and a capital-intensity shock

AI can lower the marginal cost of intelligence, but creating that intelligence demands extraordinary investment in scarce physical resources. AI can be inflationary before it becomes deflationary — one reason the Supercycle and higher long-term rates can coexist.

03

The state is returning to price formation

Treasury buybacks shape long-duration bonds, U.S. sanctions shape trade flows, European industrial policy shapes sourcing, and governments regulate grid access for data centers. Capital allocation is becoming geopolitically conditioned.

04

Europe's real problem is scale

Germany's China debate, the Schwarz data center and UniCredit–Commerzbank point to one issue: fragmentation. Europe has technology, savings and engineering; its next advantage must come from scale in capital, energy, AI infrastructure, banking and industrial platforms.

03

What to Watch Before the Close

The decisive variables today

Key takeaways

The Velocity reading

  • Nvidia confirmed the numerator: revenue, cash flow and data-center demand are still accelerating.
  • Warsh confronts the other side of the equation — what price capital should demand to finance it.
  • The favored side of the Split: accelerated computing, HBM/memory, optical networking, power equipment, grids, cooling, data-center infrastructure, cybersecurity and well-capitalized banks.
  • Under pressure: high-duration businesses without free cash flow, heavily indebted firms, energy-intensive manufacturing without pricing power, and legacy automotive overexposed to China.
  • The winner is decided where two circles intersect: investment → earnings → free cash flow → new investment, against scarcity → inflation → yields → WACC → valuation pressure.

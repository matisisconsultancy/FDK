It is managing an economy in which productivity growth may no longer produce proportional employment growth.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 5, 2026 3 min read Macro · Capital

That requires: faster worker transition and technical education; portable benefits and lifelong learning; broader participation in capital ownership; deeper European equity markets; and infrastructure investment that lowers the cost of deploying AI across smaller companies. Europe must use the current recovery in activity to mobilize capital before higher rates and another energy shock close the window. AI policy cannot be separated from energy policy, capital-market policy or labor policy. The productive system is accelerating. The employment system is decoupling. The financial system is becoming more selective. ⸻ Strategic Agenda for Today Watch European industrial AI The European open will test whether investors reward Siemens’ record orders and industrial profit, Merck KGaA’s improved semiconductor-material outlook and the broader evidence that Europe’s established companies are becoming AI beneficiaries. Watch German industrial orders The data will indicate whether Europe’s improving PMI momentum is translating into real manufacturing demand rather than survey optimism alone. Watch US jobless claims Claims will provide another signal ahead of Friday’s employment report. The critical question is whether weaker hiring remains a controlled productivity transition or begins to broaden into labor-market deterioration. Watch the Asian semiconductor complex A stabilization would suggest profit-taking and valuation adjustment. Continued selling would indicate that investors are reducing exposure to the capital intensity of the AI trade despite strong physical demand. Watch Hormuz throughput—not the headline A credible agreement must produce measurable increases in shipping, exports and insurance availability. Gulf exports remaining 40% below pre-conflict levels is incompatible with genuine normalization. Watch the ten-year Treasury The 4.60% area is now the intersection of lower energy risk, slowing employment and persistent capital demand. A failure of yields to decline further would confirm that the structural term premium remains intact. ⸻ The Velocity Edge The services economy is growing. Employment is contracting. Corporate profits are accelerating. Technology shares are becoming less forgiving. Europe is discovering that its industrial incumbents may be better positioned for AI than its start-ups. Oil prices are falling while physical exports remain impaired. Bond yields are declining without returning to the old regime. These are not separate stories. They form the next architecture of The Great Split. Growth without hiring is not conventional stagnation. It is a reallocation of income, bargaining power and economic control. The strategic question is no longer only whether AI will raise productivity. It is whether companies and countries can translate that productivity into broader prosperity before the concentration of its gains destabilizes demand, politics and capital formation. The next market leaders will be those that convert intelligence into output. The next political leaders will be those that ensure output still becomes opportunity. AI is growing. Jobs are not. And that may become the most consequential economic divergence of the Age of Industrial AI. One by FDK

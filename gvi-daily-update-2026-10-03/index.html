GVI Daily Update

When weak jobs lift equities but fail to lift bonds, the market is telling us where the next scarcity lies — not in intelligence, but in credible capital.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 3, 2026 5 min read Capital
108.0Weighted headline GVI — underlying bias marginally lower
5.28%US 10-year yield, up ~5 bps despite a weak payroll report
~150 bpsFrance's 10-year spread over Germany, highest since the debt crisis
$34.76BGlobal equity fund inflows — but technology lost $2.63B

“Bond yields refused to believe weaker growth would make capital cheap again.”

Friday produced one of the most revealing market signals of the current cycle. US payrolls rose by only 29,000 in September, versus 90,000 expected; unemployment increased to 4.2%, July and August were revised down by a combined 60,000, and wage growth slowed to 3.0% y/y.

Yet after initially rallying, Treasuries reversed: the US 10-year yield ended around 5.28%, up almost 5 bps on the day, while the Nasdaq rose 1.2% and the S&P 500 0.7%. That divergence matters enormously.

The long end is beginning to trade independently of the Fed. A weaker economy reduced the probability of an October rate increase, but did not bring long-term borrowing costs down — fiscal risk, energy inflation, geopolitical uncertainty and term premium are increasingly dominating the monetary-policy transmission mechanism.

THE BIG TAKE

The long end increasingly reflects fiscal, geopolitical and inflation risk rather than merely Fed expectations — and that would fundamentally change the valuation regime for long-duration assets.

01

The European Mirror

The second half of the same problem

Euro-area inflation accelerated to an estimated 3.8% in September from 3.2%, with energy inflation at 18.8%. France's 10-year risk premium over Germany reached roughly 150 bps, its highest since the euro-area debt crisis, and French five-year CDS climbed to around 87 bps — nearly three times their level a month ago.

Beneath the macro, capital is already moving. Global equity funds attracted $34.76 billion in the latest week, but technology funds lost $2.63 billion while financials gained $1.13 billion and utilities $468 million — the market beginning to price the next phase of the AI Supercycle.

02

Recalculation

Only developments crossing both thresholds enter today's scores

Weighted headline GVI remains 108.0 at published precision; the unrounded underlying index edges marginally lower, with no ranking changes. The US adjustment (94.1, −0.1) reflects measured deterioration in labour-market breadth, with AI strength offsetting weakening macro. The France adjustment (81.2, −0.1) reflects a material repricing of sovereign risk.

04

Five-Pillar Readout

Where the scoring thresholds were met

Intelligence Infrastructure — strong, but selective. US hyperscaler capex is estimated at roughly $800 billion in 2026, with consensus near $1.1 trillion in 2027. Yet technology funds saw outflows while utilities and financials attracted money: the cycle stays strong; its ownership is broadening.

Enterprise Transformation — the US economy is splitting. Headline hiring weakened, yet construction added 11,000 jobs and manufacturing 9,000; factory employment has risen 72,000 since its December trough — labour reallocation: services down, AI infrastructure and industry up.

Smart Capital — the principal decelerator. The 10-year rising to ~5.28% despite weak payrolls suggests long-duration costs are decoupling from the next Fed meeting. In Europe, energy inflation lifts prices as sovereign fragmentation tightens financing independently of the ECB. France's spread versus Germany is ~150 bps against Italy's ~120 bps — an inversion that makes France, not Italy, the euro area's most visible large-economy sovereign stress point.

Innovation Velocity and Discovery Capacity — stable. No new variable clears today's scoring threshold.

05

Measured vs Provisional

What is scored and what is not

Measured and scored: US payrolls, unemployment and wages; the Treasury close; French spread and CDS; weekly fund flows. Provisional: Eurostat's 3.8% flash inflation estimate, not independently scored. Policy, impact not yet scored: the G7 release of 100 million barrels of diesel and crude reserves — Brent settled around $102.25, but the durable effect remains uncertain.

06

The Second-Order Effects

Five transmission chains to watch

  1. Weak payrolls first pushed yields down; then the long end reversed — a move that may matter more than the number itself. The mechanism is shifting toward FISCAL RISK + ENERGY + GEOPOLITICS + TERM PREMIUM → LONG-END YIELDS. signal: If that persists, a Fed pause may not automatically lower corporate WACC.

  2. Investors are still buying equities, but selling technology while buying utilities and financials: POWER → GRID → UTILITIES → CREDIT → INDUSTRIAL INFRASTRUCTURE. signal: The market is moving downstream into the assets required to finance and power AI.

  3. A French-German spread near 150 bps is not merely a French problem; France sits at the core of Europe's credit architecture and banking system. signal: France has moved above Italy in the risk hierarchy — the fragmentation story is being rewritten.

  4. A slower labour market reduces growth but raises pressure to automate. signal: Weak data could sharpen the divide between firms able to finance automation and those unable to.

  5. The G7 release may temporarily lower prices, but the system is constrained by refining, diesel and logistics, not crude. signal: Abundance at the headline conceals scarcity in the conversion layer.

Five Takeaways

For boards and allocators

  • Do not interpret weaker payrolls as an automatic duration signal — the US 10-year rose despite the jobs miss.
  • The AI trade is already rotating: technology funds are losing capital while utilities and financials gain it.
  • Europe has entered a three-variable stress regime: energy inflation + sovereign fragmentation + weak growth.
  • Watch France before Italy — its ~150-bp spread and rising CDS are now a central European systemic variable.
  • At a 5%+ long-end hurdle rate, AI deployment without economic conversion is expensive optionality.

GVI 108.0. Structural velocity remains high; financial velocity is becoming increasingly selective. The most consequential signal of the week is not that American employment weakened — it is that bond yields refused to believe weaker growth would make capital cheap again.

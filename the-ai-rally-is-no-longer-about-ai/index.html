The AI Rally Is No Longer About AI

The AI Supercycle remains intact, but value is migrating from the companies that produce intelligence toward those that control the memory, networking, electricity, infrastructure and capital required to industrialize it.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 15, 2026 7 min read Capital

“The cost of generating intelligence will keep falling; the value of everything required to industrialize it may keep rising.”

Value is migrating from models toward the physical and financial architecture required to industrialize intelligence. Will the next trillion dollars of AI value accrue to the companies producing intelligence—or increasingly to those controlling the memory, networking, electricity, infrastructure and capital required to deploy it?

Yesterday delivered the signal markets had wanted. U.S. CPI rose only 0.1% in July and 3.4% year over year, while core inflation increased 0.2% monthly and 2.5% annually. Combined with July's weak employment report, that has pushed market pricing toward a Federal Reserve pause in September: traders now assign roughly a 62% probability to no change.

But the equity reaction tells a deeper story. The S&P 500 rose only 0.26%, while CoreWeave surged 19%, Nebius 34%, Super Micro 19%, Nvidia 3%, Micron 4.9%, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index 2.5%. This was not simply another technology rally. Capital migrated specifically toward the companies building the industrial architecture of intelligence.

Overnight, Cisco reinforced the message. It now forecasts fiscal-2027 revenue of $72.2–73.4 billion, versus Wall Street's $68.69 billion expectation, after receiving $9.3 billion of AI-infrastructure orders in FY2026. Yet the stock fell after hours because expectations have risen almost as rapidly as revenues. The AI Supercycle remains intact. The valuation regime inside it is becoming much more selective.

KEY NUMBERS

The VIX fell to 14.45, its lowest since January, as the S&P closed 7,748.50 and the Nasdaq 26,588.49. Cisco expects ~$7.5B of AI infrastructure revenue in FY2027. Brent sits near $88 despite the continuing Hormuz impasse, with U.S. commercial crude stocks jumping +17.4M barrels, the largest weekly build since January 2023. Japanese wholesale inflation ran 7.2% in July.

01

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

verified and interpreted

  1. July inflation arrived around consensus: headline CPI eased to 3.4% from 3.5%, core moderated to 2.5%, and gasoline fell 2.9% during the month. The transmission mechanism has shifted—softer labor, contained inflation, lower probability of further tightening, less incremental cost-of-capital pressure, renewed appetite for AI infrastructure.

    This does not mean money is cheap. It means monetary policy has temporarily stopped becoming more restrictive—crucial because the AI Supercycle is becoming more capital intensive precisely as financial conditions remain historically demanding. signal: The winners will be companies generating returns well above the cost of financing their infrastructure.

  2. CoreWeave may be today's most important corporate signal: near-term accelerated-computing capacity is effectively sold out, backlog has reached $104.2 billion, and new contracts are being signed at increasingly attractive prices. Cisco provides the next link—AI is now driving networking demand, with $9.3 billion of AI infrastructure orders in FY2026, roughly $4 billion in the final quarter alone.

    The causal architecture is widening: Models → Accelerated Computing → Memory → Networking → Data Centers → Electricity → Grid Infrastructure → Financing. signal: The next winners may be found one, two or three layers downstream from the GPU.

  3. Brent has fallen toward $88 with no breakthrough on Hormuz. The reason is weakening demand: the IEA now expects world oil demand to contract 1.6M bpd in 2026, while OPEC cut its growth forecast to 580,000 bpd. Yet the IEA also expects supply to decline 4.3M bpd, leaving supply ~1.27M bpd below demand and a Q3 deficit near 1.8M bpd; Middle East output remained 8.3M bpd below pre-war levels in July.

    Oil below $90 should not automatically be read as bullish disinflation. Part of the decline reflects demand destruction—another Great Split, in which producers generate extraordinary cash flows while consuming manufacturers and households reduce real consumption. signal: For Europe in particular, the distinction between scarcity and demand destruction matters enormously.

  4. Norway's $2.3 trillion fund generated a record $184.3 billion first-half profit, driven principally by technology equities, and disclosed a $1.2 billion SpaceX holding for the first time. South Korea's new Seven Major SEED program targets SMRs, fusion, advanced renewables, quantum, space, biotechnology and critical materials—including a domestic 100-qubit processor by 2029 and 10 trillion won of materials investment by 2030.

    Sovereign capital and industrial policy are converging around the same portfolio: AI + energy + semiconductors + quantum + space + critical materials. This is not diversification. It is the construction of national productive capacity. signal: Countries are allocating capital as if competitive advantage were itself an investable asset class.

  5. European Q2 earnings are expected to grow about 22%, but nearly half comes from energy. Yesterday defense gained 1.1%, Vestas surged 19.7%, Nokia jumped 9.6%, TKMS gained 8.5%, and luxury fell 2.9%. This morning Thyssenkrupp raised the lower end of its 2026 adjusted operating-profit forecast to €600–900 million. Bank of America committed $250 billion to U.S. infrastructure financing by July 2027, following Morgan Stanley, while Germany's PBB returned to quarterly profitability.

    Europe is separating into two systems: one of energy infrastructure, defense, electrical equipment, automation, networks, banks and selective champions; the other of energy-sensitive consumers, luxury, structurally challenged manufacturing and businesses unable to pass costs through. signal: This is Europe's Great Split—and capital is increasingly choosing sides.

02

Hidden Patterns Beyond the Headlines

signals beneath the tape

01

Networking may become the next bottleneck

The market spent two years obsessing over GPUs; Cisco's results suggest the bottleneck is broadening. Accelerated computing without high-speed connectivity cannot become an AI factory. The next scarcity premium can migrate toward optical networking, switching, memory bandwidth and power delivery.

02

Monetary divergence is becoming industrial divergence

America's CPI is cooling enough to reduce rate-hike expectations; Japan's wholesale inflation runs at 7.2%, strengthening the case for the opposite move. Different costs of capital will shape where factories, data centers and semiconductor capacity earn acceptable returns. Capital geography is becoming monetary geography.

03

The market is distinguishing AI demand from AI economics

CoreWeave's backlog says demand is extraordinary; Cisco's guidance says it is spreading downstream. But Cisco falling after hours despite a strong forecast shows growth alone is no longer sufficient once expectations become extreme. The next phase will be judged by revenue growth × utilization × margins × free cash flow × capital intensity.

What to Watch Before the Close

four markers and a preview

  • Whether Europe's opening confirms the overnight rotation into technology infrastructure and industrials—Euro Stoxx 50 futures were up 0.35%, the Nikkei gained 1.61%.
  • Brent around $88: a decline from expanding inventories and weaker demand is economically different from one caused by a Hormuz settlement.
  • UK Q2 GDP, UK and euro-zone industrial production, and U.S. weekly jobless claims—can benign inflation coexist with enough real-economy momentum to protect earnings?
  • Cisco and the networking complex at the U.S. open: rotation from GPUs into networking would strengthen the value-migration thesis.
  • South Korea, whose chip stocks lead Asia while its government converts semiconductor success into energy, quantum, space and critical-materials investment—the clearest preview of a Discovery Nation.

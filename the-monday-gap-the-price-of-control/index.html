The Monday Gap: The Price of Control

Friday priced resilience; Monday will test whether it was justified — with AI leadership, 5%+ yields and $100+ oil in an unstable mix.

5.23%U.S. 10-year — the first key pressure point
$100+Oil per barrel weighing on inflation risk
5%+The prevailing hurdle rate for capital

“The price of capital is becoming a competitive moat.”

Friday priced resilience. Monday will test whether it was justified.

The market enters Monday with an unstable mix: AI leadership, 5%+ yields, $100+ oil. The key question is no longer whether AI remains the dominant investment theme. It is whether AI can still command capital when money and energy are both expensive.

That is the next phase of The Great Split.

Monday Morning — Three Tests

The order that matters

  1. If Brent reverses Friday's relief, inflation risk immediately returns to the front of the market. The chain is simple: oil up → inflation up → yields up → multiples down. The first losers are the most energy-intensive and rate-sensitive businesses; the first beneficiaries are scarcity assets. signal: Watch Brent for the fastest route back to inflation risk.

  2. Around 5.23% is the first key pressure point. If yields push decisively higher, Monday becomes a capital-allocation session rather than a growth session. The market will start asking which businesses genuinely earn above the new hurdle rate — favoring cash flow, pricing power, contracted infrastructure, power, grids and semiconductors, and punishing leverage without visible returns. signal: Above 5.23%, allocation replaces growth as the story.

  3. This is the decisive test. If AI leaders hold while oil and yields rise, the market is confirming extraordinary pricing power and earnings confidence. If AI leadership breaks as yields rise, the market would begin questioning not AI itself, but the economics of the AI capex cycle. signal: The most consequential signal of the Monday open.

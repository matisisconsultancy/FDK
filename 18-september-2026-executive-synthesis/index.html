18 September 2026: Executive Synthesis

The AI Supercycle survives; cheap capital does not — as central banks converge on tightening precisely when AI turns physical, energy-intensive and capital-hungry.

“Can AI productivity rise faster than the cost of financing the infrastructure required to produce it?”

Today's signal is bigger than another move in equities. The world's major central banks are converging toward a new tightening cycle precisely when the AI Supercycle is becoming more physical, energy-intensive and capital-intensive. The Fed has delivered its first rate increase since 2023; today the Bank of Japan raised rates to 1.25%, a 31-year high; markets expect further tightening across several major economies.

The fundamental question is therefore: can AI productivity rise faster than the cost of financing the physical infrastructure required to produce it? That is now the equation driving markets.

THE DENOMINATOR

5% money changes everything. Capital remains available, but every data center, AI factory, nuclear plant, railway, defense facility and acquisition must now compete against an increasingly attractive risk-free return.

01

The New Rate Regime

five forces reshaping the cost of capital

  1. U.S. Treasury yields moved higher again today as investors absorbed the new global rate regime. Wall Street was modestly lower in late trading, with the Dow down about 0.36%, the S&P 500 about 0.10% and the Nasdaq approximately flat. Europe performed worse: the STOXX 600 fell 1.1% to 635.45, with autos down 3.4%. signal: Every project now competes against an attractive risk-free return.

  2. The BOJ increased its policy rate to 1.25%, the highest in 31 years, by a 7–2 vote. Governor Ueda signaled that policy has entered a more pre-emptive phase focused on preventing inflation from overshooting. Curiously, the yen weakened because the two dissenters created uncertainty over the pace of subsequent tightening. For decades, cheap yen fed the global carry trade and exported capital abroad; now higher Japanese rates mean better domestic returns and greater competition for Japanese savings. signal: The world has lost its last major near-zero-cost funding anchor.

  3. Oil remains above $100, despite some easing today as China reportedly pressed Iran to restrain Houthi actions. The seven-month Middle East conflict continues to keep energy costs embedded in the global inflation outlook. More important than crude alone is diesel: U.S. diesel has exceeded $6 per gallon and European diesel is around $210 per barrel amid refinery disruption and historically tight inventories. Diesel directly feeds trucking, agriculture, construction, mining and industrial logistics. signal: Energy → physical costs → inflation → yields → WACC → the AI hurdle rate.

  4. This is the counter-signal that matters most. AI infrastructure provider Crusoe has just raised $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion post-money valuation. Demand for specialized AI-cloud and data-center capacity remains enormous despite higher yields. And today an even larger architecture became visible: South Korea and the United States are discussing projects within a $350 billion investment framework, including a proposed $22.3 billion, 6.3-GW Texas gas-power complex to serve AI data centers and semiconductor fabs, plus potentially as many as eight U.S. nuclear reactors. signal: AI is becoming an energy-and-infrastructure Supercycle.

  5. Today's European selloff exposed the other side. Volkswagen fell 5.6% after cutting its profit outlook amid restructuring costs, job-reduction provisions and weak Chinese performance. Porsche lost 4.9%. Europe possesses extraordinary engineering assets, but capital must migrate faster from low-return legacy capacity toward industrial AI, power, grid, defense, autonomy, rail, robotics, accelerated computing and intelligent mobility. signal: Europe's problem is not lack of capital — it is Capital-Conversion Velocity.

The Next 90 Days

three forces on a collision course

THE Q4 EQUATION

AI productivity velocity versus WACC + energy + physical scarcity. Whichever side accelerates faster will determine the next market regime.

The Velocity Insight

return on intelligence

Key takeaways

the dividing line for investors, companies and countries

  • The Fed raised rates, the BOJ raised to a 31-year high of 1.25%, and global sovereign yields moved higher.
  • Oil held above $100 and diesel remained at extraordinary levels, keeping physical inflation embedded.
  • European autos sold off — Volkswagen -5.6%, Porsche -4.9% — while AI infrastructure kept raising billions.
  • Governments and corporations began discussing energy projects measured in gigawatts and tens of billions.
  • The Great Split is no longer AI versus non-AI, but capital that produces productivity faster than WACC rises versus capital whose return arrives too late.

Intelligence creates possibility. Power makes intelligence physical. Capital makes it scalable. Productivity makes it economic. Velocity determines whether the return arrives before the hurdle rate catches it.

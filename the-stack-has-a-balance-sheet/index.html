The Stack Has a Balance Sheet

With 5% Treasuries, $104 oil and Microsoft's +3.66% rally, the AI Supercycle has moved from building the stack to making the stack pay.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 26, 2026 3 min read Capital
5.158%U.S. 10-year Treasury close
$104.32Brent crude settlement
+3.66%Microsoft, on its push into enterprise agents

“The supercycle remains intact. The free pass is over.”

Three numbers define the AI trade entering the weekend: 5.16% Treasuries, $104 oil, and a +3.66% move in Microsoft. Capital is expensive. Energy is costly. The market still pays for monetization.

The AI race has moved from building the stack to making the stack pay. Models proved capability. Compute proved demand. The next phase must prove economics — and the fundamental question is now simple: who can turn compute into cash flow fast enough to outrun energy and capital costs?

THESIS

Capital is expensive. Energy is costly. The market still pays for monetization.

01

Five Signals

The evidence entering the weekend

  1. The U.S. 10-year closed above 5.15%. Long-duration AI assets must now justify a materially higher risk premium. signal: ROIC minus WACC is the test.

  2. Microsoft expanded Copilot into coding, always-on agents and deeper Office integration. The market is shifting from model capability toward paid enterprise productivity. signal: The premium is moving toward monetization.

  3. Brent settled at $104.32, with Hormuz uncertainty unresolved. Energy raises both compute costs and, through inflation and rates, capital costs. signal: The electron is now inside the valuation.

  4. Europe wants data-center capacity to rise substantially by 2030, while gas inventories remain well below last year's level. The opportunity sits increasingly in grids, electrical equipment, semiconductor equipment, nuclear and automation. signal: AI ambition requires competitive power.

  5. Qatar has created Doha Investment, a new domestic investment arm within its sovereign-capital architecture. The broader signal is strategic: sovereign capital is increasingly directed toward domestic infrastructure, energy security and resilience, rather than portfolio diversification alone. signal: The race now extends into national balance sheets.

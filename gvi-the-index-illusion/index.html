GVI the Index Illusion

The next phase of the AI Supercycle is splitting technological leadership from equity performance — and the next alpha may arrive while the index falls.

The next phase of the AI Supercycle is producing a fundamental divergence between technological leadership, corporate profitability and equity-market performance. The result could be a new investment cycle in which the fastest-growing companies are no longer necessarily the strongest-performing stocks.

October 29 provided unusually revealing evidence. Alphabet declined 12.6%, contributing to a 1.4% fall in the Nasdaq. Yet approximately four out of five S&P 500 constituents advanced, even as the capitalization-weighted index fell 0.3%.

After the close, Amazon reported its fastest AWS growth in almost five years — revenue up 39.3%, group operating margin a record 13.9% — yet trailing-twelve-month free cash flow was negative $42.4 billion, against positive $14.8 billion a year earlier. Its shares fell roughly 6% after hours.

Five Hidden Patterns — Where the Next Alpha Will Emerge

second-order effects

01

Rotation beneath the indexes

A single exceptionally large company's decline can conceal broader resilience. Equal-weighted equities, selected industrials and firms outside the largest platforms may begin outperforming cap-weighted benchmarks — an emerging signal, not yet a confirmed rotation.

02

The capital-productivity phase

AI investment is accelerating but cash-flow performance is diverging. Valuations may grow increasingly sensitive to capital intensity, incremental ROIC and the timing of free-cash-flow recovery.

03

Scarcity moving through infrastructure

Advanced memory, electrical equipment, networking and automation are becoming indispensable to AI deployment. Vertiv, Amphenol, Schneider Electric and SK Hynix may capture additional value as constraints persist.

04

Velocity vs shareholder returns

A corporation can accelerate technologically while its equity underperforms. Earnings revisions relative to valuations may become more useful signals than absolute revenue growth.

05

Financial resilience as advantage

With the ECB holding its deposit rate at 2.50% on October 29 amid tighter financing conditions, firms with strong internal financing capacity gain strategic flexibility.

02

Where Capital Could Rotate Next

the transmission mechanism

Capital will increasingly differentiate between corporations generating productivity and those financing capacity that is not yet economically productive. The sequence to watch: Corporate Velocity → Earnings Revisions → Cash-Flow Expectations → Valuation Adjustment → Relative Equity Performance — the opportunity lies in identifying it before consensus adjusts.

Over a six-month horizon, FDK assigns 50% probability to selective broadening (infrastructure and enterprise software gain relative leadership), 25% to productivity acceleration (stronger cash conversion supports broader gains), and 25% to financing-led consolidation (credit pressure and investment delays widen differentiation). Probabilities are illustrative analytical judgments.

Five Takeaways

investors, executives and policymakers

  • Equity investors: focus on the divergence between earnings revisions, cash-flow expectations and valuations — growth alone is insufficient.
  • Credit investors: monitor infrastructure financing, customer concentration, refinancing exposure and the sustainability of investment commitments.
  • Portfolio managers: look beyond mega-cap technology toward companies controlling critical infrastructure and industrial capabilities.
  • Boards and executives: prioritize incremental ROIC, operating productivity, time-to-production and free-cash-flow conversion.
  • Policymakers: accelerate electricity infrastructure, advanced manufacturing, industrial AI adoption and capital-market development.

In the Next Economy, competitive advantage will determine who creates economic value — and valuation discipline will determine who captures the returns.

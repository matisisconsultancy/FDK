Where Markets, Capital and Geopolitics Collide

As AI borrowing reaches historic levels and cash-rich investors begin deploying, the next rotation will reward financial freedom and productive assets.

The signal1 min
  1. Record issuance meets record cash: US investment-grade bond issuance hit $1.88tn YTD, while Berkshire held $356.9bn in cash at September 30.
  2. Five tech giants — Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle — raised $370.7bn, roughly one-fifth of the total.
  3. Berkshire deployed capital, repurchasing about $11.6bn of shares July–October while keeping extraordinary flexibility.
  4. US growth stays resilient: 165,000 October jobs and 4.1% unemployment lifted the odds of a December Fed hike.
  5. AI volatility persists: the Nasdaq fell 3.4% on the week and South Korea's KOSPI dropped 6.02% Friday.
  6. The Great Split's new phase divides those needing continuous capital from those free to choose when to invest.
Read the full note ↓

The most consequential development emerging from global markets this weekend is a fundamental change in the distribution of financial power: the companies building the Next Economy are borrowing at an unprecedented scale, while some of the world's most financially resilient investors are beginning to deploy substantial cash reserves.

The technology companies are mobilizing extraordinary capital to build future computing capacity. Berkshire is using an enormous existing liquidity position to pursue investments and shareholder returns while retaining the freedom to respond. The Great Split is entering a new phase — between those whose growth requires continuous access to capital and those with the freedom to choose when, where and how to invest.

The next financial advantage is not access to capital. It is the freedom to allocate it.

01

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

the week ahead

  1. The five largest AI-related borrowers have issued more than $370bn this year, and Goldman Sachs estimates bonds could finance roughly 35% of hyperscaler capex in 2027. Yet markets already differentiate: Oracle's five-year CDS spread hit a record 325.45 basis points in October, while other borrowers access very different terms. signal: Reward financing quality and marginal returns, not the scale of AI investment.

  2. Operating earnings fell 6% on weaker insurance, but BNSF rose 19%, Berkshire Hathaway Energy 8% and Precision Castparts 30%. Financial optionality lets companies choose among opportunities rather than be governed by their obligations. signal: The next rotation may favour productive assets backed by financial flexibility.

  3. October added 165,000 jobs with unemployment at 4.1% and 0.4% monthly wage growth — keeping pressure on financing costs and raising December hike expectations. The November 10 CPI is the week's key macro test. signal: Lower sovereign yields are not the same as easier corporate financing.

  4. Inflation sits at 4.0%, corporate lending standards tightened a net 9%, and energy inflation runs at 20.8%. The November 9 Eurogroup weighs fiscal conditions, Banking Union and defence financing; France remains the critical test. signal: Europe's competitiveness now turns on the capacity to finance productive investment.

  5. Brent settled at $98.32 as Qatar advances a Strait of Hormuz proposal while Washington tightens pressure on Iran. Germany awarded about €12bn for 5.16 GW of capacity; Siemens estimates smart charging could save €10.6bn in grid investment by 2030. signal: The next edge is infrastructure productivity, not expansion alone.

02

Three Scenarios for the Next Phase

illustrative, next three months

50%

Disciplined reallocation

AI demand holds while capital turns selective — rotating toward cash generation, financing quality and productive assets.

30%

The financing squeeze

Inflation and credit spreads maintain pressure on returns, bringing further valuation compression and selective project delays.

20%

The productivity breakthrough

AI monetization and infrastructure efficiency improve faster than expected, driving broader earnings acceleration and renewed investment.

Five Takeaways

for investors, boards and policymakers

  • Equity investors: financial optionality is becoming an investment factor.
  • Credit investors: the quality of AI financing matters more than its aggregate volume.
  • Corporate boards: preserve the freedom to invest when competitors face constraints.
  • European policymakers: capital-market integration is now an industrial competitiveness priority.
  • Global strategists: the next Great Split is between financial commitment and financial freedom.

In the Next Economy, the greatest financial advantage may no longer be the ability to raise capital — it is the freedom to decide when, where and whether to deploy it.

The 5.35% Paradox

The Nasdaq is at a record and Nvidia is worth $5.76 trillion even as the U.S. 10-year touches 5.3493% — the market is not ignoring the cost of capital, it is deciding who can afford it.

“At 5.35%, the market is not saying growth is cheap. It is saying certainty is priceless.”

The Nasdaq is at a record. Nvidia is worth $5.76 trillion. The U.S. 10-year just touched 5.3493%. The market is not ignoring the cost of capital — it is deciding who can afford it.

Monday produced the clearest expression yet of The Great Split. The Nasdaq rose 1.05% to a record close, Nvidia gained 2.1%, and the S&P 500 advanced 0.66%. Yet the U.S. 10-year Treasury touched 5.3493%, its highest since 2002, while the 30-year briefly reached 5.7029%. The probability of another Fed hike this month has collapsed to roughly 23%, but long yields are still climbing.

That is not a contradiction. It is a repricing of who gets charged the new hurdle rate. Cash-rich AI leaders with visible earnings are trading through it. Sovereigns, leveraged infrastructure, weaker corporates and capital-intensive laggards increasingly cannot.

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

What if 5%+ long-term rates do not kill the AI boom — but instead concentrate it? The market may be moving from a single discount rate to two economic realities: one for companies able to self-finance acceleration, another for everyone dependent on external capital. That makes higher rates not merely a valuation problem — they become a competitive-selection mechanism.

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

verified reads

  1. VERIFIED. Treasury yields reached fresh 24-year highs even after weak U.S. employment data pushed the implied probability of an October Fed hike down from about 71% a week ago to roughly 23%. At the same time, the Nasdaq closed at a record and consensus points to roughly 27% S&P 500 earnings growth in the latest quarter, with more than half expected from companies benefiting from AI infrastructure spending.

    FDK READ. The market is separating the Fed rate from the structural price of capital. Weak jobs can restrain the central bank without lowering the long-end hurdle rate if fiscal deficits, inflation, energy risk and extraordinary AI investment keep demand for capital high. Duration is becoming expensive, but earnings certainty is becoming more valuable. The real risk is not a broad technology collapse — it is a widening valuation gap between self-financing growth and financed growth. signal: Duration is expensive; earnings certainty is the premium asset.

  2. VERIFIED. Euro-area business activity accelerated sharply in September. The composite PMI rose to 53.1, its strongest since April 2023, and new orders increased at the fastest pace in 41 months. Yet inflation jumped to 3.8%, while markets now price more than two additional ECB hikes by mid-2027. France's sovereign stress remains elevated and Spain has added political uncertainty with a snap election.

    FDK READ. Better growth should improve the investment case. Instead it may delay monetary relief precisely when governments need enormous capital for AI, defence, grids, rail, energy security and industrial renewal. The transmission chain is becoming dangerous: better activity → persistent inflation → higher ECB path → higher sovereign yields → higher corporate hurdle rates. If the euro remains weak while activity strengthens, growth alone cannot offset fragmented capital markets. signal: Stronger growth, weaker FX, wider spreads — Europe's problem is now the cost of financing expansion.

  3. VERIFIED. DeepSeek is reportedly close to raising at least 80 billion yuan — about $11.9 billion — backed by Tencent and CATL, per Bloomberg reporting carried by Reuters, which said it could not independently verify the report. Separately, Nvidia-backed Reflection AI launched its Beam open-weight model to compete with low-cost Chinese models. Morgan Stanley estimates U.S. data-centre developers face a 34% net power shortfall through 2028, equivalent to roughly 32 GW.

    FDK READ. Competition is pushing the price of intelligence downward, but the physical inputs to deploy it — power, accelerators, memory, networking, land, grid access, capital — remain scarce. Model economics and infrastructure economics are moving in opposite directions. The more intelligence commoditizes, the more valuable the bottlenecks beneath it become. Nvidia and Broadcom may retain stronger order visibility; secondary suppliers face greater risk if power delays push installations outward. signal: Own the bottlenecks, not the commodity above them.

  4. VERIFIED. Brent traded near $100.59 this morning. Gulf oil flows excluding Iran recovered to more than 81% of pre-war levels in September, supported by Saudi export resilience. Yet tanker attacks, insurance costs, rerouting and record Gulf-to-Asia freight rates remain elevated, while G7 countries are releasing 100 million barrels of emergency crude and diesel stocks.

    FDK READ. The headline oil price increasingly understates the economic shock. Barrels may move, but moving them is becoming more expensive — a second-order inflation channel: shipping risk → insurance → refined fuels → logistics → industrial margins. High hydrocarbon costs also improve the relative economics of EVs, storage, rail and urban mobility. The energy transition is being accelerated by the very instability that makes capital more expensive. signal: Barrels are flowing; friction is the new inflation channel.

  5. VERIFIED. Intesa Sanpaolo's improved €35 billion bid for Monte dei Paschi has secured the support of MPS's largest shareholder, Delfin, which owns 17.6%. MPS shareholders vote on their defence strategy on October 29. Meanwhile South Korean authorities are investigating attacks on several major banks after President Lee Jae Myung said there were signs AI had been used in some hacking incidents; Shinhan, KB Kookmin and others reported cyberattacks and data breaches.

    FDK READ. These stories belong together. Banks increasingly need scale to absorb technology investment, AI deployment, cybersecurity, regulatory capital, data infrastructure and cross-border competition. That raises the minimum efficient size of a financial institution. Italian consolidation is not simply M&A — it is a structural response to rising fixed costs across the financial system. signal: Scale is becoming the resilience asset in banking.

Final Take — The Hurdle Has Changed

cash-flow velocity versus cost of capital

  • A 5.35% U.S. Treasury yield would once have broken a technology rally — it is not breaking this one.
  • That does not mean the price of capital no longer matters; the market has decided who is allowed to ignore it.
  • The strongest AI platforms can fund investment, secure power, capture scarcity and compound earnings despite high rates. The weakest cannot.
  • The Great Split is no longer simply AI versus non-AI — it is cash-flow velocity versus cost of capital.

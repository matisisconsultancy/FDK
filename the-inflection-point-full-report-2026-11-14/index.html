The Inflection Point: Full Report

The Fed's $3 trillion AI warning and a 5% Treasury shock are separating technological value creation from financial value capture.

The most consequential development in global markets is no longer the correction in AI equities. It is the discovery that the financial commitments underpinning the AI Supercycle are larger, more interconnected and more vulnerable to rising rates than corporate balance sheets alone reveal.

On November 13 the Federal Reserve elevated artificial intelligence to the centre of its financial-stability assessment: 87% of surveyed market contacts cited AI-related risks, up from 50% in May, while advanced-computing debt issuance reached roughly $680 billion through October 2026 — nearly five times all of 2023.

The result is not a collapse of the AI thesis. It is a separation between technological value creation and financial value capture — the Great Split entering the balance sheet.

01

The Fundamental Question

duration mismatch

Is the AI Supercycle building the productive capital of the next economy — or accumulating the next generation of financial liabilities before its productivity dividend arrives?

The liabilities arise immediately; the productivity gains arrive progressively. That financial-duration mismatch is the heart of the matter. Financing risk can be transferred, divided and repackaged across debt investors, private-credit funds, infrastructure vehicles, banks and strategic suppliers — but it cannot be made to disappear.

02

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

tap to expand

  1. The November 13 report found 87% of 23 contacts citing AI risk, in a survey conducted in September and October — before Nvidia's leadership upheaval. Issuance has expanded nearly fivefold since 2023 and now represents ~31% of nonfinancial investment-grade issuance, drawing comparison with the late-1990s telecom cycle. signal: The next premium attaches to independently sustainable cash flows, not merely contracted revenues.

  2. The ten-year yield rose ~28bps to ~5.14% (touching 5.17%); the two-year climbed ~33bps. Headline PPI rose 0.3% monthly but 6.5% annually, with electronic components up 31.7% year-on-year — a direct link between physical AI inputs and inflation. signal: At 5% yields, the question is whether value arrives soon enough to justify today's committed capital.

  3. After Jensen Huang's departure, CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO, intensifying scrutiny of strategic financing commitments. Anthropic's IPO is priced at $70–78, a fully diluted valuation near $1.4 trillion, raising up to ~$64.4B plus a contemplated $26B private placement — against ~$895B in multi-year compute commitments. signal: A successful IPO proves financing appetite, not that projected commercial returns have been achieved.

  4. The STOXX 600 closed at 664.14, up over 2% on the week; banks gained over 4%. Siemens Energy reported €8.8B pre-tax free cash flow; Germany advanced a 2027 budget with €210.4B of new borrowing; Italy was affirmed at BBB+/Positive. Euro-area Q3 GDP rose 0.4% with flat employment. signal: Europe's challenge is converting mobilized capital into productivity — not increasing the volume of spending.

  5. IEA data show global supply at 105.4 mb/d in October and inventories up 52 million barrels, with a Q4 surplus expected; Brent settled at $95.84. Yet onshore stocks are low and Hormuz risks persist, as the U.S. and China discuss reciprocal $30B tariff cuts ahead of the Xi–Trump meeting. signal: Energy security is becoming a component of the cost of capital — and of competitive advantage.

03

Three Scenarios for the Next Six Months

FDK central view: the middle case

01

Productive Broadening

AI utilization and enterprise adoption strengthen while financing remains available, producing broader earnings participation and improving capital returns.

Central

The Great Balance-Sheet Repricing

Markets differentiate aggressively on financial transparency, leverage and cash-flow quality — driving valuation dispersion, selective consolidation and migration toward productive assets.

03

Systemic Financing Squeeze

High rates and weaker commercial returns trigger credit tightening across interconnected infrastructure, causing project delays, distressed financing and wider spillovers.

Five Takeaways for Investors and Boards

what to do now

  • Treat AI financing transparency as a valuation variable — assess debt, leases, guarantees and strategic investments together, without double-counting.
  • Separate technological demand from financial resilience; strong semiconductor orders do not guarantee returns for every operator.
  • Reprice the duration of capital: higher yields raise the importance of cash-flow timing, refinancing and asset utilization.
  • Anticipate differentiated sovereign capacity — Europe must turn spending into productivity, not just debt.
  • Prepare for a new hierarchy rewarding transparent structures and returns independent of continuing external capital.

The next great redistribution of global wealth will not be decided by who can finance the largest AI ambitions — it will be decided by who can turn those ambitions into durable cash flow without letting the invisible balance sheet overwhelm the value intelligence creates.

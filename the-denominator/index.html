The Denominator

The AI Supercycle survives — but cheap capital does not, as the world's central banks tighten just as intelligence becomes physical, energy-intensive and capital-intensive.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann September 18, 2026 5 min read Capital

$100+Oil per barrel, seven months into the Middle East conflict
$3.9BRaised by AI-infrastructure provider Crusoe at a $30.9B post-money valuation
$350BU.S.–South Korea investment framework under discussion

“Intelligence creates possibility. Velocity determines whether the return arrives before the hurdle rate catches it.”

Today's signal is bigger than another move in equities. The world's major central banks are converging toward a new tightening cycle precisely when the AI Supercycle is becoming more physical, energy-intensive and capital-intensive. The Fed has delivered its first rate increase since 2023; today the Bank of Japan raised rates to 1.25%, a 31-year high; markets expect further tightening across several major economies.

The fundamental question is therefore: can AI productivity rise faster than the cost of financing the physical infrastructure required to produce it? That is now the equation driving markets.

THE DENOMINATOR

5% money changes everything. Capital remains available, but every data center, AI factory, nuclear plant, railway, defense facility and acquisition must now compete against an increasingly attractive risk-free return.

01

Today's Signals

the forces repricing the world

  1. U.S. Treasury yields moved higher again today as investors absorbed the new global rate regime. Wall Street was modestly lower in late trading, with the Dow down about 0.36%, the S&P 500 about 0.10% and the Nasdaq approximately flat. Europe performed worse: the STOXX 600 fell 1.1% to 635.45, with autos down 3.4%. signal: Every asset must now clear an increasingly attractive risk-free return.

  2. The BOJ increased its policy rate to 1.25%, the highest in 31 years, by a 7–2 vote. Governor Ueda signaled that policy has entered a more pre-emptive phase focused on preventing inflation from overshooting. Curiously, the yen weakened because the two dissenters created uncertainty over how rapidly subsequent tightening will occur. For decades, cheap yen fed the global carry trade and exported capital abroad; now higher Japanese rates mean better domestic returns and greater competition for Japanese savings. signal: The world has lost its last major near-zero-cost funding anchor.

  3. Oil remains above $100, despite some easing today as China reportedly pressed Iran to restrain Houthi actions. More important than crude alone is diesel: U.S. diesel has exceeded $6 per gallon and European diesel around $210 per barrel amid refinery disruption and historically tight inventories. Diesel directly feeds trucking, agriculture, construction, mining and industrial logistics. signal: Energy flows straight into physical costs, inflation, yields, WACC and the AI hurdle rate.

  4. This is the counter-signal that matters most. Crusoe has just raised $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion post-money valuation, with demand for specialized AI-cloud and data-center capacity remaining enormous. And an even larger architecture became visible: South Korea and the United States are discussing projects within a $350 billion framework, including a proposed $22.3 billion, 6.3-GW Texas gas-power complex to serve AI data centers and fabs, plus potentially as many as eight U.S. nuclear reactors. signal: AI investment is migrating from GPU-and-data-center toward power, nuclear, gas, grid and industrial capacity.

  5. Today's European selloff exposed the other side. Volkswagen fell 5.6% after cutting its profit outlook amid restructuring costs, job-reduction provisions and weak Chinese performance. Porsche lost 4.9%. Europe possesses extraordinary engineering assets, but capital must migrate faster from low-return legacy capacity toward industrial AI, power, grid, defense, autonomy, rail, robotics, accelerated computing and intelligent mobility. signal: Europe's problem is no longer lack of capital — it is Capital-Conversion Velocity.

THE GREAT SPLIT

The dividing line is no longer AI versus non-AI. It is becoming capital that produces productivity faster than WACC rises — versus capital whose return arrives too late.

Key takeaways

September 18 closes a remarkable week

  • The Fed and the BOJ both raised rates; global sovereign yields moved higher.
  • Oil held above $100 and diesel remained at extraordinary levels.
  • European autos sold off — Volkswagen –5.6%, Porsche –4.9%.
  • Yet AI infrastructure kept raising billions, and governments began discussing energy projects measured in gigawatts and tens of billions of dollars.
  • The new hurdle rate is the test for investors, companies and countries alike.

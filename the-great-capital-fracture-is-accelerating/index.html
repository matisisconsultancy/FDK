The Great Capital Fracture Is Accelerating

As French bonds reprice, China's industry accelerates and Wall Street prepares to test $1.2 trillion in AI capex, the hidden variable is capital productivity.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 19, 2026 5 min read Capital
~$1.2 trillionGoldman estimate for five hyperscalers' 2027 AI capex (a forecast)
~143–145 bpsFrance–Germany 10Y spread
+4.5% YoYChina Q3 GDP, above the 4.4% consensus
~$104/bblBrent crude, European morning (significant intraday volatility)

“The first AI race was about computing power; the second is about financing power.”

The global economy is entering a more consequential phase of The Great Split. French sovereign bonds are being repriced as political unrest intensifies. China has reported stronger-than-expected growth driven by industry and exports, while domestic investment remains deeply depressed. And Wall Street is preparing to test whether an estimated $1.2 trillion in hyperscaler capital expenditure planned for 2027 can generate adequate returns.

The hidden connection is capital productivity: technological acceleration is expanding investment requirements precisely as sovereign credibility, energy security and financing costs become more powerful constraints on growth.

The fundamental question: will the AI Supercycle create enough productive capital to overcome the world's growing financial constraints—or will the concentration of capital become the defining source of competitive advantage? The first phase of AI rewarded technological scarcity; the second, infrastructure accumulation. The emerging third phase will differentiate those who can transform capital into sustainable returns from those whose growth depends on increasingly expensive financing.

01

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

capital, not announcements

  1. French markets fell as the political crisis intensified: the CAC 40 declined ~1.4%, banking shares 2.2–2.6%, and 10-year yields climbed toward 4.86%, the Bund spread nearing 145bps. The core issue is reconciling fiscal consolidation, social protection and productive investment. Higher borrowing costs reduce fiscal flexibility, pressure investment and feed back into risk premiums. The Great Split is moving from industrial performance toward the capacity of governments to finance competitiveness. signal: Watch sovereign spreads and bank funding costs—equity declines alone do not establish systemic contagion.

  2. Nasdaq futures rose ~1.1% after a brutal week in which the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 11.4%, its longest losing streak since 2008. The next earnings cycle is no longer a test of revenue growth but of the economic productivity of unprecedented capex. Suppliers, operators and application providers face different exposures to financing and replacement. Microsoft's reported withdrawal from an Nscale UK data-centre project—attributed partly to grid delays into the 2030s—shows the physical dimension: a data centre without timely power cannot earn its assumed returns. signal: The next advantage is the lowest sustainable cost of delivering usable intelligence, not the largest announced capacity.

  3. Q3 GDP rose 4.5% YoY (vs 4.4% expected). Industrial production rose 5.5%, but retail sales only 1.1%; fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% and property investment 20.6% over nine months. China is strengthening as a producer of the Next Economy faster than it is strengthening consumption—a structural asymmetry. The US is testing financial returns on AI capex; China is testing whether extraordinary industrial capacity can produce a balanced expansion. Different forms of The Great Split. signal: Monitor exports, advanced manufacturing, private investment and consumption separately—not GDP as a single gauge.

  4. Brent fell ~2% after US sanctions relief on Russia, but physical vulnerabilities persist: Gulf crude flows dropped to ~8.5m b/d (seven-day average) from over 20m the prior week, with at least ten vessel incidents reported. Accelerated computing, industrial AI and electrified manufacturing all depend on reliable, competitively priced electricity. Energy volatility raises operating costs and required investment, transmitting through inflation expectations and discount rates into capital productivity. signal: The new scarcity is reliable, competitively priced electricity available when productive assets need it.

  5. After MSCI warned of a possible emerging-market declassification, Jakarta's benchmark fell more than 5% following Friday's 11.6% drop; the rupiah weakened toward 18,048 and the 10-year yield rose toward 7.4%. Goldman estimates full exclusion could trigger ~$5.6 billion in passive outflows. Access to capital depends not only on growth but on governance, liquidity and institutional credibility—a financial variable for sovereign funds. signal: In the Next Economy, capital formation is a competitive capability of national institutions, not merely an outcome of GDP.

THE HIDDEN PATTERN

Two costs of capital are emerging—the US 10-year traded near 5.07% as French yields rose toward 4.86%—technological velocity is not economic velocity, and sovereign and corporate risk are becoming more interdependent. The decisive distinction is between capital committed and capital productively employed.

02

The Midday Verdict

selective repricing

The global economy is not experiencing a uniform deterioration in investment conditions. It is experiencing an increasingly selective repricing of capital. Technological demand remains strong, but investors are differentiating more aggressively between sovereign credibility, financing resilience, technological leadership and demonstrated returns.

FDK structural assessments at midday: accelerated-computing demand very high; AI infrastructure financing risk elevated; European sovereign-risk differentiation widening; French political and fiscal risk elevated; energy-security risk critical; global financial fragmentation intensifying; The Great Split widening. These are qualitative judgments, not calculated Global Velocity Index readings.

Five afternoon tests could reprice markets: Wall Street breadth beyond megacaps at 15:30 CEST; the French–German spread and any spread into Italian bonds; credible physical supply from the Russian sanctions relief; Wednesday's Meta results and semiconductor capex guidance; and the European political calendar—France's budget negotiations and a Moody's sovereign review on Friday, October 23, with Spain's election adding a further variable.

The first AI race was about computing power; the second is about financing power; the third will be about the economic productivity of that power. The next advantage will belong to those who convert scarce capital into productive intelligence faster than financial fragmentation, energy constraints and rising risk premiums can erode its returns.

21 September 2026: Provisional Nowcast

The FDK Global Velocity Index holds at 108.0 as China rises +0.1 to 91.1 on memory sovereignty crossing into mass production.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 21, 2026 7 min read Capital
108.0Weighted Global Velocity Index (2025=100), unchanged
91.1China GVI, up +0.1 from 91.0
96%Model confidence
4.76%US 2-year yield — financing hurdle elevated

“The AI race is becoming a full-stack economic race.”

The Weighted Global Velocity Index remains 108.0 (2025=100), unchanged at one decimal place, though the provisional unrounded daily movement is +0.01. The 30-day regime is structurally accelerating — with memory, financing architecture and energy resilience becoming the next decisive layers of the AI stack. Model confidence: 96%.

Today produces one country-level revision: China rises +0.1 to 91.1. The trigger is unusually clean. CXMT's fifth-generation DRAM technology platform has entered mass production, with new 24Gb LPDDR5X products already in volume production. CXMT says the platform can produce at least 50% more gross dies per wafer than its prior generation while increasing per-die capacity by 50%.

At the same time, global Smart Capital conditions remain restrictive. US 2-year yields are around 4.76%, markets still price a meaningful probability of another Fed hike in October, and Brent — although down sharply this morning — remains above $100.

The AI Supercycle is broadening from compute scarcity into memory, networking, power and financial engineering. The next advantage lies in controlling more of that stack simultaneously.

Overnight signals

The evidence behind today's moves

  1. This is the strongest overnight GVI signal. CXMT's fifth-generation DRAM platform is no longer a research roadmap — it is in mass production. The company says its process has reduced active-area half-pitch to 11.95 nanometres, uses quadruple patterning, and yields at least 50% more gross dies per wafer than the previous generation on a normalized basis. Its newly unveiled 24Gb LPDDR5X products are also in mass production.

    The significance exceeds mobile memory alone. Memory is becoming a critical bottleneck in the AI production system: accelerated computing → HBM/DRAM → memory bandwidth → inference throughput → system economics. The platform does not eliminate China's gap in HBM or leading-edge lithography, nor should company-reported parity be treated as verified industry equivalence. But the evidence clears the GVI threshold because it is operating capacity, not prospective capex. signal: China rises 91.0 to 91.1, with contributions from Intelligence Infrastructure and Innovation Velocity.

  2. SoftBank has launched $10 billion of dollar-denominated bonds plus €1 billion of euro-denominated senior unsecured notes to finance its OpenAI investment and general corporate purposes. Proceeds are intended in part to fund the third $10 billion tranche of SoftBank's OpenAI follow-on, scheduled to close on October 1. Dollar bonds span 3½-, 5½- and 7½-year maturities; euro bonds run four and six years. Pricing is scheduled for September 24.

    SoftBank's disclosed OpenAI follow-on totals $30 billion, taking cumulative investment to about $64.6 billion, subject to completion. But the bonds have been launched, not yet priced or settled, and the productive assets financed are primarily outside Japan. signal: Japan remains 84.9 — Smart Capital momentum strengthens, domestic productive-capacity score does not yet.

  3. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng concluded talks in New York, with the US side proposing a bilateral AI safety and national-security notification mechanism ahead of the Trump–Xi summit. The two sides also discussed establishing a formal US–China AI dialogue. The proposal is not yet an agreement and has not been implemented.

    Conceptually it is important for Discovery Capacity: the two largest AI powers are beginning to consider the equivalent of an incident-notification architecture. If implemented, it could eventually increase the resilience component of Discovery Capacity for both countries. signal: Zero numerical GVI weight today — watch variable only.

  4. Brent crude fell roughly 2.1% to $101.63 early Monday, while US crude fell to about $98.15. Saudi exports recovered to just over 4 million barrels per day in September, versus only 2.4 million bpd in August, per Kpler data cited by Reuters. A meaningful improvement — but it does not yet meet the GVI persistence threshold for reversing the energy penalty.

    The East-West pipeline remains impaired, Hormuz-related risk persists, and analysts cited by Reuters estimate global oil and refined-product inventories may now hold only 5–10 weeks of cushion, versus 15–20 weeks roughly two weeks earlier. signal: Energy price relief measured; energy-system normalisation not yet demonstrated. The Smart Capital drag moderates but is not removed.

  5. South Korean jet-fuel exports to Europe reached around 129,000 barrels per day in September, the highest since October 2022. July jet-fuel production hit an almost seven-year high, while refinery runs rose 16% month on month. This is insufficient to change the already high 88.4 score, as the evidence relates more to refining resilience than directly to AI productivity. signal: Korea combines advanced semiconductors with a large, export-capable industrial and energy-processing base — increasingly important as the AI economy becomes more physical.

  6. Energy Aspects estimates Europe could face a 510,000-barrel-per-day jet-fuel deficit in Q4, while inventories in the Amsterdam–Rotterdam–Antwerp hub recently fell to a seven-year low. No European country is downgraded today, as this is a sector-specific fuel imbalance rather than a broad loss of productive capacity. signal: Europe possesses substantial intelligence, industrial and scientific capacity, but its physical-energy system remains more vulnerable than several competing blocs.

The architecture of Velocity

Key takeaways

Implications across the stack

  • Investors: the opportunity migrates from one dominant bottleneck to a portfolio — advanced lithography, memory, optical networking, transformers, power generation, grid equipment, cooling and long-duration financing. The strongest assets sit where structural scarcity meets high utilisation and contracted demand.
  • Executives: the AI question moves from "which model should we use?" toward "which parts of the intelligence stack must we control to protect our economics?" The critical capability is architectural control, not simply AI adoption.
  • Policymakers: technological sovereignty comes from systematically closing bottlenecks across a chain — research → capital → manufacturing → energy → deployment → skills → reinvestment — not from a single flagship component.
  • The next evolution of the FDK equation: VELOCITY = Intelligence × Infrastructure × Capital × Absorption × System Integration.

Europe’s AI Gigafactory Moment

The ACS–Telefónica–Santander alliance may mark the moment Europe moves from AI policy to AI-scale capital formation.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 15, 2026 7 min read Capital

“In the Next Economy, advantage belongs to those who can industrialise intelligence, not merely invent it.”

Europe's AI debate is entering a different phase. For much of the past three years, the central question was whether Europe could regulate artificial intelligence without regulating itself out of the race. Then came a second question: whether Europe possessed enough capital, energy and technological infrastructure to convert its formidable industrial base into an AI-native economy.

The news emerging from Spain this week suggests that a third—and more consequential—phase may now be beginning. The European Commission has cleared the joint venture bringing together ACS, Telefónica, Banco Santander and Spain's public technology investment vehicle behind Spain's candidacy for a European AI gigafactory.

Seen in isolation, this is a large infrastructure project. Seen through the lens of the AI Supercycle, it is something much more important: evidence that Europe is beginning to understand the fundamental economic law of the Next Economy—intelligence has become an infrastructure industry.

THE MIGRATION

The European AI race is beginning to migrate from declarations toward physical capital formation. That distinction changes everything.

Why It Matters

The recursive capital cycle

  1. The AI Supercycle is forcing the global economy through one of the largest capital reallocations since electrification. Every additional layer of intelligence requires physical systems: accelerated computing, electricity generation, grids, transformers, cooling, fibre, memory, networking, land, data centres, and enormous quantities of financial capital. signal: The emerging strategic unit is the AI industrial system, not the software company.

  2. AI demand → accelerated computing → electricity demand → grid investment → data-centre construction → fibre and connectivity → semiconductor demand → industrial AI deployment → productivity → higher returns on capital → further AI investment. signal: Countries able to compress this chain acquire a powerful Velocity Advantage.

  3. The Internet primarily connected people and information. AI increasingly connects intelligence to production—embedding inside banking, pharmaceuticals, defence, logistics, automotive manufacturing, energy, railways, robotics and public administration. signal: The demand base is far broader—it is the convergence of technology × infrastructure × energy × finance × sovereign capital.

The New European AI Infrastructure Map

Comparative advantages emerging

01

France — The Energy Advantage

Europe's strongest structural position: a nuclear fleet delivering large-scale, low-carbon, competitively priced electricity. France exported roughly 92 TWh in 2025. At Choose France 2026, SoftBank outlined plans potentially reaching €75 billion and Data4 expanded to ~€30 billion. Its AION consortium unites Ardian, Artefact, Bull, Capgemini, EDF, iliad, Orange and Scaleway.

02

Germany — The Industrial AI Advantage

Europe's largest concentration of advanced industrial demand. Deutsche Telekom and NVIDIA have launched a major Industrial AI Cloud in Munich—with Siemens, SAP, Agile Robots, PhysicsX and others—including as many as 10,000 NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs, lifting German AI processing capacity by ~50%. The prize: the operating layer of European manufacturing.

03

Spain — The Infrastructure–Finance Convergence

Renewable energy, land, telecommunications, global construction capabilities and sophisticated banks form an unusually complementary ecosystem. The ACS–Telefónica–Santander gigafactory could mobilise ~€5 billion and demonstrate that AI infrastructure is a national competitiveness platform, not merely a technology-sector investment.

04

The Nordics — The Energy and Cooling Advantage

Renewable electricity, cooler climates, strong grids, political stability and engineering expertise. Reuters finds new hyperscale facilities planned for 2026–2028 are being located on average ~175 km from major urban centres, versus only 46 km in 2022–2025. AI is beginning to redraw economic geography.

05

Italy — The Industrial Opportunity, and the Risk of Delay

A large industrial base, advanced manufacturing clusters, utilities, telecoms, banks and strategic positioning. Its danger is not the absence of assets but fragmentation. If Italy treats AI as another digital-policy program, it risks missing the deeper capital transition underway.

What Comes Next

Five scarce resources

Key takeaways

Across investors, boards and policymakers

  • For investors: treat AI gigafactories as an infrastructure asset class, and watch second-order beneficiaries—electricity, grids, transformers, cooling, fibre, networking, engineering and construction.
  • For CEOs and boards: AI strategy can no longer be delegated to the CIO; secure long-duration relationships with infrastructure providers before capacity becomes scarce.
  • For European policymakers: accelerate permitting, grid connections and approvals; public capital should de-risk and crowd in private capital, not substitute for it.
  • For national governments: identify coalitions combining energy + telecommunications + finance + construction + technology, or risk becoming consumers of intelligence generated elsewhere.
  • The next twelve to twenty-four months may determine much of Europe's AI infrastructure map for the next decade.
THE VELOCITY INSIGHT

Phase one was about models. Phase two was about chips. Phase three is about infrastructure. Phase four will be about who converts that infrastructure into industrial productivity fastest. Europe may finally be entering Phase Three.

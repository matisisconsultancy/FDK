The Bottleneck Moves

AI demand is not slowing — the constraint is migrating, from GPUs to memory to power to capital to execution.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 1, 2026 7 min read Capital

“The first AI race was for compute. The next is for control of the bottleneck.”

The first morning of Q4 opens with a revealing contradiction. U.S. inflation came in softer than expected, cutting the market-implied probability of an October Fed hike to 38% from 50%. Yet the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield still reached 5.306%, its highest since 2007, and the 30-year touched 5.634%.

Nasdaq and S&P 500 futures are up around 0.3%, while European futures are down roughly 0.75%. Brent is near $98, after gaining more than 14% in September. At the same time, Micron has delivered another extraordinary confirmation of AI infrastructure demand: quarterly revenue more than quadrupled to $54.23 billion, its next-quarter revenue guide is $61.5 billion versus $57.02 billion expected, and customers have now committed $32 billion under long-term memory-supply agreements.

The message for Q4 is becoming clearer. AI demand is not slowing. The constraint is migrating — from GPUs to memory, from memory to power, from power to capital, from capital to execution.

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

As AI scarcity migrates across the stack, who can capture the next bottleneck without destroying returns at a 5%+ cost of capital? Not who can build the most — but who can secure the scarce input, monetize it and finance the next constraint before competitors do.

01

Five Overnight Signals

What moved while you slept

  1. Softer U.S. inflation reduced expectations for an October Fed hike, but long yields barely relented. The 10-year Treasury touched 5.306% and the 30-year 5.634%, while the dollar stayed close to a two-month high.

    If front-end policy expectations soften while long yields remain above 5%, the market is increasingly pricing forces beyond the next Fed meeting: fiscal supply, energy risk, higher nominal growth and extraordinary structural demand for investment capital. The new equation is: lower policy-rate fear ≠ lower cost of capital. The supercycle can survive a 5% risk-free rate — but only if productivity and cash flow rise fast enough to justify it. signal: Watch the long end of the bond market more closely than the next Fed meeting.

  2. Micron says customer financial commitments under long-term supply agreements have risen from $22 billion in June to $32 billion, mostly through cash deposits. It expects memory and storage markets to be materially tighter in fiscal 2027–28 and has secured agreements covering most of its 2027 HBM production. Fiscal fourth-quarter revenue more than quadrupled to $54.23 billion versus $51.07 billion expected, while planned U.S. investment has risen above $250 billion through 2035.

    The accelerated-computing stack is repricing in sequence: HBM → memory capacity → packaging → power → network. A bottleneck that becomes contractual through deposits and multi-year commitments acquires a different economic quality from a simple cyclical shortage. Customers are effectively pre-financing supply, transferring investment risk upstream and strengthening pricing visibility for the owners of scarce capacity. signal: Treat memory as a strategic layer, not a secondary semiconductor category.

  3. The Financial Times, cited by Reuters, reports that Tencent agreed to a five-year arrangement with Oracle giving it access to roughly 100,000 advanced AI chips housed across Southeast Asian data centers. The reported agreement is worth about $7 billion, including roughly 30% upfront. Reuters said it could not independently verify the report. This comes immediately after DeepSeek and Huawei announced work on programming tools designed to strengthen China's domestic Ascend computing ecosystem.

    This is the Trump–Xi transmission mechanism in microcosm. China is simultaneously building technological autonomy and buying access to foreign compute where autonomy is not yet sufficient. That is not decoupling — it is selective interdependence under strategic pressure: domestic stack at the core, global access at the margins. signal: Managed interdependence is replacing binary decoupling.

  4. South Korea has formally unveiled a $22.3 billion gas-fired power project in Texas designed to supply AI data centers, as part of its wider $350 billion strategic U.S. investment package. Seoul also plans $120 billion for eight U.S. nuclear reactors, while Washington and Seoul will examine LNG infrastructure in Alaska. Separately, Singapore's $405.5 billion Temasek plans offices in Riyadh and Abu Dhabi, deepening its exposure to Gulf capital partnerships.

    Sovereign capital is moving beyond portfolio allocation; it is becoming an instrument for building the AI production function itself — state capital, energy, technology, trade access and private infrastructure fused together. AI sovereignty will increasingly be negotiated through power plants, grids, reactors and bilateral investment frameworks. The strategically important asset is becoming bankable electricity attached to compute. signal: Sovereign capital is now following the electrons.

  5. September harmonized inflation accelerated to 5.0% in Spain, 4.1% in Italy, 3.4% in France and 3.3% in Germany, driven largely by energy. Core inflation remained at 2.4%. Eurozone inflation is expected at roughly 3.6% when the bloc-wide number is released Friday, and markets are pricing four additional ECB hikes over the next year.

    Europe faces the most difficult version of the AI macro equation: it needs more investment precisely as energy is expensive, capital is expensive, and monetary policy is turning less supportive. The competitiveness metric therefore changes — not announced gigawatts or subsidy envelopes, but bankable MW × utilization ÷ time-to-cash-flow. Europe's biggest risk is no longer underinvestment alone; it is that the cost of delay compounds faster than the capital it mobilizes. signal: The inflation shock is colliding with the need to accelerate investment.

THE CRITICAL TEST

Δ Free Cash Flow / Δ AI Capital — increasingly the difference between velocity and financial leverage disguised as velocity.

Key Takeaways

For investors, executives and policymakers

  • The AI cycle is not weakening — it is becoming more discriminating. Own the bottlenecks before they commoditize, but demand contracts, utilization and pricing power.
  • Distinguish AI companies that buy scarcity from those that sell it; the second group currently has the stronger economics.
  • For executives: pilots are no longer enough. Track time-to-deployment, percentage of workflows at scale, incremental productivity and cash return on AI capital.
  • For policymakers: the competitive unit is no longer the company but the national capital stack — energy × compute × capital × permitting × talent × software.

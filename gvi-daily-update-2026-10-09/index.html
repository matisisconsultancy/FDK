GVI Daily Update

Intelligence is becoming abundant; the capital to industrialise it is becoming increasingly concentrated.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 9, 2026 5 min read Capital
108.0FDK Global Velocity Index (unchanged)
4.4%Annual euro-area corporate lending growth, July
$5BFirmus IPO withdrawn amid valuation concerns
197,000US initial unemployment claims

“Intelligence is becoming abundant. The capital to turn it into power is not. Own the conversion.”

Intelligence is becoming more accessible; the capital required to industrialise it is becoming concentrated. The next Great Split will not be between those who possess AI and those who do not — it will be between those who can finance intelligence, convert it into cash flow and compound their advantage, and those who cannot.

Three developments define the regime. The ECB confirms credit is selective: euro-area corporate lending grew 4.4% annually in July, but larger and AI-related borrowers are better positioned, with no significant economy-wide crowding out yet identified. Australia's Nvidia-backed Firmus abandoned its $5 billion IPO after investors challenged valuation and financing assumptions, turning to private capital. And scrutiny of frontier AI intensifies — reported differences between OpenAI revenue measures underline the need to distinguish recognised revenue, run rates, contractual commitments and realised cash flow.

The FDK Global Velocity Index remains at 108.0. No new national observation clears the materiality, persistence, additionality and confidence thresholds required for a score change.

03

Five Signals Defining Global Velocity

The financial hierarchy hardens

  1. The ECB's account reveals a differentiated market: large companies retain better access, smaller businesses depend on banks, and AI-related borrowers attract capital while higher-risk borrowers face constraints. Access to capital is becoming a determinant of access to intelligence. signal: Access to capital is becoming a determinant of access to intelligence.

  2. Initial jobless claims fell to approximately 197,000 and August wholesale sales rose 1.8% — resilience, but not proof of faster structural productivity. Elevated long-duration borrowing costs raise required returns on data centres, power and compute. The financial advantage now rests on balance-sheet strength. signal: Technological advantage endures; financial advantage now rests on balance sheets.

  3. German exports fell 0.8% in August, including a 6.3% drop in shipments to the US, complicating stronger production evidence. Germany must convert engineering, automation and AI into competitiveness — not merely investment. signal: Convert adoption into competitiveness, not just more investment.

  4. Firmus's withdrawn IPO shows investors may accept AI infrastructure's importance while rejecting its price. The underwriting questions — operational versus announced capacity, contracted demand, utilisation, time to free cash flow, residual value — are now unavoidable. signal: AI ambition no longer guarantees access to public capital.

  5. Europe's strengths in research, engineering, power and industrial software meet an uneven ability to finance transformation. Thousands of industrial firms may lack scalable capital to deploy AI across operations. signal: Europe's competitiveness challenge is now financial as much as technological.

04

Second-Order Effects

The evolution of financial power

01

Concentration compounds

Strong balance sheets keep investing while weaker rivals postpone, dilute or sell. Cheaper models need not mean democratised ownership of the infrastructure that monetises them.

02

Compounders vs. capital consumers

Cash-generative leaders reinvest internally; externally financed developers carry refinancing sensitivity. The test is whether cash flows arrive fast enough to support the capital structure.

03

Rotation toward capture

Capability → compute → infrastructure → deployment → productivity → cash flow. The next reward goes to businesses embedding intelligence into real economic activity.

04

Credit as early warning

Credit markets scrutinise contracted revenue, coverage and refinancing. Widening AI-related spreads could signal stress earlier than equities.

05

Europe needs capital-market integration

If only large firms can finance transformation, the intra-European productivity gap widens. The answer is deeper long-term capital markets, not subsidies alone.

Five Key Takeaways

From promise to returns

  • Investors: own the balance-sheet advantage — self-financing capacity, contracted demand, strong cash conversion; treat repeated external funding as a material risk.
  • Boards: impose financial discipline on AI, using the framework applied to acquisitions and industrial expansion.
  • CEOs: accelerate the conversion cycle — time from AI investment to productivity, margin and free cash flow is decisive.
  • Policymakers: democratise access to productive capital; smaller firms' ability to finance adoption may matter as much as access to models and compute.
  • Capital allocators: enforce the test — capital → capacity → utilisation → productivity → cash flow → ROIC > WACC.
05

Financial Verdict

GVI 108.0 — structural acceleration intact

GVI: 108.0. Structural acceleration remains intact; financial selection is intensifying. The AI Supercycle is not running out of technological opportunity — it is entering a regime in which ownership of financial resources determines who can transform opportunity into advantage. That is why the next Great Capital Divide reaches beyond technology, shaping industrial concentration, markets, national competitiveness and the distribution of productivity gains.

Intelligence is becoming abundant. The capital to turn it into power is not. Own the conversion.

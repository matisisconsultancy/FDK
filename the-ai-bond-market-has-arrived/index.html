The AI Bond Market Has Arrived

AI is no longer only a capex cycle — it is becoming a global credit cycle, and that will decide who can keep scaling when the hurdle rate stays above 5%.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 7, 2026 8 min read Capital

“When AI enters the bond market, velocity acquires a maturity date.”

AI is no longer only a technology capex cycle. It is becoming a global credit cycle — and that will decide who can keep scaling when the hurdle rate stays above 5%.

Three developments overnight make the transition unusually visible. SpaceX is reportedly seeking $40 billion of financing, led by Apollo, to acquire Nvidia chips — around $10 billion in bank loans and $30 billion in investment-grade debt. Black Hills will invest $1.8 billion in new generation capacity to power a Google data centre in Wyoming. And Marvell has lifted its fiscal-2028 revenue forecast to around $20 billion as hyperscalers accelerate custom-chip investment.

Meanwhile, the U.S. 10-year is back around 5.3%, India has raised rates for the first time in almost four years, Brent is again above $101, and Asian equities are weaker despite fresh records on Wall Street. The contradiction is becoming the regime: AI investment is accelerating precisely as the cost of financing acceleration remains structurally high.

THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION

When AI infrastructure becomes an asset class, does scale become safer — or does leverage become the next systemic risk? For three years the decisive question was access to compute. The next one is harder: who can finance compute, power and infrastructure at scale — and still earn above the cost of capital? The Great Split is moving from AI versus non-AI to self-financing AI versus externally financed AI.

01

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

capital formation, not AI, is the common thread

  1. Reported, not yet company-confirmed. Reuters, citing the Financial Times, reported SpaceX is seeking around $40 billion to fund Nvidia chip purchases — roughly $10 billion of bank loans and $30 billion of investment-grade debt. Morgan Stanley estimates AI infrastructure may require $1.5 trillion of external financing by 2028.

    This is the structural shift. AI infrastructure is moving from corporate capex → structured finance → bank lending → bond markets. Once that happens, the cycle transmits directly into credit spreads, duration, refinancing risk and institutional fixed-income portfolios. The key variable is no longer demand for Nvidia chips — it is the borrower's ability to convert those chips into cash flow before the debt matures. signal: The next AI warning signal may appear in credit before it appears in equities.

  2. Verified. Black Hills plans $1.8 billion of investment between 2027 and 2029 for Google's proposed Cheyenne data centre, constructing 564 MW of new generation within a broader 2.7-GW resource mix, with Google covering the full cost of electricity supply. Blackstone-backed AirTrunk also announced an additional $1 billion for its Japan hyperscale campus, taking capacity beyond 300 MW.

    The important word is not electricity — it is contracted electricity. Long-duration commitments convert megawatts into predictable cash flows, moving the scarcity premium toward generation, grid access, microgrids, nuclear, gas backup, storage and transmission. The physical infrastructure around AI has what the model layer often does not: visible duration of revenues. signal: Contracted power is one of the most investable parts of the AI supercycle.

  3. Verified. Marvell raised its fiscal-2028 revenue forecast to around $20 billion, versus $18.2 billion expected. It now sees roughly $12 billion of custom-chip revenue in fiscal 2029 and 2031 revenue between $70 billion and $90 billion; its disclosed Google contract could generate as much as $120 billion through 2033. Marvell shares rose about 6%, Broadcom roughly 4%.

    The market is broadening from general-purpose accelerators to custom ASICs, networking, optical interconnects, HBM, power management and rack-scale architecture. Hyperscalers want more control over economics and power consumption. That does not end Nvidia's dominance — it creates a parallel market for specialization. signal: The semiconductor trade is moving from one bottleneck to an ecosystem of bottlenecks.

  4. Verified. India raised its repo rate 25 basis points to 5.5%, its first hike in nearly four years, shifting from neutral to "calibrated tightening" while raising its growth forecast to 7.1%. India's 10-year moved to 7.2655%. In the U.S., the 10-year remains around 5.3% despite markets cutting October Fed-hike odds to around 20%. Today's $39 billion 10-year auction and the September Fed minutes will test whether investors distinguish a slower Fed from a higher term premium.

    The monetary story is no longer synchronization — it is global capital scarcity. Energy, defence, AI infrastructure, sovereign deficits and industrial policy all compete for the same capital, pressuring long rates even when central banks hesitate. signal: Monetary relief does not guarantee cheap capital.

  5. Verified. French 10-year yields fell more than 11 basis points Tuesday after Marine Le Pen outlined spending cuts, narrowing the France–Germany spread from almost 160 bps last week to around 132 bps; the euro recovered toward $1.125. Yet France's borrowing costs remain near multi-decade highs and fiscal space has narrowed materially. Meanwhile Brussels is weighing a broad corporate levy — including on U.S. technology companies — to find new EU revenues without a direct digital-services tax.

    Europe's industrial ambition now meets its funding constraint. AI, defence, rail, grids and energy transition all require scale, but sovereign credibility determines the price of that scale. The new equation: industrial ambition + fiscal credibility + capital-market depth — miss one, and the other two become harder. signal: Sovereign credibility is now priced as a financial asset.

The Hidden Pattern

the common denominator is capital formation

03

Where Capital Is Likely to Move

own the asset that gets paid before the risk arrives

01

Accelerating

Investment-grade AI infrastructure, custom silicon and networking, power generation and grid assets, contracted data-centre infrastructure, private credit and infrastructure finance, nuclear, gas backup and storage, large banks able to fund infrastructure, and AI-enabled productivity.

02

Becoming More Selective

Secondary semiconductor suppliers, data-centre developers without secured power, AI companies dependent on serial funding rounds, European industrials exposed to high sovereign funding costs, and EV manufacturers without scale or pricing power.

03

Decelerating

Uncontracted compute, long-duration growth without free cash flow, leveraged infrastructure dependent on falling rates, labour-heavy financial-service models with weak digital economics, and sovereign duration where fiscal credibility continues to deteriorate.

Key takeaways

the third AI race is about capital structure

  • The AI supercycle began as an equity story; it is becoming a bond-market story — and debt imposes a timetable equity narratives do not.
  • Interest must be paid, assets utilized, cash flows materialized, debt refinanced.
  • The strongest platforms will use investment-grade capital to secure chips, power and infrastructure before competitors can.
  • The weakest will find that demand is not enough if the financing structure cannot survive the cycle.
  • The first AI race was about intelligence, the second about compute, the third is about capital structure — and it may prove the most decisive.

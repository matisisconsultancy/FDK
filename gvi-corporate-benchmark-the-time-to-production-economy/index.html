GVI Corporate Benchmark the Time-to-production Economy

Corporate leadership is increasingly decided by the ability to turn committed capital into operational deployment, productivity and cash — faster than capital becomes more expensive.

FdK Francesco De Leo Kaufmann October 22, 2026 5 min read Capital

30%Lower power consumption targeted for equivalent AI output (DSX)

“Megawatts into productivity, productivity into cash.”

The Next Economy is crossing a new frontier. Corporate leadership is increasingly determined by the ability to shorten the interval between committed capital, operational deployment, customer productivity and cash generation.

Three developments on October 21 reinforce this thesis. Nvidia introduced its DSX POD architecture, claiming preassembled AI factories could cut deployment at suitably prepared sites from six months to as little as 14 days, with related DSX technology targeting up to 30% lower power consumption for equivalent AI output — conditional claims still awaiting broader verification. SAP reported 26% cloud-revenue growth and 36% free-cash-flow growth. Iberdrola disclosed €10.1 billion of investment across the first nine months of 2026, 62% allocated to electricity networks.

The central question

Which corporations can convert technological capabilities and scarce infrastructure into sustainable cash returns faster than their cost of capital increases?

01

The New Corporate Accelerators

time, cash and power

Nvidia — time-to-production. The DSX POD architecture transfers onsite assembly, cabling, testing and commissioning into standardized manufacturing, implying a sequence of factory integration → faster commissioning → earlier utilization → revenue acceleration → improved capital productivity, plus a second variable: productive AI output per unit of electricity. The economics must still be demonstrated at scale; a 14-day target does not eliminate grid-connection constraints, permits or customer financing.

SAP — enterprise cash conversion. Third-quarter cloud revenue reached €6.67 billion, free cash flow rose to €1.72 billion, and cloud backlog reached €24.14 billion — evidence that enterprise software converts digital infrastructure into cash, though the AI-specific contribution cannot be isolated.

Iberdrola — the power infrastructure platform. The company expects regulated grid assets to rise from roughly €56 billion to more than €90 billion by 2030 — a growth target, not realized capital productivity, still dependent on regulation, financing and execution.

SAP and Iberdrola are significant candidates for future GVI leadership-universe expansion; neither receives a provisional Top-10 score without a complete comparable assessment.

02

Five Hidden Patterns

what the data signals

01

Time-to-production is a financial variable

Every month between capital commitment and revenue affects project economics. The premium may migrate toward companies controlling infrastructure already connected, commissioned and commercially productive.

02

Power efficiency is competitive differentiation

The next key operating metric may be useful intelligence output per megawatt-hour, adjusted for utilization, cost and reliability.

03

Physical and digital are converging

Semiconductor systems, electrical networks, cooling, automation and industrial software increasingly operate as one production architecture.

04

Cash conversion counterweights capex

Investors are likely to distinguish companies generating cash from AI-enabled applications from those primarily financing capacity.

05

Europe's industrial capabilities matter more

Schneider Electric, Siemens, Siemens Energy and ASML occupy critical infrastructure positions; the opportunity is to translate them into faster productivity and sustainable returns.

03

Investor, Board And Policy Takeaways

from announced capacity to demonstrated output

Investors should evaluate leadership through time to productive revenue, infrastructure utilization, power efficiency, free-cash-flow conversion and incremental ROIC. Monitor credit conditions, capital commitments, electricity availability and customer concentration together — strong technological demand does not automatically produce attractive equity returns.

Boards should make time-to-production a formal capital-allocation KPI, evaluate AI investment against measurable gains in productivity and returns, and preserve balance-sheet flexibility, particularly when large projects depend on external suppliers, financing partners and electricity infrastructure.

Policymakers face a frontier defined by the interaction of innovation, electricity networks, financing and industrial execution. For Europe, faster grid permitting, reliable electricity, engineering talent and effective industrial AI adoption will be decisive. The objective should be to accelerate productive investment, not merely increase announced expenditure.

The Next Economy will be won by corporations that turn megawatts into productivity, and productivity into cash, faster than capital becomes more expensive.

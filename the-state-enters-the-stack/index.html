The State Enters the Stack

AI is no longer financed by technology companies alone — governments, utilities, banks and industrial balance sheets are now moving into the same capital chain, and capital formation itself is becoming industrial policy.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 4, 2026 7 min read Capital
$4.2BU.S. nuclear financing — state capital enters the AI power stack
$42BBroadcom financing for Anthropic — vendor finance becomes strategic
$125.2BAnthropic's 5-year TPU commitment — compute becomes long-duration credit
72%Euro-area AI funding from internal cash — Europe's scaling constraint

“Do not just own scarcity. Own scarcity with financing power.”

AI is no longer being financed by technology companies alone. Governments, utilities, banks and industrial balance sheets are now moving into the same capital chain.

The newest evidence is unusually clear. Washington is preparing a $4.2 billion loan to Vistra to expand nuclear output as AI data centres push U.S. electricity demand higher. Broadcom has agreed to provide Anthropic with up to $42 billion of infrastructure financing. In Japan, JERA, Dell and RHAELM are developing a $15 billion, 400-MW AI data-centre project with Apollo as financial partner. Europe, by contrast, still finances most corporate AI investment from retained earnings.

The implication is bigger than AI. Capital formation itself is becoming industrial policy. And because today is Sunday, the operative market horizon is Monday, 5 October.

The old hierarchy was simple: algorithms, GPUs, models. The new hierarchy is broader — compute → power → financing → infrastructure → strategic control. That is where The Great Split is moving next.

01

Five Signals Shaping the Next Economy

compute to capital

  1. VERIFIED. The U.S. government plans to lend Vistra about $4.2 billion to uprate at least three nuclear stations. Vistra's fleet produces more than 6.5 GW, and Washington has explicitly linked rising electricity demand to AI data centres, transport electrification and other power-intensive uses. The administration has set a goal of quadrupling U.S. nuclear capacity by 2050.

    This is a structural break. AI infrastructure is becoming too strategic — and too capital intensive — to remain a purely private technology story. The state is entering the stack because electricity has become part of computational sovereignty. The scarcity premium is migrating from chips toward firm power, grids, nuclear, storage and transmission. signal: The next advantage depends as much on megawatts secured as GPUs purchased.

  2. VERIFIED. Broadcom has agreed to lend Anthropic up to $42 billion, potentially financing roughly one-third of Anthropic's $125.2 billion five-year TPU lease commitment. Broadcom is expected to become deeply tied to Anthropic both as supplier and financier.

    The AI cycle is evolving from sell the chip to finance the customer who buys the chip. That accelerates demand but changes the risk. The critical variable is no longer simply backlog — it is the quality of the balance sheet underneath the backlog. That makes credit spreads, lease structures, utilization rates and refinancing schedules increasingly important leading indicators for AI equities. signal: Revenues, hardware sales, customer credit and financing exposure now reinforce one another.

  3. VERIFIED. ECB data show 72% of euro-area companies expect to finance AI investment through internal funds. Only 16% expect bank loans, 6% equity or venture capital and just 1% debt securities. More than 80% expect to rely on a single source of finance. Meanwhile, France is seeking around €54 billion in budget savings for 2027 as debt-market pressure and domestic protests rise.

    The U.S. is using corporate debt, private capital, vendor financing and public balance sheets to scale the Next Economy. Europe is still asking many companies to finance transformation largely out of cash flow. Internal finance favors incumbents, tangible assets and incremental projects — and penalizes intangible, high-risk, high-velocity investment. signal: Europe risks losing the AI race not because it cannot invent, but because it cannot finance scale fast enough.

  4. VERIFIED. Renault plans to invest more than €10 billion in France over five years in electric vehicles and affordable cars. EVs reached a record 42% of French new-car registrations in September, with Renault explicitly citing higher fuel prices as a demand catalyst. At the same time, the G7 has agreed to release 100 million barrels of oil and diesel reserves after China restricted fuel exports, while a reported Houthi attack targeted an Aramco facility in Riyadh over the weekend.

    The energy shock raises inflation and financing costs, but also shortens the economic payback of electrification: oil insecurity → higher fuel cost → better EV economics → faster electrification → more grid demand. The winners are not merely EV manufacturers — they are batteries, charging, storage, grids and electricity providers. signal: The mobility transition is becoming an energy-security trade.

  5. VERIFIED. Intesa Sanpaolo raised its offer for Monte dei Paschi di Siena, adding another €800 million of cash value; its broader bid is worth about €34 billion. MPS shareholders vote on 29 October on a defensive strategy involving possible bids for Banco BPM and Banca Generali; approval could allow Intesa to withdraw its offer.

    The Italian banking contest is no longer a series of independent transactions. Each move now changes the feasible moves of every other player. The strategic question is shifting from who buys whom to which balance sheets become large enough to compete across Europe. signal: The Banking Union is increasingly being built by transaction rather than treaty.

03

Where Capital Is Likely to Move

accelerating vs decelerating

01

Accelerating

Nuclear and firm power; grid infrastructure; advanced semiconductors; HBM and networking; contracted AI infrastructure; infrastructure credit; defence technology; batteries and storage; electrified mobility; critical minerals; large European banks with consolidation optionality.

02

Decelerating

Uncontracted compute capacity; high-duration technology without cash-flow visibility; energy-intensive businesses without pricing power; leveraged infrastructure exposed to refinancing; fragmented European corporates dependent solely on retained earnings; weaker financial institutions unable to absorb rising technology and compliance costs.

Key takeaways

the Great Split moves into the balance sheet

  • The AI race began with models, moved to chips, and is now moving into power and capital.
  • State capital, vendor finance and private balance sheets are entering the same AI stack.
  • Europe's constraint is financial: 72% of firms rely on internal funds, against America's debt-plus-private-plus-public mix.
  • The strongest players will combine technology, infrastructure, cash flow and privileged financing into one system.
  • The first Great Split was technological; the next will be financial.

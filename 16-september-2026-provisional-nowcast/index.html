16 September 2026: Provisional Nowcast

The FDK Global Velocity Index holds at 108.0, but the Great Split is migrating from technology-versus-the-rest toward AI productive capacity versus the cost of absorbing it.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann September 16, 2026 7 min read Macro
+0.01Intelligence Infrastructure contribution
+0.01Innovation Velocity contribution
−0.01Enterprise Transformation contribution
−0.03Smart Capital contribution

“Volatile oil is a market variable. Persistent energy insecurity is a production variable.”

The weighted headline GVI is unchanged at 108.0 (2025=100) to one decimal place, though the unrounded movement is −0.02 points. Model confidence is 96%. Today produces a single country-level recalculation: the United Kingdom falls −0.1 to 82.3 as official labour data finally clear the persistence threshold.

The 30-day direction remains structurally accelerating — but the constraint on the AI Supercycle is no longer technological demand. It is the physical cost of sustaining it: energy, capital and grid capacity. Japan captures the central paradox today, with AI-driven industrial Velocity rising at the same moment the physical cost of maintaining it rises faster.

THE ENERGY TAX

The physical economy is beginning to impose an Energy Tax and a Capital Tax on intelligence — and the required AI productivity rises with every turn of the cost-of-capital screw.

01

Today's country signals

the movements and the material holds

  1. The August labour report is unusually consistent. Vacancies fell to 702,000, their lowest since 2014 outside the pandemic. Preliminary payroll employment declined 26,000 in August, with July revised to a 19,000 decline. Regular earnings growth slowed to 3.5%, and private-sector regular pay rose only 2.9% — the weakest since late 2020.

    The UK had recently been upgraded as digital services, computer hardware investment and business investment accelerated. Today reveals the missing transmission: investment and productive capacity rise while vacancies, hiring and payroll employment fall. The GVI reduces the UK by only 0.1 — via Enterprise Transformation and Discovery Capacity — with Smart Capital providing a partial offset, because softer wage growth makes an immediate wage-price spiral less likely. signal: This is not AI causing job losses; it is productive investment and labour absorption moving in different directions.

  2. August exports rose 19.3% year on year, a twelfth consecutive monthly increase, led by semiconductor-related shipments; exports to the US rose 24.9% and to China 20.6%. Yet imports surged 28%, their largest jump in nearly four years. Crude-oil import volumes rose only 3.6% but their value jumped 58.7%, producing a ¥1.106 trillion trade deficit.

    Two chains of causality run simultaneously: AI demand → semiconductors → exports → corporate Velocity; and geopolitical disruption → oil above $100 → import bill → inflation → BOJ tightening → higher capital cost. The effects offset almost exactly, so Japan stays at 84.9. signal: Energy intensity is becoming a tax on Intelligence Velocity for import-dependent economies.

  3. Hitachi Energy announced a $528 million transformer factory in Mississippi, its largest single US manufacturing investment. Transformers are a critical physical bottleneck for electricity-intensive data centres and grid capacity — high-quality Intelligence Infrastructure evidence, but below the national materiality threshold. The financial offset is larger: the 10-year Treasury yield breached 5%, its highest since 2007, and markets assigned a 94.5% probability to a 25bp Fed increase later today. Wall Street fell for a second session. signal: The Fed decision falls after the evidence cutoff and is excluded; the US remains 94.4.

  4. Eindhoven-based Axelera AI launched its second-generation Europa inference chip, with its processors set to feature in selected Dell and Supermicro products. It claims more than 600 customers, signed contracts worth tens of millions, and a potential pipeline of as much as $1.5 billion — though Reuters notes this is not yet orders or revenue. It extends the chain ASML lithography → advanced-node manufacturing → European chip design → inference → AI factories. signal: Additionality is real, but one day after the +0.2 High-NA upgrade the realised magnitude stays below threshold; the Netherlands holds at 80.4, momentum strengthening.

02

The material holds

signals that inform but do not move the score

81.5

France — domestic demand weakens

The Banque de France cut its 2026 GDP projection to 0.4% from 0.5%, citing weak consumption, cautious investment, expensive financing and energy uncertainty, and expects unemployment at 8.4% by year-end. A survey of 8,500 executives still points to ~0.1% Q3 growth. A forecast revision, not realised deterioration — France holds at 81.5.

83.2

Germany — conditions improve, risk remains

ZEW current conditions improved materially from −61.1 to −47.1 and expectations edged up to 34.7, but the expectations reading undershot consensus and ZEW warned elevated energy costs remain a significant risk. Positive but insufficiently directional — Germany holds at 83.2.

75.0

Spain — energy-led inflation

Harmonised inflation rose sharply to 4.6% in August from 3.9%, though core eased to 2.9%. The final reading largely confirms the flash estimate and the acceleration is energy-related rather than broad-based — Spain holds at 75.0.

Key takeaways

what today changes

  • The GVI holds at 108.0; the United Kingdom is the only recalculation, falling −0.1 to 82.3.
  • UK labour data show productive investment and labour absorption diverging — vacancies at their lowest since 2014 outside the pandemic.
  • Japan's offsetting export surge (+19.3%) and import shock (crude value +58.7%) leave it unchanged at 84.9 but define today's paradox.
  • The US holds at 94.4 as a $528m transformer plant is outweighed by a 10-year yield breaching 5%.
  • Persistent energy insecurity — not volatile oil — is what enters the GVI.

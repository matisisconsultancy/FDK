The Intelligence Trade Paradox

The AI Supercycle is making the world more technologically interconnected — and more strategically contested.

The AI Supercycle is accelerating global industrial trade while increasing the geopolitical and financial risks surrounding the technologies that drive it. Three developments define today's Global Velocity assessment, with evidence cut off November 10, 2026.

China's October exports rose 35.2%, pushing its cumulative merchandise surplus beyond $1 trillion. Nvidia's deepening governance and national-security controversy has renewed congressional pressure to restrict China's chip access. Brent crude fell 5.8% to $92.57, easing inflation concerns and helping equities recover. The global AI economy is becoming more commercially interconnected precisely as the technologies underpinning it become more strategically contested.

01

China — The AI Supercycle Reshapes Global Trade

record surplus

October exports rose 35.2% year over year in dollar terms (36.3% in September); imports accelerated to 41.6% from 31.0%. The cumulative merchandise surplus reached roughly $1.04 trillion for January–October, while October's monthly surplus narrowed to $105.3 billion from about $133.5 billion.

China is capturing a significant share of global AI-infrastructure, electronics and advanced-manufacturing demand. Mechanical and electrical exports rose 25.2% in renminbi terms over ten months; trade with ASEAN grew 24.2%, versus 8.5% with the EU and 6.3% with the US.

The supply chain remains globally interconnected, China's export performance increasingly depends on capital expenditure outside China, and its growing surplus may intensify pressure for protective measures. Dollar trade values reflect prices, FX, volumes and base effects — not equivalent gains in physical output.

02

United States — AI Security Becomes a Capital-Market Variable

repricing

On November 8, Nvidia's board announced an independent investigation into reports that CEO Jensen Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, stating it had no reason to believe security or confidential information had been compromised. On November 9, President Trump said the FBI was examining the matter; the allegations remain unproven. Nvidia shares fell 4.3%, cutting roughly $190 billion in market value.

Reuters reported lawmakers reviving three measures: the AI Overwatch Act (chip-export oversight), the Chip Security Act (chip-tracking to limit smuggling) and the MATCH Act (allied coordination on equipment restrictions). None are law. The debate raises a new question: how to value strategically essential technology companies when market access depends on national-security decisions.

03

Europe and Smart Capital

uneven, then relief

Euro-area services production rose 0.3% in August and 2.2% year over year; information and communication output gained 1.2% month over month and 5.9% annually, while professional and technical services fell 0.8%. Italy's services output declined 0.3% annually and Denmark's 5.3% — Europe's digital economy is expanding faster than aggregate activity, but unevenly.

The November 9 recovery provided a counterweight: the S&P 500 rose about 2.3%, the Nasdaq 2.7%, AMD 13.3% and CoreWeave 5.6%, as Brent fell 5.8% to $92.57. Lower energy costs eased inflation concerns, but one session does not establish a durable change in policy or financing costs — the US 10-year yield remains about 4.87%.

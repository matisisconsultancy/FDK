The Cost of Capital Strikes Back

Jobs are cooling the Fed and France is fracturing the euro, but energy, sovereign debt and AI infrastructure are keeping the long end dangerously expensive.

FdK Francesco de Leo Kaufmann October 5, 2026 6 min read Capital
$1.1178EUR/USD, after a 17-month low of $1.1161
5.27%U.S. 10Y Treasury — the global capital hurdle rate

$101.75Brent crude, inflation floor still elevated

“Speed still creates advantage — but only for those who can afford the cost of speed.”

Markets enter Monday with a deceptively supportive combination: softer U.S. employment, a sharply lower probability of an October Fed hike, and resilient Asian equities. But underneath, the global cost of capital is not falling. It is being redistributed.

Sovereign risk is migrating toward Europe, energy remains above the inflation threshold, and AI is absorbing capital, electricity and balance-sheet capacity at industrial scale. The next market rotation will therefore be determined less by whether central banks pause than by who can still finance growth above a 5% long-term risk-free rate.

The fundamental question for October: can equities continue to price an AI-led productivity supercycle while sovereign yields, energy costs and infrastructure financing simultaneously raise the hurdle rate on every new dollar of capital? The market is no longer debating whether AI investment continues. It is debating who can finance it, at what cost, with which energy base — and with what economic return.

KEY NUMBERS BEFORE EUROPE OPENS

The France–Germany 10Y spread is above 150 bps, the U.S. 2Y sits at 4.81%, gold trades at $4,132/oz, and the October Fed-hike probability has fallen below 20% from 64% a week ago — while eurozone inflation jumped to 3.8% (core 2.5%) and unemployment ticked to 4.2%.

The Five Overnight Signals

one system, not six stories

  1. The euro fell more than 0.8% in Asia to $1.1161 before recovering. The French 10-year premium over Germany has moved above 150 basis points, French bond futures weakened and Bund futures rose. This is no longer principally an FX story — it is a capital-allocation story. France is beginning to reprice the euro-area sovereign hierarchy. The first transmission channel is EUR/USD; the second will be banks; the third will be peripheral spreads. signal: Watch Italy — if French stress widens without dragging BTPs higher, the euro sovereign hierarchy is changing.

  2. Payrolls rose only 29,000 against 90,000 expected; unemployment rose to 4.2% and wage growth slowed to 3.0%. Markets now assign under 20% to an October hike. Yet the 10-year yield remains around 5.27%. Monetary tightening may pause; financial tightening has not. The front end is responding to weaker employment; the long end is responding to fiscal supply, energy inflation, AI investment demand and structural capital scarcity. signal: A Fed pause is bullish for duration only if inflation and sovereign supply cooperate — right now neither does.

  3. OPEC+ kept November targets unchanged and Brent remains above $100 despite rising Middle Eastern exports and reserve releases. Core OPEC+ output stays well below pre-war levels, and the 2027 capacity review is delayed by the Iran conflict. Eurozone inflation simultaneously jumped from 3.2% to 3.8%, driven by energy and food, with core at 2.5%. Energy now raises inflation, widens fiscal deficits and lifts the discount rate on AI infrastructure at once. signal: $100 Brent raises the hurdle rate for data centers, logistics, reshoring and European fiscal policy — the second-order effect is capital rationing.

  4. Japan offers the clearest template: JERA, Dell and RHAELM are developing a $15 billion, 400-MW hyperscale AI data center near Tokyo, with JERA supplying power for 15–25 years and Apollo expected as strategic financial partner. The model is designed to scale into several gigawatts. It combines energy, land, accelerated computing, infrastructure finance and long-duration contracts — the emerging architecture of the Industrial-AI economy. signal: Competitive advantage is moving from the chip alone to the full stack: power, grid, cooling, land, financing, compute and deployment speed.

  5. ECB research shows roughly 72% of euro-area firms expect to finance AI internally, only 16% via bank loans, 6% equity or venture capital, and just 1% debt securities. Meanwhile Reuters estimates worldwide data-center investment could exceed $30 trillion by 2050. The U.S. finances AI through capital markets, hyperscaler balance sheets, private credit and infrastructure finance; Europe still finances much of it from retained earnings. signal: Capital markets let winners scale before cash flow arrives — internal financing forces companies to wait. Velocity compounds the gap.

MORNING VELOCITY READING

FDK Reading: 8.4 / 10 — High Velocity, Higher Fragility. Growth velocity is moderating, capital velocity is accelerating toward infrastructure, financial conditions remain restrictive, and European sovereign risk is rising. The system keeps accelerating, but the cost of maintaining that acceleration is rising faster.

The Five Takeaways

what to carry into the open

  • The Fed pause is not financial easing — the 10-year Treasury is the real hurdle rate.
  • France is now the euro-area stress test — watch sovereign-to-bank transmission at the European open.
  • $100 oil is becoming structural enough to alter inflation, fiscal policy and infrastructure returns.
  • AI is becoming an energy-and-finance industry — the chip is no longer the full strategic unit.
  • The Great Split is moving from technology adoption to capital formation — those who can finance acceleration will compound.
